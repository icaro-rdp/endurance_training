# The 'Right' VO2max Training and 30/15s Epilogue: De-Dogmatizing High-Cadence, Hard-Start, and Intermittent Protocols — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Rory Porteous (Watts Doc #55)_

---

## What Is the "Right" VO2max Training?

Historically, endurance training culture has swung between rigid interval dogmas: from traditional steady $4 \times 4\text{ min}$ or $5 \times 5\text{ min}$ at fixed percentages of FTP (e.g., $110\text{--}120\%$), to the high-cadence/hard-start "Empirical Cycling protocol," to intermittent micro-intervals ($30/15\text{s}$ or $40/20\text{s}$).

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                          THE SPECTRUM OF VO2MAX INTERVENTIONS                          │
├───────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┤
│ Format                │ Primary Physiological Target   │ Best Applied Scenario         │
├───────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┤
│ **High-Cadence Long** │ • Maximal stroke volume ($SV$) │ • In-season ceiling raising   │
│ ($3\text{--}5$ min)   │ • High preload / low torque    │ • Experienced riders needing  │
│                       │ • Pure central aerobic stimulus│   clean central stimulus      │
├───────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┤
│ **Short Intermittent**│ • $VO_2$ + Anaerobic Cap ($W'$)│ • Early season / time crunches│
│ ($30/15$, $40/20$)    │ • Repeated sprint repeatability│ • Criterium, CX, and MTB      │
│                       │ • High mechanical power output │   event-specific preparation  │
├───────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┤
│ **Steady Sub-Max**    │ • High cumulative duration     │ • Diesel / TT phenotypes      │
│ ($3 \times 8$ min)    │ • Fractional utilization       │ • Low risk of neuromuscular   │
│                       │ • Moderate cardiac stretch     │   exhaustion                  │
└───────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┘
```

**The Core Principle:** There is no single universal "best" protocol. VO2max training is a physiological objective (maximizing central cardiac output, stroke volume, and pulmonary ventilation time), not a specific wattage or interval duration.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. High Cadence and the Preload/Muscle-Pump Mechanism

Why prescribe high cadence ($\ge 105\text{--}115\text{ RPM}$) during maximal aerobic intervals?

```
  High Pedaling Cadence (105-115+ RPM)
     │
     ├─► Muscle Pump Action ↑ ──► Venous Return ↑ ──► End-Diastolic Preload ↑ ──► Stroke Volume ↑
     │
     └─► Muscular Torque per Pedal Stroke ↓ ──► Peripheral Fatigue ↓ ──► Repeatability & Volume ↑
```

1. **Venous Return & Frank-Starling Law:** Rapid, rhythmic muscular contractions compress intramuscular veins, augmenting venous blood return to the right atrium. The resulting increase in end-diastolic volume stretches left ventricular walls, maximizing stroke volume via the **Frank-Starling mechanism**.
2. **Torque Sparing:** High cadence decreases the required force (torque) per pedal stroke for a given wattage ($P = \tau \times \omega$). This minimizes high-tension recruitment of fast-twitch glycolytic motor units, reducing localized muscular acidification and preserving interval completion.

### 2. Debunking "VO2max Power" as a Fixed Number

* **The Fallacy:** Prescribing VO2max intervals at a rigid percentage of threshold (e.g., "$120\% \text{ FTP}$").
* **Physiological Reality:** 
  * Maximal oxygen uptake ($VO_{2\text{max}}$) can be elicited across a wide range of power outputs depending on interval duration, starting pacing, and anaerobic work capacity ($W'$).
  * An athlete with a large $W'$ can hold $140\% \text{ FTP}$ for 3 minutes, while a diesel athlete with a small $W'$ might reach $VO_{2\text{max}}$ at $112\% \text{ FTP}$ over 5 minutes.
  * Prescribing by fixed watts leads to under-stimulating anaerobic phenotypes (who cruise sub-maximally) and blowing up diesel phenotypes (who fail in minute 2).

### 3. Re-Evaluating the Rønnestad 30/15s Study

The landmark study by Rønnestad et al. (2015) claimed "superior adaptations" following $3 \times 9.5\text{ min}$ of $30/15\text{s}$ compared to $4 \times 5\text{ min}$ continuous intervals in well-trained cyclists. A deeper scientific breakdown clarifies the findings:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        RØNNESTAD 30/15s DATA DECONSTRUCTION                            │
├───────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┤
│ Metric                │ 30/15s Group vs. 4x5 min Group│ Statistical & Scientific Truth │
├───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┤
│ **$VO_{2\text{max}}$ Gain (L/min)**│ Identical (+1.5–2.0%)         │ $p = 0.49$ (No group diff.)    │
├───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┤
│ **$VO_{2\text{max}}$ (mL/kg/min)** │ Identical                     │ $p = 0.28\text{--}0.23$ (No diff.)│
├───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Ramp Peak Power**   │ 30/15s higher (+8.5% vs +4.5%)│ Statistically significant      │
├───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┤
│ **20-Minute Power**   │ 30/15s higher (+4.7% vs -1.4%)│ Statistically significant      │
└───────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┘
```

* **The Missing Mechanism — Anaerobic Work Capacity ($W'$):**
  * The short-interval group performed high-power intermittent sprints ($>130\%\text{ FTP}$ bursts), driving significant neuromuscular and anaerobic capacity adaptations ($W'$).
  * An expanded $W'$ allows an athlete to produce significantly higher power in a ramp test and a 20-minute time trial **without requiring an increase in true $VO_{2\text{max}}$**.
  * The continuous group rode at self-paced sub-maximal levels ($4 \times 5\text{ min}$), likely pacing too conservatively to maximize either central or anaerobic systems.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The VO2max Pacing Spectrum: Finding What Works

```
  ALL-OUT SPRINT START              "START HARD" PACING                EVEN / PROGRESSIVE PACING
  (1000W -> Fade to 200W)           (+10-15% over target)              (Steady Watts, Max Breathing)
  ┌──────────────────────┐          ┌──────────────────────┐           ┌──────────────────────┐
  │ • Severe fatigue     │          │ • Accelerates VO2    │           │ • Highly repeatable  │
  │ • Ruptures set       │          │ • Breaks wattage lock│           │ • Low motor fatigue  │
  │ • Avoid for multi-rep│          │ • Smooth fade allowed│           │ • Great for diesels  │
  └──────────────────────┘          └──────────────────────┘           └──────────────────────┘
```

1. **The "Start-Hard" Prescription:** 
   * Begin the first 30–45 seconds $\sim 10\text{--}15\%$ above your sustainable 3–5 min power.
   * As breathing and heart rate pin to maximum, allow power to settle naturally into a maximal sustainable effort.
   * **Do not start with an all-out 1000-watt sprint**, which creates catastrophic neuromuscular fatigue and ruins interval repeatability.
2. **The Progressive Ramp Prescription (Alternative for High-Anxiety Riders):**
   * Start 10–20 watts below estimated target for the first 60 seconds.
   * Build power into the middle of the interval, driving cadence to $>110\text{ RPM}$ and finishing the final 90 seconds at absolute maximum ventilation.

### 2. High-Yield Interval Formats

| Protocol | Structure | Target Cadence | Pacing Rule | Best Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Classic $5 \times 4\text{ min}$** | 4 min on / 4 min easy | $105\text{--}115\text{ RPM}$ | Maximal sustainable; allow power to float | Gold standard for raising $VO_{2\text{max}}$ ceiling |
| **Short-Interval $30/15\text{s}$** | $3 \times (10 \times 30\text{s}$ on / $15\text{s}$ off) | Natural ($95\text{--}105\text{ RPM}$) | Hard-start on each 30s; spin easy on 15s | Criterium/CX preparation; builds $W'$ + aerobic flux |
| **Decreasing $5\text{--}4\text{--}3\text{--}2\text{--}1$** | Descending durations (1:1 rest) | $>105\text{ RPM}$ | Increase power as duration drops | High psychological engagement; novice friendly |
| **Sub-Max Diesel $3 \times 8\text{ min}$** | 8 min on / 4 min easy | $90\text{--}100\text{ RPM}$ | $\sim 108\text{--}112\%\text{ FTP}$ (Severe domain) | Time-trialists; high volume $VO_2$ accumulation |

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The Extreme VO2max "Block" Trap:** Prescribing 3–4 weeks of continuous daily or near-daily VO2max sessions (e.g., 6–9 sessions in 2 weeks). In elite research (e.g., Berger et al., 1999), extreme shock blocks caused profound immunosuppression, marked ferritin depletion, and severe mood disturbance. Over 90% of amateur and master cyclists crash their endocrine systems before realizing any supercompensation.

1. **Viewing Heart Rate as an Immediate On-Screen Target:**
   * Heart rate has an inherent lag ($30\text{--}90\text{ seconds}$). Chasing target heart rate during the first 60 seconds leads to excessive over-pacing. Check heart rate only after the interval to confirm maximal cardiac drift.
2. **Assuming Maximum Historic HR Must Be Reached:**
   * In a well-trained state, expanded blood volume and enhanced stroke volume lower maximal heart rate by 3–8 bpm. Reaching historic peak HR is not required for a 100% $VO_{2\text{max}}$ training effect.
3. **Terrain Inconsistency:**
   * Attempting high-cadence VO2max intervals on undulating terrain where changing road gradients force cadence down into low-torque grinding ($<75\text{ RPM}$). Perform on steady climbs, flat roads with open space, or an indoor ergometer.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### VO2max Protocol Selector

| Athlete Profile / Scenario | Primary Goal | Recommended Format | Key Execution Constraint |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Experienced Cyclist (Mid-Season)** | Raise absolute $VO_{2\text{max}}$ ceiling | $5 \times 3.5\text{--}4.5\text{ min}$ @ $110+\text{ RPM}$ | Rest as needed (3–5 min); unconstrained watts |
| **Punchy Criterium / CX Racer** | Repeatability + Anaerobic capacity | $30/15\text{s}$ or $40/20\text{s}$ sets ($3 \times 8\text{ min}$) | Keep 15s recovery strictly easy ($<50\%\text{ FTP}$) |
| **Diesel / Long Time-Trialist** | High time in severe domain | $3 \times 8\text{ min}$ or $4 \times 6\text{ min}$ @ $110\%\text{ FTP}$ | Controlled pacing; avoid early failure |
| **Returning from Illness (Time-Crunched)** | Fast combined $VO_2$ & $W'$ surge | $2 \text{ sessions/wk of } 30/15\text{s}$ | Limit block to 2 weeks maximum |

### Coach & Athlete Action Checklist

* [ ] **Eliminate Fixed Wattage Targets:** Instruct athletes to ride by maximum breathing (hyperventilation) and perceived exertion rather than staring at a fixed power number.
* [ ] **Implement High Cadence:** Target $\ge 105\text{ RPM}$ to engage the muscle pump, maximize venous return, and spare peripheral muscular torque.
* [ ] **Monitor Systemic Recovery:** Track morning resting HR, sleep quality, and motivation; pull the plug on a VO2max block if power drops by $>10\%$ across consecutive sessions.
* [ ] **Match Rest to Intended Stimulus:** Allow full recovery ($3\text{--}5\text{ minutes}$) between long continuous efforts to ensure each repetition hits maximal cardiac output.
