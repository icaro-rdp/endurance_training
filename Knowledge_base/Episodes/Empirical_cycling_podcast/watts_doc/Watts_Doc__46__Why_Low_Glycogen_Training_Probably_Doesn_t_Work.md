# Why Low Glycogen Training Probably Doesn't Work — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Rory Porteous (Watts Doc #46)_

---

## What Is the "Train Low" Paradigm?

The **"Train Low, Compete High"** model is a dietary and periodization strategy based on the hypothesis that exercising with reduced muscle glycogen stores amplifies the cellular stress signal, thereby augmenting aerobic training adaptations (mitochondrial biogenesis, capillarization, and oxidative enzyme capacity).

Popularized by studies exploring double-session training (two workouts in one day with no carbohydrate refilling between bouts), fasted morning training, and carbohydrate restriction, the framework assumes:

$$\text{Low Glycogen} \longrightarrow \uparrow \text{Cellular Metabolic Stress} \longrightarrow \uparrow \text{p38 MAPK / AMPK} \longrightarrow \uparrow \text{PGC-1}\alpha \longrightarrow \uparrow \text{Endurance Performance}$$

However, a granular evaluation of the molecular data and human performance trials demonstrates that while substrate oxidation shifts toward higher fat usage, **endurance performance is not enhanced**, and training quality is systematically compromised.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The p38 MAPK Pathway and PGC-1α Transcription

Mitogen-activated protein kinase **p38 MAPK** is a key cellular stress transducer in skeletal muscle. In response to exercise-induced stress, p38 MAPK phosphorylates downstream transcription factors, driving mitochondrial proliferation:

* **The Molecular Link (Akimoto et al., 2005):** In cell culture and transgenic rodent models, constitutively active p38 MAPK directly activates the **PGC-1α promoter**, leading to robust increases in PGC-1α protein expression and cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV) activity.
* **The Low-Glycogen Disconnect:** Popular literature often claims that glycogen depletion potentiates p38 MAPK activation during exercise. However, controlled human biopsy trials (e.g., Yeo et al., 2008) show **no statistically significant difference in p38 MAPK phosphorylation** between normal-glycogen and low-glycogen exercise bouts.

### 2. The Double-Day Training Protocol: Molecular Markers vs. Real-World Performance

The landmark Yeo et al. (2008) protocol examined two groups of trained cyclists ($VO_2\text{peak} \sim 61\text{ mL/kg/min}$) over a 3-week block:
* **High Glycogen Group (Norm):** Day 1: 100 min tempo ($70\% VO_2\text{peak}$); Day 2: $8 \times 5\text{ min}$ HIT ($1\text{ min}$ rest); alternating daily with full carbohydrate refueling.
* **Low Glycogen Group (Double):** Exercised every second day, performing the 100 min tempo ride in the morning, followed by 1 hour of carbohydrate-restricted rest, then completing the $8 \times 5\text{ min}$ HIT session in a glycogen-depleted state ($\sim 30\text{–}50\%$ reduction in pre-interval muscle glycogen).

```
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │                     YEO ET AL. (2008) PROTOCOL & RESULTS                         │
 ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │  MOLECULAR / ENZYMATIC FINDINGS:                                                 │
 │  • AMPK Phosphorylation: +40% higher in Low Glycogen group                       │
 │  • Citrate Synthase & β-HAD: Significantly higher in Low Glycogen group          │
 │  • Substrate Oxidation: +30% higher Fat Oxidation during submaximal cycling     │
 │  • p38 MAPK Activation: NO SIGNIFICANT DIFFERENCE between groups                │
 ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │  PERFORMANCE OUTCOMES:                                                           │
 │  • Interval Training Power: Low Glycogen group produced significantly LESS power │
 │  • 60-Minute Time Trial: Both groups improved by identical ~10% (NO ADVANTAGE)   │
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 3. Substrate Adaptation vs. Performance Adaptation

A fundamental flaw in the "train low" rationale is conflating **enzymatic substrate adaptation** with **functional athletic performance**:
1. **Substrate Adaptation:** When muscle cells are starved of carbohydrate, they up-regulate fat transport proteins and $\beta$-oxidation enzymes ($\beta$-HAD, citrate synthase) simply to survive on available substrates.
2. **Performance Irrelevance:** Greater fat oxidation at submaximal intensities does **not** translate into higher threshold power or faster race times. Because cycling performance at threshold and above is governed by oxygen delivery and high-flux carbohydrate oxidation, sacrificing glycolytic power impairs competitiveness.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The Fueling Mandate for High-Quality Training

* **Fuel High-Intensity Sessions:** All interval workouts at or above Sweet Spot ($>88\%$ FTP) require high carbohydrate availability to maximize absolute mechanical power and total work output (kilojoules).
* **Target Intake:** Ingest $60\text{–}90+\text{ g/hr}$ of carbohydrates during strenuous workouts and maintain full baseline muscle glycogen prior to key sessions.
* **Progressive Overload Over Dietary Manipulation:** To stimulate mitochondrial adaptation and improve fatigue resistance, progressively increase **Time in Zone (TIZ)** at Sweet Spot and FTP rather than attempting to artificially increase metabolic strain via glycogen depletion:

$$\text{Progression: } 2 \times 20\text{ min} \longrightarrow 3 \times 20\text{ min} \longrightarrow 2 \times 35\text{ min} \longrightarrow 1 \times 60\text{ min at } 90\text{–}95\% \text{ FTP}$$

```
                PROGRESSIVE OVERLOAD VS. TRAIN-LOW STIMULUS
 ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │  FUELED PROGRESSIVE OVERLOAD:                                              │
 │  High Glycogen ──► High Absolute Power ──► Extended Duration ──► Robust    │
 │  Mitochondrial Biogenesis + High Neuromuscular Output + Rapid Recovery.    │
 ├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │  TRAIN-LOW REGIMEN:                                                        │
 │  Low Glycogen ──► Reduced Power ──► High RPE & Lethargy ──► Equal 60m TT   │
 │  Gains + Suppressed Immune System + Prolonged Recovery Lag.                │
 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2. The Niche Exception: Ultra-Endurance Logistics

The single context where low-carbohydrate or fat-adapted training may offer a practical (non-physiological) advantage is **ultra-endurance events ($>24\text{ hours}$, Audax, self-supported bikepacking)**:
* **Logistical Benefit:** In events where carrying or acquiring $90\text{ g/hr}$ of carbohydrate is impossible and the intensity remains strictly low ($\le \text{LT1}$ / Zone 2), enhanced baseline fat oxidation reduces reliance on dense exogenous food supplies.
* **Execution Rule:** Even in ultra-distance scenarios, athletes should still consume carbohydrates on the bike to protect liver glycogen, support central nervous system function, and prevent cognitive fatigue.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The Systemic Breakdown of Chronic Low-Glycogen Training:** Chronic or unmonitored train-low protocols trigger elevated baseline cortisol, severe muscle protein breakdown (gluconeogenesis), immunosuppression, and sleep impairment due to cerebral glucose deficiency.

### Key Errors and Misconceptions:

1. **Fasted Morning Rides as a "Base Shortcut":**
   * Fasted morning rides do not accelerate base aerobic adaptations compared to normally fueled Zone 2 rides. They merely elevate perceived effort and limit ride duration.
2. **Impaired Interval Training Power:**
   * Attempting HIIT, VO2max, or anaerobic capacity intervals with low glycogen results in immediate power collapse. The athlete trains below their functional neuromuscular capacity, blunting the central cardiovascular and high-threshold motor unit adaptations.
3. **Severe Central Nervous System Fatigue:**
   * The human brain consumes $\sim 120\text{ g}$ of glucose per day. Exercising with low liver and muscle glycogen deprives the brain of stable glucose flux, leading to brain fog, poor coordination, and disrupted slow-wave sleep.
4. **Failure to Inspect Study Details:**
   * Many claims supporting low-glycogen training rely on surrogate molecular markers (e.g., transient increases in PGC-1α mRNA) rather than measurable end-point performance tests (Time Trials, Critical Power, or TTE).

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Low Glycogen vs. Normal Glycogen Training Comparison

| Metric / Outcome | Low Glycogen Training ("Train Low") | Normal / Fueled Training ("Train High") | Physiological Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **p38 MAPK Activation** | No significant elevation vs. normal | Baseline exercise-induced increase | Low glycogen provides **zero extra p38 stimulus**. |
| **AMPK Phosphorylation** | Elevated ($\sim +40\%$) | Moderate / Intensity-dependent | Elevated due to high cellular energetic distress. |
| **Fat Oxidation Rate** | Increased by $\sim 20\text{–}30\%$ | Normal submaximal reliance | Reflects substrate scarcity acclimation, not fitness. |
| **Interval Power Output** | Significantly reduced | Maximized | Low glycogen directly impairs high-intensity training. |
| **Time Trial Performance** | $\sim 10\%$ improvement (3 weeks) | $\sim 10\%$ improvement (3 weeks) | **Identical performance gains**; no train-low superiority. |
| **Immune & Sleep Quality** | High stress, suppressed immunity, poor sleep | Stable recovery, optimal sleep | Train-low introduces severe non-functional fatigue. |

### Coach & Athlete Action Checklist

* [ ] **Fuel All High-Intensity Workouts:** Ingest ample carbohydrates in the 24 hours preceding threshold, sweet spot, or VO2max intervals.
* [ ] **Avoid Purposeful Fasted Hard Training:** Never perform high-intensity or interval sessions in a fasted or calorie-deprived state.
* [ ] **Prioritize Ride Duration Over Glycogen Starvation:** To build aerobic base and fat oxidation naturally, increase weekly Zone 2 volume while remaining well-fed.
* [ ] **Monitor Recovery and Sleep Quality:** If experiencing persistent lethargy, heavy legs, or insomnia, immediately increase daily carbohydrate intake.
* [ ] **Apply Progressive Overload Mechanically:** Increase interval volume ($2 \times 20\text{m} \rightarrow 3 \times 20\text{m}$) to drive adaptations rather than seeking dietary "hacks."
