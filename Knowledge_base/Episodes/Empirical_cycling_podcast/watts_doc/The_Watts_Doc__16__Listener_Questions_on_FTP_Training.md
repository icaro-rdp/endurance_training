# Listener Questions on FTP Training & Periodization — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson (Watts Doc #16)_

---

## What Is the Empirical Cycling FTP Training Framework?

Functional Threshold Power (FTP) training forms the cornerstone of endurance cycling preparation. In response to widespread listener inquiries across the cycling community, this guide synthesizes the 11 foundational coaching and physiological questions regarding threshold interval prescription, progression, execution, and troubleshooting.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
              FTP Progression Cycle: Push "Out", Then Pull "Up"
  
  [1. Base / Early Season] ──► Test Baseline FTP & TTE (e.g. 280W @ 40 min)
                                      │
  [2. Extend TTE "Out"]   ──► Progress 4x10 ──► 3x15 ──► 2x20 ──► 3x20 ──► 2x30 min @ 95% FTP
                                      │
  [3. Maximize TTE]       ──► TTE Reaches 65–75 min (280W @ 70 min) ──► Fractional Cap Hit
                                      │
  [4. Raise the Ceiling]  ──► 3–4 Week VO2max Block (Lift aerobic ceiling from above)
                                      │
  [5. Retest & Reset]     ──► New FTP: 300W; New TTE: ~35–45 min ──► Repeat Cycle "Out"
```

### 1. The Power-Duration Trade-off (Why 95% Beats 105%)
* **Metabolic Steady State:** At 92–98% FTP, lactate clearance matches production, and $W'$ (anaerobic work capacity) remains largely untouched. Muscle fibers operate in an oxidative steady state, permitting 45–90 minutes of total time-in-zone.
* **The >100% Failure Point:** At 102–105% FTP, hydrogen ions $[H^+]$ and inorganic phosphate $[P_i]$ accumulate, fast-twitch motor units rapidly fatigue, and $W'$ is depleted within 15–25 minutes, forcing the workout to end prematurely before sufficient aerobic volume is achieved.

### 2. TTE Contraction Following FTP Increases
* When an athlete gains 10–20 Watts of FTP (e.g., moving from 280W to 300W), the newly recruited higher-threshold motor units lack deep mitochondrial density and fatigue resistance.
* Consequently, **TTE naturally drops** (e.g., from 65 minutes down to 35–45 minutes). Coaches and athletes must expect this contraction and rebuild interval duration progressively at the new wattage.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions (11 Core Listener Questions)

### Q1: What is the ideal interval length and structure to start an FTP block?
* **Recommendation:** Begin with interval lengths scaled to tested baseline TTE.
* If baseline TTE is 35–45 minutes, start with $4 \times 10\text{ min}$ or $3 \times 12\text{ min}$ (rest: 3–4 min).
* Consolidate into longer continuous reps: $3 \times 15\text{ min} \rightarrow 2 \times 20\text{ min} \rightarrow 2 \times 25\text{ min} \rightarrow 3 \times 20\text{ min} \rightarrow 2 \times 30\text{ min}$.

### Q2: What exact percentage of FTP should intervals be ridden at?
* **Target:** **92% to 98% of FTP**.
* *Rationale:* Never target 100–105% on routine threshold days. Riding 5–10 Watts below FTP yields identical mitochondrial and capillarization stimulus while drastically reducing autonomic fatigue and guaranteeing workout completion.

### Q3: How long should recovery intervals be between threshold efforts?
* **Work-to-Rest Ratio:** Keep rest short, typically **3 to 5 minutes** (e.g., 4 min rest after a 15–20 min interval).
* *Mechanism:* Extended rest (>8–10 min) allows heart rate, core temperature, and oxygen kinetics to drop completely to baseline, requiring several minutes of the subsequent set to regain target aerobic flux.

### Q4: How many threshold sessions should be performed per week?
* **Standard Cadence:** **2 sessions per week** separated by 48–72 hours (e.g., Tuesday and Friday/Saturday).
* **3 Sessions per Week:** High risk of chronic overreaching; only appropriate for short 2-week overload blocks in highly trained athletes with high recovery capacity.
* **1 Session per Week:** Ideal for maintaining threshold fitness during racing blocks or high-volume base phases.

### Q5: Sweet Spot (88–93%) vs. Threshold (95–100%) — which is better?
* **Sweet Spot:** Highly repeatable, lower mental strain, excellent for building baseline volume (60–90 min time-in-zone) and winter training.
* **Threshold:** Higher time-efficiency, superior specificity for TTs and race-pace demands, stronger stimulus for raising lactate clearance kinetics. Both should be utilized progressively.

### Q6: Why is indoor trainer FTP often lower than outdoor FTP?
* **Thermoregulation:** Inadequate convective airflow indoors elevates core temperature and skin blood pooling, depressing stroke volume and raising heart rate for the same power.
* **Flywheel Inertia:** Indoor trainers often feature lower rotational inertia than outdoor riding, altering pedal dead-spot biomechanics and muscle activation patterns.
* **Fix:** Use industrial-grade blower fans (e.g., Lasko blowers), calibrate trainers after warm-up, and adjust indoor targets down 5–10 Watts if cooling is constrained.

### Q7: What cadence should be used during threshold intervals?
* **Preferred Cadence:** Self-selected natural cadence (typically **88–96 RPM**) to minimize neuromuscular fatigue.
* **Low-Cadence Variation (55–65 RPM):** Excellent secondary variation to increase motor unit recruitment tension and adapt fast-twitch fibers oxidatively, provided the athlete has no patellar/knee issues.

### Q8: How should threshold workouts be fueled?
* **Pre-Workout:** High carbohydrate availability (consume 1–2 g/kg carbohydrates 2–3 hours prior).
* **In-Ride:** Ingest **60–90g of carbohydrates per hour** (glucose/fructose mixes). Threshold work is heavily glycolytic; training fasted blunts interval quality and accelerates central fatigue.

### Q9: When and how should over/unders or bursts be introduced?
* **Timing:** Introduce during the final 3–4 weeks of a threshold block prior to racing.
* **Structure:** In a $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ or $2 \times 25\text{ min}$ set at 95% FTP, execute a **10-second seated maximal surge every 3–5 minutes**, immediately returning to threshold. This trains the body to clear lactate spikes under continuous oxidative load.

### Q10: How do you know when an FTP block has run its course?
* **Key Indicators:**
  1. Total time-in-zone reaches **60–75+ minutes** comfortably ($3 \times 20\text{ min}$ or $2 \times 30\text{ min}$).
  2. Power output and TTE fail to progress across 2 consecutive microcycles.
  3. Fractional utilization reaches $\sim 85\%$ of $VO_2max$ power.
* **Action:** Take a recovery week and pivot to a dedicated $VO_2max$ block to raise the aerobic ceiling.

### Q11: If you fail a workout halfway through, what should you do?
* **Protocol:** If power drops $>5\%$ or RPE spikes to maximal within the first 10–15 minutes, downshift immediately to Zone 2 endurance or spin easy and end the session.
* Do not attempt to salvage the session by doing short, ragged micro-intervals; failure indicates unrecovered systemic fatigue, glycogen depletion, or incipient illness.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The Constant Retesting Trap:** Re-testing FTP via formal ramp or 20-minute tests every 2–3 weeks. Progress should be tracked dynamically by adding interval duration during regular workouts rather than interrupting training blocks with exhausting tests.

1. **Forcing Wattage Increases Too Early:**
   * Trying to lift power by 5 Watts every week rather than extending interval length creates an unsustainable trajectory that ends in blown workouts.
2. **Ignoring Fueling on Hard Days:**
   * Attempting to perform $3 \times 20\text{ min}$ threshold sessions fasted or on low-carbohydrate diets drastically increases stress hormones (cortisol) and degrades muscular adaptations.
3. **Over-Extending Beyond 90 Minutes:**
   * Pushing single-session threshold time-in-zone past 80–90 minutes yields diminishing aerobic returns while creating massive recovery deficits.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### FTP Block Troubleshooting Decision Table

| Symptom / Scenario | Likely Root Cause | Immediate Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Failing rep 2 or 3 of threshold set** | Starting power set too high ($>100\%$ FTP) | Drop power target by 5–10 Watts (ride at 93–95%) |
| **HR drifts into $VO_2$ zone during interval** | FTP baseline is overestimated | Re-evaluate true MLSS using an open-ended long test |
| **Legs feel heavy / RPE 9/10 within 5 min** | Inadequate glycogen or accumulated fatigue | Convert session to Zone 1/2 easy spin; take rest day |
| **Indoor power is 15W lower than outdoors** | Inadequate fan cooling / thermal drift | Add high-velocity blower fans; lower indoor target 5–10W |
| **TTE reaches 70 min, but power won't budge** | Fractional ceiling reached ($VO_2max$ cap) | Conclude block, recover, and transition to $VO_2max$ block |

### Coach & Athlete Decision Checklist

* [ ] **Set Conservative Targets:** Prescribe threshold work at 92–98% of tested FTP.
* [ ] **Progress Duration Weekly:** Increase total time-in-zone by 5–10 minutes per week (e.g., $40 \rightarrow 50 \rightarrow 60 \rightarrow 70\text{ min}$).
* [ ] **Limit Frequency to 2x/Week:** Schedule adequate rest and endurance days between threshold sessions.
* [ ] **Fuel with Carbohydrates:** Consume 60–90g carbs/hr during all threshold workouts.
* [ ] **Transition to $VO_2max$ When Plateaued:** Recognize when fractional ceiling is reached and shift focus to raising the absolute aerobic ceiling.
