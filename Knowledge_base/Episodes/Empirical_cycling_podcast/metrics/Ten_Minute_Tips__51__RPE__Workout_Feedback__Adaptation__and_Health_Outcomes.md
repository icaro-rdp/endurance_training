# Ten Minute Tips #51: RPE, Workout Feedback, Adaptation, and Health Outcomes — Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Dr. Fabiano (Ten Minute Tips #51)_

---

## What Are RPE and Workout Feedback in Applied Exercise Physiology?

The **Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE)** is the psychophysiological integration of internal strain—originating from cardiorespiratory work, metabolic acid-base disturbance, thermal strain, and central nervous system (CNS) motor unit drive.

Rather than being a "soft" or unscientific metric, perceived exertion represents the brain's integrated calculation of physiological stress. When combined with objective metrics (power, cadence, heart rate) and contextual metadata (sleep, nutrition, environmental stress), RPE completes the feedback loop required to drive physiological progression while avoiding overtraining.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
[External Work: Power] ──> [Physiological Strain: HR, Lactate, Heat] ──> [Brain Integration (Master Gland)]
                                                                                  │
                                                                       ┌──────────┴──────────┐
                                                                       ▼                     ▼
                                                                [Borg 6-20 Scale]     [Borg CR10 Scale]
                                                                (Linear / HR proxy)   (Exponential / Pain & Lactate)
```

### 1. The Physiological Origins of Borg Scales
- **Borg 6–20 Scale (Linear Model):**
  Developed by Gunnar Borg to correlate directly with heart rate in healthy young males:
  $$\text{Target HR} \approx \text{BPE (6–20)} \times 10$$
  A rating of 6 corresponds to resting baseline ($\approx 60\text{ bpm}$), while 20 corresponds to maximal effort ($\approx 200\text{ bpm}$). However, this linear relationship breaks down in older adults, elite athletes with sport-specific bradycardia, and children.
- **Borg CR10 Scale (Category-Ratio / Exponential Model):**
  Engineered to track **non-linear, exponential biological responses**—such as exponential blood lactate accumulation, nociceptive pain signaling, and high-threshold Type IIx motor unit recruitment above Critical Power / FTP. The perceptual jump between an 8, 9, and 10 on the CR10 scale represents massive non-linear escalations in metabolic acidosis ($[H^+]$), potassium flux, and central motor drive.

### 2. Homeostasis vs. Allostasis in Training Adaptation
- **Homeostasis:** The biochemical tendency of the organism to maintain internal equilibrium (temperature, pH, blood glucose, ATP levels).
- **Allostasis:** Achieving physiological stability through structural and functional change. When a training stressor sufficiently disrupts homeostasis, cellular signaling cascades (AMPK, PGC-1α, CaMK, mTOR) stimulate allostatic adaptation—such as mitochondrial biogenesis, increased capillarization, and left ventricular remodeling.
- **The Well-Trained Plateau:** In untrained individuals, minimal exercise disrupts homeostasis. In well-trained athletes, the system easily absorbs moderate loads without disturbing homeostasis. Progression requires targeted, heavy training "punches" (e.g., concentrated VO2max blocks or extended threshold intervals) to force allostatic remodeling.

```
[Untrained Athlete]  ──(Moderate Load)──> [Homeostasis Disrupted] ──> [Rapid Adaptation]
[Well-Trained Rider] ──(Moderate Load)──> [Easily Handled]       ──> [No Adaptation / Homeostasis Wins]
[Well-Trained Rider] ──(Targeted Shock)─> [Homeostasis Broken]   ──> [Allostatic Supercompensation]
```

### 3. HRV Mechanics During High-Strain Mesocycles
- **Autonomic Modulation:** High-density interval blocks (e.g., VO2max microcycles, double-threshold days) drive heavy sympathetic nervous system activation and suppress parasympathetic (vagal) tone.
- **Multi-Day Trends vs. Daily Noise:** Single-day HRV readings are heavily confounded by hydration, circadian timing, and optical wrist-sensor noise. However, a downward 3–5 day rolling trend during heavy blocks indicates accumulating systemic fatigue.
- **Recovery Slopes:** Following high-strain blocks, true easy recovery spins produce a steep, rapid rebound in HRV, whereas continuing to ride at moderate "endurance" volume flattens the recovery slope.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Structuring the Workout Feedback Loop (Metadata)
Athletes and self-coached riders should systematically log qualitative metadata alongside power and HR files:

| Feedback Category | Key Metrics to Record | Physiological Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Internal Intensity** | RPE (CR10: 1–10) | Calibration against expected zone targets; identifies decoupling |
| **Recovery Status** | Sleep duration/quality, acute life stress | Determines whether elevated RPE is training-induced or systemic |
| **Thermal / Environment** | Indoor trainer vs outdoor, humidity, temp | High core temp elevates HR and RPE independently of fitness |
| **Fueling / Hydration** | Pre-ride carbs, intra-ride g/hr, fluids (L) | Identifies low-glycogen premature failure vs true functional limits |
| **Equipment Context** | Power meter change, bike fit adjustments | Distinguishes measurement error from true physiological change |

### 2. Longitudinal Testing & Model Anchors
- **Frequency:** Conduct formal performance testing (Time-to-Exhaustion / progressive FTP testing, max sprint tests) every **8 to 12 weeks**.
- **Model Calibration:** Software models (like WKO5 or CP models) require true maximal historical efforts to set accurate intensity targets. When transitioning from indoor winter training to outdoor spring riding, perform fresh baseline testing to recalibrate RPE and power zones.

### 3. Health & Longevity: The J-Shaped Volume Curve

```
Morbidity /
Mortality Risk
   │
   │  \  (Sedentary: High Risk)
   │   \
   │    \____________________  (Sweet Spot: 150-300 min/wk + Strength)
   │                         \
   │                          \________/¯¯¯¯¯¯  (Elite Volume: High Performance,
   └──────────────────────────────────────────   Requires Regular Health Checks)
      0 hrs/wk        3-5 hrs/wk     15-25+ hrs/wk
```

- **Health Baseline:** The ACSM recommendation of **150–300 minutes/week** of moderate aerobic exercise + 2 resistance training sessions achieves the vast majority of all-cause mortality reduction.
- **High-Volume Athlete Considerations:** Training 10–25 hours/week produces elite sports performance but moves past the nadir of the health curve. High-volume master athletes should undergo annual cardiovascular evaluations (ECG, echocardiogram, coronary artery calcium scoring) to screen for subclinical arrhythmias (e.g., atrial fibrillation) or structural anomalies.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **The "Data-Only" Trap (Algorithmic Myopia):**
   Relying exclusively on power files, TSS, or automated fitness scores without logging subjective RPE and sleep leads to prescribing high-intensity intervals into exhausted or ill physiology.
2. **Treating RPE as Universal Across Athletes:**
   Sprinters with high anaerobic capacity experience extreme neural RPE ramps near threshold, while diesel/steady-state riders experience gradual RPE changes. RPE must be calibrated to individual athlete profiles.
3. **Over-Indexing on Single-Day HRV:**
   Canceling workouts based solely on an isolated low morning HRV reading causes unnecessary training disruptions. Only modify planned sessions when low HRV aligns with elevated resting HR, lethargy, or heavy leg heaviness.
4. **Winter Indoor Fatigue Accumulation:**
   Indoor stationary training generates higher thermal strain, continuous pedal engagement (zero coasting), and increased psychological fatigue. Demanding the same continuous durations indoors as outdoors without extended recovery often leads to mid-winter burnout.

### Coach & Athlete Monitoring Checklist
- [ ] **Log RPE on CR10 Scale:** Record an overall session RPE within 30 minutes of workout completion.
- [ ] **Record Contextual Outliers:** Note any significant anomalies in sleep, hydration, indoor temperature, or life stress in training logs.
- [ ] **Assess Multi-Day HRV Trends:** Look for consistent 3–5 day downward trends rather than reacting to single-day fluctuations.
- [ ] **Schedule Periodic Performance Testing:** Re-anchor threshold and power duration curves every 8–12 weeks.
- [ ] **Prioritize Health Checkups:** If logging >10–12 hours/week over multiple seasons, maintain annual medical screenings for cardiovascular health.
