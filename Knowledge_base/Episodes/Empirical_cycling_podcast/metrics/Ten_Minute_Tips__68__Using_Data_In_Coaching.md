# Using Data In Coaching — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Gediminas Bagdonas (Ten Minute Tips #68)_

---

## What Is Data-Informed vs. Algorithm-Driven Coaching?

In modern endurance sport, data collection tools (power meters, heart rate monitors, WKO5, lactate sensors, HRV trackers) provide immense streams of information. However, a major coaching failure occurs when training decisions are abdicated to automated algorithms or simplistic single-metric targets (e.g., chasing a specific CTL value, forcing TSB to zero for a peak, or relying on auto-generated interval targets).

Empirical, data-informed coaching treats data as **a diagnostic tool to answer specific physiological questions** within an $N=1$ experimental framework—combining objective performance trends with subjective athlete feedback.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms & Analytical Concepts

### 1. The "Holy Trinity" of Training Metrics
Effective performance analysis requires triangulating three complementary data streams:

```
                     [RPE / Subjective Feel]
                     (Autonomic, CNS, Substrate)
                               / \
                              /   \
                             /     \
                            /       \
  [Power Output / External Load] <---> [Heart Rate / Internal Strain]
   (Mechanical Work, Watts, kJ)        (Cardiac Output, Stroke Volume, Drift)
```

1. **Power (External Load):** Measures absolute mechanical work output ($W = J/s$). It is objective and instantaneous, but blind to the physiological cost required to produce it.
2. **Heart Rate (Internal Strain):** Measures the cardiovascular response (cardiac output and sympathetic drive). When paired with power, HR reveals decoupling, plasma volume shifts, cardiac drift, and autonomic suppression.
3. **RPE (Subjective Strain):** Integrates central nervous system fatigue, glycogen depletion, thermal stress, and muscle damage. RPE detects systemic non-functional overreaching long before power curves show decline.

### 2. Biological Systems as Complex "Black Boxes"
Unlike computer code or mechanical engineering—where inputs create perfectly deterministic outputs—human physiology is a non-linear adaptive system. 
- The cellular signaling cascades governing adaptation (e.g., AMPK, CaMKII, PGC-1$\alpha$, mTOR) are influenced by sleep, nutrition, psychological stress, and genetic trainability.
- Training interventions cannot be micro-optimized by combining isolated 5% gains from single scientific papers. Real-world adaptations are governed by broader foundational levers: total volume, progressive duration at threshold, maximal aerobic stimulation ($VO_2max$), and recovery.

### 3. The "McScience" Phenomenon in Sports Analytics
"McScience" describes highly palatable, hyper-marketed scientific concepts that offer immediate intellectual appeal but lack robust methodological support or large effect sizes:
- **Immature Literature:** Emerging concepts (e.g., early durability studies, isolated heat adaptation protocols) often suffer from small sample sizes, lack of standardization, and modest correlation coefficients ($r \approx 0.3\text{--}0.4$).
- **The Heuristic Trap:** Athletes and coaches often gravitate toward simplistic formulas (e.g., "burn more fat at FatMax to spare glycogen," even though substrate oxidation during sub-maximal riding is largely an epiphenomenon rather than the primary adaptation driver).

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The Diagnostic Season Review Workflow
When auditing an athlete's training file in WKO5 or TrainingPeaks:
- **Step 1: Check the Intensity Factor (IF) Floor:** Plot IF across all endurance and recovery rides. An IF floor consistently above $0.72\text{--}0.75$ indicates the athlete is riding too hard on easy days ("fatigue security blanket"), compromising interval execution on hard days.
- **Step 2: Track Progressive Overload in Intervals:** Verify that time-in-zone (TIZ) or mean power for specific target blocks (e.g., sweet spot, threshold) is progressively increasing over weeks without requiring higher heart rates or surging RPE.
- **Step 3: Analyze Workout Compliance Patterns:** Clusters of missed sessions, sudden power drop-offs, or uncharacteristic heart rate suppression signal illness, under-fueling, or unmanaged life stress.

### 2. Deconstructing Misleading Metrics

| Metric | Common Misinterpretation | Physiological Reality & Proper Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **TSB / Form** | $+TSB = \text{Peaked/Ready}$; $-TSB = \text{Tired/Must Rest}$. | TSB simply reflects a mathematical drop in acute workload (ATL). Getting sick, detraining, or skipping rides elevates TSB without creating race readiness. |
| **CTL / "Fitness"** | Higher CTL automatically equates to higher race performance. | CTL measures chronic training volume/stress, not functional capacity. A rider with a 110 CTL doing unfocused junk miles will lose to an 80 CTL rider with sharp threshold/VO2max adaptations. |
| **Compound Score ($W \times W/kg$)** | The definitive predictor of cycling ability. | Oversimplifies nuanced race demands (positioning, drafting, anaerobic repeatability, aerodynamics). |
| **FatMax / Substrate Testing** | Riding at exact FatMax is necessary for mitochondrial adaptation. | Mitochondrial biogenesis is driven by metabolic flux, calcium signaling, and glycogen depletion—not the absolute ratio of fat burned on the cart. |

### 3. Athlete Communication as Primary Data
Quantitative data is useless without qualitative context. Key non-numerical data points required for coaching decisions:
- **Illness / Infection Status:** Immediate disclosure prevents turning minor acute viral loads into systemic overtraining.
- **Menstrual Cycle & Energy Availability:** Tracking regular cycles in female athletes serves as an early warning system for Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S).
- **Session Auto-Regulation Feedback:** Logging whether an interval session felt like an RPE 7 vs. RPE 10 dictates whether the next microcycle advances or recovers.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Abdicating Coaching to Automated Algorithms:** Relying on software to auto-prescribe interval wattage or recovery days without assessing athlete biomechanics or life stress.
2. **The "Fatigue Security Blanket":** Refusing to ride slowly on easy days because low-intensity watts make the athlete feel unproductive.
3. **Over-Engineering Workout Prescriptions:** Creating hyper-complex interval sessions (e.g., alternating 15-second power micro-bands) to look "sciency," when simple sustained progressive overload is physiologically superior.
4. **Data Isolation:** Evaluating power files without matching heart rate and perceived exertion, missing cardiac drift or autonomic fatigue.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Diagnostic Check | What to Look For | Corrective Coaching Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Endurance Ride IF** | $\text{IF} > 0.75$ on recovery/base rides. | Force strict lower cap ($\text{IF} \le 0.65\text{--}0.70$); re-establish easy base pacing. |
| **Threshold Interval Trend** | Power steady, but RPE and HR spiking earlier each rep. | Cumulative fatigue detected; schedule a 3–5 day recovery block. |
| **Power Meter Discrepancies** | Sudden $\pm 15\text{ W}$ shift after hardware change. | Validate hardware (single vs dual-sided, calibration); anchor to historical RPE. |
| **New Scientific Study Claims** | Claims of 20% performance jump via novel micro-drill. | Check sample size, methodology, and effect size; prioritize core training principles first. |

> [!TIP]
> **Coaching Golden Rule:** Data is a spotlight, not a steering wheel. Use metrics to test whether your training hypothesis is working, but let biological adaptation and athlete feedback drive the program.
