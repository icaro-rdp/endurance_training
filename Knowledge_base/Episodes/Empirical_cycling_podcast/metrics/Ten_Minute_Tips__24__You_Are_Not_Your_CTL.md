# Ten Minute Tips #24: You Are Not Your CTL — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Houston_

---

## What Is the "You Are Not Your CTL" Problem?

The **Performance Management Chart (PMC)** and its core metrics—**Training Stress Score (TSS)**, **Chronic Training Load (CTL / "Fitness")**, **Acute Training Load (ATL / "Fatigue")**, and **Training Stress Balance (TSB / "Form")**—are widely used in endurance sports software (TrainingPeaks, WKO5, Intervals.icu).

The problem arises when athletes become **"CTL Junkies"**:
- **Metric Worship:** Letting a dashboard score dictate perceived readiness, forcing high-intensity workouts when legs are exhausted simply because TSB is positive, or feeling anxiety when CTL drops during a planned deload.
- **The "Fitness" Misnomer:** Confusing the software label "Fitness" (which merely tracks historical training volume/load) with real biological performance capacity.
- **TSS Hunting:** Adding gratuitous intensity or junk miles to sub-LT1 base rides to artificially inflate daily TSS scores.

```
                  Software Model vs. Biological Reality
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │                 Software Model (PMC)                        │
  │   - CTL = 42-day rolling average of TSS                     │
  │   - Assumes 100 TSS (VO2max) == 100 TSS (Endurance)         │
  │   - Blind to sleep, nutrition, and life stress              │
  └──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                                 │
                   Discrepancy & Real Physiology
  ┌──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┐
  │                 Biological Reality                          │
  │   - Autonomic recovery & hormonal balance                   │
  │   - Actual mechanical work performed (Kilojoules)           │
  │   - Peripheral adaptations: capillarization & mitochondria  │
  │   - True performance = Power @ TTE on race day              │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Mathematical Foundation of TSS & Normalized Power
Training Stress Score is calculated from Normalized Power ($\text{NP}$) relative to Functional Threshold Power ($\text{FTP}$):

$$\text{TSS} = \left(\frac{t \times \text{NP} \times \text{IF}}{3600 \times \text{FTP}}\right) \times 100 \quad \text{where} \quad \text{IF} = \frac{\text{NP}}{\text{FTP}}$$

- **The 4th-Power Distortion:** $\text{NP}$ calculates a 30-second rolling average raised to the 4th power ($\sqrt[4]{\frac{1}{N} \sum P^4}$). Highly stochastic rides or short, maximal sprint efforts heavily skew $\text{NP}$ upward, generating inflated "TSS buster" scores that do not reflect sustained aerobic load.
- **The False Equivalence:** $100\text{ TSS}$ generated by $4\times 5\text{ min}$ VO2max intervals causes severe glycogen depletion, high acidosis, and autonomic sympathetic disruption requiring 48–72 hours of recovery. Conversely, $100\text{ TSS}$ from $2.5\text{ hours}$ of easy sub-LT1 spinning generates minimal autonomic disturbance.

### 2. The "CTL Party Trick": Volume Dictates the Number
Because most endurance riding settles at an Intensity Factor of $\text{IF} \approx 0.70–0.75$:

$$\text{Daily TSS} \approx \text{Hours} \times (0.72)^2 \times 100 \approx \text{Hours} \times 52\text{ TSS}$$

$$\text{Weekly TSS} \approx \text{Weekly Hours} \times 50–70$$

$$\text{Equilibrium CTL} \approx \frac{\text{Weekly TSS}}{7} \approx 7–10 \times \text{Weekly Hours}$$

An athlete training **10–12 hours per week** will almost inevitably stabilize at a **CTL of 70–85**, regardless of interval structure. Attempting to force CTL to 110+ on a 10-hour budget requires dangerously excessive intensity that inevitably triggers chronic fatigue.

```
Weekly Training Hours vs. Predicted Stable CTL (at IF ~0.72)
 ┌──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┐
 │ Weekly Volume        │ Weekly TSS Range     │ Expected Stable CTL  │
 ├──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┤
 │ 6 – 8 hrs / week     │ 300 – 450 TSS        │ 45 – 65 CTL          │
 │ 10 – 12 hrs / week   │ 550 – 750 TSS        │ 75 – 95 CTL          │
 │ 15 – 18 hrs / week   │ 800 – 1,100 TSS      │ 110 – 135 CTL        │
 │ 20 – 25+ hrs / week  │ 1,200 – 1,600+ TSS   │ 145 – 180+ CTL       │
 └──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┘
```

### 3. How Athletes Get Fitter at the Same CTL
A rider who maintains an 80 CTL across three consecutive seasons can experience massive performance gains without the CTL number changing:
1. **Rising Absolute Mechanical Work (kJ):** If FTP increases from $250\text{W}$ to $320\text{W}$, an IF of 0.72 represents $230\text{W}$ instead of $180\text{W}$. The athlete completes significantly more mechanical work per hour for the exact same TSS.
2. **Durability & Fatigue Resistance:** Progressive years of consistent base riding increase capillary density, slow-twitch motor unit efficiency, and substrate utilization, allowing higher power output late in long races.
3. **Expanded Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE):** Holding threshold power for 65 minutes instead of 38 minutes does not raise CTL, but fundamentally changes race outcomes.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Rules for Using the Performance Management Chart

| Principle | Correct Coaching Practice | Failure Mode / Misapplication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CTL as a Volume Benchmark** | Use CTL to monitor macrocycle volume trends and prevent premature ramp rates. | Chasing arbitrary target numbers (e.g., "I must hit 100 CTL to be fit"). |
| **TSB & Race Readiness** | Use TSB as a loose historical reference; confirm freshness via RPE and leg sensations. | Refusing to race hard or skipping workouts because TSB is negative on race morning. |
| **Deload & Supercompensation** | Allow CTL to drop 10–20% during rest/taper weeks to allow structural adaptation. | Panicking that "fitness is lost" when the CTL line dips downward. |
| **Non-Bike Training** | Track gym/resistance sessions separately via Session-RPE ($sRPE = \text{RPE} \times \text{minutes}$). | Artificially adding heart-rate TSS from lifting into cycling PMC, distorting bike load. |

### 2. Monitoring Non-Training Allostatic Stress
- **The Missing Metric:** Job stress, emotional turmoil, travel, and sleep deficits drain the same central nervous system reserve that recovers muscle tissue.
- **Sensory Reality Check:** If your legs feel heavy, mood is depressed, and resting heart rate is elevated, **take rest regardless of what TSB reports**.

---

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Riding Too Hard on Endurance Days to "Bank TSS":**
   - Pushing endurance rides above LT1 to accumulate 15 extra TSS points, compromising muscle glycogen and quality for upcoming interval days.
2. **Incorrect FTP Settings Distorting the Model:**
   - Setting FTP 30 Watts too high or failing to update an old baseline, resulting in completely fabricated TSS and CTL scores.
3. **Rigid Ramp Rate Adherence:**
   - Blindly obeying a "+5 CTL/week" rule when experienced athletes returning from breaks (e.g., Mathieu van der Poel) can safely absorb faster initial volume ramps if intensity is kept strictly sub-LT1.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

```
Decision Flow: Evaluating Training Load & CTL
```

1. **Is your CTL declining during a taper or rest week?**
   - **DO NOT PANIC.** Fatigue decays roughly twice as fast as biological adaptations. A temporary drop in CTL is required for supercompensation.

2. **Are you trying to increase your CTL?**
   - **Check training hours:** If hours cannot increase, do NOT try to inflate CTL via excessive sweetspot/intervals.
   - **Focus on progressive overload:** Extend TTE, improve repeatability, and increase quality intervals within existing hours.

3. **How do your legs and autonomic markers feel vs. what the chart says?**
   - **Sensations contradict chart:** Always prioritize RPE, sleep quality, and motivation over digital readiness metrics.
   - **Chart shows high fatigue (negative TSB) but legs feel electric:** Trust your sensations and execute your race or quality workout.
