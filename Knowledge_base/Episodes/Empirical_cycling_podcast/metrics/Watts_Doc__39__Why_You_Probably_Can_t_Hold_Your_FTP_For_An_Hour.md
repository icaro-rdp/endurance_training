# Why You Probably Can't Hold Your FTP For An Hour (TTE & Threshold Dynamics) — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson (Watts Doc #39)_

---

## What Is Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE) at FTP?

In popular endurance culture, Functional Threshold Power (FTP) is routinely conflated with "one-hour power." This definition originates from early power meter literature where a 60-minute time trial was described as a practical proxy for field estimation.

Physiologically, human bioenergetics does not operate on a clock face:

* **Metabolic Quasi-Steady State:** FTP / Maximal Lactate Steady State (MLSS) is the highest metabolic work rate where blood lactate production equals systemic lactate clearance.
* **TTE as an Independent Dimension:** Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE) is the duration an athlete can sustain their exact FTP before task failure occurs. Baseline TTE in most endurance cyclists ranges from **35 to 45 minutes**.
* **Trainability:** With targeted extensive training, TTE can be extended to **60 to 80+ minutes** without altering the underlying FTP wattage.

```
 Power (W)
   ▲
   │        Anaerobic / Glycolytic Domain (W')
   │      \
   │       \
   │        \═══════════════════════════════════ FTP / MLSS (Quasi-Steady State)
   │                                           │
   │                                           │◄─── TTE Boundary (35 to 80+ min)
   │                                           ▼
   └───────────────────────────────────────────────────► Duration (Time)
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Veronique Billat Intervention Study

The relationship between threshold velocity/power, substrate utilization, and TTE was demonstrated in a classic study by Dr. Véronique Billat et al. on master distance runners (11 runners, 43–51 years old, 10-year base of running 5 days/week):

* **Intervention Protocol:** 6 weeks of 2 threshold sessions per week (progressing from 30 minutes to 60 minutes total Time-in-Zone, e.g., $3 \times 10\text{ min} \rightarrow 3 \times 12\text{ min} \rightarrow 3 \times 15\text{ min} \rightarrow 3 \times 20\text{ min} \text{ and } 2 \times 30\text{ min}$).
* **Outcome on TTE:** Baseline TTE at MLSS was **$45 \pm 10\text{ minutes}$**. After 6 weeks, TTE expanded to **$63 \pm 12\text{ minutes}$** (+40% extension in sustainable duration), while threshold speed and $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ remained virtually identical.
* **Distance Covered:** Distance covered during a continuous threshold time trial increased from **10.4 km to 15.7 km**.

```
    Pre-Training TTE:   [==== 45 min ± 10 ====]
    Post-Training TTE:  [================ 63 min ± 12 ================]
                        0min                  45min                 63min
```

### 2. Blood Lactate at Minute 10 Predicts TTE

Billat et al. observed an inverse correlation ($R^2 = 0.69$) between the blood lactate concentration measured at **minute 10** of a threshold time trial and the ultimate TTE:

* **Mechanism:** Lower blood lactate in the initial minutes of a threshold effort indicates robust intra-muscular lactate clearance (via MCT1 transporters and mitochondrial lactate dehydrogenase).
* **Glycogen Preservation:** When lactate is immediately shuttled into mitochondria and combusted via the Krebs cycle (yielding 34 ATP per molecule vs. 2 ATP from anaerobic glycolysis), the reliance on cytosolic glycogen stores is minimized.
* **Delayed Motor Unit Fatigue:** Higher early lactate turnover prevents premature accumulation of inorganic phosphate ($P_i$) and hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$), preserving sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium release and delaying recruitment of fatiguable Type IIx motor units.

```
 Low 10-min Lactate ──► High Intramuscular Oxidation ──► Slower Glycogen Depletion ──► Extended TTE
 High 10-min Lactate ──► Low Mitochondrial Clearance ──► Rapid Glycogen Depletion ──► Early Failure
```

### 3. Substrate Utilization at MLSS: The 100% Carbohydrate Myth

A common misconception is that exercising at FTP relies 100% on carbohydrate oxidation ($\text{RER} = 1.00$).

* **Empirical Data:** Across endurance cohorts, respiratory exchange ratio (RER) at MLSS averages **$0.92\text{–}0.94$**, corresponding to **$\sim 75\text{–}80\%$ carbohydrate and $20\text{–}25\%$ lipid oxidation**.
* **Carnitine Flux & Lipid Combustion:** At high glycolytic flux, free coenzyme A (Co-A) is rapidly bound by acetyl groups from pyruvate dehydrogenase. Free carnitine buffers excess acetyl groups (forming acetylcarnitine), maintaining free Co-A pools so that short- and medium-chain fatty acids can continue to enter the Krebs cycle alongside lactate.
* **TTE and Fat Oxidation:** Athletes with the longest TTEs (75–80+ min) demonstrate higher rates of concurrent lipid oxidation at threshold compared to athletes with short TTEs (35 min).

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Extensive vs. Intensive Threshold Training

```
  INTENSIVE THRESHOLD (Attempting to raise Watts)
  Target: 100–105% FTP | Duration: 2x15m -> 2x20m | Result: High CNS fatigue, plateaus quickly
  ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  EXTENSIVE THRESHOLD (Extending Time-in-Zone)
  Target: 95–100% FTP  | Duration: 3x15m -> 2x30m -> 1x60m | Result: Massive TTE & durability
```

* **Intensive Threshold:** Trying to force FTP wattage up by riding at 100–105% FTP. This draws heavily on $W'$ (anaerobic capacity) and autonomic reserves, limiting interval volume and leading to premature plateaus.
* **Extensive Threshold:** Fixing target power at true FTP (or 92–97% FTP) and systematically adding Time-in-Zone. This expands peripheral mitochondrial capacity, capillarization, and glycogen storage capacity.

### 2. Sample 6-Week Extensive Threshold Progression

For an athlete with an established FTP of 300W and a baseline TTE of 40 minutes:

| Week | Workout 1 (Multi-rep / Broken) | Workout 2 (Longer Reps) | Total Time-in-Zone |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Week 1** | $3 \times 12\text{ min @ 290–300W}$ (5m rest) | $2 \times 18\text{ min @ 290–300W}$ (6m rest) | 36m / 36m |
| **Week 2** | $3 \times 15\text{ min @ 290–300W}$ (5m rest) | $2 \times 22\text{ min @ 290–300W}$ (6m rest) | 45m / 44m |
| **Week 3** | $3 \times 18\text{ min @ 290–300W}$ (6m rest) | $2 \times 25\text{ min @ 290–300W}$ (6m rest) | 54m / 50m |
| **Week 4** | *Recovery Week:* $2 \times 12\text{ min easy tempo}$ | *Recovery Ride* | Low |
| **Week 5** | $3 \times 20\text{ min @ 290–300W}$ (6m rest) | $2 \times 30\text{ min @ 290–300W}$ (8m rest) | 60m / 60m |
| **Week 6** | $2 \times 35\text{ min @ 290–300W}$ (8m rest) | **Open-Ended TTE Test:** $1 \times 50\text{–}75+\text{ min}$ | 70m / Max TTE |

### 3. When to Switch: "Extending the Floor" vs. "Raising the Roof"

* **TTE < 45 Minutes:** Do not perform $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ blocks. Focus on extensive threshold and aerobic volume to extend TTE out past 55–60 minutes.
* **TTE > 60–70+ Minutes:** The athlete has maximized their fractional utilization of $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ (often riding at 85–90% $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ at threshold). At this point, further extensive threshold yields diminishing returns. Transition to a high-intensity $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ block (e.g., $4\text{–}5 \times 4\text{–}5\text{ min}$ hard-start intervals) to expand cardiac output and oxygen delivery capacity ("raise the roof").

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The Constant Retesting Trap:** Retesting FTP every 3–4 weeks with 20-minute ramp tests forces the athlete into a cycle of chasing marginal wattage gains, often misclassifying anaerobic improvements as threshold increases. This leads to set zones that are too high to complete extensive workouts.

1. **Equating Inability to Ride 60 Minutes with a Failed Test:** If an athlete tests an FTP of 320W with a 42-minute TTE, 320W is their genuine metabolic threshold. Forcing them to ride 320W for 60 minutes on day one will result in acute failure.
2. **Neglecting Aerobic Volume Support:** Extensive threshold progression requires high-volume Zone 2 endurance base. Volume provides the capillarization and mitochondrial surface area that supports extended metabolic clearance.
3. **Rigid Interval Rest Periods:** Forcing short 60–90 second rest intervals during threshold sets compromises subsequent reps. Use 5–8 minutes of easy spinning to ensure complete PCr restoration and systemic acid clearance.
4. **Cadence Decay:** As threshold efforts extend past 40 minutes, cadence naturally tends to drop by 5–10 RPM as fatigue shifts motor unit recruitment toward larger, less oxidative fibers. Forcing an artificially high cadence when fatigued accelerates cardiovascular strain.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Threshold Training Modality Comparison

| Metric | Extensive Threshold Training | Intensive Threshold / Over-Threshold |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Target Power** | 92–98% FTP (Sub-threshold / True FTP) | 102–108% FTP |
| **Primary Stimulus** | Mitochondrial density, capillarization, lipid/lactate oxidation | Anaerobic buffering, glycolytic flux, autonomic stress |
| **Target Progression** | Increase Time-in-Zone ($30\text{m} \rightarrow 70\text{m}+$) | Increase Wattage at fixed short durations ($2 \times 20\text{m}$) |
| **Primary Adaptation** | **Extends TTE (e.g., 40m $\rightarrow$ 75m)** | Short-term power bump (draws down $W'$) |
| **Fatigue Cost** | Low-to-moderate central fatigue; highly repeatable | Very high autonomic fatigue; high burnout risk |

### Coach & Athlete Decision Checklist

* [ ] **Establish True FTP and TTE Baseline:** Use an open-ended long test (35–60 min) to define both current wattage and failure point.
* [ ] **Program Progressive Time-in-Zone:** Start at $3 \times 10\text{m}$ or $3 \times 12\text{m}$ and systematically advance to $3 \times 20\text{m}$ and $2 \times 30\text{m}$ without changing target wattage.
* [ ] **Allow Adequate Recovery Between Sets:** Use 5–8 minutes of easy spinning (<50% FTP) between threshold intervals.
* [ ] **Monitor Durability and Race Impact:** Evaluate progress through sustained stamina, late-race repeatability, and extended TTE rather than isolated power test jumps.
* [ ] **Trigger VO2max Phase When TTE Peaked:** Once TTE comfortably exceeds 60–70 minutes, transition to an intensive $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ cycle to create room for future threshold increases.
