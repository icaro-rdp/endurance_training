_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Cory Lockwood (Perspectives #21)_

## What Is the 40km Individual Time Trial (ITT)?

The **40-kilometer Individual Time Trial (ITT)** represents the historical benchmark of sustained endurance cycling performance. Setting a record (such as Cory Lockwood’s 44:45.59 at Moriarty, NM, breaking the 45-minute barrier at an average speed over 53.6 km/h / 33.3 mph) requires the convergence of three foundational parameters:
1. **Sustained Aerobic Engine ($W_{\text{aerobic}}$ / FTP / TTE):** Maximizing absolute power at maximal steady state for 40–50 minutes.
2. **Aerodynamic Drag Coefficient ($\text{CdA}$):** Minimizing frontal area and turbulence without inducing biomechanical or respiratory restriction.
3. **Pacing and Energy Distribution Strategy ($W'$ Management):** Micro-allocating power across course gradients, wind shifts, and turnarounds to optimize overall velocity.

```
       ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
       │               TOTAL TIME TRIAL VELOCITY (v)               │
       └────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                                    │
           ┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
           ▼                                                 ▼
┌───────────────────────┐                         ┌───────────────────────┐
│ Aerobic Power (FTP)   │                         │ Aerodynamic Drag      │
│ & Energy Delivery     │                         │ & Rolling Resistance  │
│  • Cardiac Output     │   Divided / Opposed by  │  • Frontal Area (CdA) │

```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Altitude Physiology: Aerodynamic Gain vs. Metabolic Penalty

High-elevation courses (e.g., Moriarty at ~6,500 ft / 1,980 m) present a physiological trade-off:
* **Aerodynamic Advantage:** Air density ($\rho$) decreases with altitude ($\sim 3\%$ per 1,000 ft), significantly reducing aerodynamic drag ($F_{\text{drag}} = \frac{1}{2} \rho C_d A v^2$).
* **Metabolic Power Penalty:** Ambient barometric pressure drops, lowering the inspired partial pressure of oxygen ($P_i\text{O}_2$). Alveolar-capillary diffusion gradient decreases, causing arterial oxygen desaturation ($\text{SpO}_2$) and reducing $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ and threshold power by $\sim 5\text{–}8\%$ at 6,500 ft.

```
  Elevation ──► Lower Barometric Pressure (PiO2) ──► Reduced Alveolar Diffusion
                                                           │
                                                           ▼
  Net Speed Gain ◄── Decreased Air Density (rho) ◄── Lower VO2max & FTP (~5-8%)
  (Aerodynamic drag drops faster than power falls for flat, high-speed ITT)
```

#### Individual Acclimatization Kinetics
* **High $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ Phenotypes:** Athletes with exceptionally high aerobic capacities often handle moderate elevations (5,000–6,500 ft) with brief acclimatization (6–7 days) to overcome the initial acute plasma volume shifts and immune-mediated fatigue.
* **Extreme Elevation Threshold:** Above 7,000–8,000 ft, arterial desaturation accelerates non-linearly, imposing severe aerobic decrements that require weeks of specialized altitude preparation.

---

### 2. Aerodynamic Position vs. Physiological Output Trade-Off

The "lowest possible position" is frequently slower in practice due to physiological penalties:
* **Hip Angle and Gluteal Recruitment:** Overly aggressive drops close the hip angle below critical thresholds ($<40\text{–}45^\circ$ at top dead center), reducing gluteus maximus mechanical advantage and increasing reliance on the quadriceps.
* **Ventilatory Restriction:** Excessive torso flattening compresses the abdominal cavity and restricts diaphragmatic excursion, increasing the work of breathing and raising perceived exertion at threshold.
* **Muscular Fatigue & Head Position:** A position that cannot be maintained steadily causes the athlete to continually shrug, drop their head out of line, or sit up, creating large spikes in $\text{CdA}$ that negate the theoretical wind-tunnel advantages.

```
  OPTIMAL TT FIT = Maximize (Power at FTP × TTE) / CdA
  
  [Too Aggressive / Crushed Position] ──► Low CdA, but -10% Power & Short TTE ──► SLOWER
  [Balanced / Sustainable Aero Fit]   ──► Modest CdA, 100% Power & Full TTE   ──► FASTER
```

---

### 3. Anaerobic Work Capacity ($W'$) and Kinetic Energy Conservation

In a 40km ITT, stochastic surges severely impair average speed:
* Every time a rider enters a turn or turnaround too aggressively and over-brakes, rolling velocity drops from ~40 km/h to ~25 km/h.
* Re-accelerating out of a corner requires high-torque bursts (600–900W), depleting the finite anaerobic reservoir ($W'$).
* Depleting $W'$ raises metabolic byproducts, accelerates glycogen breakdown, and induces premature peripheral muscle fatigue, degrading threshold power during the final quarter of the race.

```
  Aggressive Cornering / Hard Braking ──► Kinetic Energy Destroyed (40 -> 25 km/h)
                                                       │
                                                       ▼
  Anaerobic Depletion (W' Burned) ◄── 700-900W Standing Re-acceleration
                                                       │
                                                       ▼
  Late-Race Power Collapse ◄── Intracellular Acidosis & Early Glycogen Drain
```

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Pacing Strategy: Out-and-Back & Technical Courses

```
  Course Segment          Gradient / Wind Condition       Target Power Allocation
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Outbound (Tailwind/Down)│ Slight descent / Tailwind     │ 98–100% FTP (Patience, tuck low)
  Turnaround Approach     │ Deceleration zone             │ Smooth roll-in, wide sweeping apex
  Turnaround Exit         │ Acceleration zone             │ Controlled ramp (do NOT max sprint)
  Return (Headwind/Rise)  │ False flat uphill / Headwind  │ 103–106% FTP (Overcome resistance)
  Final 5km Finish        │ Terminal run-in               │ Empty remaining W' / Max sustained
```

* **False Flats and Gradients:** When ground speed drops due to a gradient or headwind, the time spent in that segment increases. Pushing slightly above FTP (3–6% over) on slow sections and recovering on super-fast downhill sections minimizes overall time.
* **Negative Splitting Out-and-Backs:** Into a headwind return, conserve 1–2% outbound to prevent early lactate accumulation, attacking the second half with full muscular reserves.

---

### 2. Cadence Dynamics & Drivetrain Selection

* **The High-Speed "Bailout Gear":** Using ultra-large chainrings (58T to 60T) with a 10T/11T rear sprocket allows the rider to pedal at 70–80 RPM during 55+ km/h descents and false flats.
* **Physiological Impact:** Lower cadence at moderate power on high-speed sections lowers cardiac output demand and ventilatory frequency compared to spinning at 110+ RPM, providing cardiovascular relief without shedding momentum.

```
  High Cadence (>105 RPM) on Fast Sections ──► High Ventilatory & Cardiovascular Load
  Tall Gear / Moderate Cadence (75-85 RPM) ──► Lower HR, Muscle Force Stabilized, Same Velocity
```

---

### 3. Cockpit Rigidity and Real-World Aerodynamic Testing

* **Monocoque Carbon Extensions:** Eliminate pad flex. When elbow rests flex 5–10 mm under load, the athlete loses upper-body stability, altering head and shoulder alignment and leaking aerodynamic efficiency.
* **Field Testing Protocol (The RPE / Speed Wind Tunnel):**
  1. Select a flat, sheltered 3–5 km loop with zero cross-traffic and minimal wind variance.
  2. Maintain consistent power output across successive runs.
  3. Adjust **one variable at a time** (e.g., hand height, pad width, helmet tilt).
  4. Compare recorded velocity, HR, and RPE to determine net speed improvements.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **The "Wind Tunnel" Disconnect:** Replicating a pro wind-tunnel position without verifying if the athlete can generate threshold power or maintain the position for 45 minutes outdoors.
2. **Turnaround Panic Surging:** Sprinting out of the turnaround at 800W, spiking blood lactate, and spending the subsequent 3 minutes struggling to settle back into threshold pace.
3. **Over-Braking on Technical Circuits:** Failing to establish reliable visual braking markers, causing premature deceleration and requiring costly anaerobic surges out of each turn.
4. **Ignoring Altitude Acclimatization Profiles:** Assuming general literature timelines (e.g., 2–3 weeks) apply universally without conducting individual $n=1$ testing at race altitude.
5. **Punitive Coaching Mentality:** Responding to missed target powers or subpar workouts with punitive training volume instead of identifying accumulated fatigue or fit-induced strain.
