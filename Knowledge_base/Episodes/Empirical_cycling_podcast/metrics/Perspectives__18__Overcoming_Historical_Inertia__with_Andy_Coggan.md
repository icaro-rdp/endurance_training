# Perspectives #18: Overcoming Historical Inertia, FTP, and Metabolic Regulation — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Andy Coggan_

---

## What Is Functional Threshold Power (FTP)?

**Functional Threshold Power (FTP)** is the highest power output a cyclist can maintain in a quasi-steady state without fatiguing rapidly. 

Historically, exercise physiology literature developed more than 20 distinct, often contradictory definitions of "lactate threshold" (e.g., $1.0\text{ mmol/L}$ above baseline, fixed $4.0\text{ mmol/L}$ OBLA, D-max, log-log inflection, ventilatory threshold 2). These lab definitions caused widespread confusion among coaches and athletes. FTP was coined by Dr. Andrew Coggan as a **functional, pedagogical surrogate** for **Maximal Lactate Steady State (MLSS)** or **Maximal Metabolic Steady State (MMSS)**. 

Rather than relying on invasive, protocol-dependent blood lactate sampling, FTP operationalizes threshold as a field-measurable mechanical power output that delineates sustainable, oxidative equilibrium from non-sustainable, exponentially fatiguing metabolism.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     ENERGY HOMEOSTASIS AT THRESHOLD                     │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ATP DEMAND (Myosin ATPase & Ion Pumps) ──► Hydrolysis (ATP ──► ADP + Pi)│
│                                                     │                   │
│         ┌───────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────┐        │
│         ▼                                                      ▼        │
│  OXIDATIVE PHOSPHORYLATION                             GLYCOLYSIS       │
│  (Mitochondrial Flux / O2 uptake)            (Substrate-level Flux)     │
│  - Krebs Cycle / Beta-Oxidation              - Phosphorylase / PFK      │
│  - Sustainable ATP regeneration              - Pyruvate ──► Lactate + H+│
│                                                                         │
│  [AT OR BELOW MLSS/FTP]: Aerobic ATP matches demand; [Lactate] steady   │
│  [ABOVE MLSS/FTP]: Aerobic ATP insufficient; Glycolysis pulled harder;  │
│                    Rapid [ADP]/[AMP]/[Pi] accumulation; Fatigue occurs   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. The Principle of ATP Homeostasis & "Lactate Is Sucked, Not Blown"
- **Tight Defense of Intracellular ATP:** Skeletal muscle can increase its ATP demand 100- to 1,000-fold within milliseconds during contraction. Despite this immense flux, muscle $[\text{ATP}]$ is defended with extreme precision and does not drop significantly in intact human muscle (demonstrated by Cain & Davies in the 1950s).
- **Demand-Driven Flux (Suction Model):** ATP resynthesis pathways (phosphocreatine hydrolysis, glycogenolysis/glycolysis, and mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation) are strictly demand-driven. Calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$) and energetic charge indicators ($[\text{ADP}]$, $[\text{AMP}]$, $[\text{P}_i]$) stimulate rate-limiting enzymes (glycogen phosphorylase, phosphofructokinase-1, isocitrate dehydrogenase).
- **The $V\text{La}_\text{max}$ Fallacy:** The notion of $V\text{La}_\text{max}$ (an independent maximal glycolytic rate that "pushes" lactate into the bloodstream and dictates threshold) contradicts bioenergetic reality:
  - Glycolytic flux is **pulled** by the rate of cellular ATP turnover ("is sucked"), not pushed by an autonomous glycolytic engine.
  - In vivo maximal enzyme velocities ($\text{PFK}$, phosphorylase) possess significant reserve capacity and are never fully saturated during whole-body exercise.
  - Simple 15-second sprint blood lactate accumulation tests rely on flawed assumptions regarding lactate distribution volume and muscle-to-blood exchange kinetics.

### 2. Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE) & The "Hour of Power" Myth
- **TTE Spectrum:** In trained cyclists, true physiological TTE at MLSS/FTP ranges from **40 to 70+ minutes** (occasionally longer in elite, highly durable athletes).
- **Fitness Dependence:** TTE is positively correlated with aerobic conditioning; highly trained endurance athletes sustain MLSS significantly longer than untrained or recreationally trained individuals (who may only sustain it for 30–40 minutes).
- **Model Parameter vs. Physiological Reality:** In mathematical power-duration models (e.g., WKO4/WKO5), TTE is a calculated inflection/fitting parameter derived from trailing historical data. It represents the point of maximum curvature drop-off rather than an immutable biological timer.

### 3. Critical Power (CP) vs. FTP vs. MLSS
- **Conceptual Unification:** Critical Power and Functional Threshold Power are different mathematical and practical attempts to describe the exact same underlying physiological boundary: maximal metabolic steady state.
- **Divergence in Execution:** CP relies on 2- or 3-parameter hyperbolic mathematical modeling across short, high-intensity trials ($2\text{–}15\text{ min}$), which can artificially overestimate steady-state capacity by 10–25 W if trials are insufficiently long. FTP anchors on longer-duration sustained field performance ($30\text{–}60+\text{ min}$), aligning more tightly with MLSS.

### 4. Multifactorial Mechanisms of Fatigue at Threshold
Fatigue at FTP is never caused by a single isolated variable:

```
[MULTIPLE CONTRIBUTING DETERMINANTS OF THRESHOLD FATIGUE]:
  ├── 1. Localized Type I Fiber Glycogen Depletion
  │      └── Bulk muscle glycogen only drops ~50%, but primary slow-twitch
  │          fibers become fully depleted, losing contractile force.
  ├── 2. Henneman's Size Principle & Higher-Threshold Motor Unit Recruitment
  │      └── Central nervous system forced to recruit less-oxidative Type IIa/IIx
  │          fibers, accelerating [ADP], [Pi], and [H+] accumulation.
  ├── 3. High-Energy Phosphate Disturbance
  │      └── Accumulation of free [ADP] and [Pi] directly inhibits cross-bridge
  │          cycling, sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca2+ release, and myofibrillar ATPases.
  └── 4. Central Motor Drive Limitation (Perceived Exertion)
         └── Central nervous system reaches maximal tolerance (Borg RPE 18–20);
             inability to further increase descending voluntary motor drive against
             intense noxious peripheral afferent feedback (Group III/IV afferents).
```

### 5. Skeletal Muscle Mechanosensing & The Super-Relaxed State
- In resting skeletal muscle, the vast majority of myosin heads are tucked against the thick filament in a **super-relaxed state (SRX)**, consuming minimal ATP ($<1\text{ ATP/head/minute}$).
- Cross-bridge recruitment is governed not only by $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ unmasking actin binding sites on the thin filament, but by **mechanosensing**: mechanical tension along the thick filament physically pulls myosin heads out of the SRX into the active state in proportion to required external load.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Training Levels vs. Training Zones
> **"They are called levels, not zones, for a reason."**

The 7 Power Training Levels (Coggan Levels) describe a continuous physiological spectrum:

| Level | Name | % of FTP | Primary Physiological Driver | Practical Prescription & Execution |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **L1** | Active Recovery | $<55\%$ | Low-grade blood flow, metabolite clearance | Easy spinning, zero pressure on pedals, $<1\text{ hr}$. |
| **L2** | Endurance | $56\text{–}75\%$ | Mitochondrial biogenesis, lipid flux | Long base rides ($2\text{–}5+\text{ hrs}$); conversational RPE 2–3. |
| **L3** | Tempo | $76\text{–}90\%$ | Aerobic flux, glycogen turnover | Continuous sub-threshold tempo ($40\text{–}90\text{ min}$). |
| **L4** | Threshold | $91\text{–}105\%$ | MLSS maintenance, fractional utilization | Extensive intervals ($2\text{–}4 \times 15\text{–}30\text{ min}$ @ FTP). |
| **L5** | $V\text{O}_2\text{max}$ | $106\text{–}120\%$ | Maximal cardiac output, stroke volume | Repeatable iso-power or max effort ($5\text{–}6 \times 4\text{–}5\text{ min}$). |
| **L6** | Anaerobic Capacity | $121\text{–}150\%$ | Substrate-level phosphorylation, buffer capacity | Hard micro-bursts ($30\text{s}\text{–}2\text{ min}$ all-out). |
| **L7** | Neuromuscular Power | Max | Maximal motor unit recruitment, ATP-PCr flux | Maximal sprints ($\le 15\text{s}$), full recovery ($3\text{–}5\text{ min}$). |

### 2. Valid Methodologies for Determining FTP

```
[RANKING FTP DETERMINATION PROTOCOLS BY ACCURACY]:

1. Formal Empirical Cycling Open-Ended Ramp/Extensive Test (GOLD STANDARD)
   - 10 min @ 85–90% FTP ──► Ramp to target FTP ──► Ride to voluntary exhaustion (TTE).
   - Yields exact FTP wattage AND exact individual TTE duration (e.g., 285 W for 52 min).

2. 40 km Individual Time Trial / ~45–60 min All-Out Effort
   - Average power across a fully paced, race-effort TT.

3. Validated Mathematical Power-Duration Modeling (WKO mFTP)
   - Modeled from comprehensive, multi-point maximum mean power data (short, medium, long efforts).

4. 20-Minute Test Protocol (Hunter Allen 95% of 20 min)
   - Prone to significant error: athletes with large anaerobic capacity (FRC/W') overestimate
     FTP by 15–30 W unless preceded by an exhaustive 5-minute clearing effort.
```

### 3. Extensive Threshold Progression Framework
To expand threshold fatigue resistance (TTE) before attempting to raise FTP ceiling:

```
Week 1: 3 x 15 min @ 98–100% FTP (Rest: 5 min)  [Total Time-in-Zone: 45 min]
Week 2: 2 x 25 min @ 98–100% FTP (Rest: 6 min)  [Total Time-in-Zone: 50 min]
Week 3: 3 x 20 min @ 98–100% FTP (Rest: 6 min)  [Total Time-in-Zone: 60 min]
Week 4: 2 x 35 min @ 98–100% FTP (Rest: 8 min)  [Total Time-in-Zone: 70 min]
Week 5: 1 x 50–60 min continuous @ 98–100% FTP  [Targeting TTE breakthrough]
```

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall / Misconception | Mechanism of Failure | Practical Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Treating FTP as Strictly 60-Minute Power** | Individual TTE varies widely ($40\text{–}70+\text{ min}$). Prescribing 60-minute workouts for an athlete with a 42-minute TTE causes overtraining and failed sessions. | Measure individual TTE directly via open-ended testing; prescribe interval durations based on current TTE. |
| **Using 95% of 20-Min Power Without FRC Control** | Anaerobic work capacity (FRC) contributes significantly to a 20-min effort, inflating calculated FTP by 5–10%. | Use longer testing ($35\text{–}50\text{ min}$) or an open-ended test with an initial clearing burn. |
| **Chasing $V\text{La}_\text{max}$ "Detraining" Diets** | Attempting to suppress glycolysis via extreme low-carb diets or low-cadence grinding impairs high-intensity power without increasing mitochondrial capacity. | Train for functional performance; fuel adequately with carbohydrates; focus on aerobic volume and extensive threshold intervals. |
| **Rigid Zone Box Thinking (Pedaling in Silos)** | Halting on hills to avoid leaving "Zone 2" prevents natural motor unit recruitment and compromises specific riding skills. | Ride naturally within descriptive levels; use RPE to govern endurance pacing over varied terrain. |

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

```
[COACHING DECISION MATRIX: THRESHOLD DIAGNOSTICS & PRESCRIPTION]

Condition A: Athlete's TTE is short (<40 minutes) at current FTP
  ├── Cause: High anaerobic contribution, underdeveloped aerobic durability.
  └── Prescription: Cap FTP power; progress extensive intervals (3x15m ──► 2x25m ──► 3x20m) to push TTE past 50 min.

Condition B: Athlete easily completes 2x35 min or 1x60 min @ current FTP
  ├── Cause: TTE is fully extended; fractional utilization is high (>85% VO2max).
  └── Prescription: Transition to VO2max / Intensive capacity block (6x5 min @ max repeatable power) to raise the ceiling.

Condition C: Athlete experiences severe fatigue/failure during 20-min test-derived workouts
  ├── Cause: 95% of 20-min test artificially inflated FTP due to high anaerobic work capacity.
  └── Prescription: Re-test using an open-ended TTE protocol; reduce FTP target by 10–20 W to restore sustainable training.
```
