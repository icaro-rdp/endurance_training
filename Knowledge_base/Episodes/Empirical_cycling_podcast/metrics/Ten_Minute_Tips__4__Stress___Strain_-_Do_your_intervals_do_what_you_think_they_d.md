# Stress vs. Strain: Matching Interval Prescription to Internal Physiology — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore (Ten Minute Tips #4)_

---

## What Is Stress vs. Strain in Interval Training?

In classical physics and engineering, **stress** is the external force applied to an object, whereas **strain** is the internal deformation or change resulting from that stress. 

In exercise physiology:
* **External Stress:** The measurable mechanical work performed by the athlete—power output (Watts), cadence (RPM), interval duration (seconds/minutes), and total work (kJ).
* **Internal Strain:** The biological, metabolic, and hormonal perturbations occurring within the athlete's cells—oxygen uptake rate ($\dot{V}O_2$), cellular ATP/ADP ratio, lactate accumulation, motor unit recruitment, glycogen depletion, and core temperature elevation.

```
                      The Stress-Strain Relationship
┌────────────────────────────────┐         ┌────────────────────────────────┐
│        External Stress         │         │        Internal Strain         │
│ (What you measure on the bike) │ ──────► │ (What happens inside the body) │
│ • Power (Watts)                │         │ • Oxygen consumption (VO2)     │
│ • Cadence (RPM)                │         │ • Fiber recruitment (Type I/II)│
│ • Duration / Total kJ          │         │ • PCr & Glycogen depletion     │
└────────────────────────────────┘         └────────────────────────────────┘
                 ▲                                         │
                 └───────── Modulated by Fatigue, ─────────┘
                            Substrate & Temperature
```

A common coaching and training flaw is treating external stress and internal strain as an identical, static 1:1 relationship. Prescribing intervals based purely on rigid percentages of Functional Threshold Power (FTP) often fails because the exact same power number can impose drastically different internal strain depending on the athlete's aerobic kinetics, anaerobic work capacity ($W'$), fatigue state, and accumulated duration.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
                     Internal Strain Dimensions
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. Oxygen Uptake Kinetics: Cardio-pulmonary lag & VO2 slow component   │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Substrate & ATP Flux: Initial PCr/glycolysis vs. oxidative steady   │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. Individual TTE Demands: Duration relative to threshold capacity     │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. Fatigue Modulation: Power decay vs. sustained metabolic drive       │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Oxygen Uptake ($\dot{V}O_2$) Kinetics and the "VO2 Power" Fallacy
When an athlete begins an interval at 120% of FTP (a typical "VO2max power" zone):
* **Phase I (Cardiodynamic Phase):** In the first 15–20 seconds, cardiac output and muscle perfusion increase, but pulmonary gas exchange does not yet reflect cellular oxygen consumption.
* **Phase II (Fundamental Component):** Over the next 60–120 seconds, $\dot{V}O_2$ rises exponentially toward a steady state. The immediate energy deficit is bridged by intramuscular phosphocreatine (PCr) breakdown and rapid anaerobic glycolysis.
* **The Implication:** In the first minute of a 4-minute interval at 350W, the athlete is **not** at $\dot{V}O_2max$. If the power is capped too low, or the duration is too short, the athlete never reaches maximal aerobic cardiac output or peak stroke volume.

### 2. Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE) & Threshold Overload
Threshold and Sweet Spot adaptations (mitochondrial biogenesis, capillarization, increased MCT1 transporters) require sustaining metabolic flux near the Maximal Lactate Steady State (MLSS) for a duration that challenges the muscle's aerobic capacity.
* An athlete with a 45-minute TTE at FTP will experience substantial physiological strain from a $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ workout (40 minutes total at threshold).
* An athlete who has trained their sweet spot capacity out to 80–90 continuous minutes will experience almost zero progressive strain from a $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ sweet spot session; it acts merely as a low-strain maintenance workout.

### 3. Fatigue Alters the Transfer Function
Fatigue (from glycogen depletion, prior training load, or life stress) dissociates external stress from internal strain:
* **Neuromuscular / Glycolytic Training (Sprints & $W'$):** If neuromuscular fatigue prevents an athlete from reaching peak torque or fast-twitch recruitment, external power drops, and the intended neural/glycolytic strain cannot be achieved. Sprints should only be executed under low fatigue.
* **Oxidative / Aerobic Base Training (Zone 2):** Under moderate glycogen depletion or general fatigue, sub-threshold riding at lower absolute power can still induce significant energetic stress (elevated AMPK, PGC-1$\alpha$ activation) without requiring peak mechanical power.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

```
                           Interval Prescription Rules
┌─────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┐
│     VO2max Intervals    │   Threshold / SweetSpot │    Anaerobic / Sprint   │
├─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ • Prescription: Max RPE │ • Prescription: Target  │ • Prescription: All-out │
│   sustainable for time  │   power extended > TTE  │   maximal effort        │
│ • Pacing: Fast start,   │ • Focus: Progressive    │ • Recovery: Full rest   │
│   maximize breathing    │   time in zone (min)    │   (3–5+ min between)    │
└─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Prescribing VO2max Intervals by Physiology & RPE
Instead of assigning a fixed percentage (e.g., "5x4 min @ 118% FTP"), prescribe by physiological intent:
* **The Goal:** Maximize time spent at maximum oxygen uptake ($\dot{V}O_2max$) and maximal stroke volume ($SV_{max}$).
* **Execution:** Assign intervals as **maximum repeatable effort** for the target duration (e.g., $4\text{ to }5 \times 4\text{ minutes}$ with 3–4 minutes recovery).
* **Pacing Strategy:** Allow the first 30–60 seconds to start aggressively hard (fast start) to accelerate $\dot{V}O_2$ kinetics, then settle into the highest power that keeps hyperventilation and respiratory rate pinned at maximum until the end of the interval.

### 2. Prescribing Threshold & Sweet Spot by TTE Progression
* **Determine Current TTE:** Assess baseline continuous duration at FTP (typically 35–60 minutes) or Sweet Spot (typically 60–90+ minutes).
* **Progress Duration, Not Power:** Over a 4–6 week block, increase total time-in-zone while keeping power stable:
  * *Week 1:* $3 \times 15\text{ min}$ (45 min total)
  * *Week 2:* $2 \times 30\text{ min}$ (60 min total)
  * *Week 3:* $3 \times 25\text{ min}$ (75 min total)
  * *Week 4:* $1 \times 60\text{ min}$ continuous or $2 \times 45\text{ min}$ (90 min total)

### 3. Prescribing Anaerobic Capacity & Sprint Sessions
* **High-Torque / $W'$ Stimulus:** Target maximal Type IIx/IIa recruitment and rapid glycolytic flux.
* **Execution:** All-out maximal effort ($10/10$ RPE).
* **Rest Intervals:** Rest must be long and unhurried (e.g., 30-second all-out sprint followed by 4–5 minutes of easy spinning) to allow $>90\%$ PCr resynthesis. Short rest intervals turn anaerobic capacity training into an unpaced, sloppy aerobic grind.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **The "Zone 5 Power Target" Trap:**
   * An athlete targeting 380W on a VO2max interval might hold the power mechanically while remaining well below $\dot{V}O_2max$ if their anaerobic capacity is high and intervals are short. Conversely, an aerobically dominant rider may fail to hit the prescribed 120% target despite reaching true $\dot{V}O_2max$.
2. **Fixed Sweet Spot Templates:**
   * Applying generic $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ workouts indiscriminately to all athletes regardless of their training history or TTE fails to provide progressive overload for advanced riders.
3. **Attempting High-Torque Sprints in a Fatigued State:**
   * Performing sprint or maximal $W'$ intervals after 3 hours of hard riding or during deep chronic fatigue blunts motor unit recruitment, turning a neuromuscular session into a low-power, high-fatigue failure.
4. **Confusing Heart Rate with Instantaneous Oxygen Uptake:**
   * Heart rate response lags behind mechanical work and is confounded by cardiac drift, hydration status, ambient temperature, and autonomic tone. Heart rate reflects systemic stress but is an imperfect proxy for acute cellular strain.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Stress vs. Strain Diagnostic Matrix

| Training Domain | External Stress Metric | True Internal Strain Target | Optimal Prescription Method | Pacing Directive |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sprint / Neuromuscular** | Peak Watts, 5–15s Power | Maximal motor unit recruitment & rate of force development | All-out effort ($10/10$ RPE) with 3–5 min full recovery | 100% maximal effort; terminate when peak power drops $>10\%$ |
| **Anaerobic Capacity ($W'$)** | 30–90s Power | Full $W'$ depletion & maximal glycolytic flux | Fixed duration, all-out effort with full recovery | Go out hard, hold on to the end of the interval |
| **$\dot{V}O_2max$** | 3–6 min Power | Maximal oxygen uptake ($\dot{V}O_2$) & stroke volume | Max sustainable power across all sets / RPE 9–10 | Hard start to spike kinetics, then sustain hyperventilation |
| **Threshold (FTP)** | Power at MLSS | Sustained maximal oxidative steady state | Total time in zone scaled beyond baseline TTE | Even, steady power at or just below current FTP |
| **Aerobic Base (Zone 2)** | Watts in Zone 2 | Mitochondrial oxidative flux & fat oxidation | RPE 2–3, below LT1/VT1, conversational breathing | Steady, continuous endurance riding |

### Coach & Athlete Implementation Checklist

- [ ] **Define the Target System:** What cellular mechanism are you attempting to adapt (cardiac stroke volume, mitochondrial volume, motor unit recruitment)?
- [ ] **Choose the Primary Pacing Metric:** Use RPE for VO2max and sprints; use power and TTE for Sweet Spot and threshold progression.
- [ ] **Audit Athlete Freshness:** If the workout demands peak neuromuscular power, ensure the athlete is fully recovered. If fatigue is present, pivot to aerobic base or rest.
- [ ] **Extend Time in Zone for Threshold:** Before raising an athlete's target FTP wattage, extend their continuous duration and total work at their current threshold.
- [ ] **Assess Breathing During VO2 Intervals:** Verify that hyperventilation and gasping occur for the final 2–3 minutes of each rep, rather than obsessing over the power meter.
