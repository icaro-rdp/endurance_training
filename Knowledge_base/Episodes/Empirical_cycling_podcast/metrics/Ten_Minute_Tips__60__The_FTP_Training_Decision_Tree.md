# Ten Minute Tips #60: The FTP Training Decision Tree — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Rory Porteous_

---

## What Is the FTP Training Decision Tree?

Functional Threshold Power (FTP) is the cornerstone metric representing the upper boundary of quasi-steady-state aerobic power production. Above FTP, metabolic stability degrades rapidly: anaerobic glycolysis accelerates, muscle metabolites ($H^+$, $P_i$) accumulate, and Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE) is sharply constrained.

The **FTP Training Decision Tree** is a structured coaching framework designed to determine:
1. Whether an athlete should progress interval duration (Time-in-Zone / TTE) or target power (watts).
2. When threshold training has reached diminishing returns, requiring a shift to VO2max or race-specific qualities.
3. How to balance threshold development against training age, seasonal timing, and the opportunity costs of overall performance.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Central vs. Peripheral Integration
* **Central Delivery (VO2max):** Left ventricular stroke volume, maximum cardiac output, and arterial oxygen delivery define the absolute aerobic ceiling.
* **Peripheral Utilization (Fractional Utilization of VO2max):** Mitochondrial density, capillary-to-fiber ratio, and type I / oxidative type IIa fiber recruitment determine what percentage of VO2max can be sustained at threshold ($FTP / VO_2max$).
* **The Ceiling Effect:** As fractional utilization approaches 80–88% of VO2max, further threshold training extends TTE (from 35 min out to 60–90 min) but cannot elevate absolute threshold power. Raising FTP further requires expanding the central VO2max ceiling.

```
       [ Central Capacity: VO2max Ceiling ]
                      ▲
                      │ (Requires VO2max stimulus when gap closes)
                      ▼
       [ Fractional Utilization: FTP / TTE ]
                      ▲
                      │ (Built via Sweet Spot / Threshold TiZ Overload)
                      ▼
       [ Baseline Subthreshold Aerobic Base ]
```

### 2. Time vs. Power Progression Dynamics
* **Progressing Time (Extending TTE):** Gradually overloading interval length (e.g., $4 \times 10 \rightarrow 3 \times 15 \rightarrow 2 \times 20 \rightarrow 2 \times 30 \text{ min}$) increases metabolic strain, mitochondrial signaling (AMPK/PGC-1$\alpha$), and oxidative fiber fatigue resistance without excessive autonomic stress.
* **Progressing Power (Raising Watts):** Pushing power targets higher before muscular durability is established risks drifting into the severe intensity domain, driving premature glycolytic fatigue, elevated sympathetic stress, and incomplete session execution.

### 3. Training Age and Adaptive Capacity
* **Low Training Age (<1–2 years structured training):** Highly receptive to subthreshold and threshold stimuli. Simple progressive overload in volume and duration reliably yields 15–30W gains per season.
* **Advanced Training Age (3–5+ years):** Rapidly returns to previous baseline FTP with modest early-season work. Further gains require substantial overload (targeted VO2max blocks, high-torque work, or significant volume surges).

### 2. Threshold Progression Architectures

| Athlete Level | Interval Progression Sequence | Target Power & Pacing Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Novice / Pacing Developing** | $4 \times 10\text{m} \rightarrow 3 \times 12\text{m} \rightarrow 3 \times 15\text{m} \rightarrow 2 \times 20\text{m}$ | Anchor to conservative FTP; extend final interval duration if legs feel strong. |
| **Intermediate / Time-Limited** | $3 \times 15\text{m} \rightarrow 2 \times 25\text{m} \rightarrow 2 \times 30\text{m} \rightarrow 1 \times 50\text{m}$ | Maintain target power; auto-regulate duration based on daily fatigue. |
| **Advanced / High Durability** | $2 \times 30\text{m} \rightarrow 3 \times 25\text{m} \rightarrow 1 \times 60\text{m} \rightarrow 1 \times 75\text{m}$ | Target sweet spot/FTP crossover; build multi-hour fatigue resistance. |
| **In-Season Maintenance** | $2 \times 15\text{m} \text{ or } 2 \times 20\text{m} \text{ (un-progressed)}$ | Perform at 90–95% FTP; stop well short of muscular failure (RPE 6/10). |

### 3. Sweet Spot Over-Unders
* **Protocol:** 3–4 sets of 10–15 minutes alternating 2 min under (88–92% FTP) / 1 min over (105–115% FTP).
* **Application:** Ideal for race-specific conditioning (criteriums, road climbs, gravel surges) and athletes who struggle mentally with continuous steady-state blocks.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

* **Over-Testing / Incessant Ramp Testing:** Relying on frequent 20-minute or ramp tests rather than observing real-world workout progression and TTE.
* **The "Infinite TTE" Trap:** Continuing to push threshold duration beyond 90 minutes in pursuit of a higher threshold instead of addressing the low VO2max ceiling.
* **Neglecting Opportunity Cost:** Pouring all training bandwidth into gaining 5W of FTP when the athlete's primary competitive limiters are 1-minute anaerobic capacity, pack positioning, or fueling tolerance.
* **Progressing In-Season Threshold Sessions:** Treating midweek in-season maintenance workouts as max-effort tests, carrying excess residual fatigue into weekend races.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Diagnostic Observation | Primary Limiter | Actionable Training Adjustment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Athlete easily completes $2 \times 20$ min at FTP with low RPE** | Submaximal stimulus | Progress to $2 \times 25$ min or $3 \times 20$ min; auto-regulate power upward by 5W. |
| **FTP static after 6 weeks; completed $2 \times 30$ min** | VO2max ceiling constraint | Transition to a 2–3 week VO2max block (e.g., $5 \times 4\text{m}$ or $4 \times 5\text{m}$ max aerobic). |
| **Racing every weekend; legs feel flat midweek** | In-season fatigue accumulation | Reduce threshold frequency to once every 2 weeks; use un-progressed sweet spot (RPE 6/10). |
| **Athlete excels at 20-min power but drops on 5-min climbs** | High anaerobic / low aerobic capacity | De-emphasize short bursts; build prolonged sub-threshold volume and extend TTE. |
| **Targeting 100+ mile gravel / ultra event** | Muscular durability & metabolic endurance | Prioritize long TTE ($60\text{--}90\text{ min TiZ}$) and fat oxidation over raw peak FTP. |
