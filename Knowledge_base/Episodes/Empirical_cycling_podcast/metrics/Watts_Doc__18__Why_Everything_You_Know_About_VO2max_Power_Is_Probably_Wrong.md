# Watts Doc #18: Why Everything You Know About VO2max Power Is Probably Wrong — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Harrison_

---

## What Is $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ vs. "$\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ Power"?

Maximal oxygen uptake ($\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$) is the maximal volume rate of oxygen that the body can transport and utilize during exhaustive exercise. The dot ($\dot{}$) signifies **flux**—an amount of fluid or gas moving through a biological system per unit time ($\text{L/min}$ or normalized to body mass as $\text{mL}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}\cdot\text{min}^{-1}$).

* **Historical Definition (Hill & Lupton, 1923):** "The oxygen intake during an exercise intensity at which actual oxygen intake reaches a maximum beyond which no increase in effort can raise it."
* **The Gold Standard Criterion:** A distinct **plateau in $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2$** despite a continuing increase in workload or power output.
* **$\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{peak}$ vs. $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$:** $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{peak}$ is simply the highest value recorded in a single test where a definitive plateau was not confirmed. 
* **The "Power at $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$" (MAP) Fallacy:** Maximal Aerobic Power (MAP) or "$\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ power" is widely treated as a fixed, discrete power number. In reality, **there is no single power output that uniquely represents $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$**. A wide range of power outputs (from just above FTP to all-out sprint decays) can elicit $100\%\ \dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
Ramp Protocol Step Duration  --->  Impact on Final Power (Wmax)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
15–30s Fast Steps             --->  Inflates Wmax (+25–30%) via Anaerobic Work Capacity (W')
60–90s Standard Steps         --->  Optimal 8–12 min test window for true VO2 plateau
180s+ Long Steps              --->  Deflates Wmax due to premature peripheral fatigue
```

### 1. The Methodological Flaw of Ramp and Step Tests
Ramp and incremental step tests attempt to determine MAP ($W_{max}$) by incrementally increasing resistance until exhaustion. However, the final wattage achieved is heavily dictated by protocol design rather than oxidative capacity:
* **The Adami et al. Study:** Investigated step durations of 15, 30, 60, 90, 120, and 180 seconds with 25W increments.
  * Measured $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ was identical across 30s to 120s steps ($\sim 3.55\text{ L/min}$).
  * Yet the final power ($W_{max}$) dropped dramatically as step duration lengthened: **366W** (30s steps) $\rightarrow$ **322W** (60s) $\rightarrow$ **297W** (90s) $\rightarrow$ **282W** (120s).
  * This represents an **84-Watt difference ($>25\%$)** in "$\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ power" for the exact same measured aerobic capacity.
* **The 8–12 Minute Window:** A valid incremental test must last between 8 and 12 minutes. Shorter tests fail to allow $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2$ kinetics to equilibrate; longer tests cause premature peripheral muscular fatigue before central cardiorespiratory limits are reached.

### 2. The Confounding Role of Anaerobic Capacity ($W'$)
Above Critical Power / FTP, energy production is no longer purely oxidative. It relies on a combination of oxidative phosphorylation and substrate-level phosphorylation (phosphocreatine breakdown and rapid glycolysis):
* **Anaerobic Work Capacity Distortion:** Athletes with high sprint capability and large anaerobic capacities ($W'$) recruit high-threshold Type II motor units during ramp tests. This allows them to sustain power outputs far above their aerobic limit for the final minutes of a ramp, drastically inflating their calculated MAP and estimated $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$.
* **Example:** An athlete with an FTP of 290W and exceptional anaerobic capacity can produce 445W for 5 minutes. Applying standard percentage-based MAP models would erroneously calculate their FTP at 350W+ or vastly overestimate their $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$.

### 3. Non-Linearity Above FTP and the $\text{VO}_2$ Slow Component
* **Below FTP:** Oxygen consumption scales linearly with power output.
* **Above FTP:** The relationship becomes non-linear due to the recruitment of less efficient Type II fibers and the progressive emergence of the **$\text{VO}_2$ slow component**.
* Even at a constant submaximal power output above FTP (e.g., halfway between FTP and MAP), $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2$ will continually drift upward over time until it reaches $100\%\ \dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$.

### 4. Mode-Dependent Muscle Mass Recruitment
$\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ is strictly dependent on the total active muscle mass engaged:
* **Ranking:** Cross-Country Skiing $>$ Running $>$ Cycling $>$ Swimming $>$ Arm Cranking.
* Cross-country skiers exhibit the highest recorded $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ values ($>90\text{ mL}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}\cdot\text{min}^{-1}$) because combined upper and lower body recruitment demands maximal cardiac output and total oxygen transport.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Why Rigid Prescription Percentages Fail
Traditional models prescribe $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ intervals at fixed percentages:
* **Classic Coggan Level 5 (105–120% FTP):** For an anaerobically gifted athlete, 110% FTP may only provoke mild aerobic strain and zero respiratory distress. For an aerobically dominant diesel with a narrow $W'$, 115% FTP may cause task failure within 2 minutes.
* **Percentage of Ramp MAP (e.g., 85–90% $W_{max}$):** Because $W_{max}$ varies wildly based on step duration and $W'$, prescribing intervals based on ramp test output creates arbitrary, uncalibrated training stress.

### 2. Prescribing $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ Intervals by Physiological Strain
* **Target the Respiratory Response, Not Power:** True $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ intervals must drive the athlete to maximal cardiac output and ventilation ($\dot{\text{V}}_E$).
* **Sensory Cues:** The athlete should reach maximum hyperventilation ("ragged breathing", inability to speak, diaphragm burning, sensation of air hunger) within the first 60–90 seconds and sustain it to the end of the repetition.
* **Interval Formats:**
  * **Hard-Start Intervals:** Initiate the interval with a 15–30 second surge (e.g., 150–200% FTP) to rapidly deplete high-energy phosphates, accelerate $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2$ on-kinetics, and reach $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ quickly, followed by sustaining maximal repeatable power.
  * **Duration:** 3 to 5 minutes per repetition; 15 to 25 minutes of total time-in-zone per session.

### 3. How to Track $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ Changes in the Field
To monitor aerobic adaptations without a metabolic cart:
1. **Standardized 5-Minute Max Power Test:** Perform a 5-minute maximal test under identical conditions of freshness and pacing.
2. **Control for Anaerobic Capacity:** Ensure no high-intensity sprint or anaerobic capacity ($W'$) training has been performed in the preceding 3–4 weeks. When anaerobic capacity is held constant, increases in 5-minute mean maximal power directly reflect changes in aerobic capacity.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall / Misconception | Physiological Reality | Practical Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Using Ramp Tests to set FTP.** | Ramp tests measure a mixed aerobic-anaerobic peak ($W_{max}$), typically estimating FTP as $75\%\ W_{max}$. Anaerobic athletes test falsely high; diesels test falsely low. | Results in completely inaccurate training zones and unsustainable threshold intervals. |
| **Secondary criteria in lab tests ($RER > 1.10$, Max HR).** | Secondary criteria fail to guarantee true $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$. RER and blood lactate thresholds vary dramatically between individuals. | Causes lab reports to label submaximal efforts ($\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{peak}$) as true $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$. |
| **Relying on smartwatch $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ algorithms.** | Black-box algorithms rely on linear power-to-HR regressions and population tables, ignoring cardiac drift, temperature, and $W'$. | Provides misleading metrics that do not track genuine physiological progress. |
| **Targeting a strict wattage during $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ intervals.** | If an athlete fixes power at 115% FTP, they may pace the effort submaximally, failing to reach maximal oxygen consumption. | Perform intervals as **maximal repeatable efforts** focused on hyperventilation rather than power-meter staring. |
