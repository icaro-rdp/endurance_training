# Perspectives #30: Being Faster Than Your Watts — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Giancarlo Bianchi_

---

## What Is "Being Faster Than Your Watts"?

"Being faster than your watts" describes the ability of a bike racer to achieve superior race results and high sustained speeds despite producing lower absolute or relative power (W/kg or FTP) than competitors. 

In competitive mass-start road cycling, power output is not the direct determinant of victory. Rather, victory is decided by kinetic velocity at the finish line and the conservation of metabolic resources leading into the final selection. While baseline physiological parameters (such as Functional Threshold Power and VO2max) determine whether an athlete can stay in the group, racecraft—the synthesis of aerodynamic optimization (lowering $C_d A$), drafting efficiency, tactical reading of the field, course reconnaissance, and cognitive stamina—determines who wins.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Aerodynamic Drag vs. Gravitational Resistance
- **The Cubic Power Curve:** Aerodynamic drag power scales with the cube of velocity:
  $$P_{\text{aero}} = \frac{1}{2} \rho C_d A \, v^3$$
  Above $13\text{–}15\text{ mph}$ ($21\text{–}24\text{ km/h}$), aerodynamic drag accounts for over 80–90% of total resistance on flat to rolling terrain.
- **Frontal Surface Area ($C_d A$) vs. Mass ($m$):** Shaving 200–500 g of mass saves negligible seconds on moderate gradients (5–7%), whereas reducing frontal area by dropping the head, flattening the back, and bringing forearms horizontal (90-degree elbow bend) saves 20–40+ watts at race velocities ($40\text{–}50\text{ km/h}$).

### 2. Metabolic Energy Sparing in the Draft
- **Drafting Sparing Effect:** Riding in the draft reduces metabolic cost by 30–40% at speeds $\ge 40\text{ km/h}$. 
- **Substrate Kinetics:** A rider who drafts effectively stays beneath their First Ventilatory/Lactate Threshold (VT1/LT1), oxidizing primarily fatty acids and preserving finite intramuscular glycogen. A rider exposed to the wind at identical speeds operates above FTP/Critical Power, depleting glycogen stores and accumulating fatigue metabolites.

### 3. Repeatability, $W'$, and Depth of Fitness (TTE)
- **Anaerobic Work Capacity ($W'$):** Road races rarely feature continuous, steady-state threshold tests; they are characterized by intermittent, supra-threshold surges (e.g., 2–3 minute climbs at 120–140% FTP).
- **Depth vs. Ceiling:** An athlete with an FTP of 275 W and exceptional depth/durability can repeatedly clear 2-minute surges at 350 W with rapid $W'$ reconstitution. An athlete with a 330 W FTP but poor metabolic endurance or high anaerobic decay will fail once fatigue accumulates past 60–90 minutes.

### 4. Normalized Power (NP) as an Index of Field Vulnerability
- In a race setting, the peloton's collective fatigue accumulates systematically as Normalized Power approaches the group's threshold power.
- When an athlete's real-time NP reaches their own FTP after 40–60 minutes of aggressive surges, it indicates that the field has exhausted its anaerobic buffer. That physiological inflection point represents the optimal tactical window to launch a decisive attack, as the collective group is in severe oxygen debt and will hesitate to respond.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The Aerodynamic Priority Hierarchy

To maximize speed per watt without spending unnecessary energy:

```
[Level 1: Body Position]  -> 90° forearms on hoods, head dropped between shoulders, flat spine
[Level 2: Clothing/Helmet] -> Tight-fitting skinsuit/speedsuit, aero road helmet, smooth shoe covers
[Level 3: Cockpit Setup]   -> Narrow handlebars (36–38 cm), shorter crank arms (improving hip angle)
[Level 4: Wheelsets]       -> 50–65 mm deep-section carbon wheels (aerodynamics > 200g weight savings)
```

### 2. Tactical Course & Environmental Reconnaissance
- **Wind Geometry Analysis:** Identify crosswind segments followed immediately by tailwinds. In a crosswind-to-tailwind transition, the field strings out into an echelon. If a gap opens at the turn, the tailwind accelerates the lead group to 35+ mph, making closing the gap mathematically impossible due to the cubic power-to-speed curve.
- **Pinch-Point Identification:** Locate road narrowings, sharp corners, and technical transitions before climbs. Move into the top 5–10 positions *before* the pinch point at low power during tailwind/neutral sections.
- **Lap-Timer Gap Measurement:** On out-and-back or circuit courses, hit the manual lap button on the head unit at a fixed visual landmark (e.g., telephone pole, tree) when a breakaway passes it. Note the elapsed time when your group reaches the same landmark to get a precise, real-time split.

### 3. Reading Competitor Tells & Body Language
- **Breathing Rate Comparison:** Ride alongside targeted rivals after a hard climb or chase. If their respiratory rate and ventilatory drive are elevated relative to yours, they are operating near maximal capacity.
- **Pre-Attack Tells:** Watch for physical adjustments from key riders: tightening shoes, taking an aggressive swig of water, shifting to the drops, or gripping the hoods with high muscular tension.
- **Shadowing Key Competitors:** Identify the strongest all-around time trialist or puncher in the field. Shadow their rear wheel exclusively, letting them dictate positioning and bridge dangerous moves.

### 4. Breakaway Dynamics & Game Theory
- **Preserving Numerical Advantage:** Never drop a breakaway companion simply because they cannot pull through evenly. A fatigued companion still disrupts the chase behind, provides draft relief on descents/flats, and eliminates a chasing adversary.
- **Capitalizing on Hesitation:** Attack immediately when a dangerous move is caught or when competing teams look to each other to initiate a chase. A 5-second gap gained during collective hesitation requires 400+ watts to bridge once organized chasing begins.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall | Mechanism of Failure | Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chasing Weight over Aerodynamics** | Opting for shallow 30mm climbing wheels to save 200g increases aerodynamic drag, costing 10–20W on all sections $>15\text{ mph}$. | Prioritize 50–60mm rims and aero position on all courses with average gradients $<8\%$. |
| **Rushing Category Upgrades** | Upgrading by soloing away in low categories prevents developing pack-positioning and bunch-sprint racecraft. | Stay in categories long enough to master bunch navigation, draft conservation, and sprint timing. |
| **Cognitive Fatigue & Checking Out** | Mentally conceding when a breakaway establishes a 30-second lead leads to missing secondary counter-attacks. | Maintain cognitive engagement; form organized chase groups or prepare for late-race bridging moves. |
| **Soloing into Headwinds / Open Roads** | Attacking purely based on strong sensations on open, wide roads without crosswinds or technical turns allows the peloton to draft and reel you in effortlessly. | Launch attacks only at terrain transitions, out of technical corners, or into crosswind echelons where chasing is structurally penalized. |
| **Premature Victory Celebrations** | Posting up before crossing the finish line leads to catastrophic line-sprint losses against closing sprinters. | Pedal 100% through the line and only celebrate after visually confirming open road. |

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Pre-Race & In-Race Tactical Decision Table

| Scenario / Observation | Underlying Physiological/Tactical State | Optimal Prescribed Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wind shifts to crosswind after a fast section** | High risk of echelon formation; peloton will string out single-file. | Move to the top 5 positions *before* the turn; secure the gutter/draft line immediately. |
| **Real-time Normalized Power hits FTP after 40 min** | The field has exhausted anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) and is operating in heavy oxygen debt. | Prepare for decisive attacks; launch a move when rivals show signs of hesitation. |
| **A rival is panting heavily and gripping the stem** | Severe peripheral and central fatigue; unable to respond to supra-threshold accelerations. | Attack over the crest of the roller or on the subsequent false flat to force a definitive split. |
| **Breakaway companion stops pulling through** | Companion is at maximal capacity but still provides draft and tactical protection. | Keep them in the break; take longer pulls to maintain the gap rather than dropping them. |
| **Two rival teammates are alternating attacks** | They are attempting to isolate and exhaust you through sequential acceleration. | Sit on the second teammate's wheel; force the chasing burden onto other solo riders in the group. |
