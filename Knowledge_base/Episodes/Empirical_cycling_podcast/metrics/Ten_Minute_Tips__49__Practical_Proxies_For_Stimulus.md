# Ten Minute Tips #49: Practical Proxies For Stimulus — Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Rory Porteous (Ten Minute Tips #49)_

---

## What Are Practical Proxies for Stimulus?

A **proxy for stimulus** is an observable real-time physiological or perceptual cue during a workout that indicates whether the intended cellular, cardiovascular, or neuromuscular stress is being applied to the body.

Athletes frequently rely strictly on external output (power targets) or secondary metrics (heart rate zones, TSS). However, hitting a target power does not guarantee the targeted physiological adaptation is occurring, nor does missing a target power mean the workout failed. Conversely, artificially chasing a proxy (e.g., intentionally riding under-fueled to feel exhausted faster) does not equal a productive stimulus.

### Stimulus vs. Adaptation
- **Stimulus:** The acute disturbance of cellular homeostasis (calcium flux, mechanical tension, metabolic accumulation, glycogen depletion, cardiac stretch) that activates downstream signaling cascades (AMPK, PGC-1α, CaMK, sirtuins, mTOR).
- **Adaptation:** The structural, enzymatic, and functional remodeling (mitochondrial biogenesis, capillary proliferation, left ventricular eccentric remodeling, myofibrillar protein synthesis) that occurs **only during subsequent recovery and supercompensation**.
- **The Golden Rule:** Achieving the internal proxy is only half the equation; without adequate carbohydrate availability, sleep, and recovery, high-strain stimulus yields maladaptation, overtraining, and performance stagnation rather than fitness gains.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
[Target Adaptation] ──> [Cellular / Cardiovascular Trigger] ──> [Observable Real-Time Proxy]
   Endurance               Contractile time / Ca2+ flux            Total volume / hours in saddle
   Threshold / FTP         MCT1/MCT4 flux & Motor Unit Fatigue     Muscular burn, heavy focus, non-ragged breathing
   VO2max                  Max Stroke Volume & Preload Stretch     Max ventilation, hyperventilation, high cadence
   Anaerobic Capacity      Substrate depletion & [H+] accumulation Concentric failure (power drop from fresh state)
   Max Sprint              High RFD & Neuromuscular Recruitment    Peak watts fresh, mind-muscle coordination
   Hypertrophy / Gym       Mechanical tension near failure         0-3 RIR, localized target-muscle fatigue
```

### 1. Recovery Rides
- **Physiological Objective:** Promote gentle blood flow, tissue relaxation, and light metabolic clearing without generating metabolic stress or muscle damage.
- **Valid Proxy:** The ride feels effortless, mindless, and almost "pointlessly easy." You finish the ride feeling fresher and more energized than when you started.
- **Mechanism:** Low muscle tension avoids stimulating AMPK/CaMK pathways and ensures glycogen and nervous system restoration.

### 2. Aerobic Base / Endurance (Zone 2)
- **Physiological Objective:** Drive mitochondrial biogenesis, capillarization, and oxidative enzyme proliferation via prolonged intracellular calcium elevation and AMPK/Sirtuin activation.
- **Valid Proxy:** Total accumulated volume (hours in the saddle) across all intensities. All riding (including commutes, group rides, intervals, and easy spins) contributes to total aerobic contraction time.
- **Mechanism:** The myth of the "delicate metabolic zone" (where hard surges allegedly ruin endurance adaptation for 30 minutes) is contradicted by human physiology. Blood catecholamines and substrate shifts normalize rapidly within minutes. The total volume of muscular contraction drives aerobic signaling.

### 3. Threshold (FTP) and Muscular Endurance
- **Physiological Objective:** Enhance lactate clearance/utilization by oxidative fibers (MCT1), increase functional time-to-exhaustion (TTE), and maximize fatigue resistance in Type I and oxidative Type IIa motor units.
- **Valid Proxies:**
  1. **Muscular Fatigue / Deep Burn:** Sensations of heavy, burning fatigue in primary driving muscles (quads, glutes) near the conclusion of targeted interval durations.
  2. **Concentration Gradient:** Sustaining threshold demands constant mental vigilance; unlike tempo, attention cannot drift.
  3. **Breathing Mechanics:** Breathing is deeply labored and rhythmic, but **controlled and sustainable**.
- **Physiological Boundary:** The moment breathing transitions from heavy/rhythmic to **ragged, gasping, and decoupled**, the athlete has crossed above Critical Power / MLSS into the severe intensity domain.

### 4. VO2max (Maximal Aerobic Power)
- **Physiological Objective:** Maximize stroke volume and cardiac output ($Q = \text{HR} \times \text{SV}$) to stretch the left ventricle and maximize oxygen delivery.
- **Valid Proxies:**
  1. **Maximal Breathing / Hyperventilation:** Gasping for air, breathing "through your eyeballs." Respiratory drive is the primary accessible proxy for near-maximal $\dot{V}\text{O}_2$.
  2. **High Cadence Dynamics:** Pedaling at 100–115+ RPM enhances the skeletal muscle pump, increasing venous return (preload) to the heart and maximizing stroke volume.
  3. **Heart Rate Behavior:** Heart rate will be very high (typically >90–95% HRmax), but under high cadence, HR may actually sit slightly lower than at low cadences because high stroke volume achieves maximal cardiac output without requiring peak chronotropic rate.

### 5. Anaerobic Capacity (W')
- **Physiological Objective:** Maximize glycolytic flux, buffer high intracellular proton concentrations $[H^+]$, and deplete anaerobic phosphagen/glycolytic reserves.
- **Valid Proxy:** **Concentric Contractile Failure.** An athlete goes completely full-gas seated/standing and experiences a drastic inability to produce target watts despite maximal perceived exertion (e.g., surging at 1000W and decaying to 250W by second 30 at RPE 10).
- **Conditioning Prerequisite:** Must be executed fully fresh and carbohydrate-fueled. Attempting anaerobic intervals under glycogen depletion produces low power and weak neural recruitment, failing to provide an anaerobic capacity stimulus.

### 6. Neuromuscular Power / Sprinting
- **Physiological Objective:** Maximize rate of force development (RFD), motor unit recruitment synchronization, and biomechanical sprinting skill.
- **Valid Proxies:**
  1. **Peak Power Freshness:** Achieving near-record wattage values within the first 1–3 efforts. If peak power is depressed by >8–10%, the central nervous system (CNS) is not sufficiently recovered.
  2. **Mind-Muscle Connection:** The sensation of clean, uninterrupted pedal engagement and cadence fluidity even at 130–160+ RPM, rather than the pedals "running away."

### 7. Strength & Hypertrophy (Resistance Training)
- **Strength (1–5 Reps):** High neural drive and near-maximal motor unit recruitment at RPE 7–9 (1–3 reps in reserve / RIR), progressing mechanical load over multi-week mesocycles.
- **Hypertrophy (5–30 Reps):** High mechanical tension achieved by training within 0–2 RIR of concentric failure with targeted local muscle fatigue, rather than systemic cardiorespiratory exhaustion.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### Workout Execution & Proxy Monitoring Table

| Training Target | Prescribed Modality | Target Sensation / Proxy | Breathing Pattern | When to Terminate / Adjust |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Recovery** | 30–60 min easy spin or walk | Effortless, zero mental burden; fresher post-ride | Conversational, nasal | Legs feel heavy or heart rate drifts upward |
| **Endurance** | 2–6 hr Zone 2 / Mixed Volume | Steady fatigue, sustainable, total time accumulated | Rhythmic, conversational | Glycogen bonk or extreme systemic exhaustion |
| **Threshold / FTP** | 2x20m, 3x15m, 1x40–60m progression | Deep muscular ache, tight focus, heavy leg resistance | Heavy, deep, steady; **NOT ragged** | Breathing turns into uncontrollable gasping; watts drop >5% |
| **VO2max** | 4–5x 4–5 min full gas / 30-30s | Maximum oxygen demand, hyperventilation, burning lungs | Maximal, open-mouth, desperate gasping | Inability to reach severe hyperventilation / leg lockup |
| **Anaerobic** | 30s–60s maximal repeats (e.g. 5x30s full gas) | Severe concentric fade; pushing 100% effort for 300W | Extreme panting post-interval | Peak power drops >15–20% from initial interval |
| **Sprint** | 5–15s maximal sprints with 3–5 min full recovery | Explosive snap, clean pedal stroke, high peak watts | Non-limiting during effort; acute recovery gasping | Peak watts drop >10% across 2 consecutive reps |
| **Gym (Strength)** | 3–5 sets $\times$ 3–5 reps (Compound lifts) | High neuromuscular intent, tight bar path, 1–3 RIR | Valsalva maneuver during rep | Form breakdown or grinding to zero RIR |

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

### 1. The "Fake Stimulus" Fatigue Trap
A common coaching and self-coaching failure is conflating tiredness with productive training stimulus:
- **Fasted/Under-fueled Threshold:** Riding threshold with depleted glycogen causes rapid exhaustion and high RPE at sub-threshold wattages. The athlete feels destroyed, but the actual mechanical and metabolic stimulus to oxidative muscle fibers is vastly inferior.
- **Sleep Deprivation / Life Stress:** Elevated cortisol, high resting HR, and poor recovery make moderate power feel brutal. This is systemic strain, not an adaptive training stimulus.

### 2. High-Cadence VO2max Fallacy
Riding at 115 RPM at 60% of FTP elevates heart rate due to internal friction and increased muscle pump. This **does not** create a VO2max stimulus because muscular oxygen consumption and cardiac output remain submaximal. High cadence is a tool to maximize stroke volume *during maximal aerobic efforts*, not an endurance hack.

### 3. Misinterpreting Heart Rate Variability (HRV) and TSS
- **HRV Fluctuations:** For trained endurance athletes, day-to-day HRV swings correlate more heavily with sleep quality, alcohol consumption, hydration, or impending illness than acute fitness progression.
- **TSS Hunting:** Chasing high Training Stress Score numbers by riding at a constant, unstructured "moderately hard" pace creates chronic fatigue without the polarized or threshold-specific signals needed for progression.

### 4. Single-Workout Myopia vs. 90-Day Trends
Adaptations are cellular responses to multi-week cumulative stress. Judging fitness or stimulus effectiveness from a single workout's power or interval metrics leads to erratic plan changes. Use 30-day to 90-day rolling power-duration models to confirm true physiological adaptation.

### Athlete Evaluation Checklist
- [ ] **Fueling Pre-Check:** Ensure carbohydrate availability prior to high-intensity threshold, VO2max, or anaerobic workouts.
- [ ] **Check Real-Time Threshold Breathing:** Verify that breathing is deep and heavy but controlled; if gasping frantically, lower power by 2–3% to remain at true MLSS.
- [ ] **Maximize VO2max Respiratory Rate:** Push until ventilation is fully uncoupled and lungs are working maximally; don't rely solely on target watts.
- [ ] **Observe Sprint Power Ceilings:** If max sprint wattage is down >10% while testing fresh, terminate the session and convert to endurance/recovery.
- [ ] **Triangulate Internal vs. External Load:** Cross-reference **Power** (external), **Heart Rate** (internal cardiovascular), and **RPE** (internal neurological) over multi-week mesocycles.
