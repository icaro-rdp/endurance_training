# Perspectives #15: A Discussion on Strength Training — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore, RJ Boergers, & Angelo Gingerelli_

---

## What Is Resistance Training for Endurance Athletes?

Resistance training for endurance athletes is the strategic implementation of loaded movements, mobility work, and multi-planar stability drills designed to support the primary aerobic discipline (cycling, triathlon, running). 

Rather than aiming for maximal muscular hypertrophy or powerlifting totals, strength training in an endurance context serves three foundational purposes:
1. **Neuromuscular Efficiency & Motor Unit Synchronization:** Maximizing force output per active motor unit without adding non-functional mass.
2. **Postural Integrity & Kinetic Chain Efficiency:** Preventing energy dissipation ("kinetic leaks") caused by pelvic tilt, hip drop, or trunk collapse during prolonged fatigue.
3. **Connective Tissue & Bone Health:** Enhancing tendon stiffness, ligament tensile strength, and bone mineral density to prevent chronic overuse injuries and protect against traumatic crash trauma.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Does Strength Training Increase FTP? (The Physiology vs. The Myth)
A common marketing claim is that increasing 1RM leg strength automatically raises Functional Threshold Power (FTP). Physiologically, this is a misconception:

```
[ Heavy Strength Training ] ──> Neural drive, Rate of Force Development (RFD), Muscle Stiffness
                                            │
                                            ▼ (Does NOT increase)
                                Mitochondrial density, Capillary beds, Stroke volume, MCT transporters
                                            │
                                            ▼
[ FTP Outcome ]             ──> FTP remains governed by peripheral oxidative flux & central delivery.
```

- **Why 20-Minute Power Might Appear Higher:** Strength training enhances anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) and neuromuscular recruitment. An athlete taking a traditional 20-minute field test ($95\%$ rule) may produce more power by tapping into larger anaerobic reserves, artificially inflating estimated FTP while sustainable steady-state aerobic threshold remains unchanged.
- **True Cycling Power Limiters:** Submaximal cycling ($<400\text{ W}$) requires only $10\text{–}30\%$ of a rider's maximal voluntary contraction (MVC) per pedal stroke. The limiter is mitochondrial respiration, substrate delivery, and metabolite clearance—not peak muscular force.

### 2. Cycling vs. Running Biomechanics & Energy Transfer
- **Running (Impact & Elastic Recoil):** Running relies heavily on the **Stretch-Shortening Cycle (SSC)** and the spring-mass model. Heavy lifting and plyometrics increase tendon stiffness and eccentric force absorption, directly reducing oxygen cost (improving running economy).
- **Cycling (Non-Impact Concentric Torque):** Cycling is a non-impact, non-SSC closed-kinetic-chain movement. Gains from lifting translate primarily through structural endurance—maintaining a locked pelvis and stable shoulder girdle so that all generated power passes directly through the pedals rather than twisting the chassis.

### 3. Anti-Rotation & Kinetic Chain ($X$-Chain) Stability
- **Sagittal vs. Multi-Planar Core Demands:** Traditional abdominal exercises (crunches, sit-ups) only train flexion in the sagittal plane. Cycling, however, involves alternating reciprocal unilateral leg drives that induce rotational and frontal-plane torque across the pelvis and spine.
- **The Posterior and Anterior $X$-Chains:** Force transfers diagonally from the left leg through the thoracolumbar fascia to the contralateral latissimus dorsi and right shoulder girdle. Anti-rotation exercises prevent pelvic rocking, lateral hip drop, and lower-back shear forces under heavy pedaling torque.

### 4. The Concurrent Training Interference Effect (Hickson Model)
- First documented by Robert Hickson (1980), simultaneously training high-volume endurance and heavy resistance creates molecular competition:
  - **AMPK / PGC-1$\alpha$ Pathway:** Stimulated by glycogen depletion and cellular energy turnover during endurance rides, promoting mitochondrial biogenesis.
  - **mTOR Pathway:** Stimulated by mechanical tension and amino acid availability, promoting myofibrillar protein synthesis.
- AMPK phosphorylation can actively inhibit mTOR signaling. When endurance volume is high, strength adaptations plateau or regress unless loading, recovery, and session sequencing are strictly managed.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Periodized Seasonal Structure

```
┌─────────────────┬─────────────────┬─────────────────┬─────────────────┐
│   Off-Season    │   Pre-Season    │    In-Season    │   Peak/Taper    │
│  (Base / Build) │ (Intensity In)  │ (Racing / Key)  │ (Championship)  │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────┼─────────────────┼─────────────────┤
│ 2–3 sessions/wk │ 2 sessions/wk   │ 1–2 sessions/wk │ 1 session/wk    │
│ Focus: Strength │ Focus: Power,   │ Focus: Neural   │ Focus: Mobility │
│ & Hypertrophy   │ Anti-Rotation   │ & Posture Maint │ & Activation    │
│ 45–60 min       │ 35–45 min       │ 25–35 min       │ 15–20 min       │
│ RPE 7–9         │ RPE 6–8         │ RPE 5–7         │ RPE 4–6         │
└─────────────────┴─────────────────┴─────────────────┴─────────────────┘
```

### 2. Session Daily Sequencing & Autoregulation
- **Session Order:** Always perform the primary sport (cycling/aerobic intervals) **first** in the morning when the central nervous system (CNS) and glycogen stores are fresh. Perform resistance training 4–8 hours later in the afternoon/evening.
- **Autoregulated Loading (RPE / Reps in Reserve):** Because prior cycling fatigue varies wildly day-to-day, fixed 1RM percentages fail. Use **Reps in Reserve (RIR)**:
  - Select loads where $2\text{–}3\text{ clean reps}$ remain in the tank on every set (RIR 2–3, RPE 7–8).
  - Stop all sets immediately if barbell velocity decelerates drastically or spinal alignment breaks down.

### 3. Core Anti-Rotation & Postural Exercise Bank

```
1. Plank Shoulder Taps / Plate Drags
   - Setup: Wide-stance push-up position.
   - Execution: Slowly lift one hand to touch opposite shoulder (or drag a plate across under torso)
     without allowing the hips or pelvis to rotate even 1 degree.

2. Renegade Rows
   - Setup: Push-up position gripping dumbbells on floor.
   - Execution: Row one dumbbell to hip pocket while driving opposing foot hard into floor;
     engages contralateral glute-to-lat posterior sling.

3. Around-the-Worlds (Halos)
   - Setup: Tall-kneeling or standing half-kneeling.
   - Execution: Move a weight plate or kettlebell smoothly around head circumference in tight circles,
     resisting all torso sway.

4. Pallof Press Variations
   - Setup: Standing perpendicular to cable or resistance band anchor.
   - Execution: Press handle straight forward from chest, holding against rotational torque.
```

### 4. Foundational Movement Template (40-Minute Routine)

- **Block A: Mobility & Activation (8–10 min):**
  - Foam rolling / soft-tissue work $\to$ 90/90 hip switches $\to$ Ankle dorsiflexion rocks $\to$ Thoracic spine rotations.
- **Block B: Foundational Strength Superset (15 min):**
  - Goblet Squats or Trap-Bar Deadlifts: $3 \times 5\text{ reps}$ (RIR 2–3).
  - Standing Single-Arm Overhead Dumbbell Press or Push-ups: $3 \times 6\text{–}8\text{ reps}$.
- **Block C: Unilateral & Anti-Rotation Superset (15 min):**
  - Bulgarian Split Squats or Walking Lunges: $3 \times 6\text{ reps/leg}$.
  - Renegade Rows or Plank Plate Drags: $3 \times 8\text{ reps/side}$.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **The "Circus Trick" Functional Trap:** Performing heavily loaded lifts on unstable surfaces (e.g., loaded barbell squats on Bosu balls or Swiss balls). This dramatically increases injury risk without providing sufficient mechanical tension for strength or sports transfer.
2. **Chasing Muscular Soreness (DOMS) In-Season:** Training to failure ($0\text{ RIR}$) creates severe eccentric muscle damage that compromises pedal stroke kinematics and ruins high-intensity interval execution for 48–72 hours.
3. **Dropping Strength Training Completely During Race Season:** Cutting out lifting entirely results in losing neural recruitment gains and bone mineral density protections within 4–6 weeks, followed by severe muscle soreness when resuming in the autumn.
4. **Treating Endurance Athletes as Powerlifters:** Forcing rigid 1RM percentage charts regardless of weekly saddle volume and cumulative fatigue.
5. **Over-Emphasizing Sagittal-Only Core Work:** Relying on hundreds of crunches or sit-ups that reinforce hunched cycling posture while neglecting rotational and lateral hip stabilizers.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Quality | Cycling Goal | Resistance Protocol | Target Metric |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maximal Strength** | Increase motor unit recruitment & muscle stiffness | Trap-bar deadlift, goblet/box squat: $3 \times 3\text{–}5$ reps | RPE 7–8 (2–3 RIR), clean velocity |
| **Unilateral Balance** | Correct side-to-side asymmetries, knee tracking | Bulgarian split squat, reverse lunges: $3 \times 6\text{ reps/leg}$ | Controlled tempo, zero pelvic wobble |
| **Core Anti-Rotation** | Eliminate kinetic leaks and pelvic sway on bike | Plank shoulder taps, Renegade rows, Pallof press: $3 \times 8\text{–}10$ reps | Zero hip rotation |
| **Seasonal Maintenance**| Retain neural adaptation without fatigue interference | 1–2 sessions/week, 30 min, 1–2 compound sets | No residual DOMS on interval days |
