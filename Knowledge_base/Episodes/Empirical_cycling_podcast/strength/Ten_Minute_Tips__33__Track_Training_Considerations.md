# Track Training Considerations — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hilson (Ten Minute Tips #33)_

---

## What Is Track Training?

Track cycling encompasses a diverse spectrum of fixed-gear, brakeless velodrome racing formats divided broadly into **Sprint disciplines** (Match Sprint, Keirin, Team Sprint) and **Endurance disciplines** (Individual Pursuit, Team Pursuit, Omnium, Points Race, Scratch Race, Madison, and the Kilo TT). 

Unlike road cycling, track racing imposes distinct biomechanical and environmental constraints:
1. **Fixed-Gear Transmission:** Athletes cannot change gear ratios during competition and cannot coast. Power output at any speed is strictly a function of cadence and gear inch selection.
2. **Velodrome Geometry & Steep Bankings (up to 45°):** Navigating high centrifugal loads, converting height to velocity (potential to kinetic energy), and overcoming lateral sliding thresholds (typically requiring >18–20 mph above the blue stayers line) impose severe cognitive and neural demands.
3. **Multi-Event Competition Schedules:** Endurance omniums and sprint tournaments condense multiple maximal or supra-threshold efforts into a single morning/evening session, placing a premium on inter-event recovery kinetics.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The 1-Minute Aerobic Threshold (The Kilo Benchmark)
The 1000m Time Trial (Kilo) marks the biological inflection point between anaerobic alactic/glycolytic dominance and aerobic oxidative metabolism:
* **Energy System Split:** In efforts lasting 55–65 seconds, oxidative phosphorylation accounts for **50–60% of total energy turnover**.
* **Pacing Kinetics:** Pure sprinters front-load the effort with maximal neuromuscular torque, opening massive gaps in the first 250–500m, but suffer catastrophic speed deceleration over the final 250m due to hydrogen ion/inorganic phosphate accumulation and phosphocreatine (PCr) depletion. Conversely, pursuiters/enduros pace more evenly, leveraging aerobic power to maintain velocity across laps 3 and 4.
* **Mass vs. Aerobic Power:** While road racers optimize power-to-weight ratio ($W/\text{kg}$), track endurance and sprint performance is governed primarily by raw drag-adjusted power ($W/CdA$) and absolute force production ($N$), favoring heavier, muscular athletes (e.g., Bradley Wiggins adding ~15 kg of lean mass for the Rio 2016 Team Pursuit).

### 2. Neuromuscular Energetics & The Big Gear Revolution
Historically (1990s), track sprinters used small gears (e.g., 51×14 / ~98 inches) reaching cadences exceeding 160–180 RPM. Modern track sprinting utilizes massive ratios (e.g., 60×12 to 68×14 / 115–125+ inches).
* **Contraction Count & Peripheral Fatigue:** Pedaling at 115–125 RPM versus 170+ RPM reduces total cross-bridge cycling cycles per second, mitigating neuromuscular junction fatigue, acetylcholine depletion, and calcium ($Ca^{2+}$) reuptake stress.
* **Force-Velocity Matching:** Peak power on a bicycle occurs at ~50% of maximal shortening velocity ($\sim 110\text{--}130\text{ RPM}$). Larger gears maintain the athlete closer to this peak power window at terminal speeds (>70–75 km/h).

### 3. Bilateral Deficit & Force Expression in the Gym
* **Bilateral Deficit Phenomenon:** Athletes can typically generate **70–80% of their two-leg 1RM on a single-leg exercise** (e.g., single-leg leg press, Bulgarian split squat).
* **Ground Contact vs. Pedal Stroke Time:** Unlike track & field sprinting (ground contact time ~80–100 ms), the cycling pedal stroke at 120 RPM provides a **~200 ms force-application window**. This longer duration allows direct transfer from heavy, high-load resistance training ($50\text{--}85\% \text{ 1RM}$) without requiring extreme ballistic plyometrics.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Training Prescriptions for Track Endurance (Omnium / Points / Scratch)
Endurance track racers must build robust aerobic capacity ($V\text{O}_2\text{max}$ and FTP) combined with high-cadence neuromuscular tolerance:

* **Aerobic Base:** 10–16+ hours/week of low-intensity endurance riding. Sprinters do not require massive aerobic volume, but pursuiters and bunch racers require complete aerobic development to recover between heats.
* **High-Cadence Over-Unders:**
  * **Structure:** 3–4 sets of 10–15 minutes (e.g., 2 min Under @ 88–92% FTP, 90 RPM / 1 min Over @ 110–120% FTP, 120–135 RPM).
* **"Death Sentence" High-Cadence Micro-Intervals:**
  * **Structure:** 2–3 sets of $(30\text{s ON} \text{ @ Max Race Cadence [125--145 RPM]} / 30\text{--}60\text{s UNDER} \text{ @ Sweet Spot [88--92\% FTP]})$.
  * **Purpose:** Simulates the unrelenting surges of a 110-inch points race without allowing active recovery.

### 2. Track Sprint Prescriptions (Match Sprint / Keirin / Team Sprint)
* **Standing Starts:**
  * **Execution:** 3–6 reps of 3–5 maximal pedal strokes from a dead stop in a heavy gear (100–120 inches). Roll around the apron for 3–5 minutes full recovery between reps.
  * **Safety:** Perform on track bikes; avoid high-torque standing starts on road bikes with angled chainlines to prevent thrown chains and broken collarbones.
* **Flying 200m Prep:**
  * **Execution:** 2–3 reps with full 15–20 minute recoveries. Full-gas top-banking windup (30–45s seated acceleration), diving off Turn 3–4 into the measurement line.
* **Team Sprint Positional Phenotypes:**
  * **P1 (Starter):** Highest force-to-bodyweight ratio, maximal 0–15s acceleration, smaller gear (~100–106 inches).
  * **P2 (Transition):** High acceleration + sustained 250–500m speed.
  * **P3 (Anchor):** Large body mass, massive $W'$, maintains top speed into lap 3 (~118–125+ inches).

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **The "All-Sprint" Myth for Track Endurance:** Assuming Omniums and Points races are purely anaerobic sprints. Without high mitochondrial density and FTP, athletes cannot clear lactate during mid-race lulls.
2. **Over-Gearing in Bunch Races:** Selecting excessive gearing (e.g., 116+ inches) for local amateur bunch races, resulting in slow out-of-corner acceleration when the field surges.
3. **Excessive Ballistic Plyometrics:** Replacing heavy, controlled resistance training with high-risk box jumps and depth drops. Biking requires force applied over ~200ms, not 80ms tendon recoil.
4. **Neglecting Velodrome Technical Adaptation:** Failing to spend structured time riding the banking lines. Cognitive anxiety on 45° slopes wastes neural drive needed for race tactics and peak wattage.
