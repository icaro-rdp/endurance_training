# The Origins of Newbie Gains: Central Hemodynamics vs. Peripheral Adaptations — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson (Watts Doc #53)_

---

## What Are "Newbie Gains" in Endurance Training?

**"Newbie gains"** describe the rapid, non-linear improvement in aerobic capacity ($VO_{2\text{peak}}$), Functional Threshold Power (FTP), and exercise tolerance experienced by previously untrained individuals or athletes returning after prolonged detraining. 

```
  Aerobic Performance (VO2max / FTP)
       ▲
       │          Logarithmic Adaptation Curve
       │             ┌─────────────────────────────────── (Elite Plateau)
       │            /
       │           /   <-- Diminishing Returns (Requires High Volume / Specific Stimuli)
       │          /
       │        /
       │       /  <-- "NEWBIE GAINS" (Rapid, Multi-System Hematological Surge)
       │      /
       │     /
       └────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────► Training Age / Time
```

Historically, debate persisted over whether these rapid improvements stemmed from peripheral muscular remodeling (mitochondrial biogenesis, enzyme upregulation, capillarization) or central cardiovascular adaptations (blood volume expansion, cardiac output, stroke volume). Rigorous physiological investigations (notably Montero et al., 2015) resolved this question: **early aerobic gains are overwhelmingly hematological and central hemodynamic in origin.**

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Fick Equation & The Limiting Factor

Aerobic capacity is defined by the Fick equation:

$$VO_{2\text{max}} = Q_{\text{max}} \times (C_aO_2 - C_vO_2)_{\text{max}}$$

Where:
* $Q_{\text{max}} = \text{HR}_{\text{max}} \times \text{SV}_{\text{max}}$ (Maximal Cardiac Output)
* $(C_aO_2 - C_vO_2)_{\text{max}} = \text{Arteriovenous Oxygen Difference (Peripheral Extraction)}$

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                          CENTRAL VS. PERIPHERAL LIMITATION                             │
├──────────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Central Hemodynamics (The True Limiter)  │ Peripheral Muscle Extraction (Excess Cap.)  │
├──────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Maximal Stroke Volume (SVmax)          │ • Mitochondrial Volume Density (+40%)       │
│ • Blood Plasma Expansion (+5.5–5.8%)     │ • Citrate Synthase Activity                 │
│ • End-Diastolic Preload (Frank-Starling) │ • Capillary-to-Fiber Ratio                  │
│ • Total Hemoglobin Mass (Hb_mass)        │ • Muscle can consume >300 mL O2/100g/min    │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Bengt Saltin (1986) demonstrated that when small muscle masses are isolated (e.g., single-leg quadriceps extension), human skeletal muscle can accommodate blood flows $>200\text{ mL/100g/min}$ and consume $>300\text{ mL } O_2/100\text{g/min}$. In whole-body exercise (cycling, running), skeletal muscle demands far exceed the heart's pumping capacity. Thus, **cardiac delivery is the primary bottleneck.**

### 2. The Phlebotomy Experiment: Montero et al. (2015)

To isolate the exact contribution of blood volume versus muscular adaptations, Montero et al. put 16 untrained subjects through 6 weeks of endurance training ($65\% VO_{2\text{peak}}$, 3–4 sessions/week):

```
                       6-Week Training Program (65% VO2peak)
                                        │
           ┌────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┐
           ▼                                                         ▼
  Peripheral Muscular Changes                               Central Hematology
  • Mitochondrial density: +40%                             • Blood Volume: +5.8%
  • Type 2X fiber CSA: +140%                                • Cardiac Output: +7.2%
  • Wpeak: +16% | VO2peak: +9%                              • Plasma Volume: +6.5%
           │                                                         │
           └────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                                        ▼
                         [Acute Phlebotomy Intervention]
                     (Withdrew Blood Volume Gained in Training)
                                        ▼
                           RETEST VO2peak & Wpeak:
                     ★ DROPPED EXACTLY BACK TO BASELINE ★
```

* **The Finding:** Removing the newly expanded blood volume completely erased the $9\%$ gain in $VO_{2\text{peak}}$ ($r = 0.84$ correlation between blood volume and $VO_2$ gain), despite the fact that subjects retained all $40\%$ of their new mitochondrial density.
* **Corroboration (Direct Catheterization, 2023):** Sprint Interval Training ($3 \times 30\text{s}$ all-out, 3x/week for 6 weeks) yielded identical results: $+5.5\%$ blood volume, $+8.8\%$ cardiac output ($r = 0.93$), and immediate baseline reversal upon phlebotomy.

### 3. The Frank-Starling Law and Stroke Volume

Why does plasma volume drive $VO_{2\text{max}}$?
1. **Hypervolemia & Venous Return:** Rapid expansion of blood plasma volume (driven by albumin synthesis and renal fluid retention) increases venous return to the right atrium.
2. **Ventricular Preload & Myocardial Stretch:** Increased end-diastolic volume stretches left ventricular cardiomyocytes.
3. **Enhanced Contractility:** By the **Frank-Starling mechanism**, stretched cardiac fibers contract with greater force, increasing stroke volume ($\text{SV}$) without requiring an increase in maximal heart rate ($\text{HR}_{\text{max}}$).

```
  Plasma Volume ↑ ──► Venous Return ↑ ──► End-Diastolic Preload ↑ ──► Frank-Starling Stretch ↑ ──► SV & Qmax ↑
```

### 4. Untrained vs. Well-Trained Distinctions

* **Untrained Athletes:** Have unused cardiac stretch compliance. Simple plasma expansion (even saline infusion) immediately increases stroke volume and $VO_{2\text{max}}$.
* **Well-Trained Athletes:** The pericardium and left ventricle already operate near maximal elastic stretch. Infusing excess plasma beyond normal trained levels hemodilutes blood without increasing stroke volume, resulting in zero net $VO_{2\text{max}}$ gain. Advanced athletes require long-term **eccentric cardiac remodeling** (left ventricular cavity expansion) driven by sustained volume and high stroke-volume training.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Training Modality Equivalence for Novices

For the first 6–12 weeks of structured training, almost any metabolic stimulus stimulates plasma volume expansion and central gains:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        NOVICE TRAINING PRESCRIPTION COMPARISON                         │
├───────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────┤
│ Modality                      │ Protocol Example              │ Primary Mechanism      │
├───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
│ **Low-Intensity Continuous**  │ 45–60 min @ 60–70% HRmax      │ Steady venous return,  │
│ (Zone 2 Base)                 │ (3–4 days/week)               │ renal fluid retention  │
├───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
│ **Sprint Interval (SIT)**     │ 3–4x 30s all-out (2-3 min rest│ High sympathetic surge,│
│ (Time-Efficient)              │ (2–3 days/week)               │ fast blood volume bump │
├───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
│ **Mixed Over-Under Tempo**    │ 30–45 min alternating tempo   │ Compliance & habit     │
│ (Fun & Varied)                │ (5 min high / 5 min low)      │ formation              │
└───────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┘
```

### 2. The Retraining Athlete Protocol

Athletes returning from illness, injury, or off-season detraining experience a rapid loss of blood volume (decays within 7–14 days). 
* **Retraining Time Course:** Blood volume and $VO_{2\text{max}}$ restore rapidly within 10–14 days of resumed training.
* **Prescription:** 
  * Avoid panicking over early high heart rates and low power numbers in week 1.
  * Use a 10-day block of moderate volume (Zone 2) with 1–2 short threshold touches.
  * Hydrate aggressively with sodium-containing fluids ($500\text{--}1000\text{ mg Na/L}$) to support rapid vascular volume expansion.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The "Copying the Old Plan" Mistake:** Repeating the introductory training plan that yielded massive 50-watt improvements during your first year of cycling. Newbie gains are largely independent of plan optimization; as an intermediate or advanced athlete, continuing the same low-complexity stimuli leads to plateaus.

1. **Over-Attributing Gains to Mental Toughness:**
   * While familiarization with high RPE occurs, physiological testing confirms that $>80\%$ of measured $VO_{2\text{peak}}$ gains are explained by quantifiable hemodynamic factors ($Q_{\text{max}}$, plasma volume, Hb mass), not psychological tolerance.
2. **Relying on Heat Training Hacks Early On:**
   * Using passive heat stress or sauna protocols to force plasma volume expansion in beginners adds unnecessary autonomic stress. Novices expand plasma volume naturally and rapidly through standard riding without thermal fatigue.
3. **Misinterpreting Early Body Mass Increases:**
   * Beginning endurance training often increases body mass by 1–3 kg. This is not fat gain; it represents expanded plasma volume (~500 mL) and intracellular water bound to restored muscle and liver glycogen (3–4 g $H_2O$ per gram of glycogen).

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Central vs. Peripheral Adaptations in Newbie Gains

| Adaptation Component | Untrained (0–6 Months) | Well-Trained (>2 Years) | Primary Determinant |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$VO_{2\text{max}}$ Driver** | **Blood Plasma & $Q_{\text{max}}$ Expansion** | Cardiac Structural Remodeling | Central Hemodynamic |
| **Mitochondrial Growth** | Rapid (+40%), excess capacity | Incremental quality / respiration | Peripheral Cellular |
| **Stroke Volume Compliance** | Readily responsive to hypervolemia | Near pericardial/structural limit | Central Elasticity |
| **Detraining Sensitivity** | Fast drop (7–10 days blood loss) | Fast blood loss, preserved capillaries | Central Volume |
| **Optimal Stimulus** | Any consistent riding (Z2 or HIIT) | High-volume Z2 + Target VO2max blocks | Periodized Specificity |

### Coach & Athlete Action Checklist

* [ ] **Focus on Consistency Over Complexity:** Novices do not require complex periodization; regular weekly riding (3–5 sessions) triggers full initial plasma and cardiac adaptations.
* [ ] **Support Hydration & Electrolytes:** Ensure adequate dietary sodium and fluid intake to facilitate natural exercise-induced hypervolemia.
* [ ] **Do Not Benchmark Long-Term Progress on Early Trajectory:** Educate athletes that initial 10–20% performance jumps will transition into 1–3% incremental gains requiring specific training blocks.
* [ ] **Recognize Retraining Dynamics:** When returning from layoffs, allow 10–14 days for plasma volume restabilization before re-testing FTP or setting new power targets.
