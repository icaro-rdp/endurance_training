# Over-Unders Are Not Special (Lactate Kinetics & Shuttling) — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson (Watts Doc #43)_

---

## What Are Over-Under Intervals?

Over-under intervals are a staple training format in endurance cycling, characterized by oscillating power output above and below Functional Threshold Power (FTP):

* **Typical Structure:** 2–4 sets of 10–20 minutes, cycling through **1–2 minutes "over"** (105–115% FTP) followed by **2–3 minutes "under"** (85–90% FTP).
* **The Popular Narrative:** Athletes are commonly told that surges above threshold generate high blood lactate, and dropping below threshold forces working muscles to "clear and combust" the lactate, thereby training the lactate shuttle, multiplying monocarboxylate transporters (MCTs), and raising FTP.

While over-unders are valuable for race-specific simulation, their physiological mechanism is widely misunderstood. **Over-unders do not confer unique metabolic adaptations that cannot be achieved via continuous sub-threshold, sweet spot, or endurance training.**

```

  120% ┌───────────┐           ┌───────────┐           ┌───────────┐  ◄── "Over" (105–115% FTP)
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Kinetics of Lactate Clearance During Recovery

To identify where and how lactate is cleared most rapidly, exercise physiologists measured systemic blood lactate clearance following high-intensity bouts ($5\text{ min @ }90\%\text{ VO}_2\text{max}$) across varying active recovery intensities ($0\%, 40\%, 60\%, 80\%, 100\%\text{ LT}$):

* **Peak Clearance Rate at 80% LT:** Systemic blood lactate clearance is fastest during active recovery at **$\sim 80\%\text{ of threshold}$** (and self-selected recovery averaging 78% LT), removing **$10\text{–}12\%$ of accumulated lactate per minute**.
* **Second Fastest at 100% LT:** Clearance at 100% LT remains high because muscular energy turnover is maximal, though net clearance is slightly lower due to concurrent lactate production.
* **Passive Rest is the Slowest:** Resting completely (0% LT) yields the slowest clearance rate, as low metabolic demand in resting muscles reduces the rate of mitochondrial pyruvate/lactate combustion.

```
 Recovery Intensity (% LT) │ Peak Clearance Rate (%/min) │ Time to Baseline
 ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Passive Rest (0% LT)     │ ~3–4% / min (Slowest)       │ >35–45 minutes
  Low Spin (40–60% LT)     │ ~6–8% / min                 │ ~25–30 minutes
  Optimal Under (80% LT)   │ ~10–12% / min (Fastest)     │ ~15–20 minutes
  Threshold (100% LT)      │ ~9–10% / min                │ ~20–25 minutes
```

### 2. Monocarboxylate Transporters (MCT1 and MCT4)

Lactate crosses the muscle cell sarcolemma and mitochondrial membranes via **monocarboxylate transporters (MCTs)**, which operate on a facilitated-diffusion, proton-linked bi-directional gradient:

* **MCT1 (High Affinity / Low $K_m \approx 5\text{ mM}$):** Predominantly expressed in oxidative Type I and Type IIa fibers and in the mitochondrial lactate oxidation complex. Highly sensitive to aerobic training.
* **MCT4 (High Capacity / High $K_m \approx 25\text{ mM}$):** Predominantly expressed in glycolytic Type IIx fibers for rapid export of lactate and $\text{H}^+$ during all-out glycolytic flux.
* **MCT1 Correlates with Mitochondrial Density ($R^2 \approx 0.80$):** Studies across trained and untrained cohorts demonstrate that **MCT1 expression tracks citrate synthase ($CS$) activity linearly**. Building more mitochondria automatically up-regulates MCT1 transporters; you do not need to perform specialized interval spikes to stimulate transporter expression.

```
       [Cytosol]                                   [Mitochondrial Matrix]
 ┌──────────────────────┐                    ┌────────────────────────────────┐
 │ Glycolysis / Lactate │ ──► [MCT1 Trans] ──► │ Lactate ──► Pyruvate ──► Krebs│
 └──────────────────────┘                    └────────────────────────────────┘
            ▲                                                ▲
            └────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
              Mitochondrial Surface Area is the True Rate Limiter!
```

### 3. The "Lactate Bathing" Fallacy

A classic training hypothesis suggested that exposing muscle cells to high concentrations of lactate ("bathing the muscle") triggers specific enzymatic adaptations.

* **Decoupling of Substrate and Signal:** Just as forcing high fat oxidation via carbohydrate restriction does not increase aerobic signaling (Watts Doc #40), flooding muscle with lactate does not uniquely stimulate mitochondrial biogenesis.
* **Universal Adaptive Drivers:** Mitochondrial proliferation is governed by intracellular energy depletion ($\uparrow\text{AMP}/\text{ATP}$ via **AMPK**), calcium flux ($\uparrow\text{Ca}^{2+}$ via **CaMK**), and mechanical strain—all of which are driven by total contractile volume and metabolic turnover, not by the presence of lactate per se.

### 4. Motor Unit Recruitment Across the Threshold Boundary

During continuous riding at 88–92% FTP (Sweet Spot), Type I and oxidative Type IIa fibers operate in a stable quasi-steady state. 

* **The Over Spike (105–115% FTP):** Forcing power 10–15% above threshold demands immediate motor unit recruitment of higher-threshold, fatiguable Type IIx fibers. These fibers generate rapid surges in intracellular inorganic phosphate ($P_i$), hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$), and ADP.
* **The Under Settle (80–90% FTP):** When dropping back to the "under," Type I and IIa fibers must combust both the incoming circulating lactate and their own intracellular substrate load under elevated systemic RPE and ventilatory drive.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. When to Use Over-Unders: Race-Specific Conditioning

Over-unders are not superior for building base aerobic capacity, but they are exceptionally effective for **specific race preparation**:

* **Breakaway & Paceline Simulation:** Road races and criteriums require hard surges out of corners or over short rollers ($110\text{–}130\%\text{ FTP}$) followed immediately by high-tempo drafting ($85\text{–}95\%\text{ FTP}$) without the luxury of coasting.
* **Psychological & RPE Calibration:** Over-unders train the athlete mentally to tolerate high breathing rates and muscular burning while maintaining discipline and power on the pedals.

### 2. Sample Over-Under Workout Architectures

#### A. Traditional Criss-Cross Over-Unders (Lactate Cycling)
* **Structure:** $3 \times 12\text{–}16\text{ minutes}$, with 6–8 minutes Zone 2 recovery between sets.
* **Pattern per set:** $3\text{–}4\text{ cycles of } [2\text{ min @ 85–90% FTP (Under)} \text{ / } 1\text{ min @ 105–115% FTP (Over)}]$.

#### B. Race-Specific Sharp Surge Over-Unders (Criterium / Rolling Road Race)
* **Structure:** $2\text{–}3 \times 15\text{ minutes}$, 8 minutes recovery.
* **Pattern per set:** $5\text{ cycles of } [2.5\text{ min @ 88–92% FTP} \text{ / } 30\text{s @ 130–140% FTP (Hard Attack)}]$.

#### C. Continuous Extensive Threshold (The Aerobic Base Alternative)
If the goal is purely threshold power, TTE expansion, or mitochondrial mass:
* **Protocol:** $2 \times 30\text{ min}$ or $1 \times 60\text{ min @ 90–95% FTP}$ steady. This accumulates more total Time-in-Zone with lower autonomic fatigue than broken over-unders.

```
 WORKOUT COMPARISON: Continuous Sweet Spot vs. Over-Unders
 ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ Continuous 2x30m @ 90% FTP:  60 min Time-in-Zone | Low CNS stress       │
 ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ Over-Unders 3x16m (1m/2m):   48 min Time-in-Zone | High CNS/Autonomic   │
 └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The "Too Hard Over" Error:** Setting the "over" at 125–140% FTP for 2+ minutes during an over-under session rapidly exhausts $W'$ (anaerobic capacity), causing acute task failure on the "under" portion. The "over" should be a controlled surge (105–112% FTP), not a maximal sprint.

1. **Treating Over-Unders as Base Training:** Prescribing over-unders throughout winter base blocks. The high neuromuscular tension and autonomic stress induce fatigue without providing any mitochondrial benefit beyond steady Zone 2/Sweet Spot riding.
2. **Dropping the "Under" Too Low:** Setting the "under" at 60–65% FTP (Zone 2). This turns the workout into ordinary short anaerobic intervals with passive recovery rather than a true lactate shuttling session. The "under" must remain at **80–90% FTP** to challenge oxidative clearance.
3. **Inaccurate FTP Calibration:** If an athlete's tested FTP is artificially inflated (e.g., from an uncorrected 20-minute test), the 88% "under" is actually at true threshold, and the 110% "over" becomes an unsustainable VO2max effort, causing workout failure within 10 minutes.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Workout Selection: Continuous vs. Over-Under

| Goal / Context | Best Workout Choice | Physiological Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Early Season Aerobic Base** | Continuous Z2 & Sweet Spot ($3 \times 20\text{m}$) | Maximizes mitochondrial biogenesis with minimal autonomic fatigue. |
| **Extending Threshold TTE** | Extensive Threshold ($2 \times 30\text{m} \rightarrow 1 \times 60\text{m}$) | Maximizes continuous metabolic clearance and peripheral glycogen endurance. |
| **Race-Specific Peak (Crits/Road)** | Over-Unders ($3 \times 16\text{m}$ with 30s–1m surges) | Trains motor unit recruitment and mental coping with unrecovered surges. |
| **Climbing on Variable Gradients** | Over-Unders (2m @ 85% / 1m @ 110%) | Simulates steep switchbacks followed by sustained grade pacing. |

### Coach & Athlete Action Checklist

* [ ] **Demystify the Workout:** Recognize that over-unders do not possess magical physiological clearance advantages over steady threshold work.
* [ ] **Calibrate Targets Accurately:** Ensure the "under" is pegged to 80–90% true FTP and the "over" to 105–115% true FTP.
* [ ] **Maintain Under Power:** Do not allow wattage to collapse during the "under" phases; sustaining 80–90% power is where peak lactate clearance occurs.
* [ ] **Periodize Appropriately:** Place over-under workouts in the final 4–6 weeks of pre-race specialization blocks, not in early winter base training.
* [ ] **Prioritize Volume for Transporter Growth:** Focus on total aerobic volume and progressive threshold duration to drive citrate synthase and MCT1 expression.
