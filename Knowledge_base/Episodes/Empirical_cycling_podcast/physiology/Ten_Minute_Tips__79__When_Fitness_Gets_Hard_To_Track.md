# When Fitness Gets Hard to Track — Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Megan & Alex (Ten Minute Tips #79)_

---

## Why Does Fitness Become Elusive to Measure?

Athletes frequently encounter phases where their true physiological capacity becomes difficult to quantify or perceive. This disconnect commonly arises during:
1. **Mid-Race Season:** Frequent weekend racing and mid-week tapers disrupt clean testing environments.
2. **Post-Intense Training Blocks:** Residual central and peripheral fatigue masks newly acquired mitochondrial and cardiac adaptations ("brain fog for the legs").
3. **Metric Distortions:** Over-reliance on algorithmic metrics (e.g., TrainingPeaks CTL) that conflate volume with metabolic capacity.

Understanding how to track fitness during these ambiguous phases prevents the destructive reflex of **panic over-training** (doubling down on intervals when the body actually requires rest).

```

### 1. The "Museum Glass" Concept of Fresh Legs
- **Finite Neuromuscular Reserve:** High-end aerobic and anaerobic power relies on fully reconstituted glycogen stores, optimal neuromuscular recruitment, and rested autonomic tone.
- **The Squandering of Freshness:** When an athlete feels exceptionally good 3–5 days before an event, they frequently over-ride their endurance pace, surge up local hills, or perform maximal "dress rehearsals." This drains $W'$ and elevates cortisol, leaving the athlete flat on race day.
- **The Rule:** *"When you have top-tier race legs, place them under museum glass. Do not touch them until the start line."*

### 2. Fatigue Masking Fitness (The Latency of Supercompensation)
- **Cellular Adaptation vs. Functional Expression:** High-intensity blocks (such as a 3-week block of $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ or high-volume threshold) stimulate robust cellular signaling (capillary remodeling, mitochondrial enzyme up-regulation). However, the immediate post-block state is dominated by micro-trauma, central nervous system fatigue, and elevated baseline inflammation.
- **The False Plateau:** Testing an athlete 3–5 days post-block often shows stagnant or regressed wattage. True supercompensation frequently takes 10 to 21 days of low-intensity endurance riding and adequate carbohydrate fueling to fully manifest.

### 3. The CTL / Banister Model Fallacy
- **CTL is Volume Load, Not Fitness:** Chronic Training Load (CTL) tracks an exponentially weighted average of daily TSS. 
- **The In-Season Illusion:** In-season racing involves lower weekly hours, long travel days, and multi-day tapers. Consequently, CTL drops by 15–30 points. Athletes mistakenly view this algorithmic drop as lost "fitness" and attempt to artificially inflate CTL by adding junk miles, directly sabotaging race freshness.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Objective Indicators of Latent Fitness (Without Formal Testing)

| Diagnostic Signal | Fatigued State (Masked Fitness) | Recovered State (Peak Expression) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Warm-Up FTP Probe (3–5 min)** | Heart rate sluggish to rise; RPE $>8/10$; legs feel leaden. | Power feels effortlessly smooth; heart rate tracks normal linear rise. |
| **Anaerobic W' / FRC Depletion** | Immediate burn on short 10s surges; slow reconstitution. | Snappy acceleration out of corners; rapid recovery between surges. |
| **Submaximal Interval Consistency** | High power variability; unable to complete second half of rep. | High consistency and repeatable cadences (88–96 RPM) across sets. |
| **Non-Exercise Biomarkers** | Dreading stairs; low motivation; morning resting HR elevated $>5\text{ bpm}$. | Running up stairs effortlessly; eager to get kitted up; stable HRV. |

### 2. Safe In-Season Maintenance vs. Over-Testing
During active racing blocks, eliminate formal 20-minute or 40-minute all-out tests. Instead, deploy **non-depleting maintenance openers**:
- **Format:** 60–90 min Zone 2 with $2 \times 8–10\text{ min}$ at $92–96\%$ FTP (or tempo at $80–85\%$) $+ 2–3 \times 6\text{s}$ seated sprint bursts.
- **Gating Rule:** If the first 8-minute effort feels exceptionally hard ($>7.5/10$ RPE), abort the second rep and noodle home in Zone 1.

### 3. Management of Concurrent Allostatic Loads
- **The "Graduation Effect":** Off-bike stressors (academic finals, high-stress corporate projects, home renovations) suppress the autonomic nervous system. Athletes frequently set instant multi-watt PRs within 5–7 days of removing an external life stressor, without any change in training volume.
- **In-Season Weight Loss Warning:** Never attempt aggressive caloric deficits during active race seasons. Energy deficits impair glycogen resynthesis and suppress hormonal axes, making fitness impossible to express.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall | Mechanism | Practical Consequence | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Over-Racing Every Group Ride** | Burning $W'$ and glycogen on Tuesday/Wednesday rides. | Chronic mid-pack performance on Saturday/Sunday races. | Cap group ride intensity; enforce strict sub-65% FTP limits on recovery rides. |
| **Panic Training When CTL Drops** | Adding extra workouts to keep the TrainingPeaks CTL line elevated. | Severe non-functional overreaching and flat legs. | Hide CTL chart in-season; judge readiness by freshness and race execution. |
| **Rigid 4-Week Testing Schedules** | Forcing maximal power tests on exhausted legs mid-season. | Demoralization from artificially deflated test scores. | Rely on progressive interval volume (TTE) to validate fitness. |
| **Overriding Rest Weeks** | Performing hard endurance or tempo during scheduled recovery. | Prolonged latent fatigue that never resolves. | Treat recovery weeks as sacred; sleep, eat, and spin easy. |

 │    ├── YES ──► Allostatic load is blunting expression. Maintain baseline volume; do not force PRs.
 │    └── NO  ──► Evaluate interval execution.
 │
 └── [Are you riding your easy and recovery rides too hard?]
      ├── YES (Endurance rides $>65-70\%$ FTP) ──► Dial back recovery rides to $<50\%$ FTP.
      └── NO  ──► Fitness is intact; preserve legs under museum glass for race day.
```

- [ ] **Lock Fresh Legs Away:** Do not spend peak form on mid-week group rides or training dress rehearsals.
- [ ] **Decouple CTL from Fitness:** Remember that peak race form requires shedding fatigue, which drops CTL.
- [ ] **Allow Time for Supercompensation:** Expect 10–21 days of delay before hard blocks translate into top-end power.
- [ ] **Audit Daily Caloric Intake:** Ensure carbohydrate availability supports recovery and prevents false fatigue signals.
