# 1M Milestone AMA: Physiology, Energy Balance, Indoor Equivalence & Strength Mechanics — Q&A Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson_

---

## Q: Do Endurance Adaptations Require a Caloric Surplus or Energy Neutrality?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Listeners asked whether progressive endurance fitness gains (mitochondrial biogenesis, capillary growth, plasma volume expansion) require eating in a consistent caloric surplus (similar to bodybuilding/hypertrophy phases) to prevent the body from down-regulating training adaptations, referencing energy balance models from Herman Pontzer and Eric Trexler.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
Hypertrophy training requires net accretion of new contractile myofibrillar protein tissue, which demands a sustained positive nitrogen balance and a caloric surplus. In contrast, endurance adaptations are biochemical and enzymatic:
- **Tissue Synthesis Burden:** Endurance training stimulates mitochondrial enzyme expansion (citrate synthase, cytochrome c oxidase), capillary endothelial growth, and monocarboxylate transporter synthesis with minimal net structural mass increase.
- **Energy Availability Floor:** Cellular adaptive signaling (AMPK activation, intracellular calcium flux, PGC-1$\alpha$ transcription) functions optimally at **energy neutrality** (isocaloric balance), provided glycogen stores are replenished with sufficient carbohydrates ($6\text{--}10\text{ g/kg/day}$).
- **The Deficit Hazard:** Down-regulation of adaptation occurs when athletes drop into severe energy deficits ($<30\text{ kcal/kg FFM/day}$), inducing Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S), thyroid suppression, and elevated cortisol.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Maintain **energy neutrality**; do not consume a bulking surplus for endurance cycling, as excess calories will accumulate as adipose tissue rather than accelerate mitochondrial density.
- Match high training energy expenditure with proportional carbohydrate intake before, during ($60\text{--}90+\text{ g/hr}$), and after hard rides.

## Q: What Is the Physiological Lower Limit / Floor for Zone 2 Aerobic Stimulus?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes frequently ask how low power can drop (as a percentage of FTP or watts) during base endurance rides while still stimulating meaningful aerobic adaptations, and whether riding at 40–50% FTP is "wasted time."

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Contraction Time vs. Mechanical Load:** Sub-threshold aerobic biogenesis is primarily triggered by repeated intracellular calcium ($Ca^{2+}$) flux and cyclic AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) activation during sustained muscular contractions.
- **The 40–50% FTP Stimulus:** Total contraction count and continuous time-under-tension over several hours stimulate mitochondrial enzyme activity, capillarization, and fat oxidation pathways.
- **Autonomic Preservation:** Riding at $40\text{--}55\%$ FTP accumulates thousands of contractions with negligible glycogen depletion, minimal sympathetic nervous system stress, and near-zero autonomic fatigue, leaving the athlete completely fresh for high-intensity interval workouts.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Accept riding at **$40\text{--}55\%$ FTP** on easy endurance days without guilt; the physiological floor for aerobic signaling is remarkably low.
- Total weekly saddle time and consistency drive aerobic remodeling far more than forcing base rides into upper Zone 2 or Zone 3.
- Reserve glycogen and autonomic freshness for dedicated threshold and VO2max interval days.

## Q: Why Does Testing FTP Too Soon After a VO2max Block Cause False Failure?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
An athlete completed a 3-week VO2max block, attempted Kolie Moore's baseline FTP test 3 days later, and blew up 10 minutes into the test. They asked why they could not push threshold power and what the proper post-block recovery timeline is.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
Testing FTP within 3–5 days after completing a VO2max block almost always results in early failure (e.g., blowing up 10 minutes into a test).
- **Residual Autonomic & Glycogen Fatigue:** A high-density VO2max block ($6\text{--}9\text{ sessions}$ over 2–3 weeks) generates profound central cardiac fatigue, sympathetic nervous system suppression, and deep intramuscular glycogen depletion.
- **Adaptation Realization Window:** The physiological fruits of VO2max training—plasma volume expansion, eccentric left ventricular stroke volume remodeling, and capillary perfusion—require **7 to 14 days of low-intensity volume and restorative sleep** to consolidate before the newly expanded aerobic ceiling can be converted into higher threshold power.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**

```
Post-VO2max Block Transition Protocol:
- Week 1-3: VO2max Focus (e.g., 2-3 sessions/week of 5x4 min all-out @ 110+ RPM)
- Week 4 (Recovery Week):
  - Monday: Full Rest
  - Tuesday: 60 min Zone 1-2 easy spin
  - Wednesday: 90 min Zone 2 with 2-3x 10s light openers
  - Thursday: Full Rest
  - Friday: 60 min Zone 1 spin
  - Saturday: 2.0 hrs Zone 2 conversational ride
  - Sunday: Full Rest
- Week 5 (Testing & Transition):
  - Tuesday: Openers (30 min with 2x 1-min threshold bursts)
  - Wednesday: Baseline FTP / TTE Test (Progressive step test: 35-50+ min)
```

- Never test FTP within 48–72 hours of concluding a VO2max block.
- Schedule a full 7–14 day transition of easy Zone 1–2 riding and light openers before assessing threshold gains.

## Q: Why Do Supramaximal Partial ROM (Quarter) Squats Fail to Transfer to Cycling?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
A listener asked about utilizing supramaximal loads (e.g., 120–140% of 1RM) in short range of motion compound lifts (quarter squats, pin squats) to build sprint and top-end cycling power, citing track and field sprinting literature.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Lack of Stretch-Shortening Cycle (SSC) in Cycling:** In running and track sprinting, quarter squats correlate with ground contact performance because running utilizes an eccentric-to-concentric stretch-shortening cycle with tendon elasticity. Pedaling a bicycle is a purely **concentric, non-impact cyclic action** with zero eccentric stretch-shortening cycle.
- **Joint Angle Mismatch & Ligament Shear:** Quarter squats load the knee joint at $30\text{--}45^\circ$ of flexion with massive loads, exposing the patellofemoral joint and spinal structures to high compressive forces. However, cycling requires high torque production at deep hip and knee flexion angles ($70\text{--}110^\circ$) at the top of the pedal stroke.
- **Superiority of Full ROM:** Full-ROM back squats, Bulgarian split squats, and trap bar deadlifts recruit high-threshold motor units across deep joint angles with much lighter, safer absolute loads.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Perform **full range-of-motion compound lifts** in the gym (deep squats, trap bar deadlifts, split squats) for 3–5 reps at RPE 7.5–8.5.
- Develop cycling-specific explosive starting power and joint-angle force on the bike via **high-torque standing starts** ($53 \times 14$, 0–15 km/h maximal accelerations).

## Q: How Should Indoor vs. Outdoor Training Duration and Duty Cycles Be Converted?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
A listener asked whether 2.5 hours of continuous pedaling on an indoor turbo trainer provides the same training stimulus as 3 hours outdoors due to outdoor freewheeling, coasting, and descents.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Pedaling Duty Cycle:** Outdoor road riding includes 10–20% non-pedaling or soft-pedaling time (descents, corners, traffic stops). Indoor direct-drive trainers impose a near 100% continuous duty cycle without dead spots.
- **Thermal Strain:** Indoor riding lacks convective airflow, leading to rapid increases in core body temperature, cutaneous vasodilation, elevated heart rate (cardiovascular drift), and premature autonomic fatigue if cooling is inadequate.
- **Avoid Over-Engineering Formulas:** Do not attempt complex mathematical formulas to subtract coasting time. In total metabolic and muscular work, 2.0–2.25 hours indoors roughly equates to 2.5 hours outdoors.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- When converting an outdoor base ride to the indoor trainer, apply a **0.80–0.85 duration factor** (e.g., 2 hours indoors for a 2.5-hour outdoor prescription).
- Position at least **two high-velocity industrial blower fans** directly at the torso and head to maintain convective cooling and prevent artificial heart rate drift.

## Q: Does Muscle Soreness (DOMS) from the Gym Indicate Insufficient Recovery for Training?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes often wonder whether lingering delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) 48–72 hours after a heavy lifting session means they are unrecovered and must skip scheduled cycling workouts.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Inflammation vs. Systemic Fatigue:** DOMS is primarily a localized inflammatory response to micro-trauma in the muscle extracellular matrix and connective tissue, not an absolute marker of systemic metabolic or central nervous system fatigue.
- **Active Recovery Mechanisms:** Low-intensity muscle contractions (Zone 1 spinning, light walking) stimulate capillary blood flow and lymphatic drainage, clearing inflammatory exudates and resolving passive neural stiffness without adding systemic fatigue.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Mild-to-moderate DOMS does not preclude low-intensity Zone 1–2 endurance rides or structured workouts if neuromuscular force production is intact.
- Perform a 15–20 minute easy warmup; if leg stiffness resolves, proceed with training. If muscles fail to produce force or RPE remains elevated, convert the session to recovery.

## Q: How Should Cyclists Improve Climbing Weakness Biomechanically and Seasonally?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
A rider who excels on flat terrain struggles on steep climbs and asks how to train climbing ability through torque work, position adjustments, and gym training.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Biomechanical Angles:** Climbing shifts the torso and pelvis relative to gravity, altering hip-torso angles and recruiting lower back and gluteal musculature differently than flat riding.
- **Inertia & Momentum Dynamics:** Climbing is characterized by low flywheel inertia, where the bike decelerates rapidly between pedal strokes, increasing effective muscular torque requirements at the top of the pedal stroke.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Execute tempo, sweet spot, and threshold intervals on actual outdoor climbs whenever possible.
- If training indoors, **elevate the front wheel** with a climbing riser block to match climbing hip-torso geometry.
- Incorporate off-season heavy compound lifting (squats, Romanian deadlifts) to build hip extensor strength and lumbar stability.

## Q: How Should Heat Training Be Implemented for Indoor Performance?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
A listener asked whether heat acclimation protocols can be utilized to improve performance on the indoor trainer and expand plasma volume.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Plasma Volume Expansion:** Repeated thermal stress stimulates aldosterone and vasopressin secretion, leading to an expansion of blood plasma volume (up to 5–10% within 1–2 weeks), which improves cardiac stroke volume and thermoregulatory sweat rate.
- **Separation of Stresses:** Heat stress imposes substantial autonomic and cardiovascular strain; combining heat stress with high-intensity intervals degrades interval wattage and impairs adaptive signaling.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Perform heat sessions as **30–45 minutes of easy Zone 1–2 spinning in a warm room ($25\text{--}30^\circ\text{C}$)** or **20–30 minutes of passive post-ride sauna ($70\text{--}80^\circ\text{C}$)**.
- Never combine intentional heat stress with hard VO2max or threshold interval sessions.
