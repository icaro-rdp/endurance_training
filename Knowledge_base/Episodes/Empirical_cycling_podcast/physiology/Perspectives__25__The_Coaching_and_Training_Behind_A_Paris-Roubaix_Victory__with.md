# The Coaching and Training Behind a Paris-Roubaix Victory — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Adam Pulford (Perspectives #25)_

---

## What Is the Physiological Reality of Winning a Monument / Classic?

The public perception of a **Monument victory** (such as Alison Jackson winning the 2023 Paris-Roubaix Femmes from the early breakaway) is often dominated by assumptions of astronomical peak 5-minute or 20-minute power outputs.

In reality, the power file of a WorldTour Classic winner is characterized by **metabolic efficiency, tactical energy conservation, and fatigue resistance (Durability)**:
* The rider does not win by generating an unprecedented fresh power spike.
* The rider wins by producing near-fresh functional power **after absorbing 3,000+ kilojoules of chaotic, high-torque vibration stress** across 17 sectors of brutal cobblestones.

```
       ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
       │             THE PARIS-ROUBAIX PHYSIOLOGICAL ENGINE         │
       └─────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                                     │
           ┌─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
           ▼                                                   ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐                         ┌─────────────────────────┐
│ Winter Foundation Engine│                         │ Classic Execution Engine│
│  • CTL Peak: 150–160    │    Seasonal Transition  │  • Tactical Economy     │
│  • Massive Aerobic Base │ ──────────────────────► │  • Low Time Above FTP   │
│  • Lifetime FTP Peak    │  (Shedding Fatigue)     │  • Late-Race Durability │
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Durability and the "Ordinary" Power File Paradox

When analyzing the winning telemetry of a Monument:
* **The "Squiggly" Profile:** The raw trace displays constant stochastic micro-bursts (250–500W) to negotiate sector entrances and cobbles, but lacks massive sustained 10-minute threshold spikes.
* **Preserving Anaerobic Work Capacity ($W'$):** Jackson accumulated only **~35 minutes total over FTP** during the entire 3-hour-40-minute event.
* **Late-Race Power Decay Mitigation:** Riders with low durability experience progressive downward drift in motor unit recruitment and glycolytic capacity after 2,500 kJ. Riders with elite aerobic infrastructure preserve 100% of their sprint and threshold power at the velodrome finish.

```
  Low Aerobic Infrastructure ──► 2,500 kJ Expended ──► W' Depleted ──► Power Collapses
  Elite Aerobic Durability   ──► 3,000 kJ Expended ──► W' Spared   ──► Full Sprint Available
```

---

### 2. Periodization Architecture: Winter Capacity vs. Spring Freshness

Adam Pulford’s periodization framework separates **capacity acquisition** from **competitive expression**:

```
  Phase 1: Winter Base (Oct–Feb)    Phase 2: Spring Taper/Race (Mar–Apr)
  ├─────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┤
  • High Volume (18–25+ hrs/wk)     • Reduced Training Volume
  • Heavy Tempo / Threshold Blocks  • Race Specificity (FRC / W' surges)
  • CTL Peak: 150–160               • Controlled CTL Decay (150 ──► 120)
  • Set Lifetime FTP / 20-min Power • High Freshness (TSB Positive/Near-0)
```

* **The Winter Overload:** Establishing an all-time peak FTP in February creates a higher physiological "floor." A rider with a 300W FTP cruising at 220W operates at 73% of threshold (Zone 2 aerobic), whereas a 260W rider operates at 85% (Sweetspot/Tempo), rapidly accumulating peripheral fatigue.
* **The Spring classics Drain:** Travel logistics, course reconnaissances, multi-race weeks (e.g., Wednesday semi-classics + Sunday Monuments), and cognitive stress impose heavy systemic strain. Trying to *build* fitness in April leads to chronic overreaching; the coach must allow CTL to decline while maintaining sharpness.

---

### 3. Block Microcycle Architecture for High-Volume Athletes

For professional or "time-rich" endurance athletes, Pulford utilizes a **3-Day Descending Block Microcycle**:

```
  Day 1 (Maximal Load)   : 5.0 hrs with Long Tempo/Climbing (60+ min in-zone)
  Day 2 (Moderate Load)  : 3.5–4.0 hrs with Repeated Tempo/Sweetspot Intervals
  Day 3 (Consolidation)  : 2.5–3.0 hrs with Focused Interval Quality
  Day 4 (Active Recovery): 1.5–2.0 hrs Low-Torque Coffee Spin + Mobility/Yoga
  Day 5–7                : Group Simulation Ride, Threshold Work, & Rest
```

* **Progressive Fatigue Accumulation:** Day 1 applies fresh neuromuscular strain; Days 2 and 3 force motor unit recruitment under pre-existing glycogen depletion and metabolic strain, stimulating deep mitochondrial and capillarization adaptations.
* **Supercompensation Prerequisite:** Requires 9–10 hours of nightly sleep and complete nutritional replenishment between sessions.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. In-Race Energy Allocation & Pacing Rules

* **"Do Just Enough to Win":** Avoid unnecessary attacks or pulls in non-strategic sectors.
* **The Breakaway Advantage:** Getting into an early break on rough courses (cobbles, gravel) allows the rider to ride smooth, steady lines through technical sections at their own tempo, avoiding the accordion effect and violent braking/accelerations of the main peloton.
* **Cognitive Simplicity:** Replace complex metric dashboards with binary internal cues: *“Am I in the red?”*, *“Am I on the wheel?”*, *“Is this effort essential?”*

---

### 2. High-Volume Endurance Zone Guidelines

* **Prescription by RPE & Talk Test:** Set endurance riding at **RPE 4–6 out of 10** (conversational, low autonomic stress).
* **Avoiding the Segment Trap:** Long endurance rides (4–6 hours) must remain strictly aerobic. Surging up hills or chasing Strava segments converts a base-building session into an unproductive high-fatigue workout.
* **The 2-Hour "Coffee Ride":** Incorporate dedicated active recovery rides (60–90 min, ultra-low power, social/mental decompression) to stimulate blood flow and preserve parasympathetic tone.

---

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Attempting to Build CTL During Race Season:** Packing high interval volume between mid-week and weekend races, destroying autonomic recovery and suppressing race-day top-end power.
2. **Ignoring Total Life Stress (Life-TSS):** Assuming training stress exists in a vacuum without factoring in travel fatigue, media obligations, work demands, and emotional stress.
3. **Over-Relying on Single Metrics (CTL Obsession):** Chasing an arbitrary CTL number (e.g., 160) regardless of athlete fatigue, mood state, or sleep quality.
4. **Racing on the Computer Screen:** Staring at live power, TSS, or glucose values during a high-speed road race instead of reading race dynamics, wind direction, and competitor fatigue.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Classics Training & Racing Diagnostic Matrix

| Training Variable | Inefficient / High-Risk Approach | Championship Standard (Pulford Model) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Winter Preparation** | Low-volume polarized work with minimal base | High-volume Zone 2 + progressive Tempo blocks (CTL 150+) |
| **Spring Racing Phase** | Adding heavy interval sessions between race weekends | Relying on races for intensity; focusing mid-week on rest & recon |
| **In-Race Energy** | Burning matches chasing every minor move | Calculating energy spend; accumulating <40 min over FTP |
| **Breakaway Strategy** | Waiting for late peloton attacks on brutal cobbles | Early break establishment to ride smooth, steady lines |
| **Recovery Modality** | Complete multi-week bed-rest shutdowns | Active recovery coffee spins + daily communication & mobility |
| **Athlete Mindset** | Rigid perfectionism leading to burnout | Cultivating psychological safety, fun, and intrinsic motivation |
