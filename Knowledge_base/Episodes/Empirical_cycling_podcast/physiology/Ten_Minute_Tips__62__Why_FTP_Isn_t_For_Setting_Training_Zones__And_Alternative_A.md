# Ten Minute Tips #62: Why FTP Isn't For Setting Training Zones (And Alternative Approaches) — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with James and Gediminas_

---

## What Is the Problem with FTP-Based Training Zones?

In modern cycling, a ubiquitous dogma asserts: *"Test your FTP to set your training zones."* Athletes calculate FTP and rigidly follow percentage-based zones (e.g., Zone 2 = 55–75%, Zone 4 = 90–105%, Zone 5 = 106–120%, Zone 6 = 121–150%).

While FTP accurately defines the boundary of steady-state aerobic metabolic stability (the second threshold / LT2 / MLSS), **using FTP as a universal anchor to prescribe all training zones is fundamentally flawed**. Individual physiological diversity—divergent fiber type distributions, anaerobic work capacities ($W'$), and volume-dependent lower thresholds (LT1/VT1)—renders fixed percentage zones ineffective for eliciting targeted physiological adaptations.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Descriptive vs. Prescriptive Zones
* **Descriptive Origin:** Classic power levels were designed to describe what athletes were doing in training retrospectively.
* **Prescriptive Failure:** When used prescriptively, fixed percentages assume identical physiological profiles across all athletes. An athlete with an anaerobic phenotype may find 120% FTP submaximal during a 5-minute interval, while an aerobic "diesel" athlete may fail to sustain 115% FTP for 3 minutes.

### 2. Failure Above Threshold: VO2max and Anaerobic Capacity
* **The VO2max Domain:** Eliciting maximal oxygen uptake ($\dot{V}O_2max$) and maximal cardiac stroke volume requires driving the cardiopulmonary system to its limit. Prescribing a fixed 120% FTP often results in:
  * Highly anaerobic riders riding comfortably below true VO2max (insufficient central cardiac stimulus).
  * Highly aerobic riders blowing up their anaerobic reserve within 2 minutes (premature failure).
* **The Anaerobic/Sprint Domain:** Maximal neuromuscular power ($P_{max}$) and anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) are decoupled from aerobic threshold. A 200W FTP sprinter with a 2100W sprint operates at 1050% FTP; prescribing anaerobic intervals as fixed % FTP is physiologically meaningless.

### 3. Failure Below Threshold: The LT1 / VT1 Disconnect
* **The Wide Range of LT1:** The first metabolic threshold (LT1 / VT1 / moderate-to-heavy domain transition) represents the point where blood lactate begins to rise above baseline and fat oxidation peaks.
* **Percentage Variance:** In untrained or anaerobic athletes, LT1 can sit as low as **45–50% of FTP**. In elite, high-volume endurance athletes, LT1 can reach **80–85% of FTP**.
* **Independent Plasticity:** Long-term aerobic volume and capillarization raise LT1 independently of FTP. Setting Zone 2 as a blanket 55–75% FTP risks:
  * Under-stimulating high-volume riders whose LT1 is at 80% FTP.
  * Over-stressing anaerobic riders whose LT1 is at 55% FTP, accumulating unintended metabolic fatigue on "easy" days.

```
[ High Anaerobic Phenotype ]                  [ Elite Aerobic Diesel Phenotype ]
  Pmax: 2000W+ | W': High                       Pmax: 1100W | W': Low
  VO2max Power: 135–150% FTP                     VO2max Power: 110–118% FTP
  FTP (LT2): 300W                               FTP (LT2): 350W
  LT1 / VT1: ~50% FTP (150W)                    LT1 / VT1: ~80% FTP (280W)
  ────────────────────────                      ────────────────────────
  Standard 120% FTP VO2 target = Too Easy       Standard 120% FTP VO2 target = Impossible
  Standard 70% FTP Z2 = Above LT1               Standard 70% FTP Z2 = Sub-optimal Stimulus
```

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The Legitimate Utility of FTP
FTP remains a vital metric when applied correctly:
1. **Prescribing Threshold Work:** Setting targets at 95–102% FTP to extend Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE).
2. **Prescribing Sweet Spot Work:** Standardized at ~88–92% FTP as a submaximal fatigue-resistant stimulus.
3. **Fitness & Fatigue Diagnostic:** Checking how 10–15 minutes at FTP feels mid-season indicates residual systemic fatigue vs. freshness.

### 2. Alternative Prescriptions by Intensity Domain

| Intensity Domain / Quality | Why FTP Fails | Prescribed Alternative Approach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Zone 1 / Active Recovery** | % FTP can be too hard for deep fatigue. | Ride strictly by feel (<50–60% FTP / <100W); zero pedal resistance. |
| **Zone 2 / Endurance (Sub-LT1)** | LT1 varies from 45% to 85% FTP. | **Talk Test / Ventilatory Threshold:** Ride at a pace where continuous nose breathing or full sentences are comfortable; calibrate via decoupling step tests. |
| **Zone 3 / Tempo & Sweet Spot** | Standard percentages are rough approximations. | Set at 80–92% FTP; auto-regulate duration ($1 \times 40\text{m} \rightarrow 1 \times 60\text{m} \rightarrow 1 \times 90\text{m}$). |
| **Zone 4 / Threshold (FTP)** | Fixed durations miss individual TTE. | Prescribe by known FTP or RPE 7–8/10; progress interval duration ($3\times 15\text{m} \rightarrow 2\times 30\text{m}$) to individual limits. |
| **Zone 5 / VO2max** | 120% FTP misses maximal aerobic kinetics. | **Maximal Repeatable Pacing:** 100–110+ RPM cadence, RPE 9–10/10, breathing to hyperventilation ("air hunger"); hide power meter to prevent artificial capping. |
| **Zone 6 / Anaerobic Capacity** | FRC/$W'$ is decoupled from FTP. | Prescribe by duration ($30\text{s to }2\text{ min}$) and maximal repeatable intent; ride until legs feel like "pushing rope." |
| **Zone 7 / Neuromuscular Sprint** | $P_{max}$ has zero mathematical link to FTP. | All-out explosive efforts from specific cadences/starting speeds; evaluate power purely post-hoc. |

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

* **Using Erg Mode for Above-Threshold Intervals:** Forcing a trainer in Erg mode to hold a fixed percentage during 30/30s or VO2max sets, which either bogs the cadence down into a spiral of death or prevents maximal aerobic power production.
* **Obsessing Over Power Meter During Sprints:** Looking down at the screen during maximal sprints rather than focusing on explosive biomechanics and safety.
* **Capping VO2max Power at 120%:** Artificially holding back during VO2 intervals because a spreadsheet or app prescribed a specific wattage, failing to drive the cardiovascular system to true $\dot{V}O_2max$.
* **Assuming Zone 2 Is Identical for Everyone:** Believing that 70% FTP is universally easy, leading low-LT1 athletes to accumulate chronic autonomic fatigue on baseline endurance rides.

| Training Target | Traditional FTP-Percentage Method | Physiological Alternative Prescription |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **VO2max Interval Session** | Set Erg mode to 120% FTP for $5 \times 3\text{ min}$. | $5 \times 3\text{--}5\text{ min}$ all-out pacing; high cadence (100–115 RPM); gasping ventilation. |
| **Anaerobic Surge Conditioning** | $30\text{s on} / 30\text{s off}$ at 135% FTP. | $30\text{s} / 30\text{s}$ maximal sustainable surge power; adjust duration based on muscular failure. |
| **Base Endurance Ride** | Ride strictly at 65–75% FTP. | Anchor to conversational/nasal breathing pace; stay strictly below perceived LT1/VT1. |
| **Threshold Progression** | $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ at 100% FTP every week. | Auto-regulate power and extend duration ($3\times 15\text{m} \rightarrow 2\times 25\text{m} \rightarrow 1\times 50\text{m}$) based on TTE. |
| **Maximal Sprint Practice** | $6 \times 15\text{s}$ at 250% FTP. | $6 \times 15\text{s}$ full-gas sprints with 4–5 min full recovery; analyze power post-session. |
