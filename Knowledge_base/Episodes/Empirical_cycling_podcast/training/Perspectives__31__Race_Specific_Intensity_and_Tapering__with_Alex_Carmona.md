# Perspectives #31: Race Specific Intensity and Tapering — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Alex Carmona_

---

## What Is Race-Specific Preparation and Tapering?

Race-specific preparation is the targeted phase of training that bridges general aerobic foundation (Zone 2 volume, FTP extension, and VO2max capacity) to the distinct metabolic, neuromuscular, and cognitive demands of competition. 

While base and threshold training build the total physiological "engine," race-specific training sharpens the ability to extract and express that fitness under chaotic, intermittent, and high-cadence conditions. Tapering is the subsequent, highly calculated manipulation of training volume, intensity, and frequency designed to shed accumulated physiological and central nervous system (CNS) fatigue while preserving enzymatic activity, blood volume, and neuromuscular priming.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. From Aerobic Infrastructure to Fractional Utilization and $W'$ Flux
- **General Aerobic Base:** Expands capillary density, mitochondrial surface area, and muscle glycogen storage capacity.
- **Race-Specific Flux:** Races demand rapid and repeated reconstitution of Anaerobic Work Capacity ($W'$), high peak torque generation at unpredictable cadences, and tolerance to rapid shifts between oxidative phosphorylation and glycolytic flux.
- **The Dose-Response Threshold:** Athletes often perform too much high-intensity race-specific work year-round. It requires only 4–6 weeks of targeted race preparation to fully adapt neuromuscular and glycolytic pathways once the aerobic base is established.

### 2. Neural Desensitization and High-Speed Motor Skill Acquisition
- **Autonomic Threat Response:** Cornering at $28\text{–}35\text{ mph}$ in a dense peloton triggers sympathetic arousal (excessive handlebar gripping, elevated respiratory rate, muscular bracing), which wastes glycogen and creates erratic deceleration/acceleration cycles.
- **Race-Pace Motor Learning:** Neuromuscular coordination and braking points must be trained at actual race velocities. Pacing through corners below race speed fails to train the vestibular and motor unit firing adaptations needed on race day.

### 3. Tapering Dynamics: Fatigue Dissipation vs. Neuromuscular Tone
- **Fatigue Decay vs. Fitness Decay:** Fatigue dissipates roughly twice as fast as fitness. A well-designed taper drops fatigue rapidly while maintaining fitness.
- **The Staleness Trap:** Total inactivity or excessive volume reduction causes a drop in blood plasma volume, decreased muscle glycogen synthase activity, and a downregulation of central motor unit excitability (leading to "sluggish legs"). Maintaining short, high-intensity efforts preserves plasma volume and motor unit recruitment without generating structural fatigue.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The General-to-Specific Race-Prep Progression Model

Structure race-specific training blocks across 4–6 weeks using a 3-tier hierarchical progression:

```
[Phase 1: Capacity]    -> 30s/30s or 40s/20s with incomplete recoveries (builds repeatability & buffering)
        │
[Phase 2: Peak Power]  -> 30s–60s all-out efforts with full recovery (5–10 min) to maximize raw torque/FRC
        │
[Phase 3: Specificity] -> Terrain- and duration-matched intervals (e.g., 13x90s hill repeats, course simulations)
```

#### Starting Workout: 30/30 Capacity Introductions
- **Protocol:** 2–3 sets of $5\text{–}6 \times (30\text{s MAX} / 30\text{s easy spin})$ with 5–8 minutes between sets.
- **Purpose:** Re-introduces high motor unit recruitment and rapid glycolysis with manageable metabolic hangover.

---

### 2. Event-Specific Preparation Frameworks

#### A. Time Trial Preparation (e.g., Rolling / Technical TT)
- **6 Weeks Out:** Sub-maximal course simulation (riding target course or simulator profile at 85–90% FTP to map braking, cornering lines, and cadence transitions).
- **3–4 Weeks Out:** Broken race-pace efforts (e.g., 2–3 laps of the course broken into segments at 102–105% target race power with short rests).
- **2 Weeks Out:** Full-gas pacing test over 50–70% of race duration in full aerodynamic position.

#### B. Punchy Circuit / Road Race (e.g., Short Repeated Climbs)
- **Protocol:** 1–1.5 minute maximal efforts matching the course's primary climb duration.
- **Progression:**
  - *Week 1:* $8 \times 90\text{s}$ with 4-minute recoveries.
  - *Week 3:* $10 \times 90\text{s}$ with 2.5-minute recoveries.
  - *Week 5:* $12\text{–}14 \times 90\text{s}$ with race-matched recoveries (simulating peloton speed into the base of each climb).

#### C. Stage Race Preparation (e.g., 3–5 Day Stage Races)
- **3–4 Weeks Out (Simulation Block):** Cluster 3–4 consecutive hard days:
  - *Day 1 (Fri):* Steady-state Threshold intervals ($3 \times 15\text{ min}$).
  - *Day 2 (Sat):* Criterium or race-pace group ride + 2 hours Zone 2 endurance.
  - *Day 3 (Sun):* Practice race / simulated road race with repeated punchy surges.
  - *Day 4 (Mon):* Fatigued threshold with sprints ($3 \times 10\text{ min}$ tempo with 10s max sprints every 2 min).

---

### 3. Tapering Architecture & Protocols

```
[2 Weeks Out] ──> Normal volume (80–90%), reduce moderate-intensity days; keep 2 key hard sessions.
[1 Week Out]  ──> Cut total volume by 40–50%; keep session frequency high; retain high-intensity touches.
[Race Week Microcycle]:
  - Mon: Rest Day (off bike or 30 min active recovery)
  - Tue: 90 min Zone 2 with 3x1 min @ Race Pace
  - Wed: Maintenance Hard Day (e.g., 2x10 min @ Sweet Spot / Threshold + 2x15s sprints)
  - Thu: 60 min easy spin (<55% FTP)
  - Fri: Race Openers (45–60 min: 3x30s build sprints + 1x3 min @ FTP)
  - Sat/Sun: RACE DAY
```

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall | Mechanism of Failure | Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The "Double Rest" Trap** | Taking a full rest week followed immediately by a taper week drops plasma volume, neuromuscular tone, and autonomic readiness. | Do not stack a rest week before a taper; taper directly from a training block by reducing volume while maintaining intensity touches. |
| **Panic Training Upon Disruption** | Cramming missed workouts within 10 days of a goal race due to travel, illness, or work stress adds unrecoverable fatigue. | Accept disruptions; 85% of fitness is already established. Default to rest and easy aerobic maintenance. |
| **Year-Round Race Intensity** | Doing group rides, crits, and high-intensity intervals all season blunts aerobic enzyme upregulation and induces chronic fatigue. | Limit high-intensity race-prep blocks to 4–6 week windows prior to target A-priority events. |
| **Excessive Carb Loading for Short Events** | Overeating dense carbohydrate meals before 30–60 min crits or hill climbs causes gastric fullness and water retention without benefit. | Maintain standard carbohydrate intake (6–8 g/kg) and consume a normal pre-race meal 2.5–3 hours prior. |
| **Logistical Allostatic Overload** | Staying up late packing, cleaning bikes, and managing travel stress spikes cortisol and disrupts sleep architecture. | Complete all bike maintenance, packing, and nutrition staging $\ge 48$ hours before departure. |

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Race Prep & Taper Decision Matrix

| Athlete Status / Race Context | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Athlete feels lethargic/flat 3 days before race** | Schedule a 45-min activation session with 2x2 min @ Threshold and 3x10s high-cadence sprints to restore neuromuscular firing. |
| **Athlete is racing weekly during peak season** | Eliminate midweek interval sessions; use midweek exclusively for low-intensity volume (Z2) and active recovery. |
| **Stage race is 3 weeks away** | Execute a 3-day clustered overload weekend (Friday threshold, Saturday crit, Sunday long road ride) followed by a 4-day recovery buffer. |
| **Disruption occurs 5 days out (mild cold / flight delay)** | Remove all remaining interval work; prioritize sleep, hydration, and easy 30–45 min spins. |
| **Athlete questions race readiness during taper** | Reassure athlete that taper-induced phantom sensations (lethargy, heavy legs) are normal; trust the training volume accumulated. |
