# FTP Workout — The 2×20 Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kelly Moore & James McKay_

---

## Why 2×20 Has a Special Place in Cycling

The 2×20 is the canonical FTP workout. It entered cycling culture both as a training session and as a reflection of the classic 20-minute FTP test from _Training and Racing with a Power Meter_. It's often the first structured workout a cyclist learns when they start training with power.

> Likely originated from Andy Coggan doing a 5-minute loop 4× (1 rep + rest + repeat), progressively getting faster each week.

---

## Minimum Effective Duration for FTP Intervals

**The lag problem:** When you start an FTP interval, heart rate takes 2–3 minutes to catch up to the power output. During those first minutes, muscles work partly anaerobically (creatine phosphate + glycogen). You are only achieving the target metabolic intensity — aerobically — after that lag passes.

| Interval length | Effective metabolic FTP time |
| --------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 10 min          | ~7 min                       |
| 20 min          | ~15–17 min                   |

**Minimum effective duration:** ~10 minutes per interval at 95–100% FTP.

**Minimum effective dose per session:** ~30 minutes of metabolic FTP work (e.g., 2×20 or 4×10).

**Implication for 3×10:** You only get ~20 minutes of true metabolic FTP stimulus — equivalent to 1×25.

---

## Intensity: How Hard Is FTP Work?

Target: **95–100% of FTP**. Going slightly below (e.g., 95%) produces the same metabolic stimulus, with lower fatigue — allowing more volume across the block.

**What happens above FTP:** Blood lactate rises, meaning you are burning carbohydrates faster than you can aerobically metabolize them. Going to 103–105% FTP is not more effective than 100% FTP for threshold adaptation — and costs significantly more glycogen and recovery.

> If you feel scared of riding at FTP, first do longer FTP tests to calibrate what it actually feels like. Many athletes set FTP too high from short tests, then ride "sweet spot" thinking it's hard when it's actually threshold.

---

## Pacing: The Most Common Mistake

Going too hard on interval 1 causes intervals 2 and 3 to fall below the target zone. You get one good interval and two junk ones.

**Goal:** consistent effort from interval 1 through the last. You should not finish a threshold workout looking destroyed — get the work in, go home.

---

## How to Progress FTP Workouts

### Core principle
Increase **time-in-zone**, not power. Retest FTP at the end of a training block to reset the target.

### Progression path (example: FTP = 250 W, work intervals at 235–240 W)

| Step | Session                                | Metabolic FTP time |
| ---- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| 1    | 4×10 or 3×10                           | ~20–28 min         |
| 2    | 2×15 or 3×15                           | ~24–36 min         |
| 3    | **2×20** ← canonical starting point   | ~34 min            |
| 4    | 3×20                                   | ~51 min            |
| 5    | 2×30 or 4×20                           | ~54–68 min         |
| 6    | 1×40–60 (continuous preferred)         | ~37–57 min         |

**Continuous > intermittent.** A single 40-minute interval is at least as effective as 2×20 at slightly higher power. Once you can do 2×20 well, progress toward longer continuous blocks.

If feeling good, two options:
1. **Connect intervals** — 3×20 → 2×30 → 1×60
2. **Add a rep** — 3×15 → 4×15

---

## Time-Crunched Athletes

If you only have 40–60 minutes:

- Account for 3–5 min warm-up lag on each interval
- Prioritize **fewer, longer intervals** over more short ones (less total lag wasted)
- If you have 45 min total: 15 min warm-up + 1×30 min at 95–100% FTP
- Do not try to compensate with intensity over FTP — same stimulus, more fatigue, worse next day

---

## Athletes With Lots of Time (2–3+ hr rides)

### Option 1 — Intervals embedded in a long ride
- Hour 1: endurance pace
- Hour 2: 2×20 FTP
- Hour 3: ride home at endurance pace

### Option 2 — Spread intervals across the ride
- One 20-min FTP effort per hour (e.g., 3×20 over 3 hours with ~40 min between)
- Trains the body to produce power while fatigued — more race-specific

### Option 3 — Manipulate recovery intensity
Once the athlete has built a solid FTP base, raise recovery power between intervals:
- Start: ~50% FTP between efforts
- Progress to: 65–70% FTP between efforts
- This is an advanced stimulus — not appropriate for beginners

> These advanced structures are not scalable down to beginner or intermediate athletes. There's a reason 2×20 is the default — it works, and it's always a reliable fallback.

---

## Key Metrics to Track Progress

| Metric                       | What it tells you                                      |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Total time-in-zone**       | Primary progression marker — keep driving it up        |
| **Ability to connect reps**  | Readiness to move from 2×20 → 1×40                    |
| **RPE at target power**      | Same watts feeling easier = adaptation                 |
| **FTP retest at block end**  | Power target resets; don't increase mid-block          |

---

## Fueling FTP Sessions

Above FTP, glycogen burns at maximum rate. At FTP, lactate is at its highest stable level — any harder and carbohydrate expenditure spikes further.

| Priority                         | Recommendation                                                     |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Muscle glycogen (day before)** | Eat adequate carbs 24–48h before a hard FTP session                |
| **On-bike fueling**              | Required for sessions >60 min; prevents blood sugar crash mid-set  |
| **Post-session**                 | Fueling during the ride accelerates recovery vs. eating it all after |

## FTP Work vs. Other Intensities

| Intensity      | Relative fatigue | Best for                              |
| -------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Sweet spot     | Moderate         | Muscular endurance, early season      |
| **Threshold**  | **Moderate-high**| **FTP development, time-efficient**   |
| VO2max         | High             | Ceiling raising, anaerobic capacity   |

---

## Decision Checklist Before Prescribing FTP Work

1. Is the athlete's FTP **accurately estimated**? (Overestimated FTP → threshold work mislabeled as sweet spot)
2. Do they have **enough time** to reach ≥30 min of metabolic FTP?
3. Are they **recovered enough** to hold consistent power across all reps?
4. Is the goal **FTP development**, not VO2max or anaerobic capacity?
5. Are they **pacing correctly** — starting conservatively enough to hold the last interval?

If all five are yes → prescribe it. If time is short → shorten rest, not intervals. If going 2–3×/week → monitor fatigue carefully; quality degrades before the signal is obvious.
