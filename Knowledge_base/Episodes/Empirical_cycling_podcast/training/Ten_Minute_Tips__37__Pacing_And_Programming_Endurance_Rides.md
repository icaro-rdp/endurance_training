# Ten Minute Tips #37: Pacing And Programming Endurance Rides — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Rory Porteous (Ten Minute Tips #37)_

---

## What Is the True Role of Endurance Rides?

In endurance cycling, low-intensity base training (often termed "Zone 2") represents the foundational volume of athletic development. However, athletes frequently undermine their training by:
1. **Pacing Endurance Rides Too Hard:** Riding at the upper boundary of mathematical power zones (e.g., 70–75% FTP) rather than physiological sub-threshold limits, accumulating chronic "gray-zone" fatigue.
2. **Confusing Descriptive Models with Prescriptions:** Attempting to force arbitrary 80/20 polarized distributions onto constrained schedules (e.g., 6–8 hours/week) instead of prioritizing interval quality and recovery.
3. **Misinterpreting Metabolic Signals:** Believing that burning fat or riding fasted confers unique mitochondrial adaptations, ignoring the master transcriptional regulators of cellular respiration.

```
                      THE ENDURANCE PACING SPECTRUM
0% FTP                     LT1 / VT1 (Inflection Point)                  FTP
◄──────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────►
  Optimal Base Volume (RPE 2–3/10) │ Excessive "Gray-Zone" Base (RPE 4–6)
  • 60–80% of LT1 Power            │ • Non-linear fatigue accumulation
  • Fully conversational breathing  │ • Saps subsequent interval quality
  • Low autonomic fatigue          │ • High glycogen drain
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Cellular Signaling for Mitochondrial Biogenesis
Exercise-induced mitochondrial adaptations (capillarization, citrate synthase activity, Cytochrome C oxidase upregulation) bottleneck through a family of transcriptional coactivators:
* **The Master Regulators:** **PGC-1$\alpha$**, **PGC-1$\beta$**, and **PRC** (PGC-1-related coactivator).
* **The Stimulus Mechanism:** These cascades are activated by intracellular energy turnover ($AMP/ATP$ ratio triggering $AMPK$, calcium flux triggering $CaMK$), **not by substrate selection**.
* **Fat Burning Fallacy:** Maximizing fat oxidation rates ($FatMax$) does not accelerate mitochondrial biogenesis. Withholding carbohydrates during base rides elevates systemic cortisol, impairs immune function, and compromises workout duration without additive mitochondrial gene expression.

### 2. Fatigue Burden vs. Energy Burden ($kJ$)
Understanding the systemic cost of endurance rides requires separating mechanical/metabolic fatigue from caloric expenditure:

```
Total Ride Burden:
├── 1. Fatigue Burden (Neuromuscular / Metabolic):
│   ├── Exceeding LT1 recruits larger, less oxidative Type II motor units.
│   └── Produces acute metabolite accumulation, elevating autonomic recovery time from hours to days.
└── 2. Energy Burden (Total Caloric / Kilojoule Deficit):
    ├── 4–8 hour rides burn 4,000–8,000+ kJ ("FoodMax" barrier).
    └── Even with 90–120 g/hr intra-ride carbohydrate ingestion, complete liver and muscle glycogen resynthesis requires 48–72 hours of dedicated off-bike fueling.
```

### 3. Locating LT1 / Aerobic Threshold in the Field
* **Laboratory vs. Field Discrepancy:** Fixed blood lactate markers (e.g., $2.0\text{ mmol/L}$) frequently overstate an athlete's sustainable endurance power, leading to chronic overreaching.
* **The Ventilatory & Perceptual Anchor:** Real-world LT1 is identified when **breathing transitions from completely unhurried nasal/conversational flow to rhythmic ventilatory engagement**, accompanied by a sharp jump in perceived exertion ($RPE$ jumping from 2–3 to 5/10).
* **Practical Wattage Target:** For high-volume sustainability, target **60–80% of LT1 power** (which often translates to **45–65% of FTP** for amateur and diesel riders).

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Programming Framework Across Volume Tiers

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    VOLUME-TIER PROGRAMMING MATRIX                       │
├─────────────────┬───────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┤
│ Weekly Volume   │ Key Sessions      │ Endurance Pacing & Structure      │
├─────────────────┼───────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
│ Low Volume      │ 1–2 High-Quality  │ Short Z2 (1–2 hrs) paced easy;    │
│ (<10 hrs/week)  │ Interval Sessions │ Protects recovery; 2 full rest    │
│                 │ (FTP / VO2max)    │ days mandatory.                   │
├─────────────────┼───────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
│ Medium Volume   │ 2 (rarely 3) Key  │ Mid-length Z2 (2–4 hrs); weekend  │
│ (12–17 hrs/wk)  │ Interval Sessions │ back-to-back rides; strict sub-   │
│                 │                   │ LT1 pacing to prevent burnout.    │
├─────────────────┼───────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
│ High Volume     │ 1–2 Targeted Hard │ High volume (4–8 hr rides);       │
│ (>20 hrs/week)  │ Days Max          │ 60–75% LT1; fueling logistics     │
│                 │                   │ and off-bike rest are critical.   │
└─────────────────┴───────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2. Pacing Rules of Thumb
* **"Let the Watts Come to You":** Start every endurance ride at 40–50% FTP for the first 20–30 minutes until musculature is warm and oxygen kinetics stabilize; never force target watts out of the driveway.
* **Decoupling Rule:** If power must be reduced by >10% to maintain stable RPE over a 3–5 hour ride, the initial pace was too high, or fueling/hydration was deficient.
* **Coffee Stop Protocol:** Stopping for 20–40 minutes to consume carbohydrates triggers an insulin surge; when resuming, ride very easily for 15–20 minutes to clear transient rebound hypoglycemia.

### 3. Integrating Overload: Intervals on the Long Ride
Athletes preparing for gravel races or ultra-endurance events should embed interval work inside long rides:
* **Protocol:** During a 4–6 hour ride, insert $3 \times 20\text{ min}$ at Sweet Spot / Threshold during hours 2, 3, and 4.
* **Purpose:** Stimulates motor unit recruitment and trains substrate utilization under pre-accumulated kilojoule load ($>2000\text{ kJ}$).

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The "Junk Mile" Fallacy in Base Training:** Riding at 50% FTP is not "useless spinning." Accumulating high volume at 50–60% FTP drives eccentric cardiac hypertrophy (stroke volume), capillarization, and peripheral enzyme density with near-zero autonomic fatigue.

1. **Pre-Planning Rigid Zone Percentages (80/20 Dogma):** Trying to mathematically force an 80/20 intensity split on 6 hours/week results in under-stimulating intervals and insufficient base duration.
2. **Peppered 45-Minute "Zone 2" Commutes on Rest Days:** Adding short, unstructured endurance rides on designated rest days prevents full central nervous system and glycogen recovery.
3. **Over-Cadence Drills in Base Rides:** Forcing artificially high cadences (>105 RPM) during base rides increases cardiovascular strain, heart rate, and glycogen consumption without improving metabolic efficiency.
4. **Fasted Long Rides:** Completing 3+ hour base rides in a glycogen-depleted state elevates muscle proteolysis and systemic stress hormones, degrading adaptive signaling and recovery.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Variable / Scenario | Traditional Error | Scientific Prescription |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Endurance Ride Target** | Pegged rigidly at 70–75% FTP. | Paced at 60–80% LT1 (typically 45–65% FTP / RPE 2–3/10). |
| **Breathing / Talk Test** | Breathing hard through the mouth on base rides. | Able to speak full, uninterrupted sentences without gasping. |
| **Substrate Fueling** | Riding fasted or limiting carbs to 20g/hr. | Fuel with 50–90g/hr carbohydrates to maintain energy neutrality. |
| **Hilly Terrain Pacing** | Surging at 120% FTP up every rolling climb. | Feather power up hills (cap surges at threshold); keep overall RPE low. |
| **Fatigue vs. Rest Day** | Adding a 60-min Z2 ride on Monday recovery day. | Keep recovery days sacred (0 Watts or gentle 30-min Z1 spin <100W). |
| **Volume Spike Week** | Avoiding big volume weeks because they don't fit the weekly average. | Execute 1–2 week training camps (+50–100% volume) at strictly low intensity; rest afterward. |
