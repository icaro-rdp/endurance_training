# FTP vs. VO2max: Fractional Utilization & Scientific Testing Flaws — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson (Watts Doc #11)_

---

## What Is the Relationship Between FTP and $VO_2max$?

In exercise physiology and endurance coaching, **$VO_2max$** (maximal oxygen uptake) and **FTP** (Functional Threshold Power / Maximal Lactate Steady State) are frequently conflated, yet they represent distinctly different physiological phenomena:

* **$VO_2max$ (Aerobic Ceiling):** The maximal rate at which the cardiorespiratory and muscular systems can transport and consume oxygen to synthesize ATP aerobically. It represents the absolute physiological ceiling of aerobic power (typically sustainable for ~3–8 minutes).
* **FTP / MLSS (Endurance Threshold):** The highest mechanical power output or oxygen consumption rate sustainable in a quasi-steady state without rapid, progressive metabolic fatigue. It marks the boundary where lactate production matches maximal clearance.
* **Fractional Utilization ($\%VO_2max$ at FTP):** The percentage of an athlete's maximal aerobic capacity that can be sustained at threshold. While $VO_2max$ sets the ceiling, fractional utilization determines how much of that ceiling is operationally usable for sustained endurance performance.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
               VO2max vs. FTP / Fractional Utilization Spectrum
  
  Athlete A (High Fractional Utilization: 86%):
  0% ─────────────────────────────────────────── [FTP: 86%] ───────► [VO2max: 100%]
  [◄──────── Sustainable Aerobic Domain ────────────────►][◄─ Severe Domain ─►]
  
  Athlete B (Low Fractional Utilization: 65%):
  0% ───────────────────── [FTP: 65%] ──────────────────────────────► [VO2max: 100%]
  [◄── Aerobic Domain ────►][◄────────────── Severe / Rapid Fatigue ────────►]
```

### 1. Coyle et al. (1988): Determinants of Endurance in Well-Trained Cyclists
In this landmark paper co-authored by Dr. Edward Coyle and Dr. Andrew Coggan, 14 competitive male cyclists with nearly identical absolute and relative $VO_2max$ values (~4.5 L/min; ~66 mL/kg/min) were evaluated. Despite identical aerobic ceilings, their threshold as a percentage of $VO_2max$ diverged radically:
* **High Group:** Threshold occurred at **$80\%\text{ to }86\% \text{ of } VO_2max$** (average ~82%).
* **Low Group:** Threshold occurred at **$59\%\text{ to }67\% \text{ of } VO_2max$** (average ~65%).

### 2. Time-to-Exhaustion at 88% $VO_2max$
When all subjects were tasked with holding an identical relative intensity of **$88\% VO_2max$** until failure:
* **High Group:** Averaged **60 minutes** of time-to-exhaustion (longest was **75 minutes** by an athlete with threshold at $86\% VO_2max$, who was only $2\%$ above threshold). Post-test blood lactate averaged **7.4 mmol/L**.
* **Low Group:** Averaged only **29 minutes** (shortest was **12 minutes** by an athlete with threshold at $66\% VO_2max$). Post-test blood lactate averaged **14.7 mmol/L**.

### 3. Metabolic Divergence at Fixed 80% $VO_2max$ (30-Minute Bout)
When riding for 30 minutes at $80\% VO_2max$:
* **Sub-Threshold Athletes (High Group):** Because $80\% VO_2max$ was *below* their threshold, blood lactate remained steady ($<3.0\text{ mmol/L}$), and glycogen depletion was minimal (**$27.9\text{ mmol/kg}$**). Respiratory exchange ratio (RER) remained between $0.81–0.86$ (~50% fat / 50% carbohydrate oxidation).
* **Supra-Threshold Athletes (Low Group):** Because $80\% VO_2max$ was *well above* their threshold, blood lactate climbed continuously ($>5.3\text{ to }8.3\text{ mmol/L}$), and glycogen depletion was more than double (**$65.4\text{ mmol/kg}$**).

```
                      30 Minutes at 80% VO2max Comparison
 ┌─────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┐
 │ Physiological Parameter │ High Group (Below FTP)     │ Low Group (Above FTP)      │
 ├─────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┤
 │ Blood Lactate           │ < 3.0 mmol/L (Steady)      │ 5.3 – 8.3 mmol/L (Rising)  │
 │ Glycogen Depletion      │ 27.9 mmol/kg               │ 65.4 mmol/kg (>2.3x more)  │
 │ Substrate Utilization   │ High Fat (~50%) + Carb     │ Almost Exclusively Carb    │
 │ Perceived Exertion      │ Sustainable "Comfortable"  │ Severe Strain / Near Task  │
 └─────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┘
```

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Why $VO_2max$ Does Not Predict Race Performance
* Two riders with the same 400-Watt 5-minute $VO_2max$ power can have vastly different FTPs (e.g., 280W vs. 340W).
* In road races, time trials, and climbs lasting longer than 10 minutes, the athlete with the higher fractional utilization will ride at a lower relative strain, burn half the glycogen, and easily drop the athlete with a lower threshold.

### 2. Pushing FTP "From Below" (Threshold & Base Work)
To raise fractional utilization from ~70% towards 85%+:
* **Extensive Threshold Intervals:** $2 \times 20\text{ min} \rightarrow 3 \times 20\text{ min} \rightarrow 2 \times 30\text{ min} \rightarrow 1 \times 60\text{ min}$ at 92–97% FTP.
* **Large Aerobic Base Volume:** Zone 2 endurance riding (60–75% FTP) to drive capillarization, mitochondrial biogenesis, and type I muscle fiber oxidative capacity.

### 3. Lifting the Ceiling "From Above" ($VO_2max$ Blocks)
When fractional utilization approaches a hard ceiling (~85–88% of $VO_2max$):
* FTP development plateaus because there is insufficient headroom between threshold and maximal aerobic power.
* At this point, execute a dedicated block of high-intensity $VO_2max$ training (e.g., 3–5 minute all-out intervals with high breathing rates) to expand cardiovascular capacity and raise the absolute $VO_2max$ ceiling.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The Science Protocol Fallacy:** Do not blindly accept conclusions from exercise physiology studies that assign workload based on $\%VO_2max$ (e.g., "subjects rode at $70\% VO_2max$"). Unless individual threshold/MLSS is controlled, subjects below threshold perform light aerobic work while subjects above threshold perform exhaustive glycolytic work, confounding the study's conclusions.

1. **Fixating on $VO_2max$ Lab Test Numbers:**
   * Athletes frequently obsess over laboratory $VO_2max$ scores ($mL/kg/min$) rather than developing sustainable threshold power and time-to-exhaustion (TTE).
2. **Prescribing Team Training by %$VO_2max$:**
   * In team sports or group coaching, assigning runs or intervals based on a fixed percentage of maximal aerobic speed/VO2max subjects athletes to wildly disparate metabolic strains.
3. **Ignoring Glycogen Sparing Mechanics:**
   * A rider with a high threshold spares glycogen naturally during group rides because sub-FTP wattages demand minimal glycolytic flux and preserve carbohydrate stores for end-of-race surges.

### Coach & Athlete Decision Checklist

* [ ] **Recognize the Ceiling vs. the Room:** Treat $VO_2max$ as the roof and FTP as the floor/living space.
* [ ] **Audit Research Methodologies:** When reviewing academic papers, check if exercise intensity was controlled for MLSS/FTP or merely set to an arbitrary $\%VO_2max$.
* [ ] **Determine When to Push vs. Pull:**
  * If FTP is $<75–80\%$ of estimated 5-minute peak power: Focus on extensive threshold and base training to push threshold up.
  * If FTP is $>85\%$ of 5-minute power and plateaued: Focus on intensive $VO_2max$ intervals to lift the ceiling.
* [ ] **Account for Glycogen Kinetics:** Recognize that raising FTP lowers glycogen burn rate at all submaximal racing speeds, reducing in-race fueling vulnerability.
