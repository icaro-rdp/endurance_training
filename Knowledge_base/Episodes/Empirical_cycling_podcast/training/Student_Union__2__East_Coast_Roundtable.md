# Student Union #2: Collegiate Season Planning, Promoter Logistics, & Crisis Periodization — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore, Nick Luther, Kyle Helson & Laura Ann Leaton (Student Union #2)_

## What Is Collegiate Race Organization & Crisis Periodization?

Collegiate cycling operates as a decentralized, student-driven sporting ecosystem governed across regional conferences (such as the Eastern Collegiate Cycling Conference [ECCC], Atlantic Collegiate Cycling Conference [ACCC], and Southeastern Collegiate Cycling Conference [SECCC]). Unlike professional racing with centralized broadcast revenues and commercial sponsorships, collegiate racing relies entirely on volunteer student leadership, host institution approvals, municipal permitting, and self-funding race directors.

**Crisis Periodization** refers to the strategic restructuring of an athletic macrocycle when the competitive calendar is unexpectedly disrupted or cancelled (e.g., pandemics, severe weather, municipal permit revocations). In traditional periodization, training cycles build toward predetermined target dates via progressive overload, specific high-intensity race simulation, and tapering. In crisis periodization, coaches and athletes must decouple training structure from fixed event timelines, refocusing on foundational physiological capacity, aerobic durability, and long-term athletic longevity without inducing chronic psychological burnout or overtraining.

```
                   Traditional vs. Crisis Periodization Paradigms
                   
   Traditional Macrocycle (Event-Driven):
   [Base Aerobic] ──► [Build / TTE] ──► [Race-Specific HIIT / Peaking] ──► [Target Race] ──► [Off-Season]
   
   Crisis Macrocycle (Capacity-Driven):
   [Base Aerobic Volume] ──► [Extensive Threshold / TTE] ──► [Foundational Weakness Fix] ──► [Aerobic Maintenance]
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms & Governance Frameworks

### 1. The Physiological Shift: Peaking vs. Foundational Base Extension

During competitive race seasons, training is characterized by high anaerobic glycolytic flux, repeated suprathreshold micro-bursts, and weekly tapering to maximize freshness. However, maintaining race peak form indefinitely is physiologically impossible due to autonomic nervous system exhaustion and high metabolic costs.

```
                    Physiological Adaptations Across Modalities
                    
   Variable                 Race Peaking / Anaerobic Blocks      Extended Base / Crisis Period
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Mitochondrial Biogenesis Blunted / Maintained                Maximized via AMPK / PGC-1α
   Capillarization          Static                              Progressive increase
   Glycolytic Enzyme Flux   Upregulated (PFK, LDH)              Downregulated / Baseline
   Fat Oxidation Rate       Decreased relative to carb flux     Progressive increase (FatMax)
   Autonomic Strain         High (Elevated Sympathetic Tone)    Low-to-Moderate (Parasympathetic)
   TTE at FTP               Short (Compressed by intensity)     Extended (40–70+ minutes)
```

- **Mitochondrial Volume Density & Capillarization:** In the absence of target events, extending Zone 2 endurance volume activates AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1-alpha (PGC-1α), driving transcription of mitochondrial DNA and peripheral capillary neo-angiogenesis.
- **Lactate Clearance & MCT Transport:** Rather than stressing the glycolytic pathway with repeated high-lactate acidosis, sub-threshold training upregulates Monocarboxylate Transporter 1 (MCT1) in oxidative slow-twitch muscle fibers, enhancing lactate uptake and oxidation as a direct fuel substrate.
- **TTE Extension vs. Power Inflation:** Rather than attempting to inflate FTP power targets artificially, crisis blocks provide an ideal opportunity to extend Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE) at a stable threshold—building the physiological durability required for multi-hour racing.

### 2. The Physical & Physiological Divergence of Virtual Racing (eSports)

With outdoor cancellations, virtual platforms (e.g., Zwift, Rouvy) are frequently proposed as direct substitutes for road racing. While virtual racing offers valuable cardiopulmonary conditioning, significant discrepancies exist:

```
                            Outdoor vs. Virtual Racing Mechanics
                            
         Feature               Outdoor Road / Criterium             Virtual Racing (Zwift)
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Inertial Kinetic Energy    Variable; deceleration required       Fixed avatar physics; no real
                              in technical corners ($E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$)   lean angles or roll-out loss
   Micro-Braking & Surges     Heavy penalty for poor line choice;   Non-existent; constant pedaling
                              frequent zero-cadence coasting        without coasting
   Drafting Mechanics         Dynamic, 3D spatial alignment         Algorithmic "draft bubble";
                              subject to crosswinds/eddies          binary on/off drag reduction
   Thermal Strain             Dynamic convective cooling from       High static sweat rate; blunted
                              ambient airflow ($30\text{--}50\text{ km/h}$)     evaporative cooling without fans
   Neuromuscular Torque       High torque out of slow corners       Lower torque; high steady cadence
```

- **Absence of Cornering Deceleration:** In outdoor criteriums and road circuits, corners require hard braking, technical lean angles, and explosive out-of-the-saddle re-accelerations ($800\text{--}1200\text{ W}$). In virtual racing, avatars ride through 90-degree turns at $45\text{ km/h}$ with minimal speed scrubbing, turning races into continuous, unvarying threshold/VO2 time trials.
- **Cardiovascular & Thermal Load:** Indoor virtual racing generates massive metabolic heat without proportional convective air velocity. Without high-velocity industrial fans, core temperature climbs rapidly, accelerating cardiovascular drift and blood volume diversion to cutaneous capillary beds.

### 3. Economics and Operational Vulnerability of Race Promotion

The organizational stability of collegiate and grassroots cycling rests on delicate economic and legal structures:

```
                          Grassroots Race Promotion Cost Structure
                          
   [Municipal Permits & Road Closures] ────┐
   [Police & Traffic Safety Personnel] ────┼──► [Fixed Non-Refundable Sunk Costs (60–75%)]
   [EMS & Paramedic Standby]           ────┤
   [USAC / Conference Insurance Fees]  ────┘
                                                            ▼
   [Rider Entry Fees] ─────────────────────► [Variable Revenue Source (100% Volatile)]
```

- **Non-Refundable Sunk Costs:** Permitting authorities, police departments, and emergency medical services require non-refundable deposits months before race day.
- **Registration Drop-Off Risk:** If registrations fail to reach break-even thresholds—or if public health guidelines force last-minute cancellations—the promoting club or student union bears total financial liability, threatening the long-term solvency of collegiate cycling clubs.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Macrocycle Structuring During Competition Hiatus

When the competitive season is cancelled or postponed, restructure training into progressive 3- to 4-week microcycle blocks focused on sustainable aerobic progression:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        Hiatus Macrocycle Progression                                  │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Block 1: Foundational Aerobic Volume (Zone 2: 70–80% of total volume)                  │
│          Target: Aerobic decoupling < 3–5%, FatMax optimization                        │
│                                │                                                       │
│                                ▼                                                       │
│ Block 2: Extensive Threshold Progression (FTP TTE Extension)                           │
│          Target: Progress from 2x20min ──► 3x20min ──► 2x30min ──► 1x60min at constant FTP │
│                                │                                                       │
│                                ▼                                                       │
│ Block 3: Foundational Weakness Remediation (Strength & Neuromuscular Torque)          │
│          Target: Low-cadence torque efforts (50–60 RPM) + heavy off-bike resistance    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Workout Design: Extensive Threshold Progression (TTE Focus)
- **Objective:** Extend duration at Critical Power / FTP without increasing target wattage.
- **Structure:**
  - *Week 1:* $3 \times 15\text{ min}$ at $97\text{--}100\%$ FTP, $5\text{ min}$ recovery. Total: $45\text{ min}$ TiZ (Time in Zone).
  - *Week 2:* $2 \times 25\text{ min}$ at $97\text{--}100\%$ FTP, $6\text{ min}$ recovery. Total: $50\text{ min}$ TiZ.
  - *Week 3:* $3 \times 20\text{ min}$ at $97\text{--}100\%$ FTP, $5\text{ min}$ recovery. Total: $60\text{ min}$ TiZ.
  - *Week 4 (Recovery):* $3 \times 10\text{ min}$ at $90\%$ FTP, $5\text{ min}$ recovery; low overall volume.

### 2. Sustaining Athlete Engagement & Collegiate Team Culture

During prolonged racing hiatuses, student-athletes risk disengagement, loss of identity, and fitness deconditioning. Implement non-competitive athletic drivers:

1. **Virtual Team Time Trials (TTT):** Organize intra-conference virtual TTTs where aerodynamic alignment, power rotation, and tactical pacing require real-time team communication.
2. **Local Route Challenges & Unpaved Adventure Rides:** Shift focus away from structured criterium circuits to long, mixed-terrain gravel routes to develop bike-handling versatility and aerobic volume.
3. **Technical Skills Clinics:** Practice slow-speed bike handling, cornering lines, track stands, and bumping drills in isolated parking lot settings.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall / Mistake | Root Cause | Physiological & Practical Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Endless Race-Pace Peaking** | Treating every weekend like a race day with high-intensity simulations. | Depletes autonomic reserves; induces chronic Non-Functional Overreaching (NFOR). |
| **Over-Reliance on Virtual Racing** | Believing high Zwift ranking directly translates to outdoor peloton mastery. | Neglects cornering apex physics, spatial awareness, braking discipline, and pack dynamics. |
| **Premature Power Inflation** | Raising FTP settings on indoor trainers without physiological verification. | Pushes aerobic threshold sessions into glycolytic strain, degrading fat oxidation. |
| **Under-Recovery in Indoor Training** | High sweating indoors without adequate electrolyte and fluid replacement. | Severe cardiovascular drift, chronic hypovolemia, and impaired adaptive remodeling. |
| **Promoter Financial Overextension** | Committing non-refundable deposits without minimum registration safety clauses. | Club insolvency and potential collapse of collegiate cycling conference infrastructure. |
