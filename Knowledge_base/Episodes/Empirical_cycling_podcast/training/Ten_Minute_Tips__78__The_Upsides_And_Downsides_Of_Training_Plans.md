# The Upsides and Downsides of Training Plans — Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Gediminas (Ten Minute Tips #78)_

---

## What Is a Training Plan? (Prescription vs. Framework)

A training plan is an organized framework of physiological hypotheses designed to guide an athlete toward peak performance. However, training plans are frequently misunderstood in modern cycling culture:
- **The Toxic View (The Brittle Machine):** Treating a calendar of prescribed watts, durations, and heart rates as a rigid mathematical contract where any deviation (e.g., hitting 298W instead of 300W, or missing a single Tuesday session) causes the entire season to fail.
- **The Empirical View (The Adaptive Compass):** A hierarchy of phase priorities (Base, Build, Specialty) establishing clear intent for each mesocycle while allowing dynamic day-to-day autoregulation based on fatigue, recovery, and life stress.

### 1. The Fallacy of Prescriptive Intensity Distributions (TID)
- **Descriptive vs. Prescriptive:** Scientific literature often analyzes Training Intensity Distribution (TID)—categorizing elite training as "Polarized" ($80/20$) or "Pyramidal." 
- **The Prescriptive Error:** Forcing an arbitrary percentage distribution (e.g., mandating that exactly 80% of minutes are in Zone 1/2 and 20% in Zone 3) before analyzing the athlete's specific limiters is physiologically backward. 
- **The Limiter-First Approach:** Training must be prescribed to solve specific bioenergetic bottlenecks (e.g., raising fractional utilization of $\text{VO}_2$, building mitochondrial density, or extending TTE). The resulting TID is simply an artifact of addressing those limiters within available training hours.

### 2. Plan Brittleness and the Psychological Cost of Perfection
- **The 9-out-of-10 Rule:** In a functional build phase, an athlete should expect to successfully complete and progress ~9 out of 10 hard interval sessions. Failing or skipping 1 out of 10 workouts is a statistical certainty, not a systemic breakdown.
- **Allostatic Burden of Rigidity:** Striving for 100% compliance generates chronic sympathetic nervous system tone and elevated cortisol. When athletes obsess over minor wattage deficits (e.g., failing a rep by 2%), the mental anxiety impairs sleep and blunts muscle protein synthesis.

### 3. Over-Specification in Modern Indoor Software
- **Entertainment vs. Stimulus:** Modern indoor training applications often create hyper-complex workouts (e.g., ramping wattage by 5W every 90 seconds, micro-bursts inserted into tempo) to combat boredom on the turbo trainer.
- **Physiological Reality:** Cellular signaling cascades (AMPK, CaMK, PGC-1$\alpha$) respond to accumulated metabolic flux and muscle tension across duration, not arbitrary micro-wattage oscillations. Complex indoor step-tests frequently obscure the clear assessment of physiological progression.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Upsides vs. Downsides Matrix

| Feature | The Upside (Empirical Coaching) | The Downside (Dogmatic Application) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Structure** | Prevents panic cramming; establishes progressive overload timeline. | Creates cognitive rigidity and guilt when life forces missed days. |
| **Interval Design** | Focuses neuromuscular and metabolic stress on targeted adaptations. | Over-specifies micro-targets, disconnecting athlete from interoception. |
| **Volume Targets** | Encourages aerobic base accumulation and long-term durability. | Assumes a 1:1 linear relationship between volume and threshold gains. |
| **Periodization** | Organizes training into distinct phases (General Prep $\to$ Build $\to$ Race). | Imposes artificial 3-week on / 1-week off rules that ignore real recovery rates. |

### 2. The Hierarchy of Needs for a Training Week
When life disruptions occur, execute workouts according to a strict priority hierarchy:
1. **Primary Session (Top Priority):** The single key physiological driver of the block (e.g., extending threshold time-in-zone from $3 \times 15\text{m}$ to $2 \times 25\text{m}$).
2. **Secondary Session:** Supporting aerobic or neuromuscular maintenance (e.g., tempo with surges or short seated sprint openers).
3. **Tertiary Session:** General aerobic volume (Zone 2 endurance).
4. **Dispensable Session:** Recovery spins or supplementary interval sets—cut these first when time or recovery is constrained.

### 3. Reframing "Training" vs. "Exercising"
When life stress reaches extreme levels ($>8/10$ occupational/family strain, severe sleep loss):
- **Stop "Training":** Discontinue progressive interval overload, testing, and power-target compliance.
- **Start "Exercising":** Ride 30–60 minutes outdoors at an enjoyable pace without head-unit power displays to support mental health and preserve motor coordination.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Trap | Mechanism | Practical Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Catch-Up Training Post-Failure** | Attempting to repeat a failed hard interval session the very next day. | Treat the failed session as an indicator of fatigue; rest 24–48h before testing legs again. |
| **Volume Creep Without Caloric Support** | Increasing weekly volume by 30–50% without matching dietary carbohydrate intake. | Increase carbohydrate intake proportionally ($+1.0\text{g carbs per 100 kcal expended}$). |
| **Chasing TID Dogma** | Forcing an 80/20 polarized model on a time-crunched athlete (6h/week). | Align intensity with time availability and specific event demands. |
| **The "Perfect Plan" Trap** | Spending hours re-writing multi-month calendars after a 2-day head cold. | Adjust only the immediate 7-day microcycle; resume macro progression naturally. |

- [ ] **Eliminate Plan Brittleness:** Accept that 1 in 10 workouts will be sub-par or skipped.
- [ ] **Prioritize Limiter Analysis Over TID:** Train what is broken, not an abstract percentage split.
- [ ] **Strip Out Workout Complexity:** Use clean, repeatable interval sets to accurately track physiological overload.
- [ ] **Preserve Psychological Energy:** Never let adherence to a training plan induce more stress than the physical work itself.
