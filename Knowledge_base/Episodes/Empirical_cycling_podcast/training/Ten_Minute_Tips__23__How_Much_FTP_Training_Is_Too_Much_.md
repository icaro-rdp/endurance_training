# Ten Minute Tips #23: How Much FTP Training Is Too Much? — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Houston_

---

## What Is the "FTP Over-Emphasis" Problem?

Functional Threshold Power ($\text{FTP}$) is the single most referenced metric in modern cycling. Because threshold power correlates strongly with endurance performance and is straightforward to test and program ($2\times 20'$, $3\times 15'$), athletes and self-coached riders frequently fall into the **FTP Trap**:
- **Monotonous Programming:** Spending 4–6 consecutive months executing purely threshold-oriented intervals without seasonal variety.
- **The Low-Volume Compensation Error:** Attempting to compensate for a 6–8 hour weekly training budget by forcing 3–4 hard threshold or sweetspot sessions every week.
- **Neglecting Event Demands:** Believing that an ever-increasing FTP will automatically solve weaknesses in 1–3 minute climbing punch, explosive accelerations, or race positioning.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Ceiling and Floor Dynamics of $\text{FTP}$ and $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$
- **The Aerobic Ceiling ($\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$):** Maximal oxygen uptake sets the absolute upper boundary for oxidative ATP synthesis. 
- **Fractional Utilization:** In well-trained endurance athletes, FTP typically resides between **$80–88\%$ of $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$** (rarely reaching $>90\%$ without extreme fatigue).
- **The Compression Plateau:** Once an athlete pushes their FTP to $85–90\%$ of their current $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$, further threshold training will **not** increase threshold wattage. It only extends Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE). To raise threshold power further, the athlete must first execute a dedicated $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ block to lift the aerobic ceiling.

### 2. The Low-Volume Recovery Paradox
- A time-crunched rider executing $3\times 60\text{ min}$ of threshold intervals within a 6-hour training week performs 50% of their total weekly volume at high metabolic and autonomic stress.
- Conversely, a rider doing 14 hours per week with the same 3 hours of threshold work performs only ~20% of their volume at high stress. The remaining 11 hours of sub-LT1 riding stimulate parasympathetic tone, fat oxidation, and blood volume without autonomic overreaching.

### 3. Autonomic Indicators of Threshold Overreaching
When threshold training is excessively programmed without adequate recovery, distinct cardiac and neuromuscular signs appear:
- **Sympathetic Suppression:** Heart rate fails to rise to normal threshold values during hard efforts; legs feel heavy and wooden.
- **Parasympathetic Runaway / Cardiac Drift:** Heart rate spikes excessively fast within the first 5 minutes of a submaximal effort accompanied by severe air hunger.
- **RPE Decoupling:** Familiar power targets feel like 9/10 or 10/10 RPE, requiring intense mental struggle to complete basic repetitions.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Diagnosing When to Train FTP vs. Other Capacities

```
Athlete Limiter Assessment:

1. Did you get dropped when the pace was steady and high (e.g., long 20–40 min climb)?
   └──► ACTION: EMPHASIZE FTP / TTE (Progress Time-in-Zone from 40 to 75+ min).

2. Is your FTP stuck at the same wattage for 6+ months despite high consistency?
   └──► ACTION: PULL BACK FTP; EXECUTE A VO2max BLOCK (2–3 weeks of 3–5 min maximal efforts).

3. Do you comfortably make the final selection but lack punch on short 1–2 min steep hills?
   └──► ACTION: DE-EMPHASIZE FTP; INTRODUCE ANAEROBIC CAPACITY / W' WORK.

4. Are you failing threshold intervals by minute 12 of a 20-minute set?
   └──► ACTION: PULL THE PLUG; REST 5–7 DAYS (Active recovery / sub-LT1 spinning only).
```

### 2. Structuring an Effective Threshold Progression Block
Limit focused threshold blocks to **3 to 5 weeks** before taking a recovery week or switching stimulus:

```
Week 1: 3 x 15 min @ 97–100% FTP (45 min TIZ, 5 min recovery)
Week 2: 2 x 25 min @ 97–100% FTP (50 min TIZ, 6 min recovery)
Week 3: 3 x 20 min @ 97–100% FTP (60 min TIZ, 6 min recovery)
Week 4: 2 x 35 min @ 97–100% FTP (70 min TIZ, 8 min recovery)
Week 5: RECOVERY WEEK (Easy Zone 1/2 spinning, no efforts > LT1)
```

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Stacking Heavy Strength Work and High Threshold Density:**
   - Attempting 3 heavy gym sessions per week alongside 3 threshold interval workouts. Both draw heavily on glycolytic reserves and central nervous system recovery.
2. **Raising Power Targets Instead of Extending TTE:**
   - Forcing +5–10 Watts onto threshold intervals every week instead of extending duration at a stable, verified MLSS power.
3. **Fear of Taking Rest Weeks:**
   - Panicking that fitness will decay during an easy week. Fitness supercompensation occurs during deload periods when structural repair and glycogen overcompensation take place.

| Situation | Signal / Metric | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FTP Plateau ($>6\text{ weeks}$)** | FTP is within ~85% of 5-minute peak power | Transition to a dedicated VO2max block (2–3 weeks). |
