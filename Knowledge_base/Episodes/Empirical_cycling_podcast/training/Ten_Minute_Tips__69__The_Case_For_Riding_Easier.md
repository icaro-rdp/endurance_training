# The Case For Riding Easier — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore, Gediminas Bagdonas, Megan Heath (Ten Minute Tips #69)_

---

## What Is "Riding Easier"?

In modern cycling culture, athletes frequently pace endurance rides at the upper boundary of Zone 2 (70–75%+ of Functional Threshold Power) or chase arbitrary speed/power averages. This practice stems from the **"hard work fallacy"**—the belief that training is only effective if it leaves the athlete visibly exhausted, sweating profusely, or sore.

**Riding easier** means deliberately pacing base endurance rides at **50–65% FTP** (perceived exertion of **1–3 out of 10**) and recovery rides even lower ($<50\%$ FTP). This strategy protects autonomic balance, prevents unnecessary glycogen depletion, and ensures that high-intensity sessions achieve maximal adaptive quality.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms & Theoretical Concepts

### 1. The Cellular Equivalence of Aerobic Stimulus
At the cellular level, peripheral aerobic adaptations—such as capillary sprouting, mitochondrial biogenesis, and oxidative enzyme upregulation (citrate synthase, succinate dehydrogenase)—are triggered by cumulative **calcium flux ($Ca^{2+}/\text{CaMKII}$)** and low-to-moderate metabolic turnover (**AMPK activation**).
- Studies examining mitochondrial adaptation across endurance intensities show that long durations at 50–60% $VO_2max$ trigger equivalent transcriptional signaling ($PGC\text{-}1\alpha$) to riding at 70–75% without the compounding neuromuscular and endocrine costs.
- There is no unique adaptation exclusive to the very top edge of Zone 2 (LT1) that cannot be stimulated at lower endurance wattages when total volume is accumulated.

```
Mitochondrial & Capillary Stimulus vs. Autonomic Fatigue Cost:
  
  Adaptation Triggered:  [ 50-60% FTP ]  ===================> High
  Autonomic/Glycogen Cost: [ 50-60% FTP ]  ===> Very Low
  
  Adaptation Triggered:  [ 70-75% FTP ]  ===================> High (Equivalent)
  Autonomic/Glycogen Cost: [ 70-75% FTP ]  ========================> Extremely High
```

### 2. Protecting the $>90\%$ Interval Success Rate
High-intensity interval training ($VO_2max$, threshold over-unders, suprathreshold efforts) requires deep neurological recruitment and maximal metabolic flux. 
- Chronic upper-end endurance riding creates low-grade intramuscular glycogen depletion and sustained sympathetic nervous system tone.
- When athletes carry this fatigue into key interval days, their top-end power drops by 3–8%, converting high-quality supra-threshold work into a sub-optimal slog.
- An optimal coaching framework demands an **interval workout success rate $\ge 90\%$** across the training season.

### 3. Caloric Accounting & Energy Availability
- A 4–6 hour ride paced at 75% FTP burns substantially more total kilojoules and carbohydrates than the same duration at 55–60% FTP.
- This creates severe acute energy deficits that are difficult to replenish off the bike, triggering elevated nocturnal cortisol, fragmented slow-wave sleep, and binge-eating cycles in athletes attempting to manage body composition.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Pacing Endurance Rides by RPE & Context
- **RPE Calibration:** Target RPE 1–3 on a 10-point scale. The athlete should be able to hold a full conversation without gasping, listen to complex audiobooks/podcasts with complete focus, and finish a 3–4 hour ride feeling energized rather than physically drained.
- **Power Guidelines:** 
  - **Recovery Spin:** $<50\%\text{ FTP}$ (Intensity Factor $\le 0.45\text{--}0.50$).
  - **Standard Base Endurance:** $50\text{--}65\%\text{ FTP}$ (Intensity Factor $0.55\text{--}0.65$).
  - **Upper Endurance Cap (Rolling Terrain):** Do not exceed tempo ($75\%\text{ FTP}$) on steep pitches; swap to easier gearing ($34\times 32\text{ or }34\times 34$) to preserve cadence and keep torque low.

### 2. Auto-Regulated Diagnostics: The Freshness Test
When an athlete has plateaued for months despite high training consistency:
1. **Prescribe a 2-Week "De-Intensification" Experiment:** Drop all endurance rides to $\le 55\text{--}60\%\text{ FTP}$ while maintaining scheduled key interval sessions.
2. **Evaluate Benchmark Performance:** After 10–14 days, assess interval power and TTE. In overreached athletes, FTP and interval repeatability frequently surge by 10–30+ watts due to the elimination of chronic non-functional fatigue.

### 3. Handling Time-Crunched Schedules ($<8\text{ hrs/week}$)
- If an athlete is restricted to 4–6 hours per week across 2–3 rides, they can tolerate higher relative intensity (e.g., 2 focused interval sessions per week), but endurance filler rides should still remain easy to avoid accumulating unnecessary fatigue between high-intensity bouts.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **The "Fatigue Security Blanket":** Using acute physical exhaustion as psychological validation that training was effective.
2. **Surging Over Rolling Terrain Outdoors:** Pushing 300+ watts over every short hill during an endurance ride, turning a base ride into an unstructured fartlek session.
3. **Overheating & Heat Sinking Indoors:** Pacing indoor trainer rides at 70%+ FTP without adequate industrial fan cooling, driving heart rate decoupling and high autonomic stress.
4. **Equating Substrate Oxidation with Adaptation:** Mistakenly believing that riding at exact "FatMax" is required to adapt lipid oxidation pathways.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Metric / Sensation | Riding Too Hard (Flawed) | Riding Appropriately Easy (Optimal) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Intensity Factor (IF)** | $\text{IF} \ge 0.72\text{--}0.78$ on all endurance rides. | $\text{IF} = 0.50\text{--}0.65$ on base and recovery rides. |
| **Post-Ride Feeling** | Legs aching in shower, exhausted, ravenous hunger. | Energized, ready for normal daily tasks, normal appetite. |
| **Key Interval Execution** | Struggling to hit target power; frequent interval failure ($<80\%$). | Crisp power delivery; consistent $\ge 90\%$ session success rate. |
| **Terrain Strategy** | Sprinting/hammering over hills; pushing high torque. | Spinning up climbs in easiest gear; capping power at tempo ($\le 75\%$). |
| **Long-Term Trajectory** | Frequent plateaus, recurring mild illness, chronic stagnation. | Sustainable multi-year progression; high interval quality. |

> [!TIP]
> **The Golden Endurance Benchmark:** You should finish a routine base endurance ride feeling like you could comfortably head back out and repeat the ride, or immediately execute a short high-power effort if required.
