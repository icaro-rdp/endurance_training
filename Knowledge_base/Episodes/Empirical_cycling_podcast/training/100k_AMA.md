# 100k AMA: Zone 3 Rides, Anaerobic Capacity vs FTP, Heavy Lifting, and Indoor Equivalence — Q&A Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson_

---

## Q: Why Is FTP and Aerobic Recovery the Primary Limiter in Repeated Anaerobic Surges Rather Than W'?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Riders frequently get dropped after 10–15 hard surges in criteriums or punchy road races and assume their limiter is Anaerobic Work Capacity ($W'$), asking whether they should focus on anaerobic interval training.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **The $W'$ Reservoir and Aerobic Reconstitution:** Anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) is a small, finite energetic tank (typically 15–25 kJ) derived from intramuscular phosphocreatine ($PCr$) breakdown and anaerobic glycolysis.
  $$\text{Power Above FTP} = \text{Depletes } W'$$
  $$\text{Power Below FTP} = \text{Reconstitutes } W' \text{ (Aerobically via Oxidative Phosphorylation)}$$
- **Mathematical Impact of Higher FTP:**
  - When surging at 450 W, a rider with a 350 W FTP depletes $W'$ at $100\text{ J/s}$.
  - A rider with a 280 W FTP surging at 450 W depletes $W'$ at $170\text{ J/s}$ (70% faster depletion).
  - During the subsequent floating section at 250 W, the 350 W rider is deep in Zone 2 ($71\%$ FTP), rapidly resynthesizing $PCr$ and clearing lactate oxidatively. The 280 W rider is at $89\%$ FTP, resynthesizing $W'$ at a crawl.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  W' EXPENDITURE & RECHARGE DYNAMICS                     |
|                                                                         |
|  [Surge @ 450W] ──> Depletes W' rapidly                                 |
|                                                                         |
|  [Float @ 250W] ──> Higher FTP (350W): Rapid W' Recharge (Aerobic)      |
|                 ──> Lower FTP (280W): Minimal/No W' Recharge (Near FTP) |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- If you get dropped on the **1st or 2nd surge**, target anaerobic capacity ($W'$) and neuromuscular power.
- If you get dropped on the **8th to 15th surge**, your limiter is **FTP and aerobic recovery kinetics**, not $W'$. Prescribe threshold duration progression ($2 \times 25\text{ min}$, $3 \times 20\text{ min}$) and high Zone 2 volume.

## Q: When Is Steady Zone 3 (Tempo / Sweet Spot) Beneficial vs. a Trap for Aerobic Base Building?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes ask whether steady Zone 3 (Tempo/Sweet Spot) riding is useful or merely "junk miles" that generates unnecessary fatigue without superior aerobic stimulus over Zone 2.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Autonomic Cost vs. Stimulus:** Zone 3 ($76\text{--}90\%$ FTP) engages substantial carbohydrate flux and mechanical muscle tension. However, unlike Zone 2, it causes significant sympathetic nervous system activation, elevated glycogen depletion, and extended recovery requirements.
- **The "Black Hole" Trap:** Performing Zone 3 on days designated for base endurance creates chronic intermediate fatigue that blunts power output on subsequent VO2max or threshold interval sessions.
- **The Specificity Role:** For long gravel races, gran fondos, or stage races requiring multi-hour sub-threshold fatigue resistance (durability after 2,000–3,000 kJ), targeted Zone 3 blocks build muscular endurance and localized metabolic tolerance.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- In general base phases, keep endurance rides strictly in **Zone 2 ($60\text{--}75\%$ FTP / below LT1)** to maximize volume and minimize autonomic fatigue.
- In pre-race specialty phases for long endurance events, introduce structured tempo blocks (e.g., $3 \times 20\text{ min}$ or $2 \times 40\text{ min}$ @ $80\text{--}85\%$ FTP) followed by dedicated rest days.

## Q: How Should Heavy Strength Training Be Implemented to Maximize Economy and Rate of Force Development?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
A listener asked why cyclists should lift heavy weights (3–6 RM) rather than doing high-rep "endurance" lifting in the gym, and how to avoid gaining unwanted bulk.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Neural Drive vs. Hypertrophy:** Lifting heavy loads ($\ge 85\%$ 1RM, 3–6 reps) recruits high-threshold Type II motor units and improves motor unit synchronization and rate of force development (RFD). When combined with high endurance volume, AMPK activation suppresses mTOR signaling, preventing significant muscle hypertrophy.
- **Fiber Type Shift & Economy:** Heavy resistance training converts fatigable Type IIx fibers into fatigue-resistant, highly oxidative Type IIa fibers, and increases tendon stiffness, reducing the oxygen cost of sub-maximal pedaling (improving cycling economy).

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- **Loading Parameters:**
  - Exercises: Barbell Back Squats, Trap Bar Deadlifts, Romanian Deadlifts, Bulgarian Split Squats.
  - Repetitions: 3 to 6 reps per set at **RPE 8–9 (1–2 reps in reserve)**.
  - Sets: 3 to 5 sets with 3–5 minutes of full rest between sets.
- Never perform high-rep circuit lifting (15–25 reps) in the gym; cycling already provides thousands of low-force contractions on the bike.

## Q: What Are the Key Protocols for Developing Neuromuscular Sprint Power and Standing Starts?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Riders ask how to develop peak neuromuscular sprint power, explosive acceleration, and high-cadence coordination on the bike.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Peak Force & Phosphagen Utilization:** Maximal sprint power relies on maximal phosphocreatine ($PCr$) utilization and high firing rates of fast-twitch Type IIx motor units. Full recovery between efforts is mandatory to allow $PCr$ resynthesis and CNS replenishment.
- **Standing Starts (High-Torque Acceleration):** Overcoming static inertia from a near standstill recruits deep hip and knee extensor musculature at high force, developing specific on-bike starting torque.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- **High-Torque Standing Starts:**
  - From nearly stationary (2–3 km/h), in a big gear ($53 \times 14$), perform an all-out 8–12 revolution explosive sprint out of the saddle.
  - Rest: 5–8 minutes of easy spinning between efforts; 4–6 reps total.
- **High-Cadence Spin-Ups:**
  - Accelerate explosively from 80 RPM to $>130\text{--}150\text{ RPM}$ in a moderate gear to develop pedal smoothness and neuromuscular coordination.

## Q: How Do Indoor Trainer Duty Cycles and Inertia Compare to Outdoor Road Riding?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes ask how to convert indoor trainer time to outdoor miles and why riding the indoor trainer feels subjectively harder at the same wattage.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Continuous Pedaling Duty Cycle:** Outdoors, coasting and descending account for 10–25% of total ride time. Indoors, the pedaling duty cycle is near 100% continuous muscular tension with zero dead spots.
- **Flywheel Inertia & Thermal Load:** Low-inertia trainers increase resistance over pedal dead spots, altering muscle firing patterns. Furthermore, inadequate convective airflow indoors elevates core body temperature and accelerates cardiovascular drift.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Apply a **0.80–0.85 duration conversion factor** for indoor base sessions (e.g., 2.0 hours indoors $\approx$ 2.5 hours outdoors in total metabolic work).
- Use multiple high-velocity fans directly on the torso and head to maintain convective cooling and prevent artificial heart rate elevation.
