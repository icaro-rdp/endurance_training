# 100k AMA: Zone 3 Rides, Anaerobic Capacity vs FTP, Heavy Lifting, and Indoor Equivalence — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson_

---

## What Is the 100k AMA?

The 100k AMA (Ask Me Anything) episode addresses the most contentious and critical training debates submitted by competitive cyclists and coaches. It synthesizes Empirical Cycling's foundational coaching principles regarding:
1. **The role of steady Zone 3 (Tempo / Sweet Spot) riding** versus polarized base training.
2. **Anaerobic Work Capacity ($W'$) versus Functional Threshold Power (FTP)** as the primary limiter in punchy, repeated-surge racing.
3. **Heavy resistance training implementation** for endurance athletes and sprinters.
4. **Neuromuscular sprint mechanics** and rate of force development.
5. **Indoor versus outdoor physiological equivalence** and workload calibration.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Anaerobic Capacity ($W'$) vs. Aerobic Threshold (FTP)
Cyclists often misdiagnose their race limiters, assuming that getting dropped on sharp hills or crit corners is an "anaerobic capacity" deficiency.
- **The $W'$ Reservoir and Recovery Kinetics:** Anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) is a finite energetic tank (typically 15–25 kJ) derived from phosphocreatine (PCr) breakdown and rapid anaerobic glycolysis.
- **Why FTP Governs Repeated Surges:** 
  $$\text{Power Above FTP} = \text{Depletes } W'$$
  $$\text{Power Below FTP} = \text{Reconstitutes } W' \text{ (Aerobically)}$$
  - A rider with a 350 W FTP surging at 450 W expends $W'$ at $100\text{ J/s}$.
  - A rider with a 280 W FTP surging at 450 W expends $W'$ at $170\text{ J/s}$ (70% faster depletion).
  - Furthermore, during floating sections at 250 W, the 350 W rider is deep in Zone 2 ($71\%$ FTP), rapidly resynthesizing PCr and clearing metabolic byproducts via oxidative phosphorylation, whereas the 280 W rider is at $89\%$ FTP, resynthesizing $W'$ at a crawl.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  W' EXPENDITURE & RECHARGE DYNAMICS                     |
|                                                                         |
|  [Surge @ 450W] ──> Depletes W' rapidly                                 |
|                                                                         |
|  [Float @ 250W] ──> Higher FTP (350W): Rapid W' Recharge (Aerobic)      |
|                 ──> Lower FTP (280W): Minimal/No W' Recharge (Near FTP) |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 2. The Physiology of Zone 3 (Tempo / Sweet Spot)
- **Metabolic Stress vs. Autonomic Cost:** Zone 3 ($76\text{--}90\%$ FTP) engages high rates of carbohydrate oxidation and produces substantial mechanical tension and mitochondrial flux. However, unlike pure Zone 2, it causes significant sympathetic nervous system stimulation, elevated glycogen depletion, and extended recovery requirements.
- **When Zone 3 Fails:** If an athlete performs moderate Zone 3 on days designated for easy endurance, they create a "black hole" of chronic autonomic fatigue that degrades power output during high-intensity interval sessions (VO2max or threshold).
- **When Zone 3 Succeeds:** For long gravel races, gran fondos, or stage races where sustained sub-threshold durability (fatigue resistance after 2,000–3,000 kJ) is required, targeted Zone 3 blocks build muscular endurance and localized metabolic tolerance.

### 3. Heavy Strength Training for Endurance Performance
- **Neural Drive & Motor Unit Synchronization:** Lifting heavy loads ($\ge 85\%$ 1RM, 3–6 reps) recruits high-threshold Type II motor units without causing significant hypertrophy when paired with high endurance volume (due to AMPK-mediated inhibition of mTOR signaling).
- **Fiber Type Transition:** Heavy resistance training shifts fatigable Type IIx glycolytic fibers toward oxidative, fatigue-resistant Type IIa fibers.
- **Rate of Force Development (RFD) & Musculotendinous Stiffness:** Increased tendon stiffness and neural rate of force development allow faster force production at the top of the pedal stroke, reducing oxygen cost per pedal stroke (improving cycling economy).

### 4. Indoor vs. Outdoor Biomechanics and Duty Cycles
- **Pedaling Duty Cycle:** Outdoors, micro-coasting, braking, cornering, and descending account for 10–25% of total ride time (duty cycle: $75\text{--}90\%$). Indoors, the duty cycle is near $100\%$ continuous tension without dead spots.
- **Flywheel Inertia:** Indoor direct-drive trainers provide distinct angular momentum profiles compared to road cycling. Low-inertia trainer setups increase muscular force requirements over the dead spots of the pedal stroke, altering muscle recruitment and perceived exertion.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Diagnosing Your Limiter: FTP vs. $W'$
- **If you get dropped on the 1st or 2nd surge:** Look at absolute neuromuscular power and anaerobic work capacity ($W'$ / anaerobic interval training).
- **If you get dropped on the 8th to 15th surge:** Your limiter is **FTP and aerobic recovery kinetics**, not anaerobic capacity. Prescribe threshold extension ($2 \times 25\text{ min}$, $3 \times 20\text{ min}$, progressive TTE build) and high-volume Zone 2.

### 2. Dosing Zone 3 / Tempo Training
- **Base Phase:** Keep endurance rides strictly in Zone 2 ($60\text{--}75\%$ FTP / below LT1/VT1) to maximize volume and minimize autonomic fatigue.
- **Specificity Phase for Long Events:** Introduce structured tempo blocks:
  - *Workout Example:* $3 \times 20\text{ min}$ or $2 \times 40\text{ min}$ at $80\text{--}85\%$ FTP with high cadence (90–95 rpm) to challenge cardiovascular throughput, or low cadence (60–70 rpm) for torque development.
  - Ensure tempo days are followed by pure active recovery or rest days.

### 3. Strength Training Framework for Cyclists
- **Frequency:** 2 sessions per week in base phase; 1 maintenance session per week in race season.
- **Core Lifts:** Barbell Back Squat, Romanian Deadlift (RDL), Bulgarian Split Squat, Trap Bar Deadlift.
- **Loading Parameters:**
  - Repetitions: 3 to 6 reps per set.
  - Sets: 3 to 5 working sets.
  - Intensity: RPE 8–9 (1–2 reps in reserve, never training to absolute muscular failure).
  - Rest: 3–5 minutes between sets to ensure full phosphagen resynthesis.
- **Sequencing:** Lift on the same day after a hard bike session or on a dedicated strength day followed by an easy day. Avoid lifting immediately before high-intensity interval workouts.

### 4. Sprint & Neuromuscular Development
- **High-Torque Standing Starts:** 
  - From nearly stationary (2–3 km/h), in a big gear (e.g., $53 \times 14$ or $52 \times 15$), perform an all-out 8–12 revolution explosive sprint out of the saddle.
  - Rest: 5–8 minutes full recovery between efforts. 4–6 reps total.
- **High-Cadence Spin-Ups:**
  - In a moderate gear, accelerate explosively from 80 rpm to $>130\text{--}150\text{ rpm}$ without bouncing. Builds neuromuscular coordination and pedal smoothness.

### 5. Indoor-to-Outdoor Conversion Rules
- **Volume Ratio:** When converting an outdoor endurance ride to an indoor session, a factor of **0.80–0.85** is generally appropriate (e.g., 2.0 hours indoors $\approx$ 2.5 hours outdoors in total metabolic work).
- **Cooling Requirements:** Minimum 2 high-velocity fans to prevent heart rate decoupling caused by thermal strain.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **The Middle-Intensity Fatigue Trap:**
   - Riding Zone 3 during every training session produces chronic intermediate fatigue without delivering the maximal stroke volume stimuli of Zone 5 or the high-volume recovery profile of Zone 2.
2. **Training Anaerobic Surges When Base FTP Is the Flaw:**
   - Spending weeks doing 30-second all-out anaerobic intervals when FTP is low simply exhausts the athlete without fixing the underlying metabolic recovery engine.
3. **High-Rep "Endurance" Lifting in the Gym:**
   - Doing sets of 15–25 reps with light weights in the gym adds muscular and metabolic fatigue without delivering the neural strength, RFD, or tendon stiffness adaptations of heavy lifting. Cyclists already get thousands of low-force repetitions on the bike.
4. **Ignoring Indoor Flywheel Inertia & Cooling:**
   - Training in high gears indoors on low-inertia trainers without adequate cooling artificially elevates heart rate and alters muscle firing patterns, distorting zone accuracy.
