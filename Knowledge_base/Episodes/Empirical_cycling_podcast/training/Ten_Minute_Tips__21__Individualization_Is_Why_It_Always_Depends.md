# Ten Minute Tips #21: Individualization Is Why It Always Depends — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Houston_

## What Does "It Depends" Mean in Endurance Coaching?

In exercise physiology, "it depends" represents the foundational truth of **biological individuality**. While training platforms and canned plans seek static, one-size-fits-all recipes (e.g., "everyone must taper 14 days," "everyone should do 8-week VO2 blocks"), real physiological adaptation is mediated by:
- **Training History & Baseline Engine:** Phenotype, fiber distribution, and cumulative years of aerobic training.
- **Autonomic & Life Stress:** Career demands, sleep architecture, nutritional status, and systemic life load.
- **Individual Adaptation Kinetics:** Differing rates of mitochondrial adaptation, fatigue clearance, and muscular recovery.

```
                    Training Principle Hierarchy
  ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │                    UNIVERSAL CONSTANTS                        │
  │   - Energy systems & bioenergetics (ATP/CP, Glycolysis, OXPHOS)│
  │   - Progressive overload requirements (Volume/kJ, TIZ)        │
  │   - Primary recovery drivers (Sleep quality & Carbohydrates)   │
  └───────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┘
                                  │ Modulated by
                                  ▼
  ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │                   HIGHLY INDIVIDUAL VARIABLES                 │
  │   - Tapering strategy (Sharp rest vs. volume maintenance)     │
  │   - Tolerance to VO2max / glycolytic interval density        │
  │   - Optimal biomechanical cadence & gearing                   │
  │   - Dynamic response to external life stress                  │
  └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Universal Principles vs. Individual Variables

| Domain | Universal Biological Absolutes | Individualized Variables ("It Depends") |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bioenergetics** | ATP resynthesis pathways, lactate shuttling, oxidative phosphorylation rules. | Specific power at LT1/LT2, fractional utilization of $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$, $W'$ capacity. |
| **Recovery** | Glycogen resynthesis via exogenous carbohydrates; cellular repair during sleep. | Days required to absorb a block, daily caloric throughput, sleep duration requirements. |
| **Overload** | Mitochondrial biogenesis requires cellular energy flux ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$, AMPK, PGC-1$\alpha$). | Whether overload is achieved via high volume (kJ) vs. metabolic density (Sweet Spot). |
| **Tapering** | Supercompensation occurs when accumulated fatigue decays faster than fitness. | Need for high pre-race intensity/volume ("openers") vs. complete deloading. |

### 2. The Total Stress Reservoir (Allostatic Load)
- The autonomic nervous system does not differentiate between physical training stress (kJ / intervals) and external life stress (cognitive workload, financial stress, relationship strain, poor sleep).
- Both draw from the same systemic recovery budget mediated by the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and sympathetic tone.
- **Coaching Reality:** Forcing a planned "hardest block of the season" during a week of severe work or life disruption guarantees maladaptation and acute overreaching.

### 3. Biomechanics and Self-Selected Cadence
- The central nervous system dynamically self-optimizes pedal cadence to balance neuromuscular efficiency and metabolic cost.
- Dogmatically enforcing "spin to win" ($>100\text{ RPM}$) or "grinding" ($<75\text{ RPM}$) without a specific course demand artificially increases physiological cost. Cadence should only be manipulated for course-specific demands (e.g., fixed-gear track acceleration, steep climbs).

### 4. Sprinter vs. Diesel Aerobic Development
- A common misconception is that riders with large sprint power ($\text{P}_{\max} > 1500\text{W}$) must train threshold using short, intermittent bursts while diesel riders use steady-state intervals.
- **Physiological Ground Truth:** Aerobic adaptations in Type I and Type IIa fibers are driven by sustained aerobic ATP turnover and capillarization. Both sprinters and diesels build threshold through sustained Time-in-Zone ($2\times 20'$, $2\times 30'$, progressive sweetspot), though sprinters must be mindful of explosive start torques that drain anaerobic reserves.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The "Debugging" Framework for Stalled Progress

When an athlete experiences a plateau or unexpected fatigue, diagnose the breakdown systematically:

```
Step 1: Check Foundational Recovery (Absolutes)
  ├── Is daily sleep consistently < 7.5–8 hours?
  ├── Is on-bike carbohydrate fueling < 60–90g/hr?
  └── Is overall life stress abnormally elevated?
        └──► IF YES: Fix recovery inputs before altering training workouts.

Step 2: Evaluate Interval Prescription
  ├── Has the athlete hit a plateau during a VO2max block (no power/HR rise after 2–3 weeks)?
  │     └──► ACTION: End block early; athlete has maximized current VO2 stimulus.
  └── Is the athlete failing threshold intervals early?
        └──► ACTION: Reduce target power by 10–15W and extend Time-in-Zone.

Step 3: Evaluate Training Distribution
  └── Is volume capped at 6–8 hrs/wk?
        └──► ACTION: Increase sub-threshold density (Sweet Spot / Tempo).
```

### 2. Tapering Individualization

- **Profile A (Fast Recoverers / High Autonomic Rebound):**
  - Require short, sharp tapers ($3–5\text{ days}$).
  - High volume maintained until 5 days out; include race-pace openers 24–48 hours prior.
- **Profile B (Slow Recoverers / High Muscle Mass):**
  - Require extended volume step-downs ($10–14\text{ days}$) with progressive 30–50% volume reductions while preserving short interval intensity to maintain neuromuscular sharpness.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Rigid Adherence to Calendar Training Plans:**
   - Treating a 12-week static PDF or software template as unchangeable, ignoring daily feedback, illness, or sleep deficits.
2. **Using "It Depends" as an Excuse for Lack of Knowledge:**
   - Saying "it depends" without the ability to articulate *what* it depends upon (underlying physiological mechanisms and individual constraints).
3. **Assuming Elite Training Plans Work for Time-Crunched Amateurs:**
   - Attempting to execute a pro rider's polarized volume schedule on a 7-hour weekly availability.
4. **Forcing Cadence Manipulations Unnecessarily:**
   - Altering an athlete's natural threshold cadence without diagnostic necessity, increasing cardiovascular strain.
