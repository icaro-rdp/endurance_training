# Ten Minute Tips #64: Diagnosing And Training Weaknesses In The Off-Season — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Megan, James, Erica_

---

## What Is Off-Season Limiter Diagnosis?

The traditional cycling axiom *"train your weaknesses, race your strengths"* is frequently misapplied during the off-season. Athletes often identify arbitrary deficiencies on their power duration curve (e.g., a low 1-minute power or weak sprint) and dedicate months of high-intensity training to fix them, often compromising their primary aerobic engine.

Effective off-season programming requires a rigorous **opportunity cost evaluation**:
1. Determining whether a diagnosed limiter is truly physiological, chronic fatigue, or poor racecraft/cadence execution.
2. Assessing whether the limiter is physiologically trainable without degrading existing competitive advantages.
3. Implementing high-yield, low-opportunity-cost adaptations (strength training, position tolerance, health diagnostics) while maintaining aerobic volume.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Opportunity Cost and the "Teeter-Totter Effect"
* **Finite Adaptation Budget:** Autonomic nervous system tolerance and metabolic recovery are zero-sum. Training to turn a 2000W sprinter into a pure climber—or forcing a lightweight climber to add 3–4 kg of myofibrillar mass for flat crits—imposes severe metabolic trade-offs.
* **The Trainability Spectrum:** Neuromuscular sprinting ($P_{max}$) is predominantly governed by genetics and fiber type composition (fast-twitch Type IIx/IIa ratio). Aerobic capacity (mitochondrial density, capillarization, stroke volume) has vastly greater plasticity and higher return on investment (ROI).

```
   [ Fast-Twitch Sprinter ]                          [ Slow-Twitch Climber ]
          │                                                    │
          ▼                                                    ▼
 Attempting to maximize FTP / 60m TTE               Attempting to maximize 15s Sprint Power
          │                                                    │
          ▼                                                    ▼
 Risk of losing 300–400W peak sprint                Risk of gaining non-functional mass /
 & anaerobic punch                                  degrading W/kg on climbs
```

### 2. Differential Diagnosis: True Limiter vs. Fatigue vs. Skill
Before prescribing off-season training interventions, apply a 3-step diagnostic filter:

```
[ Step 1: Check Fatigue Baseline ]
Is the athlete riding Zone 2 too hard or in chronic under-recovery?
  ├── YES ──> Deload / enforce easy endurance; re-test when fully fresh.
  └── NO ───> Proceed to Step 2.
       │
       ▼
[ Step 2: Check Execution & Skill ]
Are surges failing due to low cadence (mashing out of corners) or poor pack drafting?
  ├── YES ──> Fix cornering lines, drafting distance, and high-cadence spin-ups (95+ RPM).
  └── NO ───> Proceed to Step 3.
       │
       ▼
[ Step 3: Confirm True Physiological Limiter ]
Athlete is fresh, executes tactically, but lacks 1-min W' or aerobic TTE.
  └── Prescribe targeted off-season or pre-season training blocks.
```

## Practical Application & Off-Season Prescriptions

### 1. Low-Opportunity-Cost Off-Season Interventions
These interventions yield massive performance improvements with zero interference to aerobic base building:

* **Heavy Strength Training:** 2 sessions per week of heavy compound lifts (squats, deadlifts, hip thrusts) to increase motor unit recruitment, rate of force development (RFD), and tendon stiffness without adding significant hypertrophy.
* **Positional Adaptation:** Perform Zone 2 endurance and active recovery rides on the specific race bike (Time Trial bike or Mountain Bike). This adapts posterior chain musculature, neck extensors, and hip angles without high physiological strain.
* **Clinical Health & Blood Diagnostics:** Run comprehensive blood panels early in the off-season (serum ferritin, iron saturation, vitamin D, complete blood count). Correcting ferritin deficiencies requires 3–4 months of oral supplementation.
* **Trialing Tapers & Warmup Protocols:** Test pre-workout opener protocols (e.g., short 30s efforts vs. easy steady spinning) before off-season baseline testing to establish individual response patterns.

### 2. Video Analysis for Racecraft Weaknesses
* Study amateur first-person GoPro race footage (Category 3/4 crits and road races) with the audio muted.
* Focus on pattern recognition: identifying when the pack surges, noticing gaps opening through corners, and observing how poor positioning forces unnecessary high-wattage micro-bursts.

---

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

* **Diagnosing Limiters at the End of a Long Season:** Assessing fitness while carrying months of accumulated systemic fatigue, leading to the false conclusion that "everything is a weakness."
* **Over-Training the Weakness:** Hammering sprint or anaerobic intervals 4–5 days a week in November, resulting in autonomic exhaustion and psychological burnout by January.
* **Ignoring Tactical Workarounds:** Forcing an athlete with poor peak sprint power to contest bunch sprints instead of developing breakaway skills, timing late attacks, or working as a leadout engine.
* **Overhauling Everything Simultaneously:** Changing nutrition, bike fit, pedal systems, lifting program, and training philosophy all at once, making it impossible to isolate which variable produced positive or negative outcomes.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Observed Symptom / Problem | Underlying Root Cause | Prescribed Off-Season Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dropped during accelerations out of corners** | Mashing a high gear at 40–50 RPM post-turn. | Shift before entering corner; practice high-cadence (95–110 RPM) accelerations. |
| **Sprint power 200W below expected target** | Chronic under-recovery from high-intensity endurance. | Reduce intensity on easy days; test sprint power strictly after 48h rest. |
| **Inability to hold target power on TT bike** | Lack of structural and tissue adaptation to aero hip angle. | Perform 2–3 easy Zone 2 rides per week in full aero extension on the trainer. |
| **Unexplained mid-season performance plateau** | Subclinical iron/ferritin depletion or low vitamin D. | Complete blood panels in October/November; initiate corrective dietary/supplement protocol. |
| **Climber wants to improve crit sprint** | Unrealistic physiological phenotype conversion. | Focus on positioning and late solo attacks; maintain climbing power-to-weight. |
| **Off-season motivation and enthusiasm cratering** | Over-optimizing too many variables simultaneously. | Simplify routine; prioritize enjoyable unstructured base miles and heavy lifting. |
