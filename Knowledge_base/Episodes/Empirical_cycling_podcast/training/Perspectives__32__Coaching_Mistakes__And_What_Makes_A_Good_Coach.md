# Perspectives #32: Coaching Mistakes, And What Makes A Good Coach — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Adam Pulford_

---

## What Is Effective Endurance Coaching?

Effective coaching is the structured integration of exercise physiology, objective performance analytics, and interpersonal psychology to guide an athlete toward their physiological ceiling. 

A coach acts as an objective, emotionally detached strategist who designs progressive overload and fatigue management schemes, while simultaneously functioning as an empathetic mentor who navigates the athlete's life stress, psychological limiters, and subjective sensations. Coaching is fundamentally a **two-way collaborative partnership**, not an authoritarian dictatorship: the athlete provides subjective biological feedback, and the coach adjusts the training stimulus to match the athlete's individual adaptive capacity.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. High-Intensity Dose-Response & Autonomic Balance
- **Sympathetic Over-Saturation:** High-intensity interval sessions ($\ge \text{FTP}$) trigger substantial catecholamine release, glycogen depletion, and central nervous system (CNS) fatigue.
- **The 2–3 Day Ceiling:** For athletes with normal allostatic life load (careers, families), the neuromuscular and endocrine systems can typically absorb and adapt to a maximum of **2 to 3 hard sessions per week**. 
- **The "Getting Away With It" Fallacy:** Highly motivated athletes can temporarily sustain 4–5 hard sessions per week through sheer willpower. However, this represents an un-buffered state where any minor life stressor (poor sleep, caloric restriction, work conflict) triggers non-functional overreaching or illness.

### 2. Testing Artifacts & Intensity Overestimation
- **Glycolytic Contribution in Short Field Tests:** Tests such as $2 \times 8\text{ minutes}$ or standard 20-minute protocols allow anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) to subsidize power output by 10–25%.
- **Cascading Zone Errors:** If an athlete's threshold is overestimated by 15–30 watts from a short test, subsequent "Sweet Spot" or "Threshold" workouts are actually performed in the severe intensity domain, driving rapid glycogen depletion, excessive lactate accumulation, and early session failure.

### 3. Subjective RPE vs. Rigid Power Zones
- **Biological Fluctuations:** Daily physiological capacity fluctuates based on glycogen status, hydration, core temperature, and autonomic state.
- **RPE as the Ultimate Integrator:** Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE) integrates afferent feedback from central motor command, respiratory muscle metaboreflex, and peripheral muscle fatigue. Rigid adherence to arbitrary wattage targets ignores daily biological variability, whereas anchoring workouts to target RPE ensures the appropriate physiological system is stimulated.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The Hierarchy of Effective Coaching

```
[Level 1: Empathy & Connection]   ──> Establish genuine care; build psychological safety and trust.
[Level 2: Objective Performance]  ──> Analyze power durations (MMP), TTE, and event-specific demands.
[Level 3: Communication Medium]   ──> Align on communication modalities (calls/video for complex issues).
[Level 4: Collaborative Autonomy] ──> Coach athlete to make intelligent in-workout and microcycle adjustments.
```

### 2. Core Coaching Mistakes and Corrective Frameworks

#### Mistake 1: Prescribing Excessive High-Intensity Frequency
- **The Error:** Assigning 4–5 interval sessions per week under the belief that "more intensity equals faster gains."
- **The Fix:** Cap high-intensity days at **2 sessions per week** (or 3 during brief, focused overload blocks). Fill remaining training volume with low-RPE Zone 2 aerobic endurance ($\le 65\%$ FTP).

#### Mistake 2: Over-Reliance on Short Testing Protocols
- **The Error:** Using $2 \times 8\text{ min}$ or 20-min tests and applying a rigid multiplier (e.g., 0.90–0.95) to set FTP.
- **The Fix:** Implement extensive, continuous threshold assessments ($35\text{–}60+\text{ minutes}$) or progressive ramped threshold tests that exhaust anaerobic capacity and establish true Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE).

#### Mistake 3: Text-Only Communication on High-Stakes Issues
- **The Error:** Attempting to troubleshoot fatigue, illness, or race anxiety via brief text messages or TrainingPeaks workout comments.
- **The Fix:** Transition immediately to phone or video calls whenever an athlete expresses confusion, low confidence, chronic fatigue, or life disruption.

#### Mistake 4: Abdicating Direction (The "Reverse Dictatorship")
- **The Error:** Allowing highly analytical or self-coached athletes to dictate their own programming, reducing the coach to a passive rubber stamp.
- **The Fix:** Maintain coach-led structure while granting operational autonomy within predefined physiological boundaries.

#### Mistake 5: "Trusting Without Verifying" Nutrition & Biomarkers
- **The Error:** Accepting athlete assertions that their diet/fueling is "good" without quantifying grams per hour or validating medical claims.
- **The Fix:** Audit intra-workout carbohydrate intake ($g/\text{hr}$) directly; cross-reference abnormal blood panels with qualified medical/nutritional professionals before adjusting training.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall | Mechanism of Failure | Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Selection Bias Coaching** | Applying high-intensity "meat grinder" programs where only genetic outliers survive, assuming the program caused the success. | Individualize training dose to the athlete's specific recovery kinetics and life constraints. |
| **Dictatorship vs Partnership** | Imposing rigid workout mandates without athlete buy-in leads to compliance collapse and hidden fatigue. | Establish shared expectations and educate athletes on the physiological intent of each block. |
| **Training via Pure "Vibes"** | Letting self-coached athletes decide workouts spontaneously on the road eliminates progressive overload and structural consistency. | Retain structured weekly targets while using RPE to modulate intra-workout execution. |
| **Compromising Group Ride Protocols** | Attempting structured intervals (e.g., $2 \times 20\text{ min}$) within an unpredictable group ride creates safety risks and ruins workout quality. | Separate solo interval execution from social/tactical group rides; use group rides for racecraft, not structured intervals. |

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Coach-Athlete Evaluation & Decision Table

| Diagnostic Observation | Root Cause | Prescribed Coaching Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Athlete fails workout 3 days in a row** | Autonomic overreaching or under-fueling; cumulative fatigue. | Cancel remaining intensity for the week; prescribe 48–72h easy active recovery and audit caloric intake. |
| **Athlete's 20-min power is high, but fails 2x15m FTP intervals** | Anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) inflated the short test; true FTP is lower. | Reduce FTP setting by 5–8%; prescribe extensive threshold intervals (e.g., $3 \times 12\text{m}$) focused on TTE. |
| **Athlete leaves cryptic, negative workout comments** | Psychological distress, impending illness, or relationship strain. | Schedule an immediate 15-minute phone/video consultation; do not rely on text replies. |
| **Athlete demands high-volume/high-intensity build before an event** | Panic training / fear of lost fitness. | Reassure athlete with historical performance data; enforce planned taper and fatigue dissipation. |
| **Athlete consistently finishes endurance rides at $>75\%$ FTP** | Ego-driven pacing ("grey zone" trap); unable to ride easy. | Mandate strict RPE ceiling (3–4/10) or remove power display from head unit during Zone 2 rides. |
