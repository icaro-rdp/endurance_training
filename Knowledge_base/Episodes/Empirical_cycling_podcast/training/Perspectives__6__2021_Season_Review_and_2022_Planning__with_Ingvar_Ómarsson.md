# Season Review & Macrocycle Periodization: Annual Planning, Durability Profiling, & Pro MTB Case Study — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Ingvar Ómarsson (Perspectives #6)_

---

## What Is an Annual Season Review & Macrocycle Plan?

An **Annual Season Review** is a systematic, evidence-based audit of an athlete's preceding competitive macrocycle. Rather than focusing exclusively on win/loss records or average training stress scores (TSS), it evaluates how the athlete’s underlying physiology responded to training load, racing density, environmental stressors, and travel fatigue.

The **Macrocycle Plan** translates these diagnostic insights into a structured 12-month periodization roadmap. It aligns mesocycles (general preparation, base building, threshold extension, race-specific sharpening, and tapering) with primary goal events (A-races) while building physiological buffers against overtraining and systemic depletion.

  └──────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘

## Key Physiological Mechanisms & Analytical Frameworks

### 1. Durability Profiling & Post-kJ Fatigue Resistance

In elite cross-country Olympic (XCO) and marathon (XCM) mountain bike racing, athletes frequently face decisive tactical moments after substantial cumulative metabolic strain.

- **Fresh vs. Fatigued Power-Duration Curves:** Standard Mean Maximal Power (MMP) curves represent fresh capabilities. Durability profiling evaluates the drop-off in threshold, 5-minute, and 1-minute power after $1,500\text{ kJ}$, $2,500\text{ kJ}$, and $3,500\text{ kJ}$ of expended mechanical energy.
- **Glycogen Depletion Kinetics & Type I Motor Unit Fatigue:** As Type I slow-twitch fibers exhaust intramyocellular glycogen stores, the neuromuscular system is forced to recruit Type IIa and IIx fibers even at sub-threshold outputs. This shifts substrate utilization toward glycolysis, driving up ventilation, heart rate drift, and perceived exertion (RPE).
- **Mitochondrial Capillarization & Lipid Oxidation:** A well-developed aerobic base increases intramuscular triglyceride oxidation and mitochondrial enzyme density (citrate synthase, $\beta$-HAD), sparing glycogen for late-race surges.

### 2. Travel Fatigue, Autonomic Strain, & Non-Training Stressors

For international and multi-discipline competitors, non-training stressors frequently dictate physiological adaptation limits:

- **Circadian Disruption & Flight Stress:** Long-haul travel induces hypoxia-induced dehydration, altered cortisol rhythms, and suppressed nocturnal melatonin release, directly impairing rapid sleep cycles and muscle protein synthesis.
- **Sympathetic Over-Activation:** Chronic travel, bike assembly logistics, and unpredictable nutrition schedules elevate baseline sympathetic tone, reducing heart rate variability (rMSSD) and blunting the parasympathetic rebound necessary for interval adaptation.
- **Periodization Adjustment:** Training load immediately following multi-time-zone travel must be shifted to low-intensity active recovery and aerobic spin sessions rather than heavy glycolytic work.

---

### 3. Threshold Progression: Power vs. Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE)

A core tenet of Empirical Cycling periodization is prioritizing threshold duration over artificial power increments:

- **TTE Extension (Extensive Threshold):** Progressing interval formats from $3 \times 15\text{ min}$ ($45\text{ min}$ total) to $2 \times 30\text{ min}$ ($60\text{ min}$ total) to $1 \times 60\text{ min}$ or $2 \times 45\text{ min}$ ($90\text{ min}$ total) at a stable, true FTP.
- **Metabolic Steady State:** This drives peripheral adaptations—increased mitochondrial density in recruited motor units, elevated monocarboxylate transporter (MCT1) expression, and increased capillarization—without the excessive autonomic fatigue of training above critical power.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The Post-Season Review Protocol

Conduct a structured 4-step debrief between coach and athlete:

1. **Calendar & Logistical Audit:** Map every race, travel day, illness, and injury across the season. Identify clusters where performance degraded due to excessive racing density.
2. **Physiological Phenotype Assessment:** Review power files across different mesocycles to assess:
   - Peak $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ responsiveness (how many weeks of HIIT before plateau).
   - Threshold TTE progression (maximum continuous work duration at FTP).
   - Late-race power reproducibility ($>2,500\text{ kJ}$ efforts).
3. **Qualitative & Psychological Check:** Assess motivation, burnout indicators, and relationship with daily training routines.
4. **Target Setting:** Establish primary target events ("A-races") separated by at least 8–12 weeks of structured preparation blocks.

---

### 2. Sample Annual Macrocycle Structure for an Elite Off-Road Cyclist

```
 Mesocycle 1: Transition / Active Rest (3–4 Weeks)
 ├─ Focus: Mental decompression, cross-training, unstructured easy riding.
 └─ Target: Complete resolution of systemic autonomic fatigue.

 Mesocycle 2: General Preparation & Base Durability (8–12 Weeks)
 ├─ Volume: High aerobic volume (Zone 2) + 2x weekly heavy resistance training.
 ├─ Intensity: Low (Z1/Z2) with occasional cadence and torque work.
 └─ Target: Mitochondrial biogenesis, capillarization, connective tissue strengthening.

 Mesocycle 3: Extensive Threshold / TTE Extension (6–8 Weeks)
 ├─ Workouts: 2x weekly threshold progression (e.g., 3x20m -> 2x35m -> 1x70m at 97–100% FTP).
 ├─ Volume: Moderate-to-high aerobic endurance filler.
 └─ Target: Extend TTE at FTP from ~35-40 min to 60-75+ min.

 Mesocycle 4: Specific Preparation & Intensive Aerobic / VO2max (3–4 Weeks)
 ├─ Workouts: High-intensity VO2max intervals (e.g., 4x5m hard-start or 5x4m all-out).
 ├─ Target: Increase cardiac stroke volume ceiling and elevate aerobic ceiling.

 Mesocycle 5: Race-Specific Sharpness, Taper, & Peak (2–3 Weeks)
 ├─ Volume: Progressive reduction (-40% to -60% volume).
 ├─ Intensity: Race-pace efforts, short anaerobic punch repetitions, openers.
 └─ Target: Supercompensation of glycogen, neuromuscular readiness, fresh form.
```

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Periodization Pitfall | Underlying Cause | Physiological Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Race-to-Race Churn** | Over-scheduling B and C races continuously without designated training blocks. | Prevents progressive overload; causes plateau at sub-optimal fitness and chronic fatigue. |
| **Premature Intensity Ingestion** | Introducing high-intensity glycolytic intervals too early in the winter base phase. | Exhausts autonomic reserves; creates early peak in February followed by mid-season burnout. |
| **Ignoring Fatigued Power Drops** | Evaluating athlete readiness purely on 20-minute power in fresh conditions. | Athlete gets dropped in the 3rd hour of a race despite high tested FTP on a stationary trainer. |
| **Ignoring Travel As Training Load** | Treating long-haul travel days as "rest days" and scheduling hard intervals immediately upon arrival. | Severe autonomic overload, high risk of upper respiratory tract infection (URTI), and poor adaptation. |
