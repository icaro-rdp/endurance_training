# Perspectives #29: Managing Fitness And Rest During Race Season — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Taylor Warren_

---

## What Is In-Season Fitness and Fatigue Management?

Managing fitness and fatigue during a competitive cycling season is the art of maintaining physiological capacity—specifically aerobic threshold power and fatigue resistance—while absorbing the acute, highly disruptive metabolic and autonomic stress of frequent racing and travel. 

A standard competitive season often presents a paradox: racing provides intense, race-specific anaerobic and neuromuscular stimuli, but the accompanying travel, cognitive stress, and irregular scheduling frequently degrade total training volume and aerobic base fitness. Successful in-season management shifts the athlete's mindset from **building** capacity (which requires progressive overload and substantial recovery reserves) to **preserving** threshold, maximizing recovery, and utilizing deliberate mid-season volume resets.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Glycogen Preservation & Inter-Session Recovery Dynamics
- **Substrate Kinetics during Base Rides:** Even at low aerobic intensities (55–65% FTP), total kilojoule expenditure over a 4–6 hour ride is substantial. Under-fueling endurance rides (e.g., <40–60 g/hr) forces significant liver and muscle glycogen depletion.
- **Cortisol & Autonomic Tone:** High caloric deficits and low intra-workout carbohydrate availability elevate systemic cortisol and sympathetic drive, blunting parasympathetic reactivation post-exercise and impairing protein synthesis.
- **The Carbohydrate Stacking Effect:** Consuming 90–100+ g/hr of carbohydrates (e.g., a 1:1 glucose-to-fructose ratio from liquid sucrose) on low-intensity endurance rides maintains high blood glucose flux, spares endogenous glycogen, and minimizes post-ride fatigue. This allows the athlete to absorb back-to-back high-volume days without chronic autonomic exhaustion.

### 2. The "Suspiciously Easy" Aerobic Stimulus vs. Ego Watts
- **Mitochondrial Biogenesis Signaling:** Adaptations driven by Zone 2 volume (capillarization, mitochondrial enzyme upregulation, Type I fiber efficiency) rely on duration and total contractile volume rather than high muscular tension.
- **Metabolic Cost Curve:** Riding at 60% FTP vs. 75% FTP generates nearly identical long-term peripheral aerobic adaptations over multi-hour durations, but 75% FTP exponentially accelerates glycogen oxidation and central fatigue. Pacing endurance rides at a "suspiciously easy" effort (low RPE, conversational) protects the neuromuscular and metabolic systems for race days.

### 3. Redundancy of High-Intensity Training During Race Season
- **Race Demands as VO2max / Anaerobic Stimuli:** Criteriums and road races inherently demand repeated surges above Critical Power (CP) / Functional Threshold Power (FTP), eliciting maximal cardiac output, high fractional oxygen utilization ($\dot{V}\text{O}_2$), and substantial anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) depletion.
- **Decoupling of Threshold Maintenance:** While racing provides ample $\dot{V}\text{O}_2$ and sprint stimulus, it rarely provides steady, sustained sub-threshold or threshold flux (unless finishing on sustained climbs). Consequently, the physiological metric most vulnerable to decay during race blocks is sustained Threshold / Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE), which requires dedicated steady-state maintenance bouts rather than extra high-intensity intervals.

### 4. Neuromuscular Capacity vs. Peak Sprint Power in Competition
- **Repeatability over Peak Watts:** In road and criterium racing, absolute peak 5-second power is rarely the limiting factor for race outcomes. Instead, race results depend on **20- to 60-second repeatability** and the ability to produce high torque/power after thousands of kilojoules of work.
- **Neuromuscular Coordination:** Decades of racing build ingrained motor unit recruitment patterns that allow experienced athletes to execute rapid accelerations with minimal metabolic overhead, whereas less experienced riders require structured high-cadence and neuromuscular micro-sessions to develop this efficiency.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. In-Season Weekly Microcycle Template

When racing weekly or bi-weekly, training between events must prioritize autonomic recovery and targeted maintenance:

```
[Race Weekend] 
   └── Day 1 (Mon): Complete Rest or 45–60 min active recovery (<50% FTP)
   └── Day 2 (Tue): 48-Hour Assessment — Easy aerobic spin (1.5–2 hrs @ 55–60% FTP)
   └── Day 3 (Wed): Maintenance Intensity — 2–3 x 15–20 min @ Sweet Spot / Threshold (88–95% FTP)
   └── Day 4 (Thu): Low-RPE Aerobic Volume (3–4 hrs @ 55–65% FTP, fueled at 90+ g/hr)
   └── Day 5 (Fri): Openers / Pre-Race Priming (60–90 min with 3x30s build sprints + 1x3min @ Threshold)
[Next Race Weekend]
```

### 2. High-Volume Intra-Ride Fueling Protocol
- **Target Intake:** 90–120 g/hr for all rides $\ge 2.5$ hours.
- **Cost-Effective Home Formulation:**
  - Table sugar (sucrose: 50% glucose, 50% fructose) measured at 90–120 g per liter bottle.
  - Lemon juice / citric acid to cut sweetness and enhance palatability.
  - 1.5–2.0 g sodium chloride (or sodium citrate) per liter to offset thermal strain and optimize intestinal sodium-glucose cotransport (SGLT1).
- **Strategy:** Drink 1 concentrated bottle of liquid carbohydrate, followed by plain water to manage osmolarity and palate fatigue.

### 3. Mid-Season Volume Reset (The 6-Week Rebuild)
For long seasons (April to September), perform a planned mid-season reset in June/July:
1. **Week 1 (De-load):** 5–8 hours of total unstructured "soul riding" (mountain biking, gravel, coffee rides) with zero structured intensity.
2. **Weeks 2–5 (Extensive Aerobic Build):** Focus on maximum sustainable volume (18–25+ hrs/week) at 55–65% FTP. Limit intensity to 1 maintenance session or C-priority race per week.
3. **Week 6 (Pre-Competition Sharpening):** Reintroduce race-pace motor unit firing via short threshold and VO2max intervals before the late-summer race campaign.

### 4. Off-Season Structural Remediation
Cycling is an entirely sagittal, non-impact, hip-flexed sport that produces tight iliopsoas, forward spinal curvature, and loss of bone mineral density:
- **Duration:** 2–3 weeks completely unstructured / off the bike.
- **Cross-Training & Bone Loading:** High-impact, multi-planar activities (hiking, backpacking, running, surfing, resistance training).
- **Eccentric Muscle Conditioning:** Progressive re-introduction of eccentric lower-body loading (squats, lunges, step-downs) to rebuild structural resiliency.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall | Mechanism of Failure | Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ego Pacing on Base Rides** | Riding at 70–78% FTP ("200W default") drains glycogen and creates systemic fatigue without adding mitochondrial stimulus. | Constrain endurance rides to $\le 60–65\%$ FTP or low conversational RPE ("suspiciously easy"). |
| **Under-Fueling Easy Days** | Fasted or low-carb Zone 2 rides elevate cortisol, impair autonomic recovery, and compromise next-day training capacity. | Ingest 80–100+ g/hr of simple carbohydrates on all multi-hour base rides. |
| **Mid-Season High-Intensity Add-ons** | Adding VO2max or anaerobic interval workouts between race weekends leads to autonomic overreaching and staleness. | Eliminate supplemental high-intensity intervals; rely on races for anaerobic stimulus and use mid-week slots for sweet spot or threshold maintenance. |
| **Over-Engineering Workout Targets** | Obsessing over hitting exact wattage targets (the mid-career bell-curve trap) creates cognitive fatigue and ignores physiological fluctuations. | Lean into RPE and subjective physiological sensations while executing simple, proven workout structures. |
| **Ignoring Travel & Allostatic Load** | Treating travel days as "rest" ignores disruption to circadian rhythm, sleep architecture, and nutritional timing. | Standardize sleep hygiene on the road (eye masks, own pillow, rapid circadian alignment with morning sunlight) and log travel as stress. |

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### In-Season Training & Race Readiness Checklist

- [ ] **Endurance Intensity Control:** Is the power on base rides strictly below 65% FTP with an RPE that feels "too easy"?
- [ ] **Intra-Workout Fueling:** Are you consuming $\ge 90\text{ g/hr}$ of carbohydrates in liquid/solid form on rides longer than 2 hours?
- [ ] **Mid-Week Intensity Selection:** If the upcoming weekend is a race, is mid-week intensity limited to steady threshold/sweet-spot maintenance rather than VO2max intervals?
- [ ] **Fatigue Triage (48h Rule):** Are you giving yourself 48 hours post-race before evaluating whether the body can absorb an interval session?
- [ ] **Mid-Season Pivot:** Is a 1-week unstructured break scheduled midway through the calendar to prevent late-season burnout?
- [ ] **Off-Season Reset:** Are multi-planar cross-training and strength training integrated post-season to restore musculoskeletal integrity?
