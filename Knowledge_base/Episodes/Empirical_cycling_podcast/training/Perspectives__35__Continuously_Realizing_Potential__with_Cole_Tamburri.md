# Perspectives #35: Continuously Realizing Potential — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Cole Tamburri_

---

## What Is Long-Term Potential Realization?

Long-term potential realization is the multi-year process of optimizing an athlete’s physiological ceiling, tactical racecraft, and psychological durability through consistent training overload, adequate energy availability, and collaborative coach-athlete communication.

Rather than chasing rapid short-term fitness spikes through extreme caloric restriction or unsustainable training volume, sustainable athletic development requires an athlete to understand the underlying physiological mechanisms of adaptation, take personal ownership of their recovery, and maintain a balanced life ecosystem outside the sport.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Energy Availability, Endocrine Health, and the "Weight vs. Power" Fallacy
- **The Low Energy Availability (LEA) Pitfall:** The traditional cycling dogma of *"train like a horse, eat like a rabbit"* forces the body into Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S). Chronic LEA ($<30\text{ kcal/kg FFM/day}$) suppresses the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis, lowering testosterone/estrogen, blunting thyroid function ($T_3$), increasing cortisol, reducing bone mineral density, and destroying sleep architecture.
- **Absolute Power vs. $W/kg$ on Variable Terrain:** Extreme leanness reduces lean muscle mass, cardiac stroke volume, and glycogen storage capacity. Gaining functional mass (e.g., moving from 145 lbs to 165 lbs at 6'2") increases absolute threshold power (e.g., $276\text{ W} \to 400+\text{ W}$), raw neuromuscular force, and high-speed momentum, resulting in faster climbing times even on steep gradients due to superior metabolic efficiency and physiological headroom.

### 2. Banister Impulse-Response: Fatigue vs. Fitness
- **Training as a Catabolic Stimulus:** Exercise does not create fitness directly; it creates fatigue, glycogen depletion, and microtrauma.
- **Supercompensation Kinetics:** Adaptations (mitochondrial biogenesis, plasma volume expansion, capillary angiogenesis) occur exclusively during subsequent rest and recovery phases. Forcing high-intensity sessions while in a state of severe autonomic fatigue deepens the catabolic hole without eliciting positive adaptation.

### 3. Neuromuscular High-Threshold Motor Unit Recruitment
- **Strength as a Skill:** High-load resistance training improves maximal voluntary muscle activation via increased motor unit firing rates, intra-muscular coordination, and tendon stiffness.
- **Year-Round Maintenance:** When strength training is completely dropped during race season, neuromuscular recruitment and Type II fiber force production rapidly decay. Maintaining low-RPE resistance work year-round preserves sprinting and surge capabilities without interfering with endurance adaptations.

### 4. Wearable Metric Pitfalls: Parasympathetic Saturation
- Commercial HRV and recovery algorithms (e.g., Whoop, Garmin) can register misleading "green" recovery scores when an athlete is severely glycogen-depleted and metabolically suppressed (parasympathetic saturation/blunted sympathetic responsiveness). Coaches and athletes must rely primarily on subjective RPE and power-duration tracking over automated wearable scores.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The Multi-Year Progression Architecture

```
[Phase 1: Foundation & Education (Years 1–2)]
  - Transition from rigid 20-min testing to extensive threshold modeling (TTE).
  - Eliminate under-fueling habits; stabilize daily caloric intake (carbohydrates $\ge 6\text{–}10\text{ g/kg/day}$).
  - Develop basic pack skills and race positioning.

[Phase 2: Volume Tolerance & Overload Blocks (Years 3–4)]
  - Introduce focused high-volume blocks (e.g., 30–40 hour base camps).
  - Execute concentrated VO2max blocks (2–3 weeks of high-cadence, high-ventilation intervals).
  - Integrate dedicated off-season heavy lifting.

[Phase 3: Elite Execution & Tactical Mastery (Years 5+)]
  - Fine-tune race-specific repeatability and high-speed cornering under fatigue.
  - Year-round in-season lifting maintenance (1x/week, low RPE).
  - Autonomic athlete agency: athlete self-regulates workout duration based on daily biofeedback.
```

---

### 2. Year-Round Resistance Training Protocol

#### Off-Season (Hypertrophy & Maximal Strength / 8–12 Weeks)
- **Frequency:** 2 sessions per week.
- **Focus:** Compound multi-joint lifts (Barbell Back/Box Squat, Romanian Deadlift, Heavy Leg Press, Seated Rows).
- **Loading:** 3–5 sets of 4–6 reps @ RPE 7–9 (75–85% 1RM), 3 minutes rest.

#### In-Season (Neuromuscular Maintenance & Joint Integrity)
- **Frequency:** 1 session per week (40–45 minutes).
- **Focus:** High movement quality, zero eccentric failure.
- **Structure:**
  - Goblet Squat or Leg Press: 3 sets $\times$ 8 reps @ RPE 4–5
  - Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift / Step-ups: 2 sets $\times$ 8 reps
  - Seated Cable Row / Lat Pulldown: 3 sets $\times$ 10 reps
  - Calf Raises & Core (Hanging Leg Lifts / Planks): 2–3 sets

### 3. The "Written in Sand" Rule: In-Workout Adjustment Protocol

Athletes must be given explicit permission to alter prescribed workouts based on real-time biological feedback:

```
[Workout Start: Interval 1]
        │
        ├──> Target power feels smooth, RPE aligns with zone ──> Execute full prescribed session.
        │
        ├──> Power requires maximum mental effort, RPE 9–10 on sub-threshold effort ──> Reduce power by 5%.
        │        │
        │        ├──> Power stabilizes, heart rate responds normally ──> Complete modified session.
        │        │
        │        └──> Heavy legs persist, heart rate suppressed (>15 bpm low) ──> ABORT interval session immediately.
        │                 │
        │                 └──> Spin easy in Zone 1 (<50% FTP) for 45 min, go home, refuel with carbohydrates.
```

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall | Mechanism of Failure | Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chronic Low Energy Availability (RED-S)** | Restricting calories to chase a low scale weight causes hormonal collapse, loss of lean muscle mass, and power stagnation. | Fuel workouts aggressively ($60\text{–}100\text{ g/hr}$ carbs); support a healthy body weight that optimizes absolute wattage. |
| **Rigid "Plan in Stone" Mentality** | Forcing completion of hard intervals when sick, sleep-deprived, or overreached deepens central nervous system fatigue. | Adopt the "Plan in Sand" approach: modify or cancel workouts when biofeedback indicates un-recovered states. |
| **Dropping Strength Training In-Season** | Stopping all lifting leads to neuromuscular detraining, reduced peak sprint power, and increased injury risk. | Maintain 1 short, low-RPE (RPE 3–5) lifting session weekly throughout the competitive season. |
| **Altitude-to-Sea-Level Pacing Shock** | Athletes descending from altitude possess higher sea-level absolute power capacity, causing them to smash group rides and over-fatigue unconditioned muscular structures. | Arrive at sea-level races close to event day or strictly cap training power during the transition week. |

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Athlete Development & Biofeedback Decision Matrix

| Athlete Status / Observation | Root Cause | Action Required |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Athlete is 15 lbs under baseline, but FTP has dropped 30W** | Catabolism of skeletal muscle mass and endocrine downregulation from RED-S. | Implement progressive caloric re-feeding; increase daily carbohydrates to $\ge 8\text{ g/kg}$; cease active weight loss. |
| **Failed 2 consecutive interval workouts despite high motivation** | Autonomic overreaching; central nervous system fatigue or glycogen depletion. | Cancel next 48 hours of intensity; prescribe 2 days of passive rest/active recovery; assess sleep quality. |
| **Whoop/Garmin displays "95% Recovery" but legs feel completely dead** | Parasympathetic saturation masking severe muscular and metabolic fatigue. | Trust subjective RPE over wearable metrics; reduce session volume/intensity accordingly. |
| **Athlete feels guilty skipping extra volume/intervals** | Psychological anxiety / fear of losing fitness ("fatigue security blanket"). | Educate athlete on the supercompensation curve; emphasize that fitness is expressed only after fatigue dissipates. |
