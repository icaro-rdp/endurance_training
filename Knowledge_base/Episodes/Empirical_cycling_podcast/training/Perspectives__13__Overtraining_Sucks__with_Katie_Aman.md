# Perspectives #13: Overtraining Sucks — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Katie Aman_

---

## What Is Overtraining?

Overtraining is an accumulation of training and non-training stress resulting in long-term decrements in performance capacity with or without related physiological and psychological signs and symptoms of maladaptation. It exists along a continuum:

```
[ Acute Fatigue ] ──> [ Functional Overreaching (FOR) ] ──> [ Non-Functional Overreaching (NFOR) ] ──> [ Overtraining Syndrome (OTS) ]
     (Days)                  (Days to Weeks)                       (Weeks to Months)                       (Months to Years)
```

1. **Acute Fatigue:** Normal post-session muscular and metabolic fatigue; full recovery occurs within 24–48 hours.
2. **Functional Overreaching (FOR):** Short-term performance suppression induced by heavy training blocks that leads to a positive supercompensation rebound after a brief taper (several days to 2 weeks).
3. **Non-Functional Overreaching (NFOR):** State where training load and life stress outstrip adaptive capacity. Performance stagnates or drops, psychological mood states sour, and full recovery requires several weeks to months of rest without a supercompensation rebound.
4. **Overtraining Syndrome (OTS):** Severe, multisystemic neuroendocrine, metabolic, immunological, and autonomic collapse. Recovery requires months to years and may end an athletic career.
5. **Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S):** A condition of Low Energy Availability (LEA) where dietary energy intake is insufficient to support the physiological energy expenditure required for health, daily function, and athletic performance. RED-S frequently drives or masquerades as OTS.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Energy Availability & Metabolic Cascade
Training adaptation is an energy-demanding cellular process requiring ATP, amino acids for protein synthesis, and glycogen for high-intensity signaling cascades.
- **Low Energy Availability (LEA):** When caloric intake (especially carbohydrate and protein) fails to cover exercise energy expenditure, the body shunts energy away from non-essential functions (cellular repair, reproductive axes, bone remodeling, immune response, thermogenesis).
- **Substrate Depletion & Muscle Catabolism:** Chronic carbohydrate restriction paired with high training density forces reliance on gluconeogenesis (protein breakdown) and fat oxidation at unsustainably high relative intensities, elevating baseline circulating cortisol and inflammatory cytokines.

### 2. Neuroendocrine & Autonomic Nervous System (ANS) Dysregulation
- **Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) & Gonadal (HPG) Axes:** Chronic overload blunts hypothalamic signaling. In females, luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) pulsatility is suppressed, leading to functional hypothalamic amenorrhea (FHA) and estrogen decline. In males, testosterone levels and spermatogenesis drop sharply, resulting in loss of libido and reduced anabolic tone.
- **Sympathetic vs. Parasympathetic OTS:**
  - *Sympathetic Form (frequent in sprint/power athletes):* Characterized by elevated resting heart rate, insomnia, restlessness, irritability, and elevated resting blood pressure.
  - *Parasympathetic Form (frequent in endurance athletes):* Characterized by suppressed maximal heart rate, bradycardia, lethargy, blunted lactate accumulation at maximal effort, and inability to reach high glycolytic flux.

### 3. Neuromuscular & Motor Unit Recruitment Failure ("Deep Fatigue")
- Overtraining is not merely muscle soreness; it is an inhibition of central drive and motor unit recruitment.
- Athletes experience acute resistance to high-torque or high-force movements (e.g., walking up stairs, standing up from a chair, high-torque standing starts). When large Type II motor units are called upon, central motor drive is blunted by protective feedback mechanisms.

### 4. Connective Tissue & Structural Breakdown
- Connective tissues (tendons, ligaments, cartilage) have low metabolic rates and minimal vascularization compared to skeletal muscle.
- In states of systemic under-recovery and LEA, collagen turnover cannot keep pace with repetitive microtrauma, manifesting as chronic, wandering tendinopathies (e.g., Achilles tendon pain, patellar tendinopathy) without identifiable bike-fit errors.

### 5. The Absence of a Single Diagnostic Biomarker
- No single clinical blood test (e.g., total cortisol, creatine kinase, urea, ferritin, testosterone-to-cortisol ratio) reliably diagnoses OTS in isolation.
- Wearable algorithmic scores (e.g., HRV, recovery percentages) can be misleading: an athlete in parasympathetic exhaustion may display abnormally high vagal tone (high HRV), giving false "readiness" indicators despite profound systemic fatigue.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Intensity Discipline: Avoiding the "Middle Intensity Trap"
- **Zone 2 Endurance Prescription:** True endurance riding must remain biologically easy. Prescribing Zone 2 as 70–75% FTP based on an overestimated threshold forces high carbohydrate expenditure and neuromuscular stress.
- **Field Guideline:** True endurance pace often sits between 50% and 65% of actual FTP. Rides should feel sustainable without systemic depletion or post-ride collapse.
- **The "Shower Test":** If an athlete finishes an endurance ride and must sit on the floor of the shower or ravenously purge the kitchen cabinets due to extreme glycogen depletion, the pace was too high and on-the-bike fueling was inadequate.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   INTENSITY POLARIZATION                    │
├──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┤
│ Hard Days (1–3 per week)     │ Truly hard: FTP, VO2max,     │
│                              │ specific intervals           │
├──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┤
│ Easy/Endurance Days          │ Truly easy: 50–65% FTP,      │
│                              │ conversation-pace            │
└──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2. Fueling Protocols to Prevent Energy Deficits
- **On-the-Bike Carbohydrate:** Consume 60–90+ g/h of carbohydrates during sessions >75 minutes, even during low-to-moderate intensity rides. Liquid or easily digestible carbohydrate mixes reduce gastrointestinal load.
- **Daily Protein Targets:** Maintain 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day spread across 4–5 boluses to sustain nitrogen balance and muscle/collagen protein synthesis.
- **Avoid Intra-Workout Deficits:** Weight loss protocols must NEVER be implemented by withholding carbohydrates during or immediately following training sessions.

### 3. Session Governance: The "Rule of Thirds" & Bailing Rules
- **The Rule of Thirds:** Over a training cycle, roughly 1/3 of days feel phenomenal, 1/3 feel average/routine, and 1/3 feel sluggish/mediocre.
- **Converting Bad Days to "Meh" Days:** If warm-up sets or the first interval reveal heavy neuromuscular resistance, drop the target intensity immediately to low-end Zone 2 or spin easily for 30–45 minutes. Never force high-intensity targets on depleted physiology.
- **Time-in-Zone (TIZ) Failure Marker:** If an athlete has been progressing a threshold block (e.g., $3 \times 15 \text{ min} \to 2 \times 25 \text{ min} \to 2 \times 30 \text{ min}$) and fails midway through the target without external life disruptions, the block has reached its ceiling: end the block and begin recovery.

### 4. Protocol for Recovery from NFOR / OTS

```
Step 1: Complete Off-Bike Unloading (5–14 Days)
- Zero structured training; prioritize sleep (8–10 hours/night).
- Full nutritional replenishment: eliminate caloric deficits, prioritize protein and micronutrients.

Step 2: Passive & Low-Stress Reintroduction (1–3 Weeks)
- Short, unstructured spins (30–60 min at <50% FTP).
- Monitor resting heart rate and psychological enthusiasm.

Step 3: Subthreshold Aerobic Progression
- Low-intensity aerobic volume progression before any intensity is reintroduced.
- Test readiness with short subthreshold efforts (e.g., 3x5 min at tempo/sweetspot); if RPE is disproportionate, pull back immediately.
```

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **The "Hero" / Meathead Volume Fallacy:** Equating high pain tolerance with athletic competence. High training volume without adaptive recovery creates chronic stagnation rather than functional adaptation.
2. **Chasing TSS and Daily CTL Targets:** Treating Training Stress Score (TSS) as a complete measure of stress. TSS does not account for life stress, poor sleep, psychological strain, or inadequate nutrition.
3. **Equating Acute Weight Loss with Superior Fitness:** Dropping body mass quickly often causes loss of lean mass, hormonal shutdown, and loss of top-end sprint and glycolytic power ($>100\text{–}200\text{ W}$ drop in sprint peak).
4. **Fear of Rest Weeks ("Fatigue Security Blanket"):** Athletes who become accustomed to chronic fatigue often feel "flat" or anxious during the first 48–72 hours of a rest week and prematurely resume training before systemic recovery is complete.
5. **Ignoring Non-Training Life Stress:** Work deadlines, academic pressures, and interpersonal conflict act on the same neuroendocrine and autonomic pathways as interval training. When external stress surges, training volume and intensity must be scaled down.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Stage | Primary Symptoms | Physiological Markers | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Fatigue** | Transient muscle soreness, mild heaviness | Elevated acute muscle metabolites, temporary glycogen depletion | Normal 24–48h recovery; proceed with plan |
| **Functional Overreaching (FOR)** | Short-term drop in power, elevated RPE, heavy legs | Muscle microtrauma, normal endocrine response | Scheduled recovery microcycle (3–7 days); supercompensation expected |
| **Non-Functional Overreaching (NFOR)** | Stagnant/declining power, unrefreshing sleep, emotional irritability, recurring minor niggles/tendon pain | Blunted heart rate response, elevated resting cortisol/stress markers, low motivation | 1–3 weeks complete training deload; increase caloric and protein intake |
| **Overtraining Syndrome (OTS)** | Severe systemic exhaustion, loss of menses/libido, chronic injury, inability to sustain submaximal power | Neuroendocrine HPA/HPG axis shutdown, profound autonomic imbalance, blunted lactate production | Multimonth cessation of structured training; medical/dietetic intervention |

### Daily Coach & Athlete Readiness Checklist

- [ ] **Neuromuscular Baseline:** Are normal daily movements (stairs, standing up, pedal strokes) pain-free and responsive, or is there deep motor unit recruitment fatigue?
- [ ] **Endocrine / Reproductive Health:** Is the menstrual cycle regular (if applicable)? Is morning resting heart rate / HRV within normal baseline variance?
- [ ] **Psychological Drive:** Is the athlete looking forward to training, or is there persistent dread toward structured intervals?
- [ ] **Nutritional Compliance:** Were carbohydrates consumed during all sessions $>75\text{ min}$? Was daily protein $\ge 1.6\text{ g/kg}$?
- [ ] **Session Rule:** If first interval fails or RPE is $>9/10$ for standard threshold work, pivot session to easy Zone 2 spin or rest immediately.
