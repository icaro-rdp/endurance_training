# Why Training Or Racing Experience Shouldn't Determine Training Volume — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore, James, Rory, Gediminas, Megan (Ten Minute Tips #65)_

---

## What Is Category-Based Training Volume Presumption?

A widespread dogma in endurance cycling suggests that an athlete's racing category (e.g., Category 5 beginner through Category 1/Pro) or years of training experience should dictate:
1. The **total annual or weekly training hours** they perform.
2. The **maximum time-in-zone (TIZ)** they are permitted to accumulate during sweet spot, threshold, or endurance blocks.

Under this misconception, lower-category riders are often arbitrarily capped (e.g., "Category 4 riders only need 6–8 hours/week and a maximum of 40 minutes at threshold"), while high-category riders are prescribed large volumes purely based on rank. In reality, physiological adaptations are driven by an athlete's internal capacity to absorb, adapt to, and recover from metabolic and mechanical strain—not by an arbitrary license classification.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms & Theoretical Concepts

### 1. Log-Linear Dose-Response of Aerobic Volume
Training volume does not scale linearly with absolute performance gains. Instead, aerobic volume follows a diminishing-returns log-linear model:
- **Low to Moderate Volume Baseline (4–8 hrs/week):** Moving from sedentary or low volume to 8–10 hours yields rapid, large-magnitude increases in capillary density, mitochondrial biogenesis, stroke volume, and baseline FTP.
- **Moderate to High Volume (10–20+ hrs/week):** Progressing from 10 to 15–25 hours per week yields modest additional fractional increases in raw FTP (e.g., +10 to +25 watts rather than doubling FTP), but drives massive secondary aerobic adaptations:
  - **First Lactate Threshold (LT1) Elevation:** Submaximal power output at low lactate concentrations rises significantly, shifting substrate utilization toward increased fat oxidation and sparing muscle glycogen at tempo/endurance intensities.
  - **Durability & Fatigue Resistance:** The rate of neuromuscular fatigue and metabolic degradation after expending substantial work (e.g., 2,000–3,000 kJ) is drastically reduced.
  - **Interval Repeatability & Clearance:** Enhanced aerobic flux accelerates phosphocreatine (PCr) resynthesis and lactate shuttling between high-intensity surges.

  |          .------------------- (Diminishing FTP gains, continued Durability/LT1 gains)

### 2. Genetic Baseline vs. Trainability Disconnect
Athletic performance represents the intersection of two distinct genetic phenotypes:
- **Baseline (Untrained) Fitness:** Governed by innate structural parameters, resting cardiac chamber dimensions, natural fiber-type distributions, and baseline hematocrit/erythrocyte volume. Some untrained individuals enter the sport with an untrained $VO_2max$ of 60+ mL/kg/min and high threshold baselines.
- **Trainability (Adaptive Plasticity):** The biological responsiveness of cellular signaling cascades (e.g., AMPK, PGC-1$\alpha$, CaMKII, mTOR) to mechanical and metabolic stress. High baseline fitness does not guarantee high trainability, and low baseline fitness does not preclude exceptional responsiveness.

### 3. Cognitive Biases in Endurance Sports
- **Survivorship Bias:** Observers only see the athletes who survived high training loads, extreme volume, or aggressive training styles without burning out or getting injured. The dropped-out population (those broken by inappropriate training) remains invisible.
- **Selection Bias:** Athletes naturally inclined toward high power-to-weight ratios or superior recovery self-select into continuing competitive cycling, skewing population observations toward elite phenotypes.
- **Counterfactual Fallacy:** Assuming that because a WorldTour pro or top local rider follows a specific training protocol (e.g., 25 hrs/week with specific micro-intervals), that protocol is optimal. In reality, highly gifted athletes may succeed *in spite* of suboptimal training.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### Volume Sizing by Life Bandwidth, Not Category
Training volume must be sized according to total allostatic load (work, family, sleep, recovery capacity) rather than race categorization:
- **Novice with High Time/Recovery Bandwidth:** If an athlete has low external life stress, good recovery habits, and the capacity to fuel and sleep for 12–16 hours of aerobic riding, capping them at 6 hours simply because they are Category 4/5 delays aerobic development.
- **Time-Crunched Athlete (6–10 hrs/week):** Must prioritize high consistency, progressive interval overload (extending TIZ at threshold/sweet spot), and well-timed high-intensity blocks, rather than panicking over lower total volume.

### Time-in-Zone (TIZ) Progression Framework
Do not artificially limit interval duration based on racing category. Progress interval duration according to individual Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE) and auto-regulation:
1. **Establish Individual TTE:** Determine baseline TTE at Functional Threshold Power (FTP) or Sweet Spot.
2. **Progressive Duration Overload:** Add interval duration systematically (e.g., progressing sweet spot work from $3 \times 15\text{ min} \to 3 \times 20\text{ min} \to 2 \times 30\text{ min} \to 1 \times 60\text{ min} \to 1 \times 90\text{ min}$).
3. **Auto-Regulation:** Terminate interval progressions when perceived exertion decouples drastically from power, pedal stroke mechanics break down, or target power cannot be maintained without excessive anaerobic contribution.

### Long-Term Multi-Year Progression
- True aerobic depth (capillarization, slow-twitch fiber remodeling, lipid oxidation kinetics) accumulates across multi-year cycles.
- Sustainable annual volume progression (e.g., 300 hrs $\to$ 450 hrs $\to$ 600 hrs per year) paired with periodic off-season decompression yields compounding performance returns over 3–5 year horizons.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Category-Bound Volume Capping:** Restricting motivated, well-recovering lower-category riders to low training hours based on arbitrary coaching charts.
2. **Copying Elite / Pro Microcycles:** Attempting to replicate the 25–30 hour weekly schedules or ultra-high intensity blocks of professional athletes without their genetic recovery machinery, lack of external stress, or baseline base accumulation.
3. **Equating Higher Volume with Instant FTP Explosions:** Expecting a 50% increase in training volume to yield an immediate 50% increase in FTP. Volume builds the floor (durability, LT1, capacity to absorb intensity), while targeted intensity raises the ceiling.
4. **Neglecting Off-Season Rest:** Constantly chasing volume without structured rest cycles leads to autonomic nervous system stagnation and plateaued adaptations.
5. **The Social Media Comparison Trap:** Benchmarking personal progress against selective, highlight-reel workout files posted on social media or Strava.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Variable | Category-Based Dogma (Flawed) | Empirical Physiological Approach (Correct) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Volume Allocation** | Determined by race category (e.g., Cat 4 = 6 hrs, Cat 1 = 18 hrs). | Determined by athlete recovery bandwidth, total life stress, and tolerance. |
| **Interval TIZ Limits** | Capped at arbitrary interval lengths (e.g., $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ max for Cat 3). | Auto-regulated and progressed based on individual TTE and physiological adaptation. |
| **Genetic Comparison** | Assuming equal volume produces equal absolute power (W/kg). | Recognizing independent variation in baseline phenotype and trainability. |
| **Primary Volume Payoff** | Expecting massive linear increases in FTP. | Gaining massive durability, higher LT1, increased fat oxidation, and fatigue resistance. |
| **Long-Term Planning** | Constant high-intensity chasing across static annual hours. | Progressive multi-year base building with structured seasonal recovery phases. |

> [!TIP]
> **Key Coaching Rule:** Progress an athlete's training volume and interval duration based on what their individual physiology and life balance can absorb and recover from—never let a racing license category dictate physiological stimulus.
