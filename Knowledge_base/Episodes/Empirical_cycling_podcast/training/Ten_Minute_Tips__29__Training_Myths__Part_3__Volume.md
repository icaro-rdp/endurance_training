# Ten Minute Tips #29: Training Myths, Part 3: Volume — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Helson (Ten Minute Tips #29)_

---

## What Is the "Volume Debate" in Endurance Cycling?

Training volume—quantified as total weekly saddle hours or energy expenditure (kilojoules)—is the cornerstone of aerobic development. Despite decades of exercise science, endurance culture remains conflicted between two opposing dogmas:
1. **The High-Intensity / Time-Crunched Dogma:** Claims that high-intensity intervals (HIIT, Sweet Spot) can fully substitute for hours in the saddle via "training efficiency" and Training Stress Score (TSS) equivalence.
2. **The "Junk Miles" Dogma:** Asserts that riding below a rigid power/lactate threshold provides zero biological stimulus and simply wastes time.

Debunking these pervasive myths requires looking past mathematical modeling (TSS, normalized power) to examine real human physiology and coaching data.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
The Five Major Volume Myths:
├── Myth 1: You can overtrain on volume, but not on intensity.
├── Myth 2: Female athletes cannot tolerate as much volume as males.
├── Myth 3: High intensity can replace high volume (TSS Equivalence).
├── Myth 4: Easy spinning and low-intensity pedaling are "junk miles."
└── Myth 5: You don't need high volume to be fast (The Genetic Outlier Trap).
```

### Myth 1: "You Can Overtrain on Volume, But Not on Intensity"
* **The Reality:** Autonomic overtraining syndrome (HPA-axis dysfunction, chronic parasympathetic suppression, glycogen depletion) is triggered far more rapidly by excessive intensity than by volume.
* **The Root Cause of Volume Overtraining:** When athletes break down during high-volume blocks, the underlying culprit is virtually never low-intensity riding itself. It is **riding too hard** (e.g., pegging endurance rides at tempo or relying on faulty $2.0\text{ mmol/L}$ lab LT1 targets) combined with **severe caloric under-fueling** (failing to replace total daily kilojoules).

### Myth 2: "Women Cannot Tolerate High Volume"
* **The Reality:** Aerobic muscle metabolism, mitochondrial biogenesis signaling, and capillarization pathways are identical between sexes. In ultra-endurance running and multi-day cycling, female athletes frequently match or exceed male durability and consistency.
* **The Societal Lag:** Discrepancies in historical training volume among elite women were driven by economic realities (holding full-time jobs alongside racing due to the historical absence of UCI minimum salaries) and shorter race formats, not biological capacity.

### Myth 3: "High Intensity Replaces High Volume (TSS Equivalence)"
* **The Biological Asymmetry:** TSS calculates load based on Normalized Power ($NP^4$). A 60-minute criterium ($100\text{ TSS}$) produces massive neuromuscular and glycolytic fatigue. A 2.5-hour easy base ride ($100\text{ TSS}$) produces widespread capillary shear stress, plasma volume expansion, and mitochondrial enzyme transcription.
* **The "Noob Gains" Illusion:** Untrained individuals gain rapid fitness from any stimulus (even 3 hours/week of HIIT) due to initial plasma volume expansion. Once initial gains plateau, further aerobic progress stalls without increasing volume.

### Myth 4: "Easy Spinning Is Junk Miles"
* **What True Junk Miles Look Like:** True junk miles occur when an athlete rides in the "black hole" of unstructured moderate intensity—surging out of stoplights, hammering short hills during commutes, and turning recovery rides into competitive group rides. This generates substantial autonomic fatigue with zero structured progressive overload.
* **The Value of Easy Pedaling:** Low-intensity muscle contractions (even at 40–55% FTP, active commuting, or cross-training hikes) maintain muscular blood flow, stimulate slow-twitch oxidative pathways, expand central stroke volume, and enhance systemic durability without adding fatigue.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Volume Management Across Athlete Categories

```
Volume Structuring Framework:
├── Time-Crunched Athletes (6–10 hrs/week):
│   ├── Maximize consistency; execute 2 high-quality interval sessions (Threshold/VO2).
│   ├── Treat easy spins, commutes, and active transport as high-value volume boosters.
│   └── Shift focus to racecraft, repeatability, sprint power, and TTE extension when FTP plateaus.
└── High-Volume Athletes (15–30+ hrs/week):
    ├── Keep endurance rides strictly easy (50–65% FTP / RPE 2–3/10; talk test passed easily).
    ├── Limit structured high-intensity sessions to 1–2 per week maximum.
    └── Fuel aggressively: 60–90+ g/hr carbohydrates to prevent chronic energy deficiency (RED-S).
```

### 2. Progressive Overload for Volume
* **Conservative Rule:** Add **30 to 60 minutes** to the weekly long ride each week, or increase total weekly volume by **1 to 2 hours** across a 3-week build before taking a recovery week.
* **Rest Week Scaling:** 
  * A 15-hr/week athlete might reduce volume to **10–12 hours** during a rest week.
  * A 30-hr/week pro athlete must drastically cut volume to **<10 hours** to clear the massive energetic and hormonal fatigue generated by high volume.

### 3. Fueling the Volume
* Do not restrict carbohydrate intake on easy base rides based on the misconception that "I only burned fat." 
* Empty glycogen stores and depleted adipose tissue trigger systemic catabolic signaling. Consume carbohydrates (30–60g/hr on base rides; 60–90g/hr on long rides) and match daily caloric expenditure.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The Genetic Outlier Comparison:** Never base your training program on an athlete who trains 8 hours a week and wins Category 1 races. That athlete is either relying on superior genetic baseline $VO_2max$, years of banked physiological adaptations, or exceptional race tactics.

1. **The Black Hole of Commuting:** Sprinting away from traffic lights and pushing hard up every rise during daily commutes ruins interval recovery. Keep commutes at "non-sweating walking effort."
2. **Ignoring Total Life Stress:** Training volume does not exist in a vacuum. If occupational, financial, or personal stress spikes to 9/10, maintaining high training volume will induce chronic maladaptation and fatigue.
3. **Chasing Infinite Linear FTP Growth on Limited Time:** Athletes with fixed 8–10 hr/week schedules will inevitably plateau in raw FTP wattage. Continuing to add intensity instead of broadening tactical skills leads directly to burnout.

| Myth / Concept | Scientific Reality | Actionable Rule for Athletes |

| **Volume vs Intensity Fatigue** | Intensity causes rapid CNS/autonomic burnout; volume fatigue is mostly energetic/caloric. | Keep base volume strictly easy; fuel rides aggressively. |
| **Female Volume Capacity** | Identical aerobic physiological capacity across sexes. | Prescribe volume based on individual schedule and recovery, not gender. |
| **TSS Equivalence** | 100 TSS from HIIT $\neq$ 100 TSS from Base. | Do not use TSS to justify replacing multi-hour rides with 45-min intervals. |
