# Watts Doc #23: Training Your VO2max, and Why Not Rønnestad 30/15 Intervals — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Harrison_

## What Is $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ Training (and Why Train It)?

$\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ is the maximal volume rate of oxygen that the cardiovascular and pulmonary systems can transport and deliver to respiring skeletal muscle ($L/\text{min}$ or $\text{mL}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}\cdot\text{min}^{-1}$). 

As established in the Fick equation ($\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max} = \dot{Q}_{\text{max}} \times a\text{-}\bar{v}\text{O}_2\text{ diff}$), skeletal muscle possesses vast excess capacity for peripheral oxygen utilization. Therefore, **maximal cardiac output ($\dot{Q}_{\text{max}}$)**—and specifically **maximal stroke volume ($SV_{\text{max}}$)**—is the ultimate long-term rate limiter of $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$.

```
                 The Cardiovascular Adaptation Objective
                 
  [ High Cadence (100-115+ RPM) ] ──► [ Accelerated Venous Return (Muscle Pump) ]
                                                        │
                                                        ▼
  [ Eccentric Left Ventricular Hypertrophy ] ◄── [ Maximal End-Diastolic Preload ]
  (Serial Sarcomeres -> Expanded SV)
```

### Why Train $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$? ("Raising the Roof")
* **FTP Ceiling:** Functional Threshold Power (FTP) typically sits between $75\%\text{ and }85\%\ \dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$. An athlete cannot elevate their FTP above their $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$.
* **Diminishing Returns of Threshold Work:** If an athlete’s FTP reaches $\sim 85\%\ \dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$, continued threshold or sweet spot grinding yields negligible gains ($<1\text{--}2\text{ W}$).
* **Expanding the Aerobic Ceiling:** A dedicated 3–4 week $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ block expands cardiac stroke volume, "raising the roof" so that subsequent threshold and base training can push FTP higher.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Preload, the Frank-Starling Mechanism, and Eccentric Hypertrophy
* **Preload (Diastolic Filling):** The volume of venous blood returning to the heart that fills and stretches the left ventricle during diastole (End-Diastolic Volume / EDV).
* **The Frank-Starling Law:** Increased diastolic stretch aligns cardiac sarcomeres to optimal contractile lengths and increases calcium sensitivity of troponin C, causing the myocardium to contract with greater force during systole.
* **Eccentric Hypertrophy:** When the left ventricle is repeatedly subjected to maximal preload stretch, costameric integrins transduce mechanical tension into transcriptional signals that add **sarcomeres in series**. This enlarges ventricular internal chamber volume without pathological wall thickening, permanently expanding stroke volume.
* **Why Afterload Fails:** Grinding at low cadences or heavy isometric bracing increases systolic vascular resistance (afterload), which triggers concentric hypertrophy (sarcomeres added in parallel, thickening heart walls without expanding chamber volume).

```
                      Cardiac Remodeling Pathways
                      
  Preload (High Flow / High Cadence)   ──► Eccentric Hypertrophy (Series Sarcomeres) ──► SV & VO2max UP
  Afterload (High Force / Low Cadence)  ──► Concentric Hypertrophy (Parallel)         ──► Wall Thickening Only
```

### 2. Cadence and Hemodynamics (The #1 Training Lever)
In a pivotal study examining trained cyclists pedaling at 200W across 70, 90, and 110 RPM:
* **Stroke Volume Response:** Stroke volume rose from **$114\text{ mL}$ (70 RPM) $\rightarrow$ $130\text{ mL}$ (90 RPM) $\rightarrow$ $145\text{ mL}$ (110 RPM)**—an increase of $+31\text{ mL}$ ($+27\%$) purely from cadence.
* **Cardiac Output Response:** Rose from **$18.2\text{ L/min}$ (70 RPM) $\rightarrow$ $24.5\text{ L/min}$ (110 RPM)** ($+34\%$).
* **Vascular Resistance:** Systemic vascular resistance decreased at higher cadences.
* **The Muscle Pump Mechanism:** High-frequency, rhythmic contractions act as an active peristaltic pump on venous vasculature, overcoming vascular impedance and maximizing diastolic filling without requiring high pedal forces.
* **Motor Unit Sparing:** High cadence relies on smaller, highly oxidative Type I and IIa motor units, preventing the premature recruitment and fatigue of large, glycolytic Type IIx motor units.

```
  Hemodynamic Impact of Cadence at Constant 200W Power:
  ┌───────────────┬────────────────┬────────────────┬───────────────────────────┐
  │ Cadence (RPM) │ Heart Rate     │ Stroke Volume  │ Cardiac Output (Q)        │
  ├───────────────┼────────────────┼────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
  │ 70 RPM        │ 160 BPM        │ 114 mL         │ 18.2 L/min                │
  │ 90 RPM        │ 163 BPM        │ 130 mL         │ 21.0 L/min                │
  │ 110 RPM       │ 169 BPM        │ 145 mL         │ 24.5 L/min (+34%!)        │
  └───────────────┴────────────────┴────────────────┴───────────────────────────┘
```

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### Kolie Moore’s Core $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ Execution Rules

```
                      KOLIE MOORE'S 5 VO2MAX PRINCIPLES
                      
  1. Increase Cadence        --> Ride at 100–115+ RPM (+10–15 above self-selected baseline).
  2. Ignore Power Targets    --> Execute as maximal repeatable efforts (all-out / decaying).
  3. Start Hard              --> First 20–30s at 120–130% of target to ramp VO2 kinetics fast.
  4. Pace by Breathing       --> Target hyperventilation ("gasping like a fish out of water").
  5. Individualize Duration  --> Use 2.5 to 5 min reps; total 15–25 min work per session.
```

### 1. Cadence Prescription
* Target **100 to 115+ RPM** (at least $+10\text{--}15\text{ RPM}$ above self-selected endurance/threshold cadence).
* Accept that mechanical power will be 5–15% lower than at a self-selected cadence; power is secondary to cardiac filling and oxygen flux.

### 2. Pacing: Hard-Start & All-Out Repeatable Execution
* **Do NOT use Erg Mode:** Erg mode fixes resistance, preventing the athlete from going fully maximal and cutting off the respiratory overdrive.
* **Do NOT ride to a fixed % of FTP (e.g. 115–120%):** 
  * High-anaerobic athletes will cruise through 120% FTP without stressing $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$.
  * Low-anaerobic diesel athletes will blow up within 2 minutes.
* **Execution:**
  * **Surge Phase (0–30s):** Start 20–30% harder than sustainable (e.g., 130–150% FTP) to rapidly deplete phosphocreatine and accelerate $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2$ on-kinetics.
  * **Sustained Phase (30s–End):** Settle into maximal repeatable effort. Allow power to decay while keeping cadence pegged $>100\text{ RPM}$ and ventilation maximal.

### 3. Sensory Calibration: Breathing as the Primary Metric
* Within 60–90 seconds, breathing must become ragged and labored (maximal hyperventilation, $\dot{\text{V}}_E > 130\text{--}160\text{ L/min}$).
* If an athlete can speak or breathe through their nose, the interval is ineffective for central cardiac remodeling.

### 4. Interval Durations and Session Architecture
* **Interval Length:** 2.5 to 5.0 minutes.
  * Athletes with high anaerobic capacity: 2.5 to 3.5 minutes (prevents excessive power drop-off and pacing hesitation).
  * Aerobic diesel athletes: 4.0 to 5.0 minutes.
* **Total Time-in-Zone:** 15 to 25 minutes of work per workout (e.g., $5 \times 3\text{ min}$, $5 \times 4\text{ min}$, or $4 \times 5\text{ min}$).
* **Rest Intervals:** 1:1 down to 1:1.5 work-to-rest ratio (e.g., 3–4 minutes easy spinning between 4-minute reps). Rest must be long enough to allow maximal cardiac drive on the subsequent repetition.

## Deconstructing the Rønnestad 30/15 Protocol

The popular Rønnestad (2020) study (*Superior performance improvements in elite cyclists following short intervals vs. effort-matched long intervals*) is widely cited as proving 30/15s are superior to continuous intervals for $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ development. A rigorous physiological critique reveals fatal methodological flaws:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|              CRITIQUE OF RØNNESTAD (2020) 30/15s VS 4x5m STUDY              |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                             |
| 1. The 4x5m "Strawman" Arm:                                                 |
|    • Prescribed at Borg RPE 17.6 ("effort matched")                         |
|    • Actual average power: 338 W (79% Wmax / ~72% VO2max)                   |
|    • For national-level cyclists (VO2max ~72 mL/kg/min), 338 W was AT OR    |
|      BELOW FTP / MLSS! The group did not perform VO2max training.           |
|                                                                             |
| 2. The 30/15s Arm:                                                          |
|    • Actual average power during 30s on: 441 W (94% Wmax / 130% FTP)        |
|    • Recruited massive fast-twitch motor units (500 N force -> 200 N)       |
|    • Trained anaerobic capacity (W') and repeated sprint buffering, not SV. |
|                                                                             |
| 3. Effective Preload Time Comparison:                                       |
|    • 30/15s: ~10–15s of max preload per 30s rep = ~6 min total cardiac time |
|    • 4x5m (Properly Executed): 2.5–3.5 min max preload per rep = 10–14 min  |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. The 4x5-Minute Group Trained Sub-Threshold
* In trained cyclists with $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max} \approx 72\text{ mL/kg/min}$ ($5.4\text{ L/min}$), FTP is typically 360–390W.
* The 4x5m cohort averaged **338 Watts** ($\sim 72\%\ \dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$). Four bouts of 5 minutes at sub-threshold power provide zero stimulus for eccentric cardiac hypertrophy or $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ expansion.
* The study compared a maximal intermittent protocol (441W on / 0W off) against an under-paced, sub-threshold continuous protocol.

### 2. Anaerobic Capacity vs. Aerobic Adaptation
* The 30/15s group improved their 20-minute post-test power by $+15\text{ W}$ ($343\text{ to }358\text{ W}$).
* Their measured $\dot{\text{V}}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ only increased by $0.13\text{ L/min}$ (which accounts for at most $\sim 6\text{ W}$ of aerobic power).
* The remaining $+9\text{ W}$ of performance gain came entirely from **anaerobic work capacity ($W'$)**, lactate buffering kinetics, and neuromuscular sprint training—not structural cardiovascular remodeling.

### 3. Cardiac Stimulus Efficiency
* **30/15s:** Over 24 minutes of intermittent work ($3 \times 9.5\text{ min}$ sets), the heart only reaches peak preload in the final 10–15 seconds of each 30s bout, yielding **$\sim 6\text{ minutes}$ of total preload stimulus** while demanding 150 kJ of high-force muscular work ($500\text{ N}$ pedal forces).
* **Continuous 4x5m (Max Effort):** Yields **$10\text{--}14\text{ minutes}$ of continuous maximal preload** at half the neuromuscular torque, producing superior cardiac adaptation with less peripheral fatigue.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall / Misconception | Physiological Reality | Practical Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Using Erg Mode for $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ intervals.** | Erg mode clamps resistance. As the athlete fatigues, cadence drops, torque spikes, and the cardiovascular system unloads while legs get crushed. | Switch to Level / Resistance mode. Maintain cadence $>100\text{ RPM}$ and allow power to fluctuate naturally. |
| **Using 30/15s to build long-term $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$.** | 30/15s build repeated sprint ability and $W'$, but provide insufficient sustained cardiac preload for eccentric ventricular hypertrophy. | Reserve 30/15s for race-specific preparation (criteriums, cyclocross); use continuous 3–5 min intervals for $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ blocks. |
| **Judging interval success by average power.** | Power drops across a $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ workout as anaerobic reserves deplete; oxygen uptake and stroke volume remain at $100\%$. | Judge workout quality by time spent at maximal ventilation and high heart rate, not flat wattage. |
| **Prescribing 5x5m at a fixed 120% FTP.** | 120% FTP is an arbitrary population average; it is too easy for sprinters and impossible for diesels. | Prescribe all $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ intervals as **open-ended, maximal repeatable efforts**. |
