# Coaching the Process, Individualization vs. The Average Fallacy, & Racecraft Physics — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Cory Lockwood (Perspectives #5)_

---

## What Is "Coaching the Process"?

In endurance sport, "Coaching the Process" refers to an athlete-centered, iterative methodology that prioritizes daily physiological execution, technical mastery, and qualitative feedback over short-term outcome fixation or rigid, static training plans. 

Competitive cycling is fundamentally a sport of high failure rates and tactical unpredictability. An outcome-only orientation (e.g., measuring success solely by podium finishes or arbitrary power targets) creates psychological fragility and vulnerability to overtraining. Conversely, process-oriented training treats every microcycle as an ongoing physiological experiment—refining movement mechanics, pacing strategies, recovery protocols, and metabolic adaptations.

```
                  Outcome-Driven vs. Process-Driven Paradigms
                  
  Outcome-Driven Model (Rigid):
  [Fixed Target / Metric] ──► [Force Prescription] ──► [Fatigue / Plateau] ──► [Frustration / Overtraining]
  
  Process-Driven Model (Iterative):
  [Physiological Intent] ──► [Execute & Monitor RPE/HR/Power] ──► [Feedback Loop] ──► [Iterative Adjustment]
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms & Coaching Principles

### 1. The Fallacy of the Average & Individual Heterogeneity

A foundational error in endurance coaching is applying group mean responses from scientific literature directly to an individual athlete without calibration:

- **Inter-Individual Adaptive Variance:** In any standardized endurance training protocol, the mean outcome (e.g., $+8\%$ increase in $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ or threshold power) masks a spectrum ranging from negative/non-responders to super-responders.
- **Phenotypic Specificity:** An athlete with high baseline fast-twitch motor unit recruitment requires distinct recovery kinetics and work-to-rest ratios compared to a slow-twitch, high-durability diesel athlete. Prescribing uniform percentage-based power targets fails because metabolic strain at a given percentage of FTP differs widely.
- **The "Copy-Paste" Champion Fallacy:** Elite athletes frequently succeed due to exceptional genetic predispositions, high baseline capillary density, or high mitochondrial responsiveness. Applying an elite athlete's personal training volume and intensity directly to an amateur or developing rider leads to maladaptation, excessive autonomic strain, and breakdown.

### 2. Pathophysiology of the Overtraining "Snowball"

Overtraining Syndrome (OTS) and severe non-functional overreaching (NFOR) do not occur overnight; they develop through a compounding series of unaddressed micro-stresses:

1. **Subclinical Fatigue & Autonomic Imbalance:** Elevated sympathetic drive and suppressed parasympathetic reactivation reduce sleep quality, resting heart rate variability (HRV), and muscle glycogen resynthesis rates.
2. **Perceptual-Physiological Decoupling:** When an athlete suppresses subjective signals (e.g., normal endurance power feeling like threshold RPE) out of fear or blind faith in a rigid training plan, mechanical output is maintained through compensatory, inefficient motor unit recruitment.
3. **Mitochondrial & Endocrine Exhaustion:** Chronic training without adequate glycogen replenishment and systemic rest leads to blunted catecholamine sensitivity, glycogen depletion, systemic inflammation, and eventual neuromuscular collapse.
4. **The Long Tail of Recovery:** Once systemic overtraining occurs, recovery is not a matter of a few rest days; hormonal and cellular restoration frequently requires months of active rest and sub-threshold rehabilitation.

### 3. Energy Conservation Physics & Cornering Mechanics in Criteriums

In criterium racing, raw functional threshold power ($\text{W/kg}$) is secondary to kinetic energy preservation and acceleration reduction:

- **Kinetic Energy Formula:** $E_k = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$. Any unnecessary braking requires active metabolic resynthesis of kinetic energy via anaerobic and suprathreshold metabolic pathways.
- **The Late Apex Line:** Taking a wide entry, braking early before the turn, and hitting a late apex maximizes the turn radius ($r$) and exit velocity ($v$), where lateral acceleration $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$.
- **Metabolic Savings:** 
  - *Poor Line (Square-off/Early Apex):* Hard deceleration into apex $\rightarrow$ out-of-the-saddle sprint at $800\text{--}1000\text{ W}$ for $5\text{--}8\text{ seconds}$ $\times\ 40\text{ corners} = \text{Severe W' depletion & lactic acidosis}$.
  - *Optimized Line (Late Apex/Momentum Carry):* Smooth high-radius arc $\rightarrow$ seated pedaling out of apex at $400\text{--}500\text{ W}$ for $2\text{--}3\text{ seconds} = \text{Purely aerobic / minimal W' usage}$.
- **Tire Contact Patch & Road Interface:** Reading pavement micro-textures (chip-seal vs. polished asphalt, temperature changes, off-camber sections) and setting appropriate tire pressure prevents tire deflection and micro-sliding, preserving traction and rider confidence at maximum lean angles.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The Iterative Coaching Framework

When prescribing training blocks, coaches and self-coached athletes must implement a closed feedback loop:

- **Individual Baseline Calibration:** Do not assume standard percentages (e.g., $120\%$ FTP for $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ or $90\%$ for Sweetspot) represent the same metabolic state across athletes. Calibrate workouts using internal metrics (HR, RPE, breathing dynamics) alongside external metrics (power, duration).
- **The "Never Add Power Mid-Block" Rule:** During a threshold progression block, keep target power constant and extend duration/TTE (Time-to-Exhaustion). Do not prematurely inflate target wattage based on perceived mid-block fitness jumps; verify threshold shifts in post-block testing or steady-state validation sessions.
- **Open Feedback Channels:** An athlete must feel empowered to challenge workout structure or report systemic fatigue without fear of appearing uncommitted. If target power requires maximal psychological survival effort during a standard endurance or tempo session, the session must be truncated immediately.

---

### 2. Technical Skill Drills & Race Preparation

To develop cornering efficiency and save kilojoules:

1. **Figure-8 & Radius Drills:** Practice progressive lean angles, counter-steering, and outside-pedal weighting in empty parking lots on both asphalt and concrete surfaces.
2. **Seated Cornering Exits:** Conduct mock criterium laps with the explicit constraint of **never leaving the saddle** upon corner exit. This forces riders to optimize entry lines and carry exit momentum.
3. **Road Surface Audits:** During course recons, map out road furniture (manhole covers, painted lines, pavement seams, gravel runoffs) to establish primary and emergency bailout lines for pack positioning.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall / Red Flag | Underlying Error | Physiological / Practical Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **"Secret Sauce" / Magic Bullets** | Claiming proprietary interval ratios or shortcuts to bypass base volume. | Fails to build foundational capillary and mitochondrial density; leads to rapid plateau. |
| **Jargon Without Scientific Basis** | Using complicated pseudoscientific terminology without physiological mechanisms. | Masks lack of coaching competence and prevents athlete understanding. |
| **Ignoring Fatigue Signals** | Forcing high-intensity sessions when basic endurance pace elicits threshold RPE. | Induces Non-Functional Overreaching (NFOR) and severe autonomic exhaustion. |
| **Inappropriate Workout Cloning** | Giving a sprinter’s or elite rider’s high-torque/high-wattage intervals to a developing diesel athlete. | Leads to acute musculoskeletal injury, extreme fatigue, and failed interval targets. |
| **Aggressive Power Bumping** | Raising threshold wattage targets mid-block because a workout felt slightly easy. | Converts a sustainable aerobic threshold session into an unsustainable glycolytic burn. |
