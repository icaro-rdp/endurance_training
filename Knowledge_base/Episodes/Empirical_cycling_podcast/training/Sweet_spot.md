# Sweet Spot Training — Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kelly Moore & James McKay_

---

## What Is Sweet Spot?

Sweet spot is a training intensity sitting **~10% below FTP**, defined as **90% of FTP**. No range needed for steady-state work — pick the number and ride it. For over/unders, a tight ±5–10 watt window around the under target is acceptable.

**Physiologically:** adaptations are essentially identical to threshold training, with meaningfully lower acute fatigue and internal strain for the same external load.

---

## How to Find Your Sweet Spot

| Method        | Value                                                              |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Power         | 90% of FTP (fixed, no adjustment)                                  |
| RPE           | ~6–7 out of 10                                                     |
| Talk test     | Failing it — noticeably labored, but not threshold-level suffering |
| Duration feel | You know exhaustion is coming, unlike tempo where it's elusive     |

> If your FTP is overestimated, your "sweet spot" is actually threshold. If intervals feel unusually hard, check your FTP before adjusting the prescription.

---

## How Long Can You Hold It? (TTE at Sweet Spot)

This is **highly individual** — no reliable formula from FTP-TTE exists. Rough benchmarks:

| Training Level     | Sweet Spot TTE                                    |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Untrained / new    | 40–60 min                                         |
| Moderately trained | ~90 min                                           |
| Well trained       | 1.5–2 hours                                       |
| Elite / World Tour | 3+ hours (with fueling)                           |
| Ultra athletes     | Limited more by daylight and food than physiology |

**Key principle:** TTE at sweet spot increases substantially with training volume and training age — more than at threshold.

## When to Use Sweet Spot in a Training Plan

### Good use cases

- **Early season / base building** — high benefit, low fatigue cost while fitness is rebuilding
- **Coming back from injury or offseason** — safe place to accumulate load
- **Time-crunched athletes with weekend volume** — do threshold midweek, sweet spot in long weekend rides
- **Mid-season maintenance** — between races, lower cost than threshold
- **3rd intensity day in a threshold block** — less interference with other sessions
- **Over/unders** — sweet spot is the ideal intensity zone to structure surges around
- **Openers** — 10–20 min of sweet spot before a race is underrated for warming up without generating fatigue
- **Ultra / endurance athletes** — when you cannot accumulate the race-specific volume, sweet spot is the best surrogate

### Poor use cases

- **Time-crunched athletes with only 1 hour available** — not enough time to reach meaningful TTE; go to threshold instead
- **Athletes who need VO2max or anaerobic development** — wrong tool
- **As the sole training modality long-term** — gains plateau; must be part of a complete program

## How to Prescribe It

### Session structure

- **Minimum useful interval length:** 20 minutes (15 min is too short for well-trained athletes)
- **Practical formats:**
  - 2×20, 2×30, 2×45, 3×20, 3×30, 4×30 — all roughly equivalent in stimulus
  - 1×60 to 1×120 for advanced athletes
  - Over/unders built around 90% FTP as the "under" target
- **Breaking intervals up is fine** — 6×20 min still accumulates meaningful time-in-zone; you are not cheating

### Weekly volume / session progression

Think **per-session, not per-week**. Add ~5 minutes of time-in-zone per session as a progression marker. Per-session maximum for most coached athletes: **2–2.5 hours**. Absolute ceiling: ~3 hours (rarely prescribed).

Avoid doing sweet spot 4–5×/week to exhaustion — fatigue accumulates faster than it appears, and session quality degrades without clear signal.

### Frequency guidelines

| Frequency              | Notes                                              |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 1×/week                | Fine as a long-ride anchor; progress session TTE   |
| 2×/week                | Solid sweet spot focus; monitor recovery carefully |
| 3+×/week to exhaustion | High fatigue risk; TTE data becomes unreliable     |

---

## Key Metrics to Track Progress

| Metric                  | What it tells you                                         |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **TTE at sweet spot**   | Primary progress marker — if it goes up, you're improving |
| **RPE at target power** | Same watts feeling easier = adaptation                    |
| **FTP**                 | May or may not move from sweet spot alone                 |
| **Race performance**    | Ultimate test — did the block solve the actual limiter?   |

> If TTE is going up session to session → stay the course.
> If TTE has stalled for 3–4 sessions and FTP isn't moving → consider shifting to threshold or VO2max work.

## Fueling Sweet Spot Sessions

Sweet spot burns through glycogen at a high rate. Fueling strategy matters more here than at lower intensities.

| Priority                         | Recommendation                                                                           |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Muscle glycogen (day before)** | Most important — eat adequate carbs 24–48h before                                        |
| **On-bike fueling**              | Critical for sessions >60–75 min; supports liver glycogen and prevents blood sugar crash |
| **Post-session recovery**        | Fueling during the ride accelerates recovery vs. eating everything afterward             |

Practical target: treat long sweet spot sessions like race-day fueling practice — high carbohydrate intake per hour.

---

## Sweet Spot vs. Other Intensities

| Intensity      | Relative fatigue | Time to exhaust well-trained | Best for                                          |
| -------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Endurance      | Low              | Hours                        | Base, recovery, volume                            |
| **Sweet spot** | **Moderate**     | **1.5–3 hr**                 | **Muscular endurance, early season, maintenance** |
| Threshold      | Moderate-high    | 30–60 min                    | FTP development, time-efficient                   |
| VO2max         | High             | 6–15 min                     | Ceiling raising, anaerobic capacity               |

## Pros and Cons Summary

**Pros**

- Strong initial gains without high fatigue
- Accomplishable even if FTP is slightly overestimated
- Ideal for over/under structuring
- Easy to insert as 3rd intensity day
- Good maintenance tool between races
- Underrated for openers

**Cons**

- Time-inefficient for short training windows
- Cannot be the only training modality — gains plateau
- Far enough from FTP that some athletes lose feel for whether threshold is improving
- Recovery cost underestimated if done at high frequency/volume

---

## Decision Checklist Before Prescribing Sweet Spot

1. Does this athlete need **muscular endurance** as a primary adaptation?
2. Do they have **time** to reach meaningful TTE in a session?
3. Is sweet spot **physiologically accessible** right now (not too fatigued, FTP accurate)?
4. What else is in the week — does this fit without crowding threshold or VO2 sessions?
5. Does the **goal race** actually reward this adaptation?

If all five are yes → prescribe it. If time is the limiting factor → move to threshold. If the goal race is short and explosive → consider whether you're picking the right tool.
