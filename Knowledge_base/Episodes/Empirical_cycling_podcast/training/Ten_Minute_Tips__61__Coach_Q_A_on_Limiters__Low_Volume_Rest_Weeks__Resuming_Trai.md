# Ten Minute Tips #61: Coach Q&A on Limiters, Low-Volume Deloads, Break Resumption, & Training Camps — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Megan, Gediminas, Alex, Erica_

---

## What Is the Coach Q&A on Training Optimization?

This coach roundtable addresses critical practical coaching questions across several core training pillars:
1. **Limiter Gap Analysis:** Differentiating between physiological power deficits and non-power skill/tactical limiters.
2. **Fitness vs. Race-Specific Training:** Balancing the pursuit of broad physiological numbers against event-specific demands.
3. **Deload Protocols for Time-Crunched Athletes:** How to structure recovery when baseline weekly volume is already low (4–8 hours).
4. **Resuming Training Post-Break:** Safe, effective re-entry progressions after illness, travel, or off-season periods.
5. **Executing High-Volume Training Camps:** Rules for scaling volume spikes without inducing non-functional overreaching.
6. **Under-Optimized Fundamentals:** Daily nutrition, sleep hygiene, and the physiology of continuous vs. sporadic intervals.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Limiter Analysis: Physiological vs. Non-Power Factors
* **Power-Duration Disconnect:** Power curves only capture static outputs. In mass-start racing, performance is governed by kinetic energy conservation: cornering lines, drafting distance (a 1-meter gap increases drag significantly), and cadence acceleration kinetics out of turns.
* **Return on Investment (ROI):** Fixing a physiological limiter (e.g., building massive sprint mass for a climber) carries significant opportunity costs. Transforming a climber into a sprinter compromises power-to-weight ratio on target climbs.

### 2. The Physiology of Continuous vs. Sporadic Time-in-Zone
* **Sustained Cellular Signaling:** Continuous threshold intervals (e.g., $4 \times 15\text{ min}$) maintain continuous glycolytic flux, sustained lactate clearance, and prolonged metabolic stress (AMPK, SIRT1, PGC-1$\alpha$ signaling).
* **The "Sporadic Interval" Flaw:** Spreading 60 minutes of threshold power randomly across a 4-hour ride (e.g., 2 minutes hard, 10 minutes easy) allows muscle phosphocreatine, intracellular pH, and oxygen kinetics to reset between efforts. The body never enters the sustained oxidative equilibrium required to extend muscular endurance (TTE).

```
   [ Continuous Threshold Block ]                    [ Sporadic Surges ]
 sustained oxidative flux & metabolite stress        intermittent surges with full recovery
                     │                                                │
                     ▼                                                ▼
 Robust PGC-1α / Mitochondrial Adaptation             Neuromuscular Touch / Sub-Adaptation
```

### 3. Scaling Volume: The 150–200% Rule for Camps
* **Structural Durability:** Connective tissue and muscular endurance adapt slowly. A sudden surge in training hours must be executed strictly at low glycolytic intensity (Zone 2).
* **The "Volume + Intensity" Trap:** Combining a 200% volume surge with high-intensity VO2max or threshold intervals causes profound autonomic exhaustion and delayed recovery lasting multiple weeks.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Rest Weeks for Low-Volume Athletes (4–8 Hours/Week)
* **Maintain Volume, Drop Intensity:** Keep total riding time consistent (e.g., 5–6 hours), but convert all sessions into easy Zone 2 / active recovery spins.
* **Reduce Mental Fatigue:** Time-crunched athletes expend significant cognitive energy squeezing rides into tight schedules. Clear the calendar, remove rigid power targets, and allow unstructured riding.

### 2. Returning to Training After a Break (Compressed Progression)
Apply a compressed "micro-recapitulation" over 7–14 days:

```
[ Phase 1: Days 1–3 ]
Easy Zone 2 Endurance (Re-establish pedaling mechanics and blood plasma volume)
       │
       ▼
[ Phase 2: Days 4–6 ]
Short Openers / Strides (2–3x 30–60s surges @ threshold/VO2 to restore neuromuscular sharpness)
       │
       ▼
[ Phase 3: Days 7–10 ]
Auto-Regulated Threshold (e.g., 2x15m by RPE; allow watts to be 10–20W below pre-break baseline)
       │
       ▼
[ Phase 4: Day 11+ ]
Full Progressive Structured Training
```

### 3. Guidelines for Big Training Weeks / Training Camps

| Baseline Weekly Volume | Camp Target Volume | Intensity Prescription | Nutrition & Recovery Protocol |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **8–10 hours** | 15–18 hours | Strictly Zone 2 endurance; limit climbing tempo. | Pre-stock lodging with dense carbohydrate foods; 60–90g carbs/hr on bike. |
| **12–15 hours** | 20–25 hours | Zone 2 endurance; max 1 unstructured hill-climb test day. | Immediate post-ride recovery fueling (high-calorie meals, cereal, ice cream). |
| **18–20+ hours** | 28–32 hours | High-volume base; omit all high-intensity interval sets. | Prioritize 9+ hours sleep; minimize off-bike walking/sightseeing. |

### 4. Correcting Critical Under-Optimizations
* **Avoid Daytime Caloric Deficits:** Never skip lunch or attempt fasting prior to afternoon/evening workouts. Chronic within-day energy deficits elevate cortisol and impair interval power.
* **Active Mental Engagement:** Rather than mentally checking out during painful VO2max or threshold sets, focus intently on breathing cadence, pedal stroke smoothness, and pain tolerance.
* **Travel Sleep Hygiene:** Always travel with earplugs, eye masks, and control ambient room temperature (cooler room temperatures significantly enhance deep sleep architecture).

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

* **Attempting to Fix Every Limiter Simultaneously:** Trying to increase 5-second sprint, 5-minute VO2max, and 60-minute FTP within the same training block.
* **Treating Deloads as Complete Inactivity for Low-Volume Riders:** Taking 7 full days off from a 4-hour weekly routine leads to loss of neuromuscular groove and sluggishness.
* **Pushing Target Watts Too Early After Illness:** Trying to match pre-illness FTP numbers immediately, causing immediate relapse or prolonged systemic fatigue.
* **Treating Training Camps as Interval Races:** Smashing every climb at full gas during a high-volume week, resulting in severe overreaching.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Athlete Context / Dilemma | Core Root Cause | Prescribed Coaching Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Athlete getting dropped in crits despite high FTP** | Poor drafting, cornering, or low cadence mashing. | Review GoPro race footage; practice high-cadence spin-ups out of corners; pack skill drills. |
| **Time-crunched athlete (<6 hrs/wk) feels burnt out** | High life/work stress competing with rigid cycling schedule. | Maintain volume; drop interval intensity; prescribe 2–3 flexible unstructured spins. |
| **Returning from 10 days of illness/travel** | Transient loss of blood volume and neuromuscular coordination. | 2–3 days easy endurance $\rightarrow$ 1 session of light openers $\rightarrow$ auto-regulated threshold. |
| **Athlete has 1 open week for training camp** | Opportunity for massive aerobic stimulus. | Scale volume by 150–200% strictly in Zone 2; fuel aggressively with dense carbs. |
| **Athlete attempts sporadic surges for threshold fitness** | Insufficient continuous cellular signaling. | Consolidate into structured, progressive continuous blocks ($3\times 15\text{m} \rightarrow 2\times 25\text{m}$). |
