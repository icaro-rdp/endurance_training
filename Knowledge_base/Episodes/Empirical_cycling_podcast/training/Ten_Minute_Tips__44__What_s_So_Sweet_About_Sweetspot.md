# Ten Minute Tips #44: What's So Sweet About Sweetspot — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & James McKay (Ten Minute Tips #44)_

---

## What Is Sweet Spot Training?

**Sweet Spot** is an intensity domain located just below the Functional Threshold Power (FTP) / Critical Power (CP) boundary, operationally defined as **$88\%\text{ to }92\%\text{ of FTP}$** (commonly centered at **$90\%\text{ FTP}$**).

* **The Theoretical Proposition:** Sweet Spot is popularized as the optimal intersection of physiological adaptation (mitochondrial biogenesis, plasma volume expansion, capillary density) and manageable physiological strain, allowing athletes to accumulate high training stress scores (TSS) with lower recovery costs than threshold riding.
* **The Empirical Reality:** While Sweet Spot reduces acute metabolic acidosis ($H^+$ and $P_i$ accumulation) compared to FTP, it is **not a magic 1:1 time substitute** for threshold work. Achieving an equivalent adaptive stimulus at $90\%\text{ FTP}$ requires extending the duration of the work intervals significantly closer to the athlete's sub-threshold Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE).

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Metabolic Strain: Sweet Spot vs. Threshold
* **Lactate Steady State:** At $90\%\text{ FTP}$, the rate of blood lactate appearance remains below or at maximal lactate steady state (MLSS), allowing oxidative Type I and fatigue-resistant Type IIa fibers to continuously oxidize circulating lactate via **MCT1 transporters**.
* **High Glycogen Flux:** Even though metabolic acidosis is lower, the absolute carbohydrate oxidation rate is exceptionally high ($2.5\text{ to }3.5\text{ g/min}$). Total kilojoule (kJ) burn during long Sweet Spot blocks ($>60\text{--}90\text{ minutes TIZ}$) severely depletes intramuscular glycogen.
* **Autonomic Balance:** Because power stays below the second ventilatory threshold ($VT2$), sympathetic nervous system overdrive is attenuated compared to supra-threshold VO2max or intensive threshold efforts, reducing systemic central nervous system (CNS) lethargy.

```
Metabolic Stress Continuum:
Zone 2 (Endurance)   <----->   Sweet Spot (88–92%)   <----->   Zone 4 (FTP 95–100%)
Low Acidosis                   Zero Acidosis Runaway            Lactate Steady State Limit
Low Glycogen Depletion         High Glycogen Burn               Max Sustained Glycolytic Rate
Low Central Strain             Moderate Central Strain          High Autonomic/CNS Fatigue
```

### 2. Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE) Kinetics at Sweet Spot
* **Untrained Athletes:** Can typically sustain continuous Sweet Spot for **40 to 60 minutes**.
* **Trained Cyclists:** Regularly achieve **90 to 120 minutes** of cumulative Sweet Spot in a single session.
* **Elite / WorldTour Cyclists:** Can accumulate **$150\text{ to }180+\text{ minutes}$** of Sweet Spot work in a single long ride, limited primarily by liver/muscle glycogen depletion and heat stress rather than cardiovascular output.

### 3. Adaptation Equivalency: State Function vs. Path Function
Endurance adaptations (mitochondrial volume, capillary density, enzymatic upregulation) behave as **state functions**: the body adapts to the total accumulated time at elevated oxidative flux near threshold, regardless of whether the session was chunked into $2 \times 45\text{ min}$, $3 \times 30\text{ min}$, or $4 \times 20\text{ min}$.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The Single-Session TIZ Progression Model
Do not program Sweet Spot as short, repeated weekly micro-doses ($15\text{ minutes}$ every day). Progress the duration of a **single weekly session** toward maximal sustainable Time-in-Zone:

```
Mesocycle Sweet Spot Progression Architecture (1–2 Sessions / Week):
├── Level 1 (Introductory / Return to Build):
│   └── 3 x 15 min or 2 x 20 min @ 90% FTP (40–45 min TIZ) — 5 min recoveries.
├── Level 2 (Intermediate Capacity):
│   └── 3 x 25 min or 2 x 35 min @ 90% FTP (70–75 min TIZ) — 6–8 min recoveries.
├── Level 3 (Advanced Durability / Ultra Prep):
│   └── 3 x 30 min or 2 x 45 min @ 90% FTP (90 min TIZ) — 8–10 min recoveries.
└── Level 4 (Elite Extensive Build):
    └── 1 x 90 min continuous or 2 x 60 min @ 90% FTP (90–120 min TIZ).
```

### 2. Pacing Rules: The "Feel vs. Number" Rule
* **Strict Anchor:** Peg the target at **$90\%\text{ FTP}$**. If target FTP is 300W, Sweet Spot is 270W.
* **RPE Calibration:** Sweet Spot should feel like a **$6\text{ to }7 / 10\text{ RPE}$** (purposeful concentration, rhythmic breathing, conversation possible in short sentences).
* **If Struggling Early:** If $90\%\text{ FTP}$ feels like an $8.5\text{--}9 / 10\text{ RPE}$ within the first 10 minutes, the baseline FTP is set too high or the athlete is carrying acute autonomic fatigue.

### 3. Fueling Guidelines for Long Sub-Threshold Sessions
Due to the intense glycolytic flux at 90% FTP:
* **Pre-Workout:** Ensure liver and muscle glycogen stores are topped up via high-carbohydrate meals ($2\text{--}3\text{g/kg}$) 2–4 hours prior.
* **On-Bike Consumption:** Ingest **$60\text{ to }90+\text{ g/hr}$** of mixed carbohydrates (maltodextrin + fructose in 1:0.8 ratio) during any session exceeding 60 minutes TIZ.
* **Post-Workout Recovery:** Immediate carbohydrate ($1.0\text{--}1.2\text{ g/kg/hr}$) and protein intake ($0.3\text{--}0.4\text{ g/kg}$) to replenish depleted muscle glycogen before the next training day.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The "Time-Crunched Shortcut" Fallacy:** Marketing often frames Sweet Spot as the ideal tool for cyclists with 4–6 hours per week. In reality, a 45-minute Sweet Spot ride fails to achieve the high TIZ required for deep aerobic adaptation while simultaneously creating too much fatigue to perform high-quality VO2max or maximal sprint sessions.

1. **Treating Sweet Spot as an All-Year Default:** Performing Sweet Spot across every mesocycle without dedicated polarized VO2max blocks or pure threshold extensions leads to a physiological ceiling plateau.
2. **The "Gray Zone" Trap:** Riding every endurance ride at low Sweet Spot ($80\text{--}85\%\text{ FTP}$) turns easy recovery/base volume into fatiguing tempo, degrading interval quality on hard days.
3. **Pacing with an Inflated Ramp-Test FTP:** If an athlete's FTP is overestimated by 15–20W via a short ramp test, prescribing "Sweet Spot" actually forces the athlete into true threshold or supra-threshold power, causing premature interval failure.
