# Perspectives #37: Practical Performance Psychology — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Billy Ryan_

---

## What Is Practical Performance Psychology?

Practical performance psychology in endurance sports is the systematic development of behavioral and attentional skills that allow athletes to execute physical capacity under high pressure, severe fatigue, and unpredictable competitive environments. 

Rather than relying on legacy "psychological skills training" (such as forced positive affirmations, emotional suppression, or mechanistic thought-stopping), modern performance psychology leverages **Acceptance and Commitment Training (ACT)**. ACT approaches internal experiences (thoughts, autonomic sensations, anxiety, fatigue) contextually: it does not seek to diagnose or eliminate negative mental states, but rather trains psychological flexibility—the capacity to stay in contact with the present moment and execute goal-directed actions aligned with core values, even when internal signals are intensely uncomfortable.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
Traditional CBT / Mechanistic Model:
[Negative Thought / Sensation] ──> [Attempt to Suppress / Rationalize] ──> [Attentional Drain & Tug-of-War] ──> [Impaired Performance]

Modern ACT / Contextual Model:
[Negative Thought / Sensation] ──> [Notice & "Drop the Rope" (Defusion)] ──> [Attentional Field Open to Task] ──> [Optimal Physical Execution]
```

### 1. Mechanistic vs. Contextual Psychological Frameworks
- **Mechanistic Models (Classic CBT / Affirmations):** Assumes thoughts and emotions directly drive physical behavior in a linear chain ($Thought \to Emotion \to Action$). If a negative thought arises ("I can't hold this pace"), the athlete is instructed to dispute it, replace it with positive self-talk, or force relaxation. In high-stakes competition, this triggers an internal "tug-of-war" that consumes working memory and central attentional capacity.
- **Contextual Models (ACT / Behavioral Functionalism):** Treats thoughts and emotions as biological indicators and historical learned responses rather than literal commands. Behavior is evaluated by **workability** (does this action move the athlete toward the physiological and tactical objective?). The internal narrative does not need to be positive for the neuromuscular system to produce target wattage.

### 2. Attentional Bandwidth, Central Governor, and RPE
- **Central Attentional Limits:** The prefrontal cortex possesses finite bandwidth. Engaging in cognitive debates over pain or doubt drains executive function, elevating subjective Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE) at a given metabolic rate.
- **Physiological Arousal vs. Attentional Calm:** High-level performance does not require physiological relaxation (parasympathetic dominance). The autonomic nervous system is heavily sympathetically driven during hard racing (elevated heart rate, catecholamine surge, hyperventilation). "Calm" in elite athletes refers strictly to **attentional flexibility**—an uncluttered cognitive field that responds to external cues without being hijacked by internal distress.

### 3. The "Rigid Flexibility" Paradox in Endurance Athletes
- **Physical vs. Emotional Capacity:** Highly conditioned endurance athletes often exhibit immense physical tolerance (willingness to accumulate high blood lactate, severe glycogen depletion, and extreme muscular burn).
- **The Emotional Trap:** These same athletes frequently possess minimal tolerance for emotional vulnerability (fear of failure, embarrassment of undertraining, anxiety over skipping a planned interval during acute injury). This leads to destructive overtraining: pushing through an acute tendon pain or illness because they cannot tolerate the psychological discomfort of resting.

### 4. Thoughts as Indicators, Not Directives
- Internal thoughts ("I'm dropped," "My legs are dead") function like dashboard warning lights in an automobile:
  - Some lights indicate real mechanical failure requiring an adjustment (e.g., severe localized sharp joint pain, systemic bonking).
  - Many lights are simply baseline background chatter generated by evolutionary threat-detection circuitry.
- **Defusion ("Treating the Mind as a Separate Entity"):** Acknowledging thoughts ("Thank you, mind, for trying to protect me") creates meta-cognitive distance, preventing the athlete from identifying with the thought ($I\text{ am a failure} \to I\text{ notice the thought that I might fail}$).

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Core Psychological Skills Framework

| Tool / Skill | Theoretical Concept | Practical Execution Protocol |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **"Dropping the Rope"** | Cognitive Defusion | When trapped in an internal argument ("You can't hold this breakaway"), visualize letting go of the tug-of-war rope. Refuse to provide counter-arguments. Return eyes to the rear wheel ahead or cadence display. |
| **Sensory Grounding (5+2 Senses)** | Present-Moment Contact | When pre-race panic or disorientation spikes, systematically identify inputs across the 5 external senses (sight, sound, touch, smell, taste) plus the 2 internal senses (**Thinking** and **Feeling**). Labeling internal states as sensory inputs strips them of threat value. |
| **T.E.A.M. Protocol** | Emotion Regulation | **T**reat **E**motions **A**s **M**essengers. Ask: "What is this anxiety or dread attempting to highlight?" (e.g., fear of undertraining, desire to perform well). Acknowledge the message without shooting the messenger or altering the training script. |
| **The Expanded "I" (Self-as-Context)** | Identity De-coupling | On paper, draw numerous small circles representing roles (cyclist, parent, professional, student, friend). Draw one large enclosing circle representing the conscious observer containing all of them. Prevents a poor race from destroying global self-worth. |
| **DNAV Model (Adolescents & Adults)** | Functional Behavioral Architecture | Utilize the 4 behavioral modes: **D**iscoverer (trying novel tactics/lines), **N**oticer (observing physical sensations non-judgmentally), **A**dvisor (evaluating the inner critic), **V**aluer (aligning actions with why the sport matters). |

### 2. Managing Pre-Race Anxiety and "Butterflies"

```
[Pre-Race Autonomic Surge (Elevated HR, Nausea, Shaking)]
  │
  ├─► Mechanistic Failure Mode: "I am terrified. I must calm down and think positive."
  │     └─► Struggle increases cortisol, elevates RPE, induces panic.
  │
  └─► ACT Functional Mode: "My sympathetic nervous system is priming fuel delivery."
        └─► Label as physiological readiness / excitement.
        └─► Lighten grip on handlebars; focus on pre-race nutrition and warm-up checklist.
```

- **Re-framing Physiological Arousal:** Shaking, elevated heart rate, and gastrointestinal butterflies are identical between terror and high excitement. They represent evolutionary sympathetic priming for physical exertion.
- **Physical Bar Grip Cue:** High cognitive anxiety manifests as "death-gripping" handlebars or clenching facial muscles. Athletes should use physical relaxation of the hands/shoulders as a direct biofeedback cue to hold competitive outcomes lightly.

---

### 3. Microcycle Rest-Week and Injury Management

- **The Rest-Week Anxiety Surge:** When physical training volume drops during deload or taper microcycles, athletes experience sudden psychological voids, often triggering catastrophic thoughts ("I'm losing all my fitness").
- **Prescription:**
  1. Mandate that athletes treat resting as an active, high-discipline training modality.
  2. Forbid using daily "feelings of readiness" to alter a deload prescription (e.g., hammering on a recovery spin because the legs feel fresh).
  3. Enforce external social and identity connection outside of cycling during rest weeks to reduce total allostatic fixation on the bike.

---

### 4. Post-Race De-escalation Protocol for Coaches and Athletes

1. **The 60-Minute "No-Problem-Solving" Rule:** Immediately post-race, the athlete's prefrontal cortex is metabolically depleted and flooded with raw emotion ("race brain"). Coaches must strictly listen and validate feelings without offering tactical critiques or metric corrections.
2. **Delayed Objective Metric Review:** Conduct power file and tactical analysis 6–24 hours later when emotional reactivity has dissipated. Separate subjective emotional distress from objective mechanical execution (e.g., setting a 20-minute power PR despite finishing in the main peloton).

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall | Mechanism of Failure | Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Toxic Positivity & Forced Affirmations** | Repeating "I am fast and cannot be dropped" creates cognitive dissonance when pain spikes, triggering deeper psychological collapse. | Replace positive affirmations with reality acceptance: "This hurts immensely, and I have the capacity to pedal through it." |
| **The "Rigid Flexibility" Trap** | Continuing to train through acute musculoskeletal injury or severe illness because the athlete cannot tolerate the emotional feeling of resting. | Identify whether continuing the workout is driven by physiological adaptation or avoidance of emotional discomfort. If the latter, stop immediately. |
| **Clinging to Confidence** | Believing high performance is only possible when feeling 100% confident and motivated. | Train the athlete to execute workouts with **courage and competence** on days when motivation and confidence are completely absent. |
| **Premature Race Problem-Solving** | Attempting to analyze mistakes in the post-race parking lot while an athlete is hyper-aroused and defensive. | Enforce a mandatory cooldown period; allow the emotional wave to clear before opening analytical software. |
| **Ignoring Dashboard Indicators** | Treating every negative thought as irrelevant noise, failing to heed legitimate physiological alarms (e.g., systemic overreaching, heat illness). | Evaluate thoughts through the filter of **workability**: Is this signal highlighting an actionable physical risk, or is it routine cognitive chatter? |

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Performance Psychology Diagnostic & Intervention Guide

| Diagnostic / Scenario | Underlying Psychological Dynamic | Prescribed Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Athlete enters a panic spiral 30 min before race start** | Entanglement with catastrophic future predictions ("I will get dropped on lap 1"). | Execute the **5+2 Sensory Grounding protocol**; name 3 sounds, 3 visual objects, and acknowledge internal "thinking" and "feeling" without trying to suppress them. |
| **Severe internal debate occurs during a 5-min VO2max interval** | Prefrontal cognitive tug-of-war draining central motor drive and elevating RPE. | **"Drop the rope."** Forbid counter-arguing with the mind. Anchor visual attention to a single data field (cadence) or physical landmark. |
| **Athlete smashes a recovery ride because they "felt amazing"** | Inability to separate internal emotional state from long-term physiological periodization. | Enforce rigid adherence to power/HR ceilings regardless of subjective mood; educate athlete on the cellular purpose of deloading. |
| **Athlete wants to quit during a high-stakes race when dropped** | Experiential avoidance of embarrassment or failure. | Encourage athlete to finish the effort as a psychological "rep" in building emotional durability, focusing entirely on process execution. |
| **Chronic loss of training motivation during base building** | Attentional narrowing and isolation; loss of values-based athletic connection. | Check interpersonal social connection; re-evaluate core athletic values beyond numerical FTP progression. |
