# Ten Minute Tips #19: FTP Testing Shortcuts — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Houston_

---

## What Are FTP Testing Shortcuts?

Traditional threshold testing protocols—such as the standard Kolie Moore progressive ramp-to-exhaustion test ($35–60+\text{ min}$), the Coggan 20-minute test, or multi-stage ramp tests—can induce significant mental fatigue, test anxiety, and physical exhaustion. When executed under fatigue or anxiety, these tests often yield misleading data.

**FTP Testing Shortcuts** are non-exhaustive, low-stress field assessment strategies designed to establish accurate Functional Threshold Power ($\text{FTP}/\text{MLSS}$) without requiring a formal, maximal test day.

```
  ┌──────────────────────┴───────────────────────┐

  │  2. Step-Up Breakpoint Exploration           │

  └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Physiology of the Threshold Boundary
Functional Threshold Power represents the **Maximal Lactate Steady State ($\text{MLSS}$)**—the highest power output where oxidative ATP generation meets metabolic demand without runaway homeostatic disturbance.

- **Sub-Threshold Steady State:**
  - ATP is generated predominantly via oxidative phosphorylation (~32–34 ATP per glucose molecule).
  - Blood lactate production equals systemic clearance (MCT1 shuttling into oxidative fibers and heart).
  - Ventilation ($\dot{V}_E$) remains proportional to $\dot{V}\text{CO}_2$ output; breathing is labored but strictly rhythmic and controlled.
- **Super-Threshold Dynamics:**
  - Above MLSS, aerobic ATP flux is insufficient to match energy demand.
  - Substrate-level glycolysis accelerates rapidly, yielding only $2\text{ ATP}$ per glucose molecule converted to lactate.
  - **EMG & Motor Unit Recruitment Breakpoint:** As smaller oxidative motor units fatigue rapidly, the central nervous system rapidly recruits larger Type II motor units to maintain power.
  - **Ventilatory Breakpoint (VT2):** Buffering of excess hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$) by bicarbonate creates excess non-metabolic $\text{CO}_2$, causing hyperventilation and ragged, uncontrolled breathing within 45–90 seconds.

```
  Intensity         Bioenergetics               Breathing Mechanics          Neuromuscular Sensation
 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Sub-Threshold     Predominantly Oxidative     Deep, regular, rhythmic      Sustainable leg tension
  (Sweet Spot)      (34 ATP / glucose)          "In control"                 Fatigue accumulates slowly

  AT THRESHOLD      Maximal Steady State        Labored, deep, sustainable   Legs burning steadily
  (MLSS / LT2)      (Equilibrium)               Rhythmic, focused            High cognitive focus

  SUPER-THRESHOLD   Rapid Glycolysis Spike      Ragged, hyperventilating     Sharp increase in neural drive
  (Anaerobic Gap)   (2 ATP / glucose)           "Running away from you"      Rapid depletion (cliff edge)
```

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### Method 1: The Blind Paced Protocol ($2 \times 20\text{ min}$ or $3 \times 15\text{ min}$)
- **Setup:** Tape over or hide the Power and Heart Rate fields on your cycling computer. Display only elapsed interval time and cadence.
- **Execution:** 
  - Warm up thoroughly ($15–20\text{ min}$ with a couple of 1-minute openers).
  - Ride interval 1 for $20\text{ min}$ at what feels like a sustainable, solid threshold effort.
  - Rest $5–8\text{ min}$ easy spinning.
  - Ride interval 2 for $20\text{ min}$ matching the same perceived exertion.
- **Assessment:** Look at the normalized/average power post-ride. For experienced athletes, the average power of the two intervals reflects true FTP within $\pm 2–3\text{ W}$ in over 90% of trials.

### Method 2: Step-Up Exploration Protocol
- **Setup:** Use a steady climb or indoor trainer in standard mode.
- **Execution:**
  1. Start $50–60\text{ W}$ below your estimated FTP.
  2. Ride a $10\text{ min}$ steady block (or $5–8\text{ min}$ for highly anaerobic riders).
  3. Increase power by $+15\text{ W}$ each successive block with $3–5\text{ min}$ recovery.
  4. Continue stepping up until you hit the **Ventilatory & Sensation Breakpoint**:
     - Controlled breathing transitions to ragged gasping.
     - Sensation changes from steady leg resistance to heavy, high-effort neural forcing.
- **Result:** The power stage immediately preceding the breakpoint represents your current functional threshold.

### Method 3: Software Look-Back Window Calibration (WKO5 / Intervals)
- Instead of using standard 90-day power duration curves (which retain stale peak data), adjust the analysis window to **30 to 45 days**.
- If no recent maximal efforts exist, verify modeled FTP against recent submaximal tempo/sweetspot sessions rather than forcing a test day.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Playing "Whack-a-Mole" with Good Days:**
   - Hitting an outlier power performance on a day with ideal cooling, caffeine, and fresh legs, and immediately raising everyday interval targets. If subsequent sessions fail, revert immediately to the lower reliable baseline.
2. **Adjusting FTP for Noise ($< 10\text{ Watts}$):**
   - Adjusting threshold settings for differences of 3–5 Watts. Power meter tolerances ($\pm 1.5–2\%$) and day-to-day hydration/glycogen shifts introduce $\pm 5–10\text{ W}$ of natural variance.
3. **The "Taint-to-Tip" Ego Measurement Trap:**
   - Overestimating FTP to boast higher numbers or show a 300W baseline, resulting in sweetspot sessions turning into grueling supra-threshold workouts that destroy aerobic consistency.
4. **Testing Too Close to Goal Events:**
   - Scheduling formal threshold tests within 10–14 days of an A-priority race. A bad test destroys confidence; a maximal test consumes valuable taper energy.

| Athlete Situation | Recommended Testing Shortcut | Key Indicator / Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
