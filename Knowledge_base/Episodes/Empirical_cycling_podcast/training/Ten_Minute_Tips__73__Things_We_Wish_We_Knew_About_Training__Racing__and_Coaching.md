# Things We Wish We Knew About Training, Racing, and Coaching — Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Erica Zaveta & Will Turner (Ten Minute Tips #73)_

---

## What Is the "Experience-Driven" Training Framework?

Athletic development in endurance cycling is frequently hindered by dogma: under-fueling during rides, rigid adherence to static training plans, equating suffering with adaptive stimulus, and fixating exclusively on podium results. 

The Empirical Cycling coaching framework synthesizes exercise physiology with practical field experience to eliminate these counterproductive paradigms. It prioritizes:
1. **High Energy Availability:** Replacing substrate during exercise to maintain quality and avoid chronic endocrine and energetic deficits.
2. **Autoregulated Periodization:** Treating training prescriptions as adaptive guidelines ("written in sand") rather than rigid mandates.
3. **Interoceptive Pacing:** Building an internal sensory calibration of physiological limits without passive reliance on trainer ERG modes.
4. **Process-Oriented Race Craft:** Developing tactical pattern recognition and physiological self-awareness over multi-season horizons.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms & Conceptual Frameworks

### 1. Carbohydrate Fueling & Glycogen Preservation
- **Historical Fallacy:** The mid-2000s to 2010s paradigm advocated minimal fueling on endurance rides (e.g., 20–25 g/hr or fasted riding) under the assumption that fat oxidation should be maximized and submaximal energetic holes did not require replacement.
- **Physiological Reality:** Endogenous glycogen stores (~400–600g in liver and muscle) deplete rapidly even at Zone 2 intensities when rides exceed 2 hours. Substrate deficit triggers elevated cortisol, central nervous system fatigue, and metabolic energy compensation (post-exercise lethargy and uncontrolled compensatory hyperphagia).
- **Practical Outcome:** Ingesting 60–100+ g/hr of exogenous carbohydrates maintains blood glucose homeostasis, blunts exercise-induced muscle protein breakdown, maintains nervous system drive, and allows athletes to finish long sessions with functional energy reserves for daily life and next-day training.

### 2. Autoregulation vs. Blind Plan Compliance
- **The "Written in Sand" Principle:** A training plan represents a hypothesis of required stimulus. External stressors (poor sleep, work demands, illness, life stress) directly modulate autonomic nervous system recovery and cellular adaptation capacity.
- **Pass/Fail Fallacy of Submaximal Targets:** Hitting a predetermined submaximal wattage (e.g., 88–92% FTP) while heavily fatigued masks underlying decline in physiological capacity. When fatigue is high, submaximal targets force disproportionate motor unit recruitment and anaerobic contribution, degrading the intended aerobic stimulus.
- **Minimum Effective Dose:** The adaptive signal for mitochondrial biogenesis and threshold development requires targeted, high-quality overload followed by supercompensation. Stacking fatigue via redundant interval days produces diminishing returns and increases injury/overtraining risk.

### 3. Open Pacing vs. ERG Mode Passivity
- **Mechanisms of ERG Mode:** ERG mode enforces a rigid power target regardless of pedaling cadence or athlete neuromuscular fatigue. When cadence drops under fatigue, resistance ramps exponentially ("spiral of death"), forcing uncoordinated muscle recruitment.
- **Interoception Development:** Unconstrained (open) resistance forces the athlete's central nervous system to self-select optimal torque, cadence, and mental focus. The inability to hit or maintain an expected power target serves as an immediate diagnostic indicator of central/peripheral fatigue that ERG mode artificially overrides.

### 4. Body Composition & Energy Compensation
- **The "Cyclist Silhouette" Myth:** Chasing arbitrary scale numbers or extremely low body fat percentages (<8% for non-specialized male athletes) frequently results in relative energy deficiency in sport (REDs), loss of skeletal muscle mass, bone demineralization, and catastrophic reductions in absolute power output.
- **Performance vs. Lightness:** Sustainable performance is governed by functional metabolic capacity and power-to-drag ($W/CdA$) or power-to-mass ($W/kg$) at healthy physiological equilibrium, not transient acute weight loss achieved through caloric starvation.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### On-Bike Fueling Protocol
- **Endurance / Zone 2 Rides (<2 hours):** Minimum 30–50 g carbs/hr.
- **Endurance Rides (>2.5 hours):** 60–90 g carbs/hr using mixed glucose-fructose sources.
- **Intensive Interval Sessions (Threshold, VO2max):** 60–100 g carbs/hr starting 15–20 minutes prior to the first work bout.
- **Post-Ride Metric:** The athlete should finish the workout feeling capable of normal daily cognitive and physical tasks, not entering an immediate metabolic "coma."

### Autoregulation Guidelines for Intervals
1. **Warm-Up Check:** If RPE during standard warm-up steps is abnormally elevated (>3 points above baseline) and heart rate is either suppressed or spiking erratically, abort high-intensity intervals and revert to active recovery or easy Zone 2.
2. **First Interval Test:** Execute the first interval without ERG mode. If power output is >5–7% below the target domain despite maximum mental effort, stop the interval session.
3. **Session Rescheduling:** Move key workouts to the next day when well-rested rather than grinding out low-quality repetitions while exhausted.

### Race Craft & Process Goals
- **Tactical Experimentation:** Dedicate lower-priority (B/C category) races entirely to specific process goals:
  - Contesting every sprint prime to master timing and pack positioning.
  - Practicing lead-out mechanics and intra-team communication.
  - Attacking to bridge to breakaways at predictable lull moments (e.g., after a major chase is neutralized).
- **Long-Term Trajectory:** Recognize that tactical pattern recognition and physiological race resilience take multi-season timelines (3–5+ years) of consistent competition.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall | Physiological / Practical Impact | Corrective Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Starving the Long Ride** | Glycogen depletion, post-ride binging, lethargy, compromised next-day session. | Ingest 60–90g carbs/hr on all rides $>2$ hours. |
| **Dogmatic Plan Compliance** | Stacking fatigue upon inadequate recovery; non-functional overreaching. | Treat plans as dynamic; drop or modify workouts when recovery metrics fail. |
| **Over-Reliance on ERG Mode** | Blunted interoception; inability to self-pace in road racing and field climbs. | Ride intervals in level/slope mode to develop sensory pacing calibration. |
| **Obsessing Over Scale Weight** | Muscle mass loss, endocrine suppression, power loss, high energy compensation. | Prioritize fueling high-quality work; allow body composition to stabilize naturally. |
| **Over-Complicated Interval Prescriptions** | Mental burnout, difficulty tracking physiological progression. | Focus on 1–2 key physiological targets per block using clean, progressive formats. |

- [ ] **Fuel Every Ride:** Treat carbs as an ergogenic tool and recovery accelerator.

- [ ] **Respect "Big Rest":** Realize fitness supercompensation occurs during recovery, not during exhausted workouts.
