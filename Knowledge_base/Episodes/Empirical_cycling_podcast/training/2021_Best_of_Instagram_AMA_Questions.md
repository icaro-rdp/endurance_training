## Q: How Should Athletes Anchor Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE) for FTP vs. Resistance Training?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes ask how to objectively calibrate subjective Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE) across endurance cycling zones and in the weight room without misjudging intensity.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Resistance Training (Reps in Reserve / RIR):**
  - **RPE 8 / 2 RIR:** Two clean repetitions remaining before failure; the target for heavy strength work ($\ge 80\%\ \text{1RM}$) to maximize high-threshold motor unit recruitment while preventing excessive neural and connective tissue damage.
  - **RPE 9 / 1 RIR:** One clean repetition remaining; high focus required.
  - **RPE 10 / 0 RIR:** Concentric failure.
- **Endurance Cycling Intensity Anchors:**
  - **Zone 2 (LT1 / Aerobic Threshold):** The highest sustainable pace that remains completely conversational and effortless (RPE 3–4/10).
  - **Zone 4 (FTP / Lactate Threshold):** Anchored at an **RPE of 7/10**. It feels distinctly hard, but power output does not require escalating mental desperation or panic during the first 15–25 minutes. If an athlete must grit their teeth within 5 minutes, power is above true MLSS.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- In the gym, execute primary compound lifts (squats, trap bar deadlifts) at **RPE 7.5–8.5 (1–3 reps in reserve)**.
- On the bike, maintain threshold intervals at a manageable **RPE 7/10**; if breathing becomes erratic and mental panic sets in early, reduce power by 5–10 Watts.

## Q: Why Do Symmetrical Over-Unders Fail and How Should Asymmetric Intervals Be Structured?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
A listener asked why standard symmetrical over-under workouts (e.g., 3 min @ 105% FTP / 3 min @ 95% FTP) feel brutally fatiguing without clear fitness gains, and how over-unders should properly be designed.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **The Symmetrical Clearance Failure:** In symmetrical workouts, 95% FTP is too close to Maximal Lactate Steady State (MLSS) to allow rapid lactate oxidation, hydrogen ion clearance, or phosphocreatine ($PCr$) resynthesis. As sets progress, $H^+$ accumulates and the interval breaks down into an unsustainable anaerobic grind.
- **Asymmetrical Kinetics:** Short "overs" (15–30 seconds at $110\text{--}120\%$ FTP) generate a controlled spike in lactate and recruitment of fast-twitch motor units. Longer "unders" ($3\text{ to }4.5\text{ minutes}$ at $80\text{--}88\%$ FTP) place the muscle in an oxidative state where monocarboxylate transporters (MCT1) uptake and combust lactate aerobically.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**

```
Asymmetric Over-Under Protocol:
- Structure: 3 x 15-20 min blocks (Rest: 6-8 min easy spin between blocks)
- Each block consists of repeating cycles:
  - 30 sec OVER @ 110-115% FTP (Cadence 95-100 RPM)
  - 4.5 min UNDER @ 80-85% FTP (Tempo, Cadence 88-92 RPM)
  - Repeat 3 cycles per block (total 15 min TiZ)
```

## Q: Does Heart Rate Decoupling (Cardiac Drift) During Zone 2 Mean an Athlete Lacks Aerobic Base?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Riders frequently observe their heart rate drifting upward by 5–10% during hours 3 and 4 of a steady Zone 2 ride while power remains constant, asking if this indicates an inadequate aerobic base and requires stopping the ride.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Thermoregulation & Cutaneous Perfusion:** As continuous exercise progresses, core body temperature rises, redirecting a portion of cardiac output to subcutaneous capillaries for evaporative heat dissipation.
- **Stroke Volume Compensation:** Reduced central venous return lowers end-diastolic ventricular filling, slightly decreasing stroke volume ($SV$). Heart rate ($HR$) must rise to maintain total cardiac output ($Q = HR \times SV$).
- **Sympathetic Tone:** Mild gradual elevation of circulating catecholamines (epinephrine/norepinephrine) naturally elevates heart rate.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Cardiac drift during prolonged base rides is normal physiological behavior; it does not indicate poor aerobic fitness.
- As long as RPE remains conversational ($\le 3\text{--}4/10$) and power is stable, continue the ride.
- Prioritize adequate hydration (electrolytes and water) and cooling to minimize drift magnitude.

## Q: Why Is High Cadence (105–115+ RPM) Essential for Maximizing Stroke Volume at VO2max?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Listeners ask why high pedaling cadence is specifically recommended during VO2max intervals if their natural road or racing cadence is 85–90 RPM.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **The Skeletal Muscle Pump:** At true $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$, rapid rhythmic contractions at $105\text{--}115+\text{ RPM}$ compress deep intramuscular veins, propelling venous blood rapidly back to the right atrium.
- **Maximizing Cardiac Preload:** Increased venous return maximizes end-diastolic ventricular filling (preload). By the Frank-Starling mechanism, this stretches myocardial fibers, achieving peak stroke volume ($SV$) and maximal central cardiac remodeling.
- **Torque and Fatigue Sparing:** High cadence reduces the peak torque per revolution, sparing fast-twitch glycolytic motor units from excessive force-induced fatigue.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Prescribe VO2max intervals ($3\text{--}5\text{ min}$ all-out efforts) at **10–15 RPM above self-selected cadence (typically 105–115+ RPM)**.
- Reserve natural self-selected cadence for racing and sub-threshold threshold/tempo work.

## Q: When Should Athletes Choose a Concentrated VO2max Block vs. a Mixed Microcycle?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes ask how to determine whether to execute a 3-week dedicated VO2max block or a mixed weekly microcycle combining threshold and VO2max touches.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Dedicated VO2max Block:** Delivers a concentrated, high-density shock stimulus (6–9 sessions over 2–3 weeks) to force central cardiovascular adaptations (stroke volume expansion). Best used when an athlete has hit an FTP plateau capped by high fractional utilization and has 4–6 weeks of recovery runway before racing.
- **Mixed Microcycle:** Combines 1 threshold progression session, 1 VO2max touch, and high-volume Zone 2 per week. Ideal for athletes with high life stress, master's athletes with slower recovery kinetics, or athletes racing regularly.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Use a **concentrated block** when FTP TTE is extended ($\ge 50\text{--}60\text{ min}$) and the calendar allows 2–3 weeks of focused load followed by 10–14 days of low-intensity recovery.
- Use a **mixed microcycle** (1 VO2 session + 1 FTP session + weekend endurance) during busy work periods or in-season maintenance.

## Q: What Are the Protein and Caloric Requirements for Endurance Adaptations and Lean Mass Retention?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
A listener asked how much protein is required during heavy endurance blocks, and how to lose weight safely without sacrificing power output and muscle mass.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Endurance Protein Turnover:** High-volume endurance cycling stimulates extensive protein breakdown and requires de novo synthesis of mitochondrial enzymes, angiogenic capillary proteins (VEGF), and transport proteins (MCT1/4).
- **Safe Caloric Deficit:** A moderate deficit targeting **$0.5\%\text{ to }1.0\%$ of body weight loss per week** protects resting metabolic rate, preserves thyroid function, and prevents the loss of skeletal muscle mass.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Consume **$1.5\text{ to }2.0\text{ g/kg/day}$ of protein** across 3–4 balanced daily meals.
- Fuel on-bike workouts aggressively ($60\text{--}90+\text{ g/hr}$ of carbohydrates during hard sessions); create the caloric deficit exclusively from off-bike meals while keeping protein high.

## Q: Should Cyclists Train Deep Range of Motion (ROM) in the Gym or Mimic Pedal Stroke Angles?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes ask whether resistance training should utilize partial range-of-motion lifts (quarter squats) to match the joint angles of the pedal stroke, or deep full-ROM compound movements.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Mechanical Tension & Joint Safety:** Full-ROM deep squats, trap bar deadlifts, and Bulgarian split squats create high mechanical tension and motor unit recruitment at much lighter absolute loads, protecting spinal discs and patellofemoral ligaments.
- **Absence of SSC:** Cycling is non-impact and lacks an eccentric stretch-shortening cycle. Attempting to mimic pedal stroke angles with supramaximal partial squats adds extreme joint shear forces without improving on-bike power.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Perform **full-ROM compound movements** in the gym (deep back squats, split squats, RDLs) for 3–5 reps at RPE 7.5–8.5.
- Develop cycling-specific explosive starting power and joint-angle force on the bike via **standing starts** on the road or track.
