## What Is the 2021 Best of Instagram AMA?

This guide synthesizes the most impactful coaching questions and physiological debates from an entire year of Empirical Cycling's weekly Instagram Q&A sessions (51 out of 52 weeks). Kolie Moore and Kyle Hanson address fundamental questions across four primary domains:
1. **Training Intensity & RPE Calibration:** Establishing concrete physiological anchors for FTP, LT1, and lifting RPE/RIR.
2. **Interval Construction & Over-Unders:** Why symmetrical over-unders fail and how to dose threshold microbursts.
3. **Periodization & Adaptation Ceilings:** Managing mixed vs. focused blocks, anaerobic capacity ($V\text{La}_{\max}$) year-round traps, and track/hill climb aerobic demands.
4. **Metabolic & Strength Principles:** Protein turnover in endurance athletes, mitochondrial retention, gym range-of-motion versus cycling specificity, and safe weight loss without power compromise.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

|                                                                               |
|  [ Zone 1 / Active Recovery ]  < 55% FTP  ──> Minimal metabolic strain         |

|  [ Zone 2 / Endurance Base ]   55-75% FTP ──> Sub-LT1; conversational;        |
|                                               maximum calcium flux / AMPK     |

|                                               lactate appearance = clearance  |
```

### 1. RPE Anchoring for Lifting vs. Endurance Cycling
- **Resistance Training RPE (Reps in Reserve / RIR):**
  - An RPE of $8/10$ represents **2 Reps in Reserve (2 RIR)** performed with flawless technique.
  - An RPE of $9/10$ represents **1 RIR**, where extreme focus is required to prevent mechanical breakdown.
  - An RPE of $10/10$ is reaching concentric failure. In heavy lifting ($3\text{--}6\text{ reps}$ at $\ge 80\%\ \text{1RM}$), athletes should operate primarily at RPE 7.5–8.5 to stimulate high-threshold Type II motor units without accumulating excessive neural or connective tissue damage.
- **Endurance Cycling RPE Anchors:**
  - **LT1 / Aerobic Threshold (Zone 2):** The highest sustainable pace that remains completely conversational and effortless (RPE 3–4/10). Riding above LT1 introduces elevated autonomic strain and glycogen depletion.
  - **FTP / Lactate Threshold (Zone 4):** Anchored at an **RPE of 7/10**. It feels distinctly hard, but power output does not require escalating mental desperation or hyper-focus to maintain for the first 15–25 minutes. If an athlete must grit their teeth or decouple mentally within 5 minutes, the target is set above true threshold.

### 2. The Physiology of Over-Under Intervals
- **Lactate Dynamics & Clearance Kinetics:** The purpose of an "over-under" workout is to fluctuate between lactate accumulation (the "over" phase, above FTP) and lactate oxidation/clearance by oxidative slow-twitch fibers and adjacent myocardium (the "under" phase, below FTP).
- **The Symmetrical Trap:** Symmetrical prescriptions (e.g., 3 min @ 105% FTP / 3 min @ 95% FTP) fail because 95% FTP is too close to the maximum lactate steady state (MLSS) to allow rapid clearance of metabolic acidosis or resynthesis of phosphocreatine ($PCr$).
- **Asymmetric Solution:** Short overs (15–30 seconds at $110\text{--}120\%$ FTP) paired with moderate-duration unders ($3\text{ to }4.5\text{ minutes}$ at $80\text{--}88\%$ FTP) allow the athlete to clear lactate via monocarboxylate transporters (MCT1) while maintaining high continuous muscular tension.

### 3. Heart Rate Decoupling (Cardiac Drift) During Zone 2
- **Mechanisms:** During prolonged sub-threshold exercise, heart rate frequently drifts upward while power remains constant. This is driven by:
  1. **Thermoregulation & Cutaneous Blood Flow:** As core temperature rises, blood is redirected to the skin for heat dissipation, reducing central venous return.
  2. **Reduced Stroke Volume:** Decreased end-diastolic ventricular filling lowers stroke volume ($SV$), forcing heart rate ($HR$) upward to maintain cardiac output ($Q = HR \times SV$).
  3. **Sympathetic Activation:** Gradual elevation of circulating catecholamines (epinephrine/norepinephrine).
- **Practical Rule:** Unless cardiac drift is accompanied by a severe surge in RPE or complete exhaustion, it is a normal physiological phenomenon and does not require ending the endurance ride or halting progression to threshold training.

### 4. High Cadence and Diastolic Filling at VO2max
- **The Muscle Pump Phenomenon:** At true $\dot{V}\text{O}_{2\max}$, high pedaling cadence ($105\text{--}120\text{ RPM}$) enhances the rhythmic compression of deep veins by contracting skeletal muscles.
- **Stroke Volume Optimization:** This increased venous return accelerates end-diastolic ventricular filling. Consequently, the heart can achieve maximal stroke volume and maximal systemic oxygen transport ($\dot{V}\text{O}_2 = Q \times (C_a\text{O}_2 - C_v\text{O}_2)$) without necessarily reaching absolute maximum beats per minute.

### 5. Protein Requirements for Endurance Biogenesis
- **Cellular Turnover in Endurance Athletes:** Endurance training drives continuous synthesis of mitochondrial enzymes (citrate synthase, cytochrome c oxidase), capillary endothelial growth factors (VEGF for angiogenesis), and contractile myofibrillar proteins.
- **Prescription:** Endurance athletes should consume **$1.5\text{ to }2.0\text{ g/kg/day}$** of protein. Inadequate protein ($<1.0\text{ g/kg/day}$) impairs recovery, suppresses lean tissue preservation, and stalls mitochondrial remodeling.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Asymmetric Over-Under Interval Session

```
Over-Under Workout Prescription:
- Warm-up: 20 min progressive (Z1 to low Z3) + 3x 15s bursts
- Main Set: 3 x 15-20 min Over-Unders (Rest: 6-8 min Z1 between sets)
  - Each 15-min block consists of:
    - 30 sec OVER @ 110-115% FTP (Cadence 95-100 RPM)
    - 4.5 min UNDER @ 80-85% FTP (Tempo, Cadence 88-92 RPM)
    - Repeat 3 cycles per block (total 15 min TiZ)
- Cool-down: 15 min easy spin
```

### 2. Periodization: Focused vs. Mixed Blocks
- **Focused VO2max Block:** Prescribe 6–9 high-cadence VO2max sessions over 2.5–3.5 weeks, followed by a full 10–14 day recovery period. Use when an athlete has hit an aerobic ceiling and needs to expand maximal cardiac throughput.
- **Mixed Training Block:** When athlete lifestyle stress, irregular calendars, or minor illnesses prevent high-density blocks, combine 1 threshold extension workout, 1 maintenance/VO2 session, and high-volume Zone 2 per week.

```
Mixed Weekly Microcycle Example:
- Monday:    Rest / Active Mobility
- Tuesday:   Threshold TiZ Progression (e.g., 2 x 25 min @ 98% FTP)
- Wednesday: Zone 2 Endurance (1.5-2.0 hrs @ 60-65% FTP)
- Thursday:  VO2max Maintenance (4 x 3.5 min all-out @ 110+ RPM)
- Friday:    Recovery Spin (45 min Zone 1) or Off
- Saturday:  Long Endurance Ride with natural terrain (3.5-5.0 hrs Zone 2)
- Sunday:    Easy Zone 1-2 Recovery or Rest
```

### 3. Strength Training: Full Range of Motion (ROM) vs. Angle Specificity
- **Full ROM in the Gym:** Perform deep back squats, trap bar deadlifts, and Bulgarian split squats through full active range of motion. Deep joint angles generate maximal mechanical tension with lighter absolute loads, reducing patellar and spinal shear stress.
- **Specificity on the Bike:** Reserve cycling-specific joint angles and high-torque demands for standing starts ($53 \times 14$, 0–15 km/h maximal explosive accelerations) on the road or track.

### 4. Safe Weight Loss Framework for Endurance Performance
- **Target Deficit Rate:** Maximum weight loss rate of **$0.5\%\text{ to }1.0\%$ of body weight per week** (e.g., 0.35–0.75 kg/week for a 75 kg athlete).
- **Fuel the Work:** Consume 60–90+ g/hr of carbohydrates during high-intensity intervals and threshold sessions; create the caloric deficit exclusively across off-bike baseline meals while maintaining high protein intake ($1.6\text{--}2.0\text{ g/kg}$).

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Year-Round Anaerobic ($V\text{La}_{\max}$) Hammering:**
   - Attempting to improve sprint repeatability by doing 30–60s all-out anaerobic intervals all year. Without an expanding aerobic engine (FTP/mitochondrial density) to clear lactate and resynthesize PCr, anaerobic repeats cause severe burnout and blunt aerobic power.
2. **Treating Fasted / Low-Glycogen Training as a Default:**
   - Performing every base ride fasted. While low glycogen can activate AMPK signaling, it elevates cortisol, degrades interval quality, and frequently causes non-isocaloric underfueling (RED-S).
3. **Misinterpreting Day-to-Day Threshold Fluctuations:**
   - Assuming that daily fatigue alters absolute FTP wattage. In reality, fatigue primarily shortens **Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE)**; if power feels excessively difficult, the solution is restorative rest, not recalibrating FTP down by 20 W every other day.
4. **Using Ketone Supplements to Mask Overtraining:**
   - Using exogenous ketones as an expensive surrogate for adequate caloric intake and sleep. Ketones are simply a caloric energy substrate ($4\text{ kcal/g}$); they do not reverse systemic autonomic burnout.
