# Listener Questions on FTP Training & Periodization — Q&A Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson (Watts Doc #16)_

---

## Q: What Is the Ideal Interval Length and Progression Structure to Start an FTP Block?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes ask how to structure their initial threshold interval workouts when beginning an FTP build block, and how to progress interval durations over several weeks.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Matching Baseline TTE:** Threshold intervals must reflect the athlete's current sub-threshold Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE). If baseline TTE is 35–45 minutes, starting with overly long intervals ($2 \times 30\text{ min}$) leads to premature failure and high autonomic fatigue.
- **Progressing Time-in-Zone "Out":** Early in a block, the primary goal is extending duration at target wattage rather than increasing power. Accumulating progressive volume allows mitochondrial enzymes and capillarization to adapt steadily.

```
              FTP Progression Cycle: Push "Out", Then Pull "Up"
  
  [1. Base / Early Season] ──► Test Baseline FTP & TTE (e.g. 280W @ 40 min)
                                      │
  [2. Extend TTE "Out"]   ──► Progress 4x10 ──► 3x15 ──► 2x20 ──► 3x20 ──► 2x30 min @ 95% FTP
                                      │
  [3. Maximize TTE]       ──► TTE Reaches 65–75 min (280W @ 70 min) ──► Fractional Cap Hit
                                      │
  [4. Raise the Ceiling]  ──► 3–4 Week VO2max Block (Lift aerobic ceiling from above)
                                      │
  [5. Retest & Reset]     ──► New FTP: 300W; New TTE: ~35–45 min ──► Repeat Cycle "Out"
```

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Start with manageable intervals scaled to baseline TTE: begin with $4 \times 10\text{ min}$ or $3 \times 12\text{ min}$ (rest: 3–4 min).
- Progress duration weekly: $3 \times 15\text{ min} \rightarrow 2 \times 20\text{ min} \rightarrow 2 \times 25\text{ min} \rightarrow 3 \times 20\text{ min} \rightarrow 2 \times 30\text{ min}$.

## Q: What Exact Percentage of FTP Should Threshold Intervals Be Ridden At (and Why 95% Beats >100%)?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Riders frequently ask whether threshold intervals should be ridden right at 100% FTP, slightly above (102–105%), or slightly below (92–98%).

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Metabolic Steady State at 92–98%:** Riding at 92–98% FTP maintains oxidative metabolic steady state where lactate appearance equals maximal rate of clearance, leaving Anaerobic Work Capacity ($W'$) intact and permitting 45–90 minutes of total time-in-zone.
- **The >100% Failure Point:** Riding at 102–105% FTP rapidly accumulates $H^+$ and inorganic phosphate $[P_i]$, depleting $W'$ within 15–25 minutes and forcing the workout to end prematurely before sufficient aerobic volume is achieved.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Prescribe routine threshold intervals at **92% to 98% of tested FTP (typically ~95% FTP)**.
- Never prescribe $>100\%$ FTP for routine continuous threshold workouts; riding 5–10 Watts below threshold provides identical aerobic adaptation with vastly lower autonomic fatigue.

## Q: How Long Should Rest Intervals Be Between Threshold Efforts?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes ask how long recovery intervals should be between threshold repetitions (e.g., between sets of $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ or $3 \times 15\text{ min}$).

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Oxygen Kinetics & Muscle Temperature:** Extended rest periods (>8–10 minutes) allow heart rate, muscle temperature, and oxygen uptake kinetics to return completely to resting baseline, requiring several minutes of the next set to regain target aerobic flux.
- **Metabolic Clearance:** A 3- to 5-minute rest interval is sufficient to clear minor acute acidosis and resynthesize phosphocreatine without losing cardiovascular momentum.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Maintain short recovery intervals: **3 to 5 minutes** of easy spinning between threshold sets (e.g., 4 min rest between $2 \times 20\text{ min}$).

## Q: How Many Threshold Workouts Should Be Performed Per Week?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
A listener asked whether doing 3 threshold sessions per week accelerates fitness gains compared to 2 sessions per week.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Autonomic Recovery Kinetics:** A thorough threshold session ($45\text{--}70+\text{ min}$ time-in-zone) imposes significant sympathetic strain and glycogen depletion, requiring 48–72 hours for complete muscular and autonomic restoration.
- **Overtraining Risk of 3x/Week:** Stacking 3 threshold sessions weekly chronically elevates resting cortisol, degrades sleep quality, and leads to non-functional overreaching within 3–4 weeks.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Schedule **2 threshold sessions per week** separated by 48–72 hours (e.g., Tuesday and Friday/Saturday).
- Use **1 session per week** for threshold maintenance during high-volume base or racing phases.
- Reserve 3 sessions per week strictly for brief 1- to 2-week shock microcycles in highly trained athletes.

## Q: How Do Sweet Spot (88–93% FTP) and Threshold (95–100% FTP) Compare in Training Adaptation?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes ask whether Sweet Spot training is superior to true threshold training for building FTP.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Sweet Spot (88–93% FTP):** Lower mental and autonomic strain; allows accumulating large weekly volume (60–90+ min TiZ per session) with fast recovery; excellent for winter base preparation.
- **Threshold (95–100% FTP):** Higher time-efficiency; develops specific lactate clearance kinetics and race-pace mental durability; essential for time trialists and road racers.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Use **Sweet Spot** during early base blocks to build muscular fatigue resistance.
- Transition to **Threshold (95% FTP)** in build blocks to maximize lactate shuttle clearance and sharpen metabolic efficiency.

## Q: Why Is Indoor Trainer FTP Frequently Lower Than Outdoor FTP and How Is It Fixed?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Riders frequently notice their sustainable power is 10–20 Watts lower on the indoor trainer compared to outdoors, asking if they have separate indoor and outdoor thresholds.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Thermoregulation & Cooling Deficits:** Outdoors, natural wind speeds (25–40 km/h) provide immense convective cooling. Indoors, lack of airflow elevates core temperature, redirects blood to skin capillaries, lowers stroke volume, and elevates heart rate at the same power.
- **Flywheel Inertia Differences:** Lower rotational inertia on some trainers alters pedal stroke biomechanics and torque distribution across dead spots.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Place **multiple high-velocity blower fans** directly at the torso and head.
- Calibrate the trainer after a 10-minute warmup.
- If cooling remains constrained, reduce indoor threshold targets by 5–10 Watts rather than assuming an entirely different metabolic threshold.

## Q: What Cadence Should Be Maintained During Threshold Intervals?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes ask what the optimal pedaling cadence is for threshold intervals and whether low-cadence grinding is beneficial.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Neuromuscular Efficiency:** Self-selected natural cadence (typically **88–96 RPM**) minimizes neuromuscular fatigue and optimizes gross mechanical efficiency.
- **Low-Cadence Torque Variations:** Grinding at 55–65 RPM increases pedal torque, recruiting higher-threshold motor units and adapting fast-twitch fibers oxidatively.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Perform primary threshold intervals at **natural preferred cadence (88–96 RPM)**.
- Introduce low-cadence blocks ($55\text{--}65\text{ RPM}$) as a secondary variation to build torque tolerance, provided the athlete has no knee or patellar issues.

## Q: How Should Threshold Sessions Be Fueled Before and During the Ride?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes ask about optimal carbohydrate fueling strategies before and during hard threshold workouts.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **High Glycolytic Flux:** Threshold riding relies predominantly on carbohydrate oxidation. Exercising with low glycogen elevates cortisol, accelerates central fatigue, and forces premature workout termination.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- **Pre-Workout:** Consume a high-carbohydrate meal ($1\text{--}2\text{ g/kg}$) 2–3 hours prior to the session.
- **In-Ride:** Ingest **60–90 grams of carbohydrates per hour** in liquid or gel form during all threshold sessions. Never train threshold fasted.

## Q: When and How Should Over-Unders and Microbursts Be Introduced in an FTP Block?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
A listener asked when over-unders or microbursts should be added into a threshold block to prepare for race demands.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Dynamic Lactate Clearance:** Incorporating short surges into steady threshold efforts forces working muscles to uptake and oxidize lactate spikes under continuous aerobic load, mirroring the power fluctuations of mass-start racing.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- Introduce over-unders during the final 3–4 weeks of a threshold block.
- In a $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ or $2 \times 25\text{ min}$ set at 95% FTP, insert a **10-second seated maximal burst every 3–5 minutes**, returning immediately to threshold power.

## Q: How Do You Know When an FTP Training Block Has Reached Diminishing Returns?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
Athletes ask how to recognize when a threshold training block is finished and what training phase should follow.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Fractional Utilization Cap:** When an athlete can sustain 95% FTP for 60–75+ minutes comfortably ($3 \times 20\text{ min}$ or $2 \times 30\text{ min}$) and power fails to progress across two consecutive microcycles, the athlete has maximized fractional utilization ($\sim 85\%$ of VO2max).

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- When TTE reaches 60–75+ minutes and threshold power plateaus, conclude the threshold block.
- Schedule a recovery week and transition to a **3- to 4-week VO2max block** to raise the absolute aerobic ceiling.

## Q: What Should You Do If You Fail a Threshold Workout Halfway Through?

**Context & Listener Inquiry:**
An athlete blows up 10 minutes into the second interval of a $3 \times 20\text{ min}$ workout, asking whether they should grind out shorter intervals or abandon the session.

**Mechanisms & Core Explanation:**
- **Indicators of Systemic Fatigue:** Inability to maintain threshold power early in a session indicates unrecovered autonomic fatigue, glycogen depletion, or impending illness. Grinding through ragged micro-intervals compounds fatigue without delivering the target aerobic stimulus.

**Coaching Rules & Practical Application:**
- If power drops $>5\%$ or RPE spikes to maximal within the first 10–15 minutes, downshift immediately to **Zone 1–2 easy spinning or end the session**.
- Take a rest day, prioritize nutrition and sleep, and resume structured training when recovered.
