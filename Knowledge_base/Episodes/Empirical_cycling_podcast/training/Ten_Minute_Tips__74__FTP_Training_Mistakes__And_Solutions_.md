# FTP Training Mistakes and Solutions — Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Erica Zaveta & Dr. Fabiano (Ten Minute Tips #74)_

---

## What Is Functional Threshold Power (FTP)?

Functional Threshold Power (FTP) represents the highest power output an athlete can sustain in a metabolic steady state where lactate production and clearance are in quasi-equilibrium. Biologically, it corresponds closely to Maximal Lactate Steady State (MLSS) and the Second Ventilatory Threshold (VT2/LT2).

In coaching practice, FTP is neither a single 60-minute absolute rule nor a 20-minute test artifact—it is an intensity domain characterized by:
- A stable blood lactate concentration over extended durations (typically 35 to 70+ minutes depending on training status).
- Controlled, rhythmic respiration (effort felt predominantly in skeletal muscle motor units rather than pulmonary distress).
- High energetic dependence on rapid oxidative glycogenolysis without catastrophic accumulation of intracellular protons ($H^+$) and inorganic phosphate ($P_i$).

```
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. The Ceiling vs. Roof Dynamics (Fractional Utilization)
- **Fractional Utilization Limit:** FTP is physiologically constrained as a percentage of maximal oxygen uptake ($\% \text{VO}_2\text{max}$). Untrained athletes operate around 60–70% of $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$, well-trained amateurs reach 75–82%, and elite athletes can sustain 85–90% of $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ at threshold.
- **The Plateau Paradox:** When progressive threshold intervals no longer yield increases in wattage, the athlete has maximized their fractional utilization ceiling. Attempting to force higher wattage without expanding aerobic capacity ($\text{VO}_2\text{max}$) results in excessive anaerobic contribution, early acidosis, and systemic overreaching.

### 2. Testing Methodologies & Overestimation Fallacies
- **Short Tests ($2\times 8$ min, 20 min $\times 0.95$, Ramp Tests):** In athletes with large anaerobic capacities ($W'$ or anaerobic work capacity), short-duration tests inflate estimated FTP by 20 to 60 Watts.
- **Lactate & CPET Fallacy:** Fixed blood lactate thresholds (e.g., $4.0 \text{ mmol/L}$) represent arbitrary population averages. An individual's actual MLSS may occur anywhere between $2.5 \text{ mmol/L}$ and $6.5 \text{ mmol/L}$. Relying on fixed lab cutoffs often leads to over-prescribed training targets.
- **Diagnostic Golden Rule:** The true validation of FTP is the ability to complete repeatable, steady-state intervals in that target domain for durations exceeding 35–45 minutes total time-in-zone without exponential cardiac drift or ventilatory runaway.

### 3. Biomechanics, Inertia, and Environmental Resistance
- **Climbing vs. Flat Pacing:** Uphill cycling involves high crank torque resistance with low flywheel inertia; flat or velodrome cycling involves continuous high flywheel inertia with low torque fluctuation per revolution.
- **Indoor Heat Accumulation:** Sub-optimal indoor cooling (ambient temperatures $>65^\circ\text{F} / 18^\circ\text{C}$ with inadequate convective airflow) elevates core body temperature, induces peripheral vasodilation, increases heart rate (cardiovascular drift), and lowers effective threshold power by 10–25 Watts.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Baseline Interval Architecture
- **Minimum Effective Duration:** 12 to 15 minutes per repetition. Avoid threshold intervals $<10$ minutes as they encourage supra-threshold pacing.
- **Starting Volume:** $3 \times 12$ min or $2 \times 15$ min at $97–100\%$ of true FTP (Total TiZ: 30–36 min).
- **Extensive Progression Protocol:**
  1. Week 1: $3 \times 15\text{ min}$ (45 min TiZ)
  2. Week 2: $2 \times 25\text{ min}$ or $3 \times 20\text{ min}$ (50–60 min TiZ)
  3. Week 3: $1 \times 45\text{ min}$ or $2 \times 30\text{ min}$ (45–60 min TiZ)
  4. Week 4: Recovery / Deload

### 2. The "Negative Split" Interval Execution Rule
- **First Repetition:** Start 5–10 Watts *below* nominal target. Focus on establishing smooth cadence (88–96 RPM) and diaphragmatic breathing.
- **Middle Repetitions:** Settle precisely on target FTP wattage.
- **Final Repetition:** Assess reserves at the halfway point. If RPE is stable, open the throttle by $+5–10\text{W}$ or extend the repetition by $3–5\text{ minutes}$.

```
Interval Pacing Blueprint:
Rep 1: [  FTP - 10W  ] ──► Calibrate legs & RPE
Rep 2: [  FTP Exact  ] ──► Target metabolic steady state
Rep 3: [ FTP + 5-10W ] OR [ Extend Rep Duration by +3-5 min ]
```

### 3. Fueling & Hydration for Threshold Sessions
- **Pre-Session:** Ingest $1.0–1.5\text{ g/kg}$ easily digestible carbohydrate 1.5–2 hours prior.
- **Intra-Session:** Consume $60–90\text{ g/hr}$ carbohydrates (liquid or gel mixes).
- **Thermal Management:** Utilize high-velocity industrial fans; maintain ambient room temperature $<60–64^\circ\text{F}$ ($15–18^\circ\text{C}$).

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Mistake | Physiological Cause | Corrective Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pacing Rep #1 as an FTP Test** | Rapid $W'$ depletion, elevated lactate/$H^+$, neuromuscular fatigue. | Start 5–10W conservative; progress intensity only on the final set. |
| **Using Ramp/Short Tests for FTP** | High anaerobic capacity skews predicted wattage upward. | Determine FTP via progressive empirical interval testing (30–45+ min continuous or long sets). |
| **Endless Linear FTP Expectations** | Fractional utilization ceiling reached ($\sim 85\%$ $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$). | Switch to a dedicated 3–4 week $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ overload block ("raise the roof"). |
| **Short (<10 min) Threshold Reps** | Inability to establish true metabolic steady state; risks drifting into $\text{VO}_2$. | Set interval duration floor at $\ge 12\text{ minutes}$. |
| **Ignoring Indoor Thermal Load** | Core temperature rise causes cardiovascular drift and premature peripheral fatigue. | Add high-CFM fans, ice towels, and recalibrate indoor targets if necessary. |
| **Interpreting Fatigue as Fitness Loss** | Central nervous system and glycogen fatigue masks true capacity. | Rest/recover 48–72h rather than dropping training zones permanently. |
