# Ten Minute Tips #54: The Truth About Junk Miles — Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore, Coach Giancarlo, & Coach Gediminas (Ten Minute Tips #54)_

---

## What Are "Junk Miles"?

In popular cycling culture, **"junk miles"** is a vague pejorative often misapplied to any riding that is unstructured, sub-Zone 2, or not strictly pegged to an arbitrary power zone (e.g., commutes or recovery spins).

### The Rigorous Empirical Definition
$$\text{Junk Miles} = \text{Any volume or intensity that accumulates fatigue without producing an adaptive, technical, or psychological dividend.}$$

- **Myth:** Riding below 65–70% of FTP is "wasted time" because it does not trigger sufficient training stress.
- **Physiological Reality:** Every muscular contraction elicits intracellular calcium flux, activates AMPK and sirtuins, and stimulates capillary proliferation. Total volume (hours in the saddle) is the single greatest driver of aerobic durability.
- **The True Culprit:** The real "junk" in endurance training is **unproductive middle-intensity riding** (riding too hard on easy days), which generates chronic systemic fatigue while failing to stimulate specific threshold or VO2max adaptations.

### 1. The Low-Intensity Volume Signaling Pathway
- **Calcium Oscillations & AMPK:** Muscular contractions at any submaximal power output maintain elevated cytosolic calcium concentrations ($[\text{Ca}^{2+}]$) and activate Calmodulin-Dependent Protein Kinase (CaMK) and AMP-Activated Protein Kinase (AMPK).
- **Mitochondrial Biogenesis:** These cellular signals activate downstream transcription factors, primarily **PGC-1α**, driving mitochondrial biogenesis, oxidative enzyme synthesis (citrate synthase), and microvascular capillarization.
- **The Volume-Durability Nexus:** High cumulative hours—including active commutes, 80W easy spins, and multi-hour low-intensity rides—dramatically enhance late-race durability (fatigue resistance after 2000–3000 kJ of work).

### 2. The "Fatigue Security Blanket" Trap
- **The Psychological Trap:** Athletes often believe a workout is only effective if they finish feeling depleted. Consequently, they push easy endurance rides up to 70–75%+ of FTP (often targeting arbitrary round numbers like 200W).
- **The Stealth Fatigue Mechanism:** Riding at the upper end of Zone 2 / lower Zone 3 accelerates glycogen depletion, elevates plasma catecholamines, and induces low-grade central nervous system fatigue. 
- **The Downstream Failure:** The athlete does not feel wrecked acutely on Day 1, but over 3–6 weeks, resting HR rises, heart rate response blunts, and target power on key threshold or VO2max days decays by 10–20 Watts.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Pacing Long Endurance Rides Correctly

```
                        [Endurance Ride Prescription]
                                      │
              ┌───────────────────────┴───────────────────────┐
    [Common Error: Fixed High Target]               [Empirical Pacing: Ride Into Watts]
    (Start immediately at 75% FTP)                  (Start at 40-50% FTP, naturally rise)
              │                                               │
    High early neuromuscular tension                 Gentle warm-up, zero forced fatigue
    Decoupling & exhaustion by hr 4                  Steady power, can ride 5-6+ hrs easily
```

- **The "Ride Into Your Watts" Rule:** Begin long endurance sessions at very low outputs (e.g., 80–120W / 40–50% FTP). Allow the cardiovascular system and musculature to warm up organically; power will naturally settle into a comfortable, sustainable aerobic output (55–65% FTP) without forced effort.
- **The 8-Hour Test Cue:** Pace solo endurance rides at an intensity you could genuinely sustain for 6–8 hours without nutritional crisis or extreme cardiac drift.

### 2. Evaluating Group Rides: Productive vs. Junk

| Group Ride Feature | Physiological / Tactical Benefit | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Drafting passively in a flat 50-person pack** | Low power (120–150W), zero sustained threshold stimulus, zero technical difficulty | **Junk Miles** (if replacing structured training) |
| **High-speed technical cornering & surging** | Neuromuscular power out of corners (500–1000W bursts), pack positioning skills | **High-Value Training** (crit/race specific) |
| **Aggressive attacks / rotating breakaways** | Over-under threshold simulation, race-pace mental conditioning | **Productive High-Intensity Session** |
| **Casual social cafe spin with friends** | Psychological rejuvenation, stress reduction, active recovery | **High-Value Recovery** |

### 3. "Periodizing Fun" and Mental Health
- **Psychological Bandwidth:** Rigorous, hyper-structured training plans generate cognitive strain. Forcing strict interval compliance during periods of high work or family stress causes psychological burnout.
- **Unstructured Rejuvenation:** Swapping a scheduled interval session for off-road trail exploring, mountain biking, or an unstructured group ride resets mental motivation while maintaining aerobic continuity.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **The 100% Green Compliance Obsession:**
   Chasing a flawless green TrainingPeaks calendar by forcing scheduled intervals when sick, sleep-deprived, or mentally exhausted converts productive workouts into destructive junk miles.
2. **Aggressive Commuting:**
   Treating daily commutes as 30-minute all-out time trials (e.g., 280W twice daily on a 350W FTP) creates un-periodized chronic fatigue that prevents meaningful adaptation.
3. **Equating Sub-Zone 2 with Zero Fitness Value:**
   Assuming a 45-minute easy spin or commuting volume is "useless" ignores the cumulative cellular signaling of low-intensity muscle contractions.
4. **The Training Camp Blast:**
   Doubling weekly volume on a training camp while attempting to hold standard high endurance watts leads to profound overreaching that takes 2–3 weeks to resolve.

### Athlete Ride Audit Checklist
- [ ] **Define the Session Intent:** Is this ride building aerobic volume (Zone 1/2), overloading a specific threshold/VO2 system, practicing race dynamics, or restoring mental health?
- [ ] **Check Easy-Day Pacing:** Are endurance rides kept conversational and easy (55–65% FTP), avoiding the temptation to chase round wattage numbers (e.g. 200W)?
- [ ] **Audit Commutes and Non-Training Rides:** Keep daily utility riding strictly easy to prevent stealth fatigue accumulation.
- [ ] **Utilize Group Rides Strategically:** Use fast group rides for race-specific pack skills and surging; avoid mindless middle-intensity group spins that disrupt recovery.
- [ ] **Permit Mental Flexibility:** If mentally fried, substitute rigid intervals with unstructured trail/road riding to preserve long-term motivation.
