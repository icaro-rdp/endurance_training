# Building Race Fitness — Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Gediminas (Ten Minute Tips #76)_

---

## What Is Race Fitness vs. General Aerobic Fitness?

**General Aerobic Fitness** represents the physiological foundation: high mitochondrial density, robust capillarization, elevated $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$, high Functional Threshold Power (FTP), and extended fatigue resistance (TTE). These adaptations are stable and take months to years to develop.

**Race Fitness (Specialty)** is the tactical, neuromuscular, and metabolic translation of that aerobic engine into discipline-specific performance. It encompasses:
- Repeated stochastic surging above Critical Power ($W'$ expenditure and reconstitution).
- Neuromuscular rate of force development (sprints, steep punchy climbs, accelerations out of corners).
- Technical efficiency (pack aerodynamics, cornering lines, terrain reading).
- Psychological tolerance for unpredictable race dynamics and pack density.

```
│                   A-Priority Peak                      │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤

├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│         General Aerobic Build (8-16 Weeks)             │

│   [+ Long-Term Subplots: 1 Sprint/Skill session / 2-3w]│
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│           Base / General Prep (4-8 Weeks)              │
│       (Aerobic Volume, Strength / Neuromuscular)       │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. The "Subplot" Model vs. Rigid Block Periodization
- **The Rigid Block Flaw:** Traditional linear block periodization postpones race-specific stimuli (e.g., anaerobic capacity, technical descending, sprint mechanics) until 2–4 weeks prior to competition. If an athlete gets sick or fatigued during that narrow window, race preparation fails entirely.
- **The Subplot Framework:** Specific physical and technical qualities are introduced early in the general build as low-frequency "subplots" (e.g., 1 high-torque or sprint session every 2–3 weeks, off-road skill rides in Zone 2). This develops neural pathways and motor coordination with zero disruption to aerobic progression.

### 2. The Proximity Rule & Panic Overload
- **Diminishing Adaptive Return:** High-intensity adaptations take 10–21 days to manifest physiologically. A maximal workout performed 7–10 days before an A-race contributes virtually zero functional adaptation.
- **Asymmetric Risk:** The risk of inducing central nervous system fatigue, glycogen depletion, or compromised immunity 1–2 weeks before a major event is exponential compared to the near-zero performance upside.

### 3. Energy System Strain in Competitive Racing
- Racing produces severe stochastic stress that cannot be replicated in ERG mode:
  - Rapid fluctuations between Zone 1 drafting and Zone 6/7 sprint surges.
  - High sympathetic nervous system arousal and elevated core body temperatures.
  - Substantial muscular micro-trauma from abrupt decelerations and high-torque corner exits.
- **The Midweek Rule:** Weekend racing *is* the primary high-intensity training stimulus. Midweek interval workouts must not attempt progressive overload; they must serve as submaximal maintenance sessions ("keeping the engine warm").

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Event Prioritization Matrix

| Priority | Objective | Taper Strategy | Midweek Training |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A-Priority** (1–3 per year) | Maximal physiological and mental peak for podium/result. | 7–14 day exponential volume reduction ($-40–50\%$) while maintaining interval intensity and short openers. | Low volume, sharp openers (2–3 short bursts), rigorous recovery. |
| **B-Priority** (3–6 per year) | High-end race simulation, fitness testing, tactical rehearsal. | 2–3 day mini-taper (rest day + easy activation ride). | Normal training load earlier in the week; use race as weekend intensity block. |
| **C-Priority** (Weekly / Bi-weekly) | Pack practice, skill drills, process goals (lead-outs, breakaways). | Zero taper; train through normally. | Full training volume; race replaces scheduled weekend interval session. |

### 2. In-Season Maintenance Workout Formats
During multi-week blocks of frequent racing (e.g., criterium or cyclocross seasons), utilize non-depleting maintenance sessions:
- **Format A (Engine Warmer):** $2 \times 15–20\text{ min}$ at Sweet Spot ($85–90\%$ FTP) with $3 \times 10\text{s}$ cadence accelerations embedded.
- **Format B (Neuromuscular Opener):** 60 minutes easy Zone 2 + $3 \times 3\text{ min}$ at FTP + $3 \times 6\text{s}$ maximal seated sprints with full recovery (4 min).
- **Format C (Stochastic Durability):** $3 \times 10\text{ min}$ Over-Unders ($2\text{ min at }90\% \text{ / } 1\text{ min at }115\%$).

### 3. Post-Race Diagnostic Debrief
Within 24–48 hours post-race, review objective data alongside sensory feedback:
1. *Where did the breakdown occur?* (Tactical positioning, positioning before climbs, raw aerobic threshold, or sprint snap?)
2. *Was the limiter physiological or tactical?* (e.g., getting dropped on a climb after spending 30 minutes in the wind vs. getting dropped from the back of an efficient pack.)
3. *Is the limiter relevant to the upcoming A-race?* (If a criterium surge dropped you, but your target A-race is a 4-hour steady gravel climb, do not overhaul training to chase anaerobic surges.)

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Race Prep Pitfall | Mechanism of Failure | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Designating 10+ Races as "A-Priority"** | Chronic fatigue state; inability to execute a supercompensatory taper. | Select 1–3 true A-events per macrocycle; downgrade others to B/C training events. |
| **Panic Overload in Final 2 Weeks** | Severe autonomic fatigue and glycogen deficit on race day. | Lock in the taper 10–14 days out; trust that "the hay is in the barn." |
| **Midweek Over-Smashing During Race Season** | Stacking maximal interval fatigue on top of weekend race degradation. | Shift midweek sessions to maintenance doses (Sweet Spot/Tempo + openers). |
| **Relying Exclusively on Lab Numbers** | Possessing a high FTP/W/kg but lacking pack positioning and race craft. | Use local practice crits and fast group rides to hone aerodynamic drafting and race lines. |
| **Over-Reacting to Early Season B-Race Results** | Misinterpreting heavy training fatigue in early races as a failure of base fitness. | Evaluate B-races as training sessions, not reflections of fully tapered form. |
