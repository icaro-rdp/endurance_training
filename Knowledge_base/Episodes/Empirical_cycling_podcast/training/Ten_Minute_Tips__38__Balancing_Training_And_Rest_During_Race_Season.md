# Ten Minute Tips #38: Balancing Training And Rest During Race Season — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Rory Porteous (Ten Minute Tips #38)_

---

## What Is In-Season Fatigue Management?

During competitive race season, endurance athletes frequently experience a plateau or decline in performance. This occurs because the physiological demands of racing are fundamentally different from structured training:
* **Stochastic Stress vs. Targeted Overload:** Races consist of unpredictable surges, tactical accelerations, and high mental stress, which do not provide the clean, progressive overload necessary to systematically raise Functional Threshold Power (FTP) or extend Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE).
* **The "Perma-Fatigue" Trap:** Treating every weekend race as an all-out priority while attempting to maintain heavy mid-week interval workouts traps the athlete in a chronic state of autonomic overreaching and depleted glycogen stores.

In-season periodization is therefore primarily an exercise in **fatigue minimization, anaerobic maintenance, and strategic recovery**, allowing the athlete to express their pre-built aerobic engine.

```
                    THE IN-SEASON FATIGUE EQUILIBRIUM
       Pre-Season Focus (Build)              In-Season Focus (Race)
├── Progressive Volume & TTE Overload     ├── Fatigue Clearance & Glycogen Restoration
├── High Autonomic Adaptation Cost        ├── Peak Power Expression & Tactical Sharpness
└── Measurable Parameter Increases (Watts)└── Low-Intensity Base Maintenance (Sub-LT1)
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The "Blast Radius" of Race Fatigue
A race effort generates far greater systemic stress than a training session of equal normalized power:
* **Autonomic & Hormonal Strain:** High adrenaline, pack anxiety, and tactical vigilance sustain elevated sympathetic tone, elevating post-race cortisol and suppressing parasympathetic Heart Rate Variability (HRV) for 48–72 hours.
* **Eccentric & Neuromuscular Impact:** Violent accelerations out of corners (cratered torque demands) and high-speed descents create peripheral muscle microtrauma and severe glycogen depletion.
* **The Overlap Dilemma:** Scheduling hard threshold or VO2max intervals within 72 hours of a race produces an overlapping fatigue curve, compromising subsequent training quality.

### 3. The Physiology of "Openers"
* **Mechanism:** Openers (short, sub-maximal efforts performed 24–48 hours before an event) aim to transition the nervous system from a parasympathetic "rest-and-digest" state to sympathetic readiness, increasing muscle perfusion and neural motor unit recruitment.
* **The "Privilege of the Well-Rested":** If an athlete is already carrying residual muscular damage or glycogen depletion, performing high-intensity openers further drains substrate stores and accelerates central fatigue. Fatigued athletes race significantly better entering competition completely rested (or with a gentle 30–45 min active recovery spin).

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Event Categorization & Strategic Execution

| Event Priority | Annual Target | Taper Protocol | In-Race Strategic Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A-Race** | 1–3 events | 7–14 day volume reduction (40–60%), intensity maintained. | Full competitive peak; contest for victory/PR. |
| **B-Race** | 4–8 events | 2–3 days of easy riding; no structured multi-week taper. | Replicate race pacing; test equipment, nutrition, and tactics. |
| **C-Race** | Unlimited | Train through; race day replaces a regular weekend workout. | Skill acquisition: tailgunning, lead-out practice, aggressive breakaways. |

### 2. Microcycle Template Between Weekend Races

For an athlete racing on consecutive weekends (e.g., Saturday criterium or Sunday road race):

```
In-Season Race-to-Race Microcycle:
├── Monday: Full Rest Day (0 Watts) or 30 min Z1 spin (<100W).
├── Tuesday: Easy Base Volume (1.5–2 hrs strictly below LT1 / RPE 2–3).
├── Wednesday: In-Season Maintenance Workout (e.g., 3 × 10 min Sweet Spot OR 2 sets of 30s/30s at 8/10 RPE).
├── Thursday: Short Base / Active Recovery (1–1.5 hrs Sub-LT1).
├── Friday: Autoregulated Openers (30–45 min easy spin + 2 × 30s at threshold + 2 × 5s seated sprint) OR Complete Rest.
├── Saturday: Race Day (A, B, or C Event).
└── Sunday: Long Easy Base (2–4 hrs Sub-LT1) or Second Race Day.
```

### 3. Golden Rule: "Never Pedal Harder Than Feels Good"
* If during an in-season workout, warming up at FTP feels like "pulling teeth" or seated sprint wattage is depressed by $>10–15\%$, **abort the workout immediately**.
* Downshift to low-intensity Zone 2 or return home for a nap and high-carbohydrate meal.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The Mid-Season Volume Panic:** When an athlete feels flat during mid-season races, their instinct is often to add volume and intensity. This turns functional overreaching into non-functional overtraining. More rest, not more training, restores peak power.

1. **Attempting to Peak for 12 Consecutive Weeks:** True physiological peak form can only be sustained for **2 to 4 weeks**. Attempting to maintain peak form across a 4-month calendar results in severe late-season stagnation.
2. **Treating Every B-Race as a Season-Defining Event:** Tapering for every local weekend race prevents the accumulation of base training volume, causing aerobic fitness to decay by mid-summer.
3. **Neglecting the Mid-Season Break:** Multi-discipline athletes (e.g., road racers transitioning to autumn cyclocross or gravel) must take a mandatory 7–10 day de-load in July/August to reset autonomic tone and hormonal health.
4. **Ignoring Biomechanical Clues:** Unexplained leg heaviness or localized muscle soreness in mid-season is frequently caused by slipping seatposts, worn cleats, or saddle tilt shifts.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Diagnostic Check | Observation | Prescribed Coaching Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Warm-up FTP Sensation** | 3–5 min at FTP feels smooth, breathing relaxed (RPE 5–6/10). | Proceed with planned workout or race execution. |
| **Warm-up FTP Sensation** | 3–5 min at FTP feels intolerable; heart rate suppressed. | Abort intensity; convert session to easy Zone 1/2 or full rest. |
| **Sprint Peak Power Check** | Seated 5-second sprint is $>10\%$ below normal baseline. | Neuromuscular fatigue present; eliminate all high-torque/sprint efforts. |
| **Mid-Season Motivation** | Dreading rides; irritable off the bike; sleep disturbed. | Assign mandatory 4–7 day mid-season recovery block (unstructured/off-bike). |
| **Openers Response** | Athlete historically feels sluggish the day after openers. | Shift openers to 48 hours pre-race (Thursday for Saturday race) or omit entirely. |
| **Post-Race Recovery** | Legs severely sore when walking up stairs 48h post-race. | Extend active recovery phase; postpone next hard session until soreness clears. |
