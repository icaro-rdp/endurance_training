# Heart Rate vs. Power vs. RPE for Pacing Endurance Rides — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson (Watts Doc #15)_

---

## What Is Aerobic Decoupling in Endurance Riding?

When cycling at a steady endurance pace (Zone 2 / sub-LT1), power output and heart rate initially track in close equilibrium. Over multi-hour durations, however, the human body deviates from a true thermodynamic steady state:

* **Cardiovascular Drift / Decoupling ($Pw:HR$):** A progressive upward drift in heart rate relative to constant power output, or a downward decay in power output at a constant target heart rate.
* **The Pacing Dilemma:** Endurance athletes are commonly advised to pace base rides exclusively by **Power (Watts)** (to ensure quantifiable workload) or exclusively by **Heart Rate (BPM)** (to prevent exceeding metabolic thresholds).
* **The Comprehensive Approach:** Evaluating the neuromuscular, thermoregulatory, and metabolic drivers of drift reveals that **Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE)** combined with contextual data interpretation yields superior long-term training adaptations.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
             Mechanisms of Cardiovascular Drift During Endurance Rides
  
  [Hours 0–2: Fresh Muscle Fibers]
  Small Type 1 Motor Units Active ──► High Gross Efficiency (~23–25%) ──► Stable Heart Rate & Power
                                                 │
                                 Progressive Glycogen Depletion
                                                 │
  [Hours 3–6: Fatigued Fibers & Henneman Size Principle]
  Central Nervous System Increases Drive ──► Recruits Larger Type 2A Motor Units
                                                 │
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  ▼                                              ▼                                              ▼
Lower Mechanical Efficiency              Higher Total O2 Flux Required               Thermoregulation / Heat
(More ATP / O2 burned per Watt)          (Cardiovascular strain spikes)              (Stroke volume drops, HR drifts)
```

### 1. The Henneman Size Principle & Motor Unit Substitution
* Skeletal muscle contractions are governed by the **Henneman Size Principle**: the central nervous system activates motor units in an orderly hierarchy from smallest (slow-twitch, Type 1) to largest (fast-twitch, Type 2A/2X).
* **Glycogen Depletion in Primary Fibers:** As slow-twitch motor units deplete their intracellular glycogen after 2–4 hours of continuous cycling, their individual force-generating capacity declines.
* **Secondary Motor Unit Recruitment:** To sustain the same mechanical pedal power (e.g., 200W), the brain increases motor drive, recruiting larger, secondary motor units (primarily Type 2A fibers).

### 2. Efficiency Disparities Across Fiber Types
* **Horowitz & Coyle (1994) Evidence:** Highly trained endurance cyclists matched for oxygen uptake ($~4.6\text{ L/min}$) showed significant divergence in power output based on fiber composition:
  * High Type 1 group (83% Type 1): Generated **357 Watts**.
  * Low Type 1 group (46% Type 1): Generated **335 Watts** (a 9% power deficit for the same oxygen consumption).
* **Consequence of Decoupling:** As larger, less oxidative fibers are recruited late in an endurance ride, **gross efficiency drops**. The body requires more oxygen ($VO_2$) and more blood flow per second to produce the exact same mechanical wattage, causing heart rate to drift upward.

### 3. Thermoregulation & Plasma Volume Shifts
* Extended metabolic work elevates core temperature, shunting blood flow to cutaneous vessels in the skin for evaporative cooling.
* Fluid loss via sweating reduces central blood volume (stroke volume), requiring the heart to beat faster ($Q = \text{HR} \times \text{SV}$) to maintain adequate cardiac output and muscle perfusion.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Method 1: Pacing by Power (Watts)
* **Prescription:** Ride at 60–75% of FTP (Zone 2).
* **Strengths:** Guarantees progressive motor unit recruitment. As Type 1 fibers tire, Type 2A fibers are recruited and forced to adapt oxidatively (increasing mitochondrial enzyme synthesis in fast fibers).
* **Risks:** On hot days, under-fueled rides, or when carrying residual fatigue, holding rigid power forces excessive autonomic strain, turning a recovery/base ride into a depleting threshold session.

### 2. Method 2: Pacing by Heart Rate (BPM)
* **Prescription:** Ride below tested LT1 heart rate (typically ~70–82% of HRmax).
* **Strengths:** Guarantees that internal cardiovascular stress remains strictly aerobic; excellent for recovery blocks or heat-stressed environments.
* **Risks:** Forces power output to decay substantially in the final hours, allowing the athlete to back off just when larger motor units would otherwise receive an adaptive training stimulus.

### 3. Method 3: Pacing by Perceived Exertion (RPE 4–5 / 10) — The Recommended Approach
* **Prescription:** *"Ride with purpose—feels like you are working, but easy enough that you could sustain it all day."*
* **Execution Rules:**
  1. **Start Conservatively:** The first 30–60 minutes should feel exceptionally easy (negative-splitting mindset).
  2. **Auto-Regulate:** If feeling fresh and fueled, allow power to stay strong as heart rate drifts naturally. If feeling flat, allow power to modulate downward without guilt.
  3. **Cover the Screen:** Periodically ride without power or HR displayed on the main head unit to cultivate internal bodily calibration and sensory awareness.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The Power Meter Neurosis:** Becoming distressed over a 5–10 Watt drop on an endurance ride due to fatigue, weather, or elevation. Power meters are measurement gauges, not strict taskmasters for submaximal volume.

1. **The Strict 5% Decoupling Dogma:**
   * Joe Friel's guideline that athletes must exhibit $<5\%$ decoupling on a 2-hour ride before progressing to intensity is arbitrary. An athlete might decouple by 12% over 5 hours simply due to heat and natural fiber turnover, while being fully adapted to handle high-intensity intervals.
2. **Ignoring In-Ride Fueling:**
   * Severe decoupling is frequently caused by glycogen depletion. Consuming 40–80 g/hr of carbohydrates on rides $>2$ hours stabilizes motor unit firing patterns and minimizes unnecessary heart rate drift.
3. **Erg Mode Overuse for Long Base:**
   * Locking a smart trainer into ERG mode for 4+ hours forces rigid cadence-power mechanics that prevent natural micro-adjustments in pedal biomechanics and neuromuscular recruitment.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Pacing Strategy Selection Guide

| Environmental / Athlete Context | Recommended Pacing Primary Metric | Secondary Guardrail | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cool Outdoor Weather / Well-Rested** | **Power (65–75% FTP)** or **RPE (4–5)** | HR Ceiling (Keep $<85\%$ HRmax) | Maximize oxidative motor unit turnover |
| **Extreme Heat / High Humidity** | **Heart Rate (LT1 HR)** | RPE (Modulate down) | Prevent dangerous thermal and cardiovascular strain |
| **Indoor Trainer Long Rides** | **RPE (4–5 / 10)** | Power Zone 2 | Compensate for trainer heat build-up & inertia |
| **Back-to-Back High Volume Days** | **RPE (Conservative start)** | Power Floor | Allow autoregulation based on residual fatigue |
| **Post-Illness / Return to Base** | **Heart Rate Floor/Ceiling** | Strictly low RPE | Protect immune and autonomic recovery |

### Coach & Athlete Decision Checklist

* [ ] **Define the Session Goal:** Is the objective maximal low-stress volume (lean toward HR/RPE) or progressive neuromuscular endurance (lean toward Power/RPE)?
* [ ] **Calibrate RPE Anchor:** Ensure "Zone 2" feels conversational (breathing through nose or talking in full sentences without gasping).
* [ ] **Fuel Consistently:** Ingest 40–80g carbs/hr on all endurance rides $>90$ minutes to prevent premature fiber exhaustion.
* [ ] **Interpret Decoupling Contextually:** Evaluate decoupling alongside ambient temperature, hydration, and fueling rather than viewing it as a pure fitness failure.
* [ ] **Practice Screen-Free Riding:** Dedicate at least one weekly base ride to pure RPE pacing to sharpen intuitive effort awareness.
