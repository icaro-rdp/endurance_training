# Aerobic Base & Zone 2 Training — Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kelly Moore & James McKay_

---

## What Is Aerobic Base?

"Base" does not mean Zone 2 miles — it means **the sum of all aerobic work** that has made you aerobically stronger across a season and a career. Threshold intervals, VO2max work, and long endurance rides all contribute.

**Zone 2 specifically** is the intensity below VT1 (first ventilatory threshold) — the point where you shift from predominantly fat oxidation to mixed fat + carbohydrate. It is not defined by a fixed % of FTP; it varies by athlete.

> The bigger-base-higher-peak pyramid model is misleading. Think food plate, not food pyramid: multiple aerobic systems developed together, not stacked sequentially.

---

## How to Find Your Zone 2

| Method     | Value                                                                              |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Power      | Below VT1 — no fixed % of FTP; varies by athlete                                  |
| Heart rate | 85–89% of threshold HR (Friel / Cyclists' Training Bible)                          |
| Talk test  | Conversational — not ragged, not effortless. Labored only over very long durations |
| RPE        | Sustainable for the first 2 hr; gets harder only as duration extends               |

> A lab respirometer gives the precise VT1. The talk test is the practical field approximation.

---

## How Long Can You Hold It? (TTE at Zone 2)

Duration tolerance at Zone 2 is **highly individual** and scales with training history and fueling discipline:

| Training Level     | Zone 2 TTE                                               |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Untrained / new    | 1–2 hr before pace degrades                              |
| Moderately trained | ~2–3 hr comfortably                                      |
| Well trained       | 3–4 hr with proper pacing and on-bike fueling            |
| Elite / World Tour | 5–6+ hr (stage racing) — fueling and pacing are limiting |
| Ultra athletes     | Limited more by food availability and daylight           |

**Key principle:** Zone 2 TTE extends significantly with training volume and training age — and with improved negative-split pacing. Most athletes underestimate how much early-ride intensity shortens their effective ceiling.

## When to Use Zone 2 in a Training Plan

### Good use cases

- **Early season rebuilding** — low fatigue, re-establishes aerobic rhythm after off-season
- **Between-season rest** — months of modest Zone 2 riding provides genuine recovery while maintaining aerobic stimulus
- **Maintenance between hard efforts** — 90–120 min the day between a criterium and a gran fondo is ideal; a stimulus that doesn't cost much
- **Building toward long races** — extending ride duration to match race duration is race-specific preparation
- **Athletes with ample time** — if 5–7+ hours per week are available, Zone 2 is appropriate filler volume
- **Mental recovery** — unstructured riding without interval targets is sustainable long-term for athletes who burn out on intensity

### Poor use cases

- **Time-crunched athletes** — if the longest available ride is 1.5–2 hr, Zone 2 at that duration will not produce meaningful endurance adaptation; increase intensity instead
- **As a prerequisite before intervals** — months of mandatory Zone 2 before threshold work is not required and not supported physiologically
- **As the only training modality** — gains plateau; VO2max and threshold work are required for continued development

## How to Prescribe It

### Session structure

- **Minimum useful duration:** long enough to actually challenge your endurance limit — if 2 hr feels comfortable, 2 hr produces no adaptation; push to 2.5, then 3 hr
- **Pacing:** negative split is non-negotiable — start significantly easier than target average and build throughout

| Phase       | Power guideline (example: 200 W target)     |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Hour 1      | ~50–75% of planned average (~150 W)         |
| Hours 2–3   | Build gradually toward target               |
| Final hour  | At or slightly above average — peak adaptation window |

- **Practical test:** if you're struggling to hold Zone 2 power late in long rides, start 20–30% lower for hour 1. Results are immediate.

### Progressive overload

Apply overload in this order:

1. **Extend duration** — add ~15–30 min to longest rides until target race duration is covered
2. **Then increase power** — once duration is covered, raise average watts for the same duration

Do not extend duration indefinitely. Once race-specific duration is covered, shifting to higher average power is the correct next lever.

### Frequency guidelines

| Frequency            | Notes                                                           |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1–2×/week            | Standard filler volume between hard days                        |
| Long ride 1×/week    | Primary endurance stimulus; focus on negative split and duration |
| Daily easy Zone 2    | Recovery-level, not development-level — monitor for accumulated fatigue |

---

## Key Metrics to Track Progress

| Metric                                  | What it tells you                                              |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mean max power at target duration**   | Primary endurance marker — if it rises, you're improving       |
| **Average power on negative-split rides** | Measures ability to sustain and build power over duration    |
| **RPE at target pace**                  | Same effort feeling easier = aerobic adaptation                |
| **Longest sustainable ride duration**   | Track extension toward race-specific duration                  |

> If average power on your long rides is rising and RPE is stable → stay the course.
> If duration has been covered for 3–4 weeks and power isn't moving → shift focus to intensity.

## Fueling Zone 2 Sessions

Despite low intensity, long Zone 2 rides deplete glycogen and require active fueling for adaptation and recovery.

| Priority                         | Recommendation                                                                           |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Muscle glycogen (day before)** | Eat adequate carbs 24–48 hr before long rides                                            |
| **On-bike fueling**              | Critical for rides >90–120 min; prevents blood sugar crash and enables negative-split pacing |
| **Post-session recovery**        | Fueling during the ride accelerates recovery vs. eating everything afterward             |

Practical target: treat long Zone 2 rides like long-race fueling practice — higher carbohydrate intake per hour than athletes typically expect at this intensity.

## Zone 2 vs. Other Intensities

| Intensity      | Relative fatigue | Time to exhaust well-trained | Best for                                          |
| -------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Zone 2**     | **Low**          | **3–6 hr**                   | **Base volume, duration development, recovery**   |
| Sweet spot     | Moderate         | 1.5–3 hr                     | Muscular endurance, early season, maintenance     |
| Threshold      | Moderate-high    | 30–60 min                    | FTP development, time-efficient                   |
| VO2max         | High             | 6–15 min                     | Ceiling raising, anaerobic capacity               |

## Pros and Cons Summary

**Pros**

- Very low fatigue — can be added around hard sessions without meaningful interference
- Builds duration-specific fitness when race duration is the actual limiter
- Accessible to athletes returning from injury or off-season
- Mental recovery tool for athletes burned out on structured intervals
- Supports fat oxidation and metabolic flexibility long-term

**Cons**

- Time-inefficient — requires long sessions to produce adaptation; wrong tool when time is limited
- Cannot be the only training modality — gains plateau without intensity work
- Frequently misunderstood as a prerequisite for intervals (it is not)
- Easy to repeat comfortable duration without ever applying progressive overload
- Fatigue accumulation underestimated when done daily at near-threshold rather than true Zone 2

---

## Decision Checklist Before Prescribing Zone 2 Base Rides

1. Does the athlete have **enough time per ride** to actually challenge their endurance limit?
2. Is the goal race **long enough** that duration-specific adaptation is the primary limiter?
3. Has the athlete already applied **progressive overload** — are they pushing beyond comfortable duration, or just repeating it?
4. Is there enough **weekly time** that Zone 2 is additive volume, not the only training modality?
5. Is this a phase where **mental recovery** from intervals is a genuine need?

If time is the limiting factor → move intensity up. If duration is the limiter and time is available → Zone 2 base rides are appropriate. If intervals have caused mental burnout → structured Zone 2 is a legitimate recovery tool.
