# Training and Season Planning During Major Disruptions — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson_

---

## What Is Disruptive Season Planning?

Disruptive season planning is the strategic modification of an athlete's training macrocycle and microcycles when competitive calendars are abruptly cancelled, delayed, or restructured due to external systemic events (such as pandemics, injuries, or life crises). 

Rather than clinging to a rigid, pre-planned peaking schedule designed for non-existent events, the coach and athlete must reassess the total stress budget, recalibrate psychological motivation, and pivot toward long-term physiological investments—primarily building multi-year aerobic base capacity, addressing weak physiological limiters, or executing concentrated development blocks with assured recovery.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Summation of Total Stress (Allostatic Load)
The human body does not isolate exercise stress from life, psychological, or environmental stress. All stressors stimulate the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and the sympathetic nervous system:
- **Cortisol and Sympathetic Tone:** Chronic baseline anxiety or external disruption elevates circulating glucocorticoids and catecholamines, impairing parasympathetic reactivation, blunting muscle protein synthesis, and compromising immune surveillance.
- **Immune Suppression via High-Intensity Work:** Severe high-intensity interval training (HIIT) or exhaustive training combined with high background stress produces an "open window" of immune vulnerability, increasing susceptibility to upper respiratory tract infections and systemic illness.
- **Autonomic Recovery Capacity:** When allostatic load is high, the heart rate variability (HRV) drops and resting autonomic balance shifts toward sympathetic dominance, significantly extending the time required to recover from standard training sessions.

```
Total Allostatic Load = Training Stress (kJ, Intensity, Durability)
                      + Systemic Life Stress (Anxiety, Schedule disruption)
                      + Environmental Stress (Thermal strain, Sleep deficit)
```

### 2. Multi-Year Aerobic Foundation (The "Base Is Everything" Principle)
Endurance fitness is not lost entirely when a racing season is delayed; rather, aerobic adaptations compound across consecutive seasons:
- **Structural vs. Enzymatic Adaptations:** High-intensity top-end power relies heavily on glycolytic enzymes and acute buffering capacity, which decay relatively fast (weeks). In contrast, structural adaptations—such as capillary bed density, mitochondrial volume, left ventricular eccentric remodeling, and substrate utilization kinetics—persist much longer and accumulate progressively over years of consistent base volume.
- **Opportunity of Extended Timelines:** Without the pressure of acute taper-and-peak cycles, athletes can invest extensive time into low-fatigue Zone 2 endurance mileage and structural development without the chronic fatigue of weekly racing.

### 3. Thermoregulation and Cardiovascular Drift in Indoor Training
Transitioning high-volume or high-intensity training indoors creates severe thermoregulatory challenges:
- **Lack of Convective Cooling:** Outdoors, convective air flow rapidly dissipates metabolic heat. Indoors, still air creates a boundary layer of warm, humid air around the athlete, drastically reducing sweat evaporation and convective heat transfer.
- **Cardiovascular Drift & Stroke Volume Compromise:** As core body temperature rises, the body diverts a substantial fraction of cardiac output toward cutaneous vasodilation (skin blood flow) for evaporative cooling. This reduces venous return (preload) to the heart, decreasing stroke volume and forcing heart rate to climb (cardiac drift) to sustain cardiac output, resulting in premature peripheral and central fatigue at lower power outputs.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Immediate Decision Protocol: Rest vs. Block Training

When a competitive schedule is disrupted or cancelled, execute a two-step triage:

```
                          [Schedule Disrupted / Races Cancelled]
                                          |
                   -------------------------------------------------
                  |                                                 |
         [High Life Stress /                               [Low Life Stress /
          Mid-Peak Fatigue]                                 High Motivation]
                  |                                                 |
         [Rest / Deload Week]                              [Strategic Overload Block]
                  |                                                 |
   - 4-7 days active recovery / low volume           - Focused VO2max or Threshold block
   - Drop high-intensity anaerobic work              - Guaranteed sleep and recovery isolation
   - Restore parasympathetic balance                 - Followed by extensive base building
                  |                                                 |
                   -------------------------------------------------
                                          |
                         [Transition to Base & Long-Term Building]
```

### 2. Structuring Base Volume with Schedule Flexibility (Split / Double Days)
When schedule flexibility increases (e.g., remote work or extended training availability):
- **Morning Session (Quality or High Volume):** Complete targeted structured work (e.g., standard endurance volume or threshold maintenance).
- **Afternoon / Evening Session (Zone 2 Flush):** 60–90 minutes of pure Zone 2 endurance spinning. 
- **Rule of Double Days:** Avoid stacking high-intensity anaerobic or severe glycolytic workouts in both sessions. Use the second session strictly for low-torque, low-glycogen-depleting aerobic volume.

### 3. Indoor Thermoregulation Setup
To prevent thermal strain and preserve training quality indoors:
- **Forced Convection:** Minimum of 2–3 high-velocity industrial fans positioned to cool the torso, head, and back.
- **Ambient Temperature Control:** Maintain room temperature $< 18^\circ\text{C}$ ($65^\circ\text{F}$) where possible, utilizing air conditioning.
- **Internal Cooling:** Drink cold/ice slurry fluids during hard threshold and suprathreshold sessions; use external ice water sponges over the neck and torso during extended efforts.

### 4. Low-Stakes Engagement & Virtual Stimuli
- **Virtual Time Trials & Solo Segments:** To maintain mental engagement and tactical pacing without the stress of formal competition, utilize weekly solo time-trial segments or controlled virtual events.
- **Tempo Structure for Focus:** When mental motivation for all-out efforts is absent, simple structured blocks such as $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ or $3 \times 15\text{ min}$ at Sweet Spot / Tempo ($80\text{--}88\%$ FTP) provide sufficient physiological stimulus and mental focus without inducing deep autonomic exhaustion.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Pushing High Intensity into High Life Stress:**
   - Attempting to hammer hard interval blocks while experiencing acute life anxiety, sleep deprivation, or illness risk leads directly to non-functional overreaching, chronic fatigue, and illness.
2. **Treating Indoor Power Identically to Outdoor Power Without Cooling:**
   - Expecting to hit identical outdoor power numbers on an indoor trainer without adequate airflow results in severe cardiovascular strain and mental burnout.
3. **The "All-or-Nothing" Trap:**
   - Abandoning training entirely because races are months away sacrifices valuable multi-year structural adaptations. Low-intensity endurance riding maintains cellular machinery and preserves baseline aerobic capacity.
4. **Canned Plan Inflexibility:**
   - Rigid adherence to a pre-packaged 12-week plan when real-world conditions change fails to account for individualized recovery dynamics and shifting personal priorities.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Athlete Scenario | Psychological State | Recommended Immediate Action | Primary Training Target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mid-Peaking / High Life Stress** | Anxious, fatigued, distracted | 5–7 Day Transition / Deload | Active recovery, low-intensity unstructured spins |
| **Highly Motivated / Well-Rested** | Eager, low life stress, isolated | 2–3 Week Concentrated Block | VO2max capacity or FTP extension block with dedicated recovery |
| **Demotivated / Long Timeline** | Lacking immediate purpose | Unstructured Base & Virtual Group | Multi-hour Zone 2 volume, fun solo segments, Strava TTs |
| **Time-Flexible / Home-Based** | Stable, ample daily time | Split Day / Double Sessions | Morning endurance/maintenance + afternoon 60 min Zone 2 spin |

> [!TIP]
> **Coaching Heuristic:** Whenever goal dates move into an undefined future, default to aerobic base accumulation. High-intensity sharpness can be sharpened in 4–6 weeks once dates solidify, but a robust aerobic engine takes months and years to build.
