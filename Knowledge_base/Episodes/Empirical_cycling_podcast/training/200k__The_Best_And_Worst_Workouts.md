# 200k AMA: The Best and Worst Workouts — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson_

---

## What Is "The Best and Worst Workouts"?

In endurance coaching and training literature, countless workout templates are passed down through tradition, software algorithms, or marketing dogma. The **200k Listener Milestone Episode** delivers an exhaustive, critical analysis of popular training prescriptions—categorizing them into fundamentally flawed ("Worst") versus physiologically sound ("Best") session designs.

The core premise of Empirical Cycling's coaching philosophy is that **training zones and power-duration models are descriptive, not prescriptive**. When coaches or software algorithms force athletes into rigid, formulaic wattage targets (e.g., assigning VO2max at $115\%$ of FTP or assigning microbursts at $130\%$ of FTP), they ignore individual physiological heterogeneity, neuromuscular recruitment dynamics, and the specific biological stimuli required for adaptation.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                    DESCRIPTIVE VS. PRESCRIPTIVE FRAMEWORK                     |
|                                                                               |
|  DESCRIPTIVE (Correct Approach):                                              |
|  Observe physiological state (Maximal ventilation / Steady-state clearance)   |
|  ──> Execute effort by physiological intent & RPE                             |
|  ──> Retrospectively analyze power output & adapt progression                 |
|                                                                               |
|  PRESCRIPTIVE (Flawed Approach):                                              |
|  Apply fixed mathematical formula (e.g., Target = 120% FTP)                   |
|  ──> Forces arbitrary power targets regardless of individual physiology       |
|  ──> Results in under-stimulation (aerobically) or early failure (anaerobically)|
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Fallacy of VO2max as a Percentage of FTP
A widespread coaching error is prescribing VO2max workouts as a fixed percentage of Functional Threshold Power (e.g., $4 \times 5\text{ min}$ at $110\text{--}120\%$ FTP).
- **Fractional Utilization Variance:** As demonstrated in classic exercise physiology literature (e.g., Coyle et al.), an athlete's threshold power as a percentage of $\dot{V}\text{O}_{2\max}$ power spans a wide physiological range—typically **$60\%$ to $85\%+$**.
  - For an athlete whose FTP is $85\%$ of $\dot{V}\text{O}_{2\max}$, riding at $115\%$ FTP is near maximal capacity and may cause premature failure within 2–3 minutes due to rapid $W'$ depletion.
  - For an athlete whose FTP is $65\%$ of $\dot{V}\text{O}_{2\max}$, riding at $115\%$ FTP is barely above threshold and will never elicit maximal oxygen uptake ($\dot{V}\text{O}_{2\max}$) or maximal stroke volume.
- **Cardiovascular Stimulus Requirements:** To stimulate central cardiac remodeling (eccentric left ventricular hypertrophy, maximal stroke volume, and maximal cardiac output $Q = HR \times SV$), the athlete must reach near-$100\%\ \dot{V}\text{O}_{2\max}$ and sustain maximal ventilation ("gasping like a fish in a boat"). High pedaling cadence (**105–120+ RPM**) optimizes venous return via the skeletal muscle pump and reduces peripheral muscular fatigue.

### 2. Low Cadence "Strength Endurance" vs. Neuromuscular Force
- **Force Deficit on the Bike:** Even during maximal aerobic intervals performed at $50\text{--}60\text{ RPM}$, peak pedal forces rarely exceed **$50\text{--}80\text{ kg}$** across a pedal stroke. In contrast, heavy resistance training in the gym (squats, deadlifts, leg presses at $\ge 80\%\ \text{1RM}$) involves forces in excess of **$150\text{--}300+\text{ kg}$**.
- **Mechanotransduction & Muscle Fiber Recruitment:** On-the-bike "strength endurance" (popularized by historical European coaching methods) does not generate sufficient mechanical tension to stimulate myofibrillar protein synthesis, tendon stiffness, or high-threshold motor unit recruitment. Instead, it merely introduces unnecessary joint and patellar tendon strain while elevating peripheral metabolic fatigue.

### 3. Ramp Tests: The Flaws of Power-Only Threshold Prediction
- **Confounding by Anaerobic Work Capacity ($W'$):** Ramp protocols (e.g., increasing power by 20–30 W every minute until failure) measure the power reached at exhaustion ($P_{\max,\text{ramp}}$). An algorithm multiplying $P_{\max,\text{ramp}} \times 0.75$ assumes an identical contribution of anaerobic capacity across all riders.
- **Ramp Kinetics & Step Rate:** A steep ramp protocol favors athletes with large $W'$ and high glycolytic flux ($V\text{La}_{\max}$), yielding an artificially inflated FTP. A shallow ramp protocol causes premature fatigue via accumulated carbohydrate depletion before maximal aerobic power is reached. Power-only ramp tests accurately identify true steady-state lactate threshold (MLSS/FTP) in only ~**40–55%** of athletes.

### 4. Severe Degradation vs. Quality in Anaerobic & High-Intensity Sessions
- **The "Pain for the Sake of Pain" Trap:** Workouts like 16 repetitions of 1-minute all-out with 1-minute rest ("Revolver" style) rapidly exhaust anaerobic stores ($W'$). By repetition 4–6, power collapses to near-threshold or tempo levels ($180\text{--}220\text{ W}$), transforming what was intended as an anaerobic or maximal aerobic workout into an agonizing, uncoordinated slog that induces extreme autonomic stress with low cellular adaptation.
- **Recovery Kinetics:** To develop neuromuscular power or anaerobic capacity, rest intervals must be long enough (e.g., 1:3 to 1:5 work-to-rest ratio) to resynthesize phosphocreatine (PCr) and clear intracellular acidosis, ensuring that every subsequent repetition maintains high mechanical power and motor unit recruitment.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Correcting VO2max Workout Design
- **Execution Rule:** Prescribe intervals as **all-out, self-paced efforts** (e.g., $4\text{ to }5 \times 4\text{--}5\text{ min}$ or $5 \times 3\text{ min}$) with equal or slightly longer rest ($1:1$ to $1:1.5$).
- **Cadence:** Maintain **105–120 RPM**. If power drops toward the end of an interval or final set, focus on maintaining maximal breathing and cadence rather than gritting teeth at low cadence to hit a predetermined wattage number.

```
VO2max Protocol Example:
- Warm-up: 20 min progressive Z1-Z2 + 2-3x 30s openers
- Main Set: 5 x 4 min ALL-OUT (Target: Maximal ventilation, 110+ RPM)
- Rest: 4-5 min soft pedaling (Zone 1) between reps
- Cool-down: 15-20 min easy spin
```

### 2. Threshold Progressive Overload (Extending TiZ)
Instead of endlessly repeating $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ and attempting to force wattage higher, extend continuous Time-in-Zone (TiZ) at a verified threshold:
- **Progression Schedule:**
  - Week 1: $3 \times 15\text{ min}$ (45 min TiZ)
  - Week 2: $2 \times 25\text{ min}$ (50 min TiZ)
  - Week 3: $1 \times 35\text{ min} + 1 \times 20\text{ min}$ (55 min TiZ)
  - Week 4: $1 \times 50\text{--}60\text{ min}$ continuous TTE test / benchmark

```
Alternative for Athlete Monotony / Mental Barrier: Mixed Tempo-Threshold Stepping
- Format: 2 x 30 min as [3 min @ 97-100% FTP + 2 min @ 80-85% Tempo] continuously.
- Reduces psychological dread of a static wattage target while accumulating 60 min of high sub-threshold metabolic work.
```

### 3. Proper Microburst & Anaerobic Session Structure
- **For Anaerobic Capacity ($W'$):** 
  - $30\text{s on} / 30\text{s off}$ or $15\text{s on} / 15\text{s off}$ performed in sets of **3 to 6 minutes** (e.g., $3 \times 6\text{ min}$ with 5–8 min full recovery between sets).
  - Pacing: Self-paced maximal repeatability, tracking average power across sets.
- **For Race Durability & Specificity:**
  - Introduce intermittent bursts (e.g., 5–10s explosive accelerations) every 10–15 minutes throughout a 3–4 hour Zone 2 endurance ride. This conditions high-threshold motor unit recruitment under glycogen depletion without compromising base aerobic recovery.

### 4. Durability & Fatigue Resistance Testing
- **In-Ride Durability Check:**
  - Format: A 4-hour endurance ride containing a **10-minute threshold effort performed at the end of every hour** (4 x 10 min total).
  - Diagnostic Value: Exposes late-ride power degradation, hydration deficits, and poor fueling strategies under accumulated work ($>2,500\text{ kJ}$).

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Relying on Single-Watt Precision:**
   - Designing workouts with minute power alternations (e.g., switching between 255 W and 260 W) creates false precision. Power meters carry a $\pm 1\text{--}2\%$ error margin, and biological homeostasis fluctuates daily with temperature, hydration, and circadian rhythms.
2. **Treating Zone Boundaries as Binary Switches:**
   - Believing that riding 1 Watt below Zone 2 allows full active recovery, or that exceeding Zone 2 by 2 Watts ruins endurance adaptations. Physiological systems operate along a continuous metabolic spectrum.
3. **Prescribing "Go Until You Fail / Vomit":**
   - Forcing athletes into catastrophic failure (e.g., $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ all-out maximal tests in a single day) creates massive autonomic and endocrine disruption that impairs subsequent training quality for days.
4. **Substituting Low-Cadence Grinds for Heavy Lifting:**
   - Attempting to build force and muscular strength via 50 RPM cycling intervals instead of performing barbell squats and deadlifts in the gym.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **VO2max Intervals** | Fixed target: $4 \times 5\text{ min}$ @ $115\%$ FTP | All-out, self-paced $4\text{--}5 \times 4\text{ min}$ @ $110+\text{ RPM}$ | Maximizes ventilation and stroke volume; avoids mismatch from individual fractional utilization. |
| **Threshold Training** | Fixed $2 \times 20\text{ min}$, constantly forcing wattage higher | Progressive TiZ ($3 \times 15\text{m} \to 2 \times 25\text{m} \to 1 \times 50\text{m}$) at stable FTP | Stimulates sustained mitochondrial flux and increases Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE). |
| **Microbursts / Short Intervals** | $40/20\text{s}$ @ $130\%$ FTP for 10 min continuous | $30/30\text{s}$ in 4–6 min blocks with full recovery between sets | Maintains high mechanical power and PCr recovery; avoids degraded power slogging. |
| **On-Bike "Strength"** | $5 \times 5\text{ min}$ @ $50\text{ RPM}$ as a substitute for gym work | Heavy barbell squats/deadlifts ($3\text{--}6\text{ reps}$ @ $\ge 80\%\text{ 1RM}$) | Generates true neuromuscular overload and rate of force development without cycling fatigue traps. |
| **FTP Assessment** | Power-only 1-min step ramp test ($P_{\max} \times 0.75$) | Progressive baseline test ($35\text{--}50+\text{ min}$) or verified TiZ | Eliminates confounding by anaerobic capacity ($W'$) and ramp step kinetics. |
| **Endurance Rides** | Hammering at the upper boundary ($75\%\text{ FTP}$) every day | Riding at the conversational "ceiling of easy" ($55\text{--}70\%\text{ FTP}$) | Minimizes autonomic stress; preserves glycogen and freshness for high-intensity days. |

> [!TIP]
> **Coaching Maxim for High-Intensity Interval Quality:** Always prioritize the **intended physiological stimulus** (maximal oxygen uptake, steady-state clearance, or peak rate of force development) over arbitrary mathematical formulas. When an interval set deteriorates into low-wattage suffering, the productive training stimulus has ended.
