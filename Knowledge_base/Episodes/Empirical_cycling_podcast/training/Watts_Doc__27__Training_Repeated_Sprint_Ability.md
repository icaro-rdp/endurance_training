# Training Repeated Sprint Ability & Motor Unit Oxidative Capacity — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson (Watts Doc #27)_

---

## What Is Repeated Sprint Ability (RSA)?

**Repeated Sprint Ability (RSA)** is the physiological capacity to execute successive short, high-power surges or sprints separated by brief recovery intervals with minimal decrement in peak and average power output.

While steady-state endurance is traditionally defined and measured by Functional Threshold Power (FTP), racing disciplines such as **criteriums, cyclocross, cross-country mountain biking (XCO), and aggressive road races** are fundamentally stochastic. In these events, performance is dictated by an athlete's ability to repeatedly recruit, clear metabolites from, and re-energize high-threshold motor units.

```
                      The Stochastic Demands Spectrum
 ┌──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┐
 │ Steady-State Endurance       │ Repeated Sprint Ability (RSA)│
 ├──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┤
 │ • Individual Time Trials     │ • Criterium corner exits     │
 │ • Long continuous climbs     │ • Cyclocross barrier surges  │
 │ • Low torque, steady cadence │ • MTB steep technical ramps  │
 │ • Recruits Type I / IIa units│ • Recruits Type IIx units    │
 └──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Henneman's Size Principle & Aerobic Training of Fast-Twitch Fibers
According to **Henneman’s Size Principle**, motor units are recruited in a strict hierarchical order from smallest (low-threshold, fatigue-resistant Type I) to largest (high-threshold, highly fatigable Type IIx) as force/power demands escalate:

```
            Motor Unit Recruitment & Metabolic Fate
            
  [Low Force / Steady State] ──► Recruits Type I Fibers (High Mitochondria, High Fat Ox)
  
  [High Force / Sprint Surge] ──► Recruits Type IIx Fibers (Glycolytic, High PCr Flux)
                                            │
               ┌────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┐
               ▼                                                         ▼
     [Unadapted Type IIx]                                      [Aerobically Adapted Type IIx]
  • Rapid Glycogen Depletion                               • High Mitochondrial Density
  • Massive Lactate/H+ Efflux                              • High Intra-Fiber Fat/Lactate Ox
  • Premature Contractile Failure                          • Rapid PCr Resynthesis (Aerobic)
```

* **The Type IIx Adaptability Principle:** Fast-twitch (Type II) fibers are not locked into purely anaerobic glycolytic metabolism. As demonstrated in elite cross-country skiers and rowers, chronic recruitment under aerobic demand stimulates mitochondrial biogenesis, capillarization, and fat oxidation capacity in Type II fibers matching that of Type I fibers.
* **Intra-Fiber Glycogen Sparing:** When large motor units possess high mitochondrial density, they generate ~30–32 ATP per glucose molecule via oxidative phosphorylation rather than yielding only 2 net ATP via fast glycolysis. This preserves muscle glycogen and delays end-race neuromuscular failure.

### 2. Phosphocreatine (PCr) Resynthesis Kinetics
During a 5–15 second maximal surge, the immediate energy supply is driven by the ATP-PCr phosphagen system:
* **Aerobic Dependence:** The recovery and resynthesis of phosphocreatine during the subsequent 15–60 seconds of low-intensity spinning is **100% dependent on mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation**.
* **Capillary Delivery:** Greater capillary density and arterial oxygen delivery accelerate PCr recovery half-time ($t_{1/2}$), enabling higher wattage on subsequent sprint repetitions.

## Practical Application & Workout Prescriptions

```
                 Repeated Sprint Training Hierarchy
                 
   [Level 1: Aerobic Base] ──► Stochastic / Fartlek Tempo (Continuous aerobic)
   [Level 2: Over-Unders]  ──► 30s Over / 2m Under (Threshold clearance)
   [Level 3: Intermittent] ──► 15s On / 15s Off (Micro-interval capacity)
   [Level 4: Pure RST]     ──► 5-10s All-Out Every 30-60s (Neuromuscular RSA)
```

### 1. Stochastic / Fartlek Tempo ("Tough as Nails")
* **Purpose:** Introduces terrain-driven, variable power output without excessive neuromuscular exhaustion.
* **Protocol:** 90–180 minutes of outdoor rolling terrain or singletrack. Push aggressively up short rises (Sweet Spot / Vo2max for 30–90 seconds) and recover at steady endurance pace (Zone 2) on flats/descents.

### 2. Threshold with Bursts / Over-Unders
* **Purpose:** Conditions high-threshold motor units to clear and consume lactate while working at threshold.
* **Protocol:** $3 \times 20\text{ min}$ structured as:
  * Every 2 minutes, perform a **15–20 second surge at 130–150% FTP**, immediately returning to **90–95% FTP** without dropping into recovery spinning.

### 3. Intermittent Micro-Intervals (15/15s & 30/15s)
* **Purpose:** Maximizes cumulative high-force mechanical work and aerobic flux across 10–20 minute blocks.
* **Protocol:** $2\text{ to }3 \text{ sets of } 10\text{ to }15\text{ minutes}$ of $[15\text{s ON} / 15\text{s OFF}]$ or $[30\text{s ON} / 15\text{s OFF}]$.
* **Pacing Rule:** Do not sprint all-out at 1,000 W on rep 1. Self-regulate the "ON" portion at roughly 5-minute to 8-minute maximum sustainable power to prevent power decay across the set.

### 4. Repeated Sprint Training (RST)
* **Purpose:** Neuromuscular sprint durability under severe metabolic fatigue.
* **Protocol:**
  * **Format:** $2\text{ sets of } 10 \times [10\text{s Max Sprint} / 50\text{s Zone 1 Recovery}]$.
  * **Progression:** Reduce recovery to $[10\text{s Sprint} / 20\text{s Recovery}]$ or increase reps to $15 \times$.

## Tracking RSA & Quantifying Stochastic Fitness

```
                     Tracking Metrics Comparison
 ┌──────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────┐
 │ Metric / Method      │ Capability                  │ Major Limitation         │
 ├──────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┤
 │ Normalized Power     │ Calculates 4th-power stress │ Distorts short <30s      │
 │ (NP / xPower)        │ for medium steady surges    │ efforts; course-biased   │
 ├──────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┤
 │ Peak Power Decay     │ Measures drop from Sprint 1 │ Sensitive to motivation  │
 │ Analysis             │ to Sprint N across a set    │ and initial over-pacing  │
 ├──────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┤
 │ Fixed Test Loop      │ Field simulation of laps on │ Weather, wind, and soil  │
 │ (Time vs. Power)     │ CX/MTB/Crit course          │ conditions introduce var.│
 └──────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Why Normalized Power (NP) Fails for RSA
Normalized Power applies a **rolling 30-second average** raised to the 4th power:
* For efforts lasting 5–15 seconds, the 30-second rolling window severely dilutes the metabolic impact of the sprint.
* Conversely, on courses with long 40-second climbs followed by zero-power descents, NP artificially balloons 30–50 W above true metabolic steady-state capacity ("NP busting").

### 2. Peak Power Decay Tracking
* Track the power drop across successive repetitions within a session (e.g., Rep 1 = 950 W, Rep 5 = 820 W, Rep 10 = 650 W).
* As RSA improves through a training block, **the slope of power decay flattens**, allowing the athlete to maintain $>80\%$ of peak sprint power on the 10th repetition.

### 3. The Dedicated Test Loop Protocol
* Establish a repeatable, technical 15–20 minute loop (cyclocross course, short-track MTB trail, or criterium circuit).
* Perform a 40–45 minute maximal simulation every 4–6 weeks. Track lap time progression, mean cadence, and power stability.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **The "Hero Sprint" Blowout:** Executing the first 2–3 intervals at 100% maximal sprint power (1,200 W), resulting in severe metabolic acidosis and power collapsing to 350 W for the remainder of the workout.
2. **Neglecting the Aerobic Foundation:** Attempting to build RSA purely through high-intensity intervals without sufficient Zone 2 volume. Mitochondrial density and capillary beds developed via low-intensity volume drive fast PCr resynthesis.
3. **Chronic Year-Round High-Intensity Traps:** Performing maximal intermittent interval sessions year-round generates deep autonomic nervous system fatigue, degrades fat oxidation capacity, and induces systemic overtraining.
4. **Treating Low Cadence as Gym Strength:** Grinding at 40 RPM on the bike produces negligible force compared to bilateral heavy squats or deadlifts while generating excessive joint shear.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Variable | Recommendation | Physiological Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Initial Pacing (15/15s)** | 5-min to 8-min power (~125–140% FTP) | Prevents premature Type II motor unit failure and maximizes total set kilojoules. |
| **RST Sprint Duration** | 5 to 10 seconds maximal | Depletes phosphocreatine without creating massive lactic acid accumulation. |
| **RST Recovery Duration** | 20 to 50 seconds Zone 1 spinning | Relies 100% on aerobic oxidative phosphorylation for PCr resynthesis. |
| **Weekly Frequency** | 1 session/week (base) to 2 sessions/week (build) | Fast-twitch motor units require 48–72 hours to resolve neuromuscular fatigue. |
| **Monitoring Method** | Peak power decay & fixed-course lap times | Bypasses NP mathematical distortion and measures field durability. |
| **Off-Road Trail Training** | 1x weekly technical trail ride | Develops discipline-specific neuromuscular coordination and terrain reading. |
