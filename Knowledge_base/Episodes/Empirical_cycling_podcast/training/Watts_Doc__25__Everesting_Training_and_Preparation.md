# Everesting Training, Physiology, and Preparation — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson (Watts Doc #25)_

---

## What Is Everesting?

**Everesting** is an ultra-endurance cycling challenge in which a rider ascends and descends a single chosen hill repeatedly until accumulating **8,848 meters (29,029 feet)** of elevation gain in a single continuous activity.

```
                         Everesting Constraints
 ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ • Elevation Target: Exactly 8,848 m (29,029 ft) in one activity        │
 │ • Single Segment: Repeats of the same road ascent/descent              │
 │ • No Sleep: Continuous clock time (typically 9 to 16+ hours)           │
 │ • Work Profile: Intermittent climbing (Zone 2/Tempo) + resting descent │
 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

From a physiological and tactical standpoint, Everesting is an **intermittent ultra-endurance hill-climb time trial**. Unlike flat ultra-distance events, power output is polarized between sustained uphill efforts against gravity and zero-power descents. Success is governed by metabolic substrate management, fatigue resistance (durability), mechanical gearing, and pacing discipline.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Metabolic Demand & Energy Expenditure Calculus
A standard Everesting attempt lasts between 9 and 16 hours. For a typical 75 kg rider producing ~200–225 W on climbs:
* **Total Work:** $\approx 7,500\text{ to }10,500\text{ kJ}$ executed through the pedals.
* **Basal & Total Caloric Burn:** Adding basal metabolic rate ($\approx 100\text{ kcal/hr} \times 12\text{ hr} = 1,200\text{ kcal}$) yields a gross caloric requirement of **9,000–12,000 kcal**.
* **Substrate Partitioning:** Even with high aerobic fat oxidation rates, carbohydrate oxidation accounts for 40–60% of total energy output across repeated uphill repetitions.
* **Glycogen Depletion vs. Exogenous Fueling:** Muscle and liver glycogen stores store ~2,000 kcal (~500 g glycogen) and are depleted within 2–3 hours. The remaining 4,000–6,000 kcal of carbohydrate flux must be supplied **exogenously**:

$$\text{Carbohydrate Target} = \frac{4,000\text{ to }4,500\text{ kcal}}{4\text{ kcal/g}} \div 11\text{ to }12\text{ hours} \approx 85\text{ to }100\text{ g CHO/hour}$$

```
                Energy Expenditure Distribution (12-Hour Ride)
                
  Total Metabolic Cost: ~10,000 kcal
  ┌───────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┐
  │ Fat Oxidation (~4,500 kcal)   │ Exogenous CHO (~4,500 kcal)   │
  ├───────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┤
  │ Endogenous Glycogen Reserve (~2,000 kcal, depleted early)     │
  └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2. Muscle Pump, Cadence, and Motor Unit Torque Fatigue
Gravity on steep gradients ($>10\%$) acts as a relentless resistance brake. If an athlete runs out of gears and is forced to climb at 50–65 RPM:
* **High Torque Demands:** Pedal force per stroke increases dramatically.
* **Motor Unit Recruitment:** To produce high torque at low velocity, the central nervous system must recruit **high-threshold Type II motor units**. These fast-twitch fibers deplete glycogen rapidly, produce excess metabolic byproducts, and exhibit poor long-term durability.
* **Cadence Preservation:** Maintaining a cadence of **75–85+ RPM** distributes mechanical work across smaller, highly oxidative Type I motor units, protecting the neuromuscular system across 10+ hours.

### 3. Biomechanics, Hip Angle, and Posterior Chain Strain
Climbing on steep gradients rotates the bike frame upward relative to gravity:
* **Closed Hip Angle:** When the torso remains in a low road position on an 8–14% incline, the angle between the femur and pelvis at Top Dead Center (TDC) closes significantly.
* **Lower Back Stress:** This places enormous sustained tension on the erector spinae, gluteus medius, and quadratus lumborum. Without postural conditioning and appropriate cockpit stack, lower back spasm or severe tendinopathy frequently forces abandonment.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Equipment & Gearing Mechanics
Athletes must select gearing that allows riding at **65–75% FTP at $\ge 75\text{ RPM}$ on the steepest pitch of the climb**.

```
 Gearing Prescriptions for Everesting:
 ┌─────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┐
 │ Average Gradient    │ Front Chainring Setup     │ Rear Cassette Range     │
 ├─────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
 │ 6% – 8% Moderate    │ Compact (50/34T)          │ 11–32T / 11–34T         │
 │ 9% – 12% Steep      │ Sub-Compact (46/30T or 1x)│ 11–36T / 11–40T         │
 │ 13% – 18%+ Extreme  │ MTB 1x (32T–36T) or 44/28T│ 10–42T / 10–51T (1:1+)  │
 └─────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘
```

* **The Bailout Principle:** Always have at least **one to two easier gears** than normal fresh climbing requires. When fatigue sets in at hour 9, power drops by 30–50 W; without lower gears, cadence will collapse into a destructive low-RPM grind.

### 2. Nutritional Protocol: Composition and Delivery
* **Carbohydrate Flux:** Ingest **80–100 g CHO/hr** utilizing a 1:0.8 maltodextrin-to-fructose or 2:1 glucose-to-fructose formulation.
* **Solid vs. Liquid Intake:** Pure liquid/gel diets often cause nausea and osmotic gut distress after 5–6 hours. Incorporate solid, real-food sources (rice cakes, boiled salted potatoes, small PB&J sandwiches).
* **The Protein Requirement:** Ingest small amounts of protein (5–10 g every 2–3 hours, e.g., nut butters, turkey roll-ups) to maintain gastric satiety, blunt circulating branched-chain amino acid depletion, and prevent central fatigue.
* **Hydration & Electrolytes:** Consume sodium at 500–1000 mg/L depending on ambient temperature to prevent hyponatremia and cramping.

```
                      12-Hour Everesting Nutrition Timeline
                      
  Hour 0-4: High CHO Liquid/Gel (80-90g/h) + Electrolytes
  Hour 4-8: Transition to Solid Carbohydrates + Small Protein Doses (Nut butters, Rice cakes)
  Hour 8-12: Palatable Comfort Carbs, Sodium, Light Caffeine Boosts as Needed
```

### 3. Training Periodization & Key Sessions
Preparation requires building extensive muscular endurance, low-cadence torque tolerance, and metabolic efficiency over an 8–12 week block:

* **Session 1: Extensive Sweet Spot / Threshold Repeats**
  * Structure: $4\text{ to }6 \times 15\text{ min}$ at 88–92% FTP (5 min rest), building up to $3 \times 30\text{ min}$.
* **Session 2: Low-Cadence Torque Intervals (Heavy Torque Endurance)**
  * Structure: $3 \times 20\text{ min}$ at 80–85% FTP alternating 2 min at 55–60 RPM and 3 min at 85–90 RPM to adapt fast-twitch fibers for low-velocity fat oxidation.
* **Session 3: Long Durability Endurance Rides**
  * Structure: 5–8 hours in Zone 2 with the final 60–90 minutes including steady climbing tempo efforts.

### 4. Reconnaissance Ride Architecture
* **Recon Ride 1 (6–8 Weeks Out):** 4–5 hours on the exact target hill. Validate gearing, turnaround points, line selection, cell coverage, and toilet access.
* **Recon Ride 2 (3–4 Weeks Out):** 60–75% of total volume (e.g., 7–8 hours, ~5,000–6,000 m climbing). Test the full race-day nutrition plan, apparel, lighting, and pacing discipline.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

```
                      The Everesting Fatigue Trap
                      
  [Start Reps 1-5 at Sweet Spot (85-90% FTP)]
                     │
                     ▼
  [Rapid Glycogen Depletion + High Torque Fatigue]
                     │
                     ▼
  [Hour 6 Power Collapse (Drops to <50% FTP, 45 RPM Grind)]
                     │
                     ▼
  [GI Distress / Hypothermia on Descents ──► DNF]
```

1. **The Fast-Start Pacing Trap:** Starting repetitions at Sweet Spot or Threshold (~85–95% FTP) because it feels effortlessly easy in the first 2 hours. This rapidly drains glycogen and destroys durability. Target strictly **65–75% FTP**.
2. **Under-Gearing:** Relying on standard road gears (e.g., 36x28T). When legs fatigue, the rider is forced to mash at 50 RPM, causing severe quadriceps and patellar tendon strain.
3. **Hypothermia on Descents:** Repeatedly cooling down during 5–15 minute zero-power descents causes severe shivering and shivering thermogenesis, which expends significant glycogen. Pack wind vests, jackets, and thermal layers at base camp.
4. **Poor Segment Selection:** Choosing roads with stop signs, heavy vehicular traffic, blind hairpins requiring heavy braking, or false flats that add distance without vertical gain.
5. **Ignoring Bike Fit:** Retaining an aggressive low road bike cockpit stack, leading to acute lumbar spasms, neck pain, and hand neuropathy.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Variable | Target / Recommendation | Risk / Failure Mode |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Climbing Pacing** | 65% – 75% FTP (Normalized Power $\le 0.70$ IF) | Pacing $>80\%$ FTP results in early bonk and muscular failure. |
| **Cadence Target** | 75 – 85+ RPM on climbs | $<65$ RPM causes severe high-torque Type II motor unit fatigue. |
| **Gearing Ratio** | $\le 1.0$ (e.g., 30T front / 34–40T rear) | Running out of gears forces cadence collapse. |
| **Carbohydrate Rate** | 80 – 100 g/hr (fructose:glucose mixed) | Insufficient intake leads to glycogen depletion within 3 hours. |
| **Solid Food & Protein**| Add real food + 5–10g protein every 2–3h | Pure gels lead to osmotic gut distress and nausea. |
| **Road Gradient** | 9% – 13% average, straight with high visibility | $<6\%$ adds excessive distance; $>16\%$ creates extreme torque demands. |
| **Recon Testing** | 2 dedicated tests (4h and 7–8h) | Untested nutrition or mechanical setups fail on race day. |
| **Base Camp Setup** | Coolers, spare wheels, tubes, clothes, first aid | Logistics delays waste critical daylight and momentum. |
