# Ten Minute Tips #39: Individualizing Training For Crits, CX, And MTB — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Erica Zaveta (Ten Minute Tips #39)_

---

## What Is High-Intensity Discipline Individualization?

High-intensity disciplines—such as Criteriums (Road), Cyclocross (CX), and Cross-Country Mountain Biking (XC MTB)—are characterized by repeated supramaximal surges ($>120–150\%\text{ FTP}$), violent corner exits, low-cadence high-torque bursts, and complex technical handling under extreme cardiovascular strain.

Coaching athletes across these disciplines requires moving beyond one-size-fits-all interval prescriptions to address two core variables:
1. **Physiological Phenotype:** Balancing aerobic diesel engines versus explosive fast-twitch profiles.
2. **Calendar Architecture (The Dual-Season Challenge):** Navigating the multi-discipline calendar (spring/summer road into autumn/winter cyclocross) without accumulating catastrophic neuroendocrine fatigue.

```
                  THE HIGH-INTENSITY DISCIPLINE CONTINUUM
    Road Criteriums                 Cyclocross (CX)                 XC MTB (XCO)
┌──────────────────────┐        ┌──────────────────────┐        ┌──────────────────────┐
│ • Smooth pavement    │        │ • Variable traction  │        │ • Extended climbing  │
│ • Predictable torque │  ───►  │ • Dismounts / Runs   │  ───►  │ • Sustained VO2 / FTP│
│ • Draft-dominated    │        │ • High-torque exits  │        │ • Technical descents │
│ • High-speed pack    │        │ • 45–60 min all-out  │        │ • 75–90 min duration │
└──────────────────────┘        └──────────────────────┘        └──────────────────────┘
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Phenotypic Differences in High-Intensity Preparation
Athletes respond radically differently to pre-race high-intensity training:
* **The Aerobic Diesel Profile:** Athletes with exceptional aerobic capacity and slow-twitch efficiency often express high anaerobic repeatability ($140–150\%\text{ FTP}$ surges over short climbs) purely from a massive base and long Sweet Spot/FTP progressions. Forcing these athletes through high volumes of anaerobic capacity intervals generates autonomic fatigue without additive race fitness.
* **The Fast-Twitch / Explosive Profile:** Athletes reliant on glycolytic power decay rapidly without neuromuscular stimulus. They require regular micro-burst intervals (e.g., $15\text{s on } / 15\text{s off}$) and maximal sprint work, but their total weekly volume must be tightly capped to protect recovery.

### 2. The Dual-Season Periodization Framework
Transitioning from Summer Road/Crit to Fall/Winter Cyclocross is the most frequent point of athletic breakdown:

```
                            THE DUAL-SEASON TIMELINE
   Spring / Early Summer             Mid-Summer                  Fall / Winter
    (Road & Crit Season)             (The Pivot)              (Cyclocross Season)
┌───────────────────────────┐   ┌───────────────────┐   ┌───────────────────────────┐
│ March ────────► July      │   │ Late July (1–2 Wk)│   │ August ──────► December   │
│ • High-volume base        │──►│ • MANDATORY REST  │──►│ • August: Specific CX prep│
│ • Crit & Road racing      │   │ • 0 Watts / Hike  │   │ • Sept–Dec: Racing blocks │
│ • TTE / Threshold builds  │   │ • Reset CNS Tone  │   │ • Skills sessions @ Z1    │
└───────────────────────────┘   └───────────────────┘   └───────────────────────────┘
```

### 3. Mechanical & Neuromuscular Cost of Off-Road Surfaces
* **Uneven Torque Demands:** Producing 300 Watts on bumpy turf or loose gravel requires erratic, non-uniform muscular recruitment. The continuous micro-adjustments engage stabilizing musculature and recruit high-threshold motor units earlier than on smooth tarmac.
* **Eccentric Impact in Cyclocross:** Running with a bike, barrier jumping, and remounting introduce eccentric muscular loading, creating delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) and prolonged glycogen depletion that pure road cycling avoids.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Hot Laps vs. Skills Drills: The Separation Protocol

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   CX & MTB TRAINING SESSION MATRIX                      │
├─────────────────┬───────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┤
│ Session Type    │ Primary Target    │ Pacing & Execution Protocol       │
├─────────────────┼───────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
│ Skills Practice │ Technique, Line   │ Zone 1 / Low Zone 2 (RPE 1–2/10). │
│ (Low Fatigue)   │ Choice, Barriers, │ 60–90 min; zero cardiovascular    │
│                 │ Mounts/Dismounts  │ strain; video review of form.     │
├─────────────────┼───────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
│ Race Hot Laps   │ Metabolic & Race- │ All-Out Race Pace (RPE 9–10/10).  │
│ (High Fatigue)  │ Specific Torque   │ 2–3 sets of 10–15 min; treated    │
│                 │ Repeatability     │ as a full high-intensity workout. │
├─────────────────┼───────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
│ Flow Laps       │ Corner Speed &    │ Sub-Race Pace (RPE 4–5/10). Focus │
│ (Mid Fatigue)   │ Momentum Carry    │ on "no brakes" and smooth lines.  │
└─────────────────┴───────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2. Microcycle Architecture for Mid-Season CX / MTB

```
In-Season Cyclocross Microcycle:
├── Monday: Full Rest Day (0 Watts; off-bike recovery).
├── Tuesday: Easy Base Volume (1.5–2 hrs Sub-LT1 / Z2) + 20 min Z1 barrier drills.
├── Wednesday: High-Intensity Day:
│   ├── Option A: Midweek Practice Crit / CX Hot Laps (2 × 15 min all-out).
│   └── Option B: Structured Micro-Intervals (3 sets of 10 × 15s on / 15s off @ 130–150% FTP).
├── Thursday: Active Recovery Spin (45–60 min Z1 <55% FTP).
├── Friday: Autoregulated Openers (30–45 min easy + 3 × 10s sprint + 2 min @ threshold) OR Rest.
├── Saturday: Race Day 1 (or Course Recon / Openers).
└── Sunday: Race Day 2 (or Long Sub-LT1 Endurance Ride 3–4 hrs).
```

### 3. In-Season Strength Maintenance
* **The Goal:** Maintain lean muscle mass and bone mineral density, not 1RM records.
* **Dosage:** 1 session per week, 2–3 sets of 3–5 reps at 70–80% 1RM (stopping 2–3 reps in reserve, $RIR \ge 2$).
* **Timing:** Schedule on the afternoon of an intensity day (e.g., Wednesday post-intervals) to keep hard days hard and preserve full rest days.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The Midweek Hot Lap Trap:** Attending an all-out Wednesday night CX practice while racing double-headers every Saturday and Sunday produces three race-effort days per week. This guarantees autonomic exhaustion, immune breakdown, or illness by October/November.

1. **Racing Every Other Weekend:** Alternating race weekends prevents an athlete from executing a true progressive training block or achieving a complete physical de-load. Prefer 3-week racing blocks followed by 3-week dedicated training/recovery blocks.
2. **Confusing Technical Drills with Cardiovascular Workouts:** Turning barrier and dismount practice into a high-heart-rate interval session degrades movement precision and increases crash risk.
3. **Overusing Creatine in Road/Crit/CX:** While creatine enhances high-power explosive sprints by 1–2%, the associated intracellular water retention ($1.5–3.0\text{ kg}$ weight gain) severely penalizes power-to-weight ratio on steep hills, run-ups, and accelerations.
4. **Neglecting Impact and Bone Health in Juniors:** Specializing purely in non-impact cycling without running, soccer, or resistance training leads to low bone mineral density and poor multi-planar coordination.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Scenario / Decision | Incorrect Approach | Prescribed Coaching Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **July Road-to-CX Transition** | Training straight through from Road Nationals into September CX. | Take a mandatory 7–14 day mid-season break (unstructured/off-bike) in late July. |
| **Technical Skill Deficit** | Doing full-gas hot laps hoping skills improve under extreme fatigue. | Execute dedicated Zone 1 skill sessions; film dismounts and barrier approach lines. |
| **Fast-Twitch Crit Sharpening** | Grinding out 3-hour Sweet Spot rides 2 weeks before a major criterium. | Prescribe 15s/15s or 30s/30s micro-burst intervals and high-torque standing starts. |
| **Mid-Season Performance Slump** | Adding an extra interval day because power numbers feel sluggish. | Cut all intensity for 5–7 days; ride strictly Zone 1/2 or take complete rest. |
| **Junior Multi-Sport Schedule** | Forcing rigid power-meter intervals on 12–15 year olds. | Focus on high-speed technical games, group rides, multi-sport agility, and gym fundamentals. |
