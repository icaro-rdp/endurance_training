# Ten Minute Tips #36: How To Leave Your Coach (or Yourself) Workout Feedback — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Rory Porteous (Ten Minute Tips #36)_

## What Is Effective Workout Feedback?

In both coached and self-coached training environments, the training plan is not a static contract etched in stone; it is a **continuous physiological feedback loop**. A workout prescription represents only an initial stimulus hypothesis—its actual adaptive outcome depends entirely on how the athlete's autonomic nervous system, endocrine profile, and muscular glycogen stores absorb that stress.

Effective workout feedback is the practice of capturing concise, actionable subjective and contextual data alongside objective telemetry (power, heart rate, cadence) to enable dynamic microcycle autoregulation.

```
                    THE COACHING AUTOREGULATION LOOP
           ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
           ▼                                               │
┌───────────────────────┐     ┌───────────────────────┐    │
│ Prescribe Microcycle  │ ──► │  Execute Workout &    │    │

                                          │                │

                              ┌───────────────────────┐    │

                              │ Power + HR + RPE +    │ ───┘
                              │ 4 Fatigue Pillars     │
                              └───────────────────────┘
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Four Non-Training Fatigue Pillars
When workout execution fails or perceived effort ($RPE$) is disproportionately high, coaches and self-coached athletes must systematically rule out four systemic, non-training stressors before modifying interval design:

```
The 4 Non-Training Fatigue Pillars:
├── 1. Immune Activation / Sickness:
│   └── Cytokine storm & systemic inflammation divert amino acids to immune synthesis, impairing muscle protein synthesis and glycogen re-synthesis.
├── 2. Sleep Architecture & Debt:
│   └── Deficits in slow-wave sleep blunt growth hormone secretion, elevate cortisol, and impair central nervous system motor unit recruitment.
├── 3. Systemic Life & Occupational Stress:
│   └── Chronic sympathetic drive elevates resting catecholamines, impairs autonomic heart rate variability (HRV), and reduces muscle recovery rate.
└── 4. Off-Bike Glycogen & Hydration Deficits:
    └── Intra-workout carbohydrate ingestion (60–100 g/hr) maintains blood glucose but cannot acutely restore depleted muscle glycogen stores during intense intervals.
```

### 2. Triangulation of Physiological Telemetry
No single metric provides a complete picture of athlete readiness. Accurate diagnosis requires **triangulating three complementary signals**:

```
                             TRIANGULATION MATRIX
                              Perceptual Strain
                                   (RPE)
                                    ▲
                                   / \
                                  /   \
                                 /     \
                                /       \
         (Mechanical Load)     ▼─────────▼     (Cardiovascular/Autonomic)
            Power (Watts)                      Heart Rate (BPM)
```

1. **Power Output (Mechanical Stress):** Measures external work executed by the skeletal musculature.
2. **Heart Rate (Internal Cardiovascular & Autonomic Strain):** Reflects stroke volume demands, thermal load, hydration status, and autonomic tone (sympathetic vs. parasympathetic).
3. **Rating of Perceived Exertion / RPE (Central Nervous System Strain):** Reflects the brain's integration of motor command output (corollary discharge), metabolic acidosis, and glycogen status.

#### Diagnostic Patterns:
* **High Power + Low RPE + Normal HR:** High functional fitness; optimal metabolic readiness.
* **Low Power + Depressed HR + High RPE ("Heavy Legs"):** Autonomic overreaching / parasympathetic saturation; severe glycogen depletion or systemic exhaustion. Immediate rest required.
* **Target Power + Elevated HR + Elevated RPE:** Thermal stress, dehydration, or acute caffeine/stimulant response; monitor cardiac drift.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The 2–3 Sentence Feedback Template
Avoid overwhelming data logs with irrelevant details. Log 2–3 structured sentences answering three key dimensions:

```markdown
1. Readiness & Context: Sleep quality (hrs/quality), work stress (1–10), and acute fueling status.
2. In-Ride Execution: How intervals felt relative to expected RPE, snappiness, or physical limiters.
3. Nutrition Log: Total intra-ride carbohydrates (g/hr) and fluid intake.
```

* **Example (Ideal Feedback):**  
  _"Slept 8.5 hrs, felt rested. First 2 threshold intervals felt crisp at RPE 6/10; 3rd interval required heavy focus (RPE 8.5/10) but held target wattage without fading. Consumed 80g/hr carbs (mix + gel) and 1.5L fluid."_

* **Example (Red-Flag Feedback Triggering Rest):**  
  _"Slept 5 hrs due to work deadlines. Legs felt dead during warm-up; couldn't get heart rate above 145 bpm during interval 1. Bailed at minute 8 due to extreme RPE (9/10). Downshifted to 45 min easy spin."_

### 2. Real-Time Autoregulation Decision Hierarchy
When an athlete initiates a high-intensity session and detects systemic failure, they must execute the following decision hierarchy without requiring prior coach approval:

```
                           IN-RIDE DECISION HIERARCHY
                               Warm-up / Interval 1
                                       │
                         Are legs responsive & RPE normal?
                                      / \
                                Yes /     \ No
                                  /         \
              Proceed with planned session   Is RPE excessively high or HR suppressed?
                                                    │
                                      ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
                                      ▼                           ▼
                           Option A: Pivot to Z2        Option B: Spin 15-20 min Z1
                           Endurance (1.5-2 hrs)        Active Recovery, then STOP
                                      │                           │
                                      └─────────────┬─────────────┘
                                                    ▼
                                     Log detailed context in feedback;
                                     prioritize 9+ hours sleep & nutrition.
```

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **Hiding Fatigue to "Protect" the Plan:** Athletes who conceal extreme soreness, poor sleep, or early symptoms of illness out of fear that the coach will remove interval workouts inevitably enter deep overreaching states that require weeks of recovery.

1. **Relying Exclusively on Automated Metrics:** Trusting black-box readiness scores (Whoop, Oura, HRV apps) over actual perceived muscular readiness and workout execution cues.
2. **Confusing Discomfort with Fatigue:** Conflating localized biomechanical discomfort (e.g., saddle sores, hot foot) with central physiological exhaustion. Biomechanical issues require positional/equipment interventions, not necessarily metabolic de-loading.
3. **Evaluating Race Performance Strictly by Watts:** Judging road race or criterium success solely on normalized power. Positioning, tactical efficiency, draft preservation, and race timing dictate outcomes far more than isolated power peaks.
4. **The "100-Watt Jail" Resistance:** Refusing to ride easy on dedicated recovery days, turning active recovery into Zone 2 endurance and blunting recovery for upcoming key sessions.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Metric / Scenario | Observation | Coaching Action / Autoregulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Suppressed HR + High RPE** | Cannot reach target HR during intervals; power feels like pulling teeth. | Abort workout immediately; assign active recovery spin or full rest day. |
| **Elevated HR + Normal Power** | HR is 10–15 bpm higher than normal; athlete feels warm or stressed. | Check ambient temperature, hydration, and caffeine; continue if RPE is manageable. |
| **Persistent Sleep Loss (<6 hrs)** | Multi-day sleep debt with high occupational demands. | Strip high-intensity intervals; replace with short Zone 2 or prioritize sleep window. |
| **Mid-Ride Interval Failure** | Athlete nails interval 1–2, then experiences sudden power collapse on rep 3. | End interval set; athlete reached the true limit of muscular endurance (TTE endpoint). |
| **Low Subjective Motivation** | Athlete dreads getting on bike; general irritability and brain fog. | Investigate non-training life stress; schedule 2–3 days of unstructured unstructured riding or off-bike rest. |
