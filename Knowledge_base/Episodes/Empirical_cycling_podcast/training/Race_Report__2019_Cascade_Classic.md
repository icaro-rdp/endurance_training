# Race Report: 2019 Cascade Classic & Multi-Day Stage Race Execution — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore, Kyle Harrison & Emma Edwards_

---

## What Is Multi-Day Stage Race Execution?

Multi-day stage racing tests an athlete’s physiological durability, aerodynamic positioning, and tactical decision-making across sequential days of heterogeneous racing formats (circuit races, mountain stages, gravel road sections, and criteriums). 

At events like the 5-day Cascade Cycling Classic, success is determined not merely by peak single-day functional threshold power, but by the ability to repeatedly express high percentage of threshold power after high accumulated work (kJ), recover rapidly overnight, and navigate complex peloton dynamics against dominant professional squads.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     5-Day Stage Race Architecture                      │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Stage 1: Rolling Circuit w/ Gravel Sectors (Pinch Point Position)      │
│ Stage 2: Mountain Road Stage (Sustained Aerobic Ascents & Wet Descents)│
│ Stage 3: Road Race w/ Critical Loose Gravel Pinch (Tactical Chasing)   │
│ Stage 4: Technical Downtown Criterium (Neuromuscular & Anaerobic)      │
│ Stage 5: High-Heat Circuit (Repeated $W'$ Surges & GC Defense)         │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Durability & Fatigue Resistance
- **Durability Defined:** The resilience of an athlete's physiological parameters (FTP, $V̇O_2\text{max}$, gross efficiency) after expending large volumes of metabolic work (e.g., >2,000 kJ per stage).
- **Mitochondrial & Glycogen Dynamics:** Multi-day racing induces progressive glycogen depletion in Type I and Type IIa fibers. High aerobic base volume enables greater baseline fat oxidation rates, sparing intra-muscular glycogen during sub-threshold paceline sections and preserving $W'$ for decisive climbs and attacks.
- **Inflammatory & Neuromuscular Load:** Repeated eccentric micro-trauma from rough road surfaces, gravel vibrations, and high-torque corner exits causes cumulative central nervous system (CNS) and muscular fatigue, raising the perceived exertion (RPE) of baseline threshold power.

### 2. Tactical Mathematics & GC Time Preservation
- **The Energy Budget in Stage Racing:** An athlete in GC contention has a limited "budget" of anaerobic matches ($W'$ expenditures) per day. Burning matches unnecessarily on defensive chases or mistimed attacks leaves the rider vulnerable when dominant teams execute coordinated selections.
- **Blocking & Tactical Alliances:** When a composite or smaller team faces dominant squads (e.g., Team TWENTY20 or Rally Cycling), understanding team dynamics is essential:
  - If a rival team has riders up the road, their trailing riders will strictly "sit on" and refuse to pull.
  - Chasing riders must form ad-hoc working coalitions or rely on road captains to pace steadily rather than doing repeated solo surges.

### 3. Pinch Points: The Physics of Mixed-Surface Racing
- **Funnel Effect at Gravel Entrances:** Transitions from wide paved roads into narrow, unpaved gravel sectors force sudden decelerations and bottleneck crashes.
- **Positioning Economy:** Entering a gravel sector in the top 5–10 wheels requires a high-intensity 1-minute anaerobic surge before the turn, but saves hundreds of kilojoules of erratic braking and acceleration inside the sector while avoiding crash pileups.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Stage-by-Stage Tactical Guidelines
- **Gravel / Narrow Entrances:** Move to the front 3–5 km before the pinch point. Treat the positioning battle as a critical effort; being caught behind a crash on loose gravel often results in an unbridgeable time deficit.
- **Climbing Selection Stages:** Gauge climbing effort by sustainable threshold power rather than immediately reacting to explosive surges from riders out of GC contention.
- **Crit & Circuit Stages:** Utilize downhill momentum and clean lines through technical turns to minimize brake usage and conserve neuromuscular power for punchy uphill finish straights.

### 2. Rapid Overnight Recovery Protocol (Short Turnarounds)
When facing a late afternoon finish followed by an early morning start (e.g., <15 hours between stages):
```
[Immediate Post-Race (0–30 min)]
  • 1.2 g/kg Rapid Carbohydrate (maltodextrin/dextrose drink) + 0.4 g/kg Whey Protein
  • 500–750 mL Electrolyte Hydration
                 │
                 ▼
[2–4 Hours Post-Race (Dinner)]
  • High-glycemic carbohydrate dinner (rice, pasta, sweet potatoes)
  • Low dietary fiber and low fat to accelerate gastric emptying
  • Active cooling and leg elevation
                 │
                 ▼
[Pre-Bed (60 min prior)]
  • Casein protein (30–40g) + Tart cherry extract / melatonin support
  • Minimum 8+ hours non-disrupted sleep in cooled environment
```

### 3. Equipment Selection for Multi-Surface Road Stages
- **Tubeless Tires:** Run tubeless setups with fresh sealant (e.g., 28–30mm at optimized low pressures: 55–65 PSI depending on rider mass) to prevent pinch flats and punctures on rocky gravel sectors.
- **Spares & Support Strategy:** Ensure team car positioning is established; understand neutral support protocol and communicate wheel compatibility.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Entering Pinch Points from the Back:** Floating in the back of the peloton before gravel sectors or narrow descents, leading to time loss behind crashes or mechanical bottlenecks.
2. **Chasing Without Alliances:** Attempting to pull back multi-rider breakaways alone while rival teammates sit in the draft, resulting in severe glycogen depletion and GC collapse.
3. **Inadequate Post-Stage Fueling:** Under-eating carbohydrates during the critical 2-hour window following an exhausting stage, compromising morning glycogen stores.
4. **Overreacting to GC Time Gaps:** Panicking over small time deficits and attacking prematurely rather than pacing systematically with a team road captain.
