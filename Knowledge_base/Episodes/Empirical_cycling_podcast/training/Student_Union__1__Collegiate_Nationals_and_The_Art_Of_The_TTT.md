# Student Union #1: Collegiate Nationals & The Art of the Team Time Trial — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore, Liam Fenlon, Emma Edwards & Berk Ozturk_

---

## What Is the Team Time Trial (TTT)?

The Team Time Trial (TTT) is cycling's purest collective discipline. Typically contested by teams of four riders over distances of 20 to 50 km, the team's final time is taken on the wheel of the third rider across the line (allowing one rider to be dropped after exhausting their energy reserves).

Unlike an individual time trial, where an athlete rides at a steady physiological equilibrium, a TTT demands repeated supramaximal surges at the front (110–130% FTP) followed by imperfect recovery in the draft (70–85% FTP). Success depends on aerodynamic alignment, collective pacing discipline, and matching individual rider physiology to course terrain.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        TTT Effort Profile                              │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Front Pull: 110–130% FTP (High $W'$ depletion / Glycolytic Flux)       │
│                                │                                       │
│                                ▼                                       │
│ Rotation: Pull off cleanly, drop to rear without disrupting line       │
│                                │                                       │
│                                ▼                                       │
│ In the Draft: 70–85% FTP (Partial $W'$ reconstitution / Aerobic Drain) │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Intermittent High-Intensity Nature of TTT
- **Micro-Interval Oscillations:** In a four-person paceline, each rider alternates between leading into clean air and drafting. Even with a 30–40% aerodynamic reduction in the paceline, the recovery is sub-threshold aerobic work rather than passive rest.
- **$W'$ (Anaerobic Work Capacity) Expenditure & Reconstitution:** Every pull above Critical Power (CP) draws down $W'$. Reconstitution of $W'$ during drafting depends on:
  - The drop in intensity below CP (the larger the delta, the faster the recovery).
  - Capillary density and oxidative capacity (aerobic base).
  - Total elapsed race duration and accumulated glycogen depletion.
- **The Upward Surge Trap:** Increasing power on uphill pitches causes massive $W'$ depletion. If the weakest rider in the scoring group is forced above their threshold, their $W'$ will fully deplete, causing them to detach instantly and permanently ruin the team's finishing time.

### 2. Lineup Optimization & Aerodynamic Physics
- **Anthropometric Sequencing:** 
  - Tall or broader riders generate a larger aerodynamic wake, providing a substantial draft advantage for smaller riders positioned directly behind them.
  - Placing a smaller, lighter rider behind a large rider maximizes energy conservation for the smaller rider.
  - Alternating rider rotations or skipping pulls for vulnerable riders preserves the critical 3rd scoring member until the final kilometers.
- **Terrain-Specific Roles:**
  - **Heavier / Higher-FTP Riders:** Lead hard on flat, rolling, and descending sectors where absolute power and aerodynamic penetration ($W/C_d A$) dominate.
  - **Lighter / High-W/kg Riders:** Set a conservative, steady tempo on uphill gradients to prevent blowing apart the heavier riders.

### 3. Thermal Strain & Cardiovascular Drift
- **Thermoregulation in High Humidity:** When ambient temperatures exceed 28°C (82°F) with high relative humidity, evaporative cooling (sweat vaporization) is severely compromised.
- **Cardiovascular Drift:** Blood volume is diverted to cutaneous vascular beds for heat dissipation, reducing venous return and cardiac stroke volume. To maintain cardiac output, heart rate rises continuously, accelerating the onset of central and peripheral fatigue.

```
[High Heat & Humidity] ──► [Compromised Sweat Evaporation]
                                  │
                                  ▼
[Cutaneous Vasodilation] ──► [Reduced Venous Return / Preload]
                                  │
                                  ▼
[Lower Stroke Volume] ──► [Elevated Heart Rate / Severe Fatigue]
```

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. TTT Execution & Rotation Choreography
- **Pull Duration:** 
  - Stronger riders: 30–45 seconds on flat/fast sections.
  - Fatigued or lighter riders: 10–15 seconds ("through and off" to maintain momentum without accumulating acidosis).
- **Rotation Mechanics:**
  - The leading rider signals, smoothly pulls off to the windward side without accelerating or coasting abruptly, and drifts backward to slot onto the final wheel.
  - The incoming rider maintains steady speed rather than surging as they take the wind.
- **Communication Protocol:**
  - Standard verbal commands must be vocalized clearly from the rear:
    - `"Clear"` — The departing rider has cleared the front wheel and can safely slot in.
    - `"Easy / Soft"` — Pacing is too high on an incline; reduce power by 10–20W immediately.
    - `"Gap"` — A separation is opening; decelerate smoothly at the front to allow re-attachment.

### 2. Heat Acclimation & Pre-Cooling Protocol
When competing in hot and humid environments:
- **Pre-Event Acclimation (7–14 Days Out):**
  - Post-ride sauna sessions (20–30 minutes at 80–90°C) or controlled indoor training sessions without fans to stimulate plasma volume expansion.
- **Race Day Pre-Cooling:**
  - Ice vests worn during warm-up.
  - Cold water and ice slushy ingestion 30–45 minutes prior to start.
  - Ice packs placed inside jerseys over the neck and torso.

### 3. Mass-Start Tactical Discipline (Road Race / Criterium)
- **Early Race Chaos Management:** In large, heterogeneous fields (e.g., collegiate or lower-category national events), maintain position in the top 15–20% of the peloton to avoid accordions and multi-rider pileups.
- **Equipment Safety Checks:** In wet or technical conditions, drop tire pressure by 5–10 PSI and use proven, high-grip tire compounds rather than unknown neutral spare wheels.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Surging on the Front:** Treating a pull like an attack accelerates the speed abruptly, creating an elastic "accordion effect" that drains energy from every trailing teammate.
2. **Dropping Teammates on Climbs:** Pushing high $W/kg$ on an uphill pitch to show individual strength, dropping the 3rd or 4th rider and leaving the remaining squad shorthanded for the subsequent flat sectors.
3. **Failing to Adapt Pull Lengths:** Enforcing rigid equal pull times across all riders regardless of varying individual fitness levels or fatigue states.
4. **Poor Pre-Ride Reconnaissance:** Driving a course in a car rather than riding it on the bike, leading to misjudgments of gradient steepness, wind exposure, and road surface quality.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Variable | Recommended Strategy | Failure Mode / Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pacing on Uphills** | Cap power at the 3rd rider's threshold; steady cadence. | Surging drops the scoring rider; catastrophic time loss. |
| **Pacing on Flats/Descents** | Maximize power from high-FTP/heavier engines. | Under-pacing fast sectors forfeits the primary speed advantage. |
| **Lineup Order** | Place smaller riders behind larger riders to maximize draft. | Smaller riders leading into tall riders offers negligible draft benefit. |
| **Pull Duration** | Variable: 30–45s for strong engines, 10–15s for recovering riders. | Rigid equal pulls overload weaker riders and cause team breakdown. |
| **Heat Management** | Pre-cooling (ice vests/slurries) + active sauna acclimation. | Hyperthermia, severe cardiovascular drift, and early power collapse. |
| **Field Positioning** | Top 15–25 riders into technical chicanes and early road sections. | Getting caught behind mid-pack bottlenecks, accordioning, and crashes. |
