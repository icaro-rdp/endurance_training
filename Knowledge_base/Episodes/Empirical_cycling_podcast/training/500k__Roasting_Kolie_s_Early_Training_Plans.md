## What Is the "Roasting Early Training Plans" Retrospective?

To celebrate reaching 500,000 podcast listens, Kolie Moore and Kyle Hanson conducted an in-depth retrospective, subjecting Kolie's earliest coaching plans from 2014–2016 to rigorous physiological critique. 

By deconstructing the flaws in early training plans (written during the transition from basic power meter charts and spreadsheet coaching to modern data analytics and exercise physiology), the episode provides an invaluable masterclass in **how coaching paradigms evolve from dogmatic, over-dense interval stacking to autoregulated, sustainable, and scientifically sound periodization**.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Autonomic Cost of High-Intensity Stacking
- **Sympathetic Overdrive:** Performing high-intensity or stochastic interval sessions across 4–5 days per week (e.g., hill repeats Tuesday, tempo accelerations Wednesday, sweet spot Thursday, threshold Saturday, VO2max Sunday) creates continuous sympathetic nervous system activation and elevated cortisol.
- **Parasympathetic Suppression & Glycogen Depletion:** Without full 24–48 hour recovery windows, muscle glycogen replenishment is incomplete and central nervous system (CNS) fatigue accumulates. This degrades power output during subsequent interval bouts, resulting in athletes performing intervals in an exhausted, sub-optimal state.

### 2. The Fallacy of the "No Rest Days" Dogma
- **Active Recovery vs. Complete Rest:** Early coaching dogma frequently mandated riding every single day (e.g., "always spin for 45–60 minutes on Monday"). However, getting on the bike creates low-grade neuromuscular contraction, postural tension, and mental commitment.
- **Endocrine & Cellular Recovery:** Full passive rest days (sitting on the couch, zero training stress) are essential for restoring hormonal balance (testosterone-to-cortisol ratio), reducing systemic inflammation, and allowing full muscle protein synthesis.

### 3. Strength Training: High-Rep "Endurance" vs. Heavy Neural Loading
- **The High-Rep Trap:** Prescribing $2 \times 15\text{--}20\text{ reps}$ in the gym under the guise of "in-season maintenance" or "muscular endurance" produces extensive metabolic byproducts, severe delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS), and peripheral fatigue. Cyclists already perform tens of thousands of low-force contractions on the bike.
- **The Heavy Neural Paradigm:** In-season lifting should focus strictly on **high force production and neural drive ($3\text{--}6\text{ reps}$ at $\ge 80\%\ \text{1RM}$, RPE 7.5–8.5, 3–5 min rest)**. This recruits high-threshold Type II motor units, increases tendon stiffness, and enhances rate of force development (RFD) without generating excessive metabolic fatigue or unwanted hypertrophy.

### 4. Survivorship Bias and Genetic Outliers
- **The Talent Mask:** Highly gifted athletes (e.g., natural collegiate champions or future elite racers) possess immense baseline aerobic capacity and rapid cellular repair mechanisms. Inexperienced coaches often assume their training plan is optimal because the star athlete improves initially.
- **The Plateau & Crash:** Eventually, even genetic outliers succumb to overtraining, stagnation, and endocrine breakdown if the underlying workload lacks adequate recovery and sustainable aerobic base volume.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Modern Weekly Microcycle Structure (In-Season & Build Phase)

```
Corrected Empirical Microcycle:
- Monday:    Full Passive Rest (0 min on the bike; sleep & nutrition focus)
- Tuesday:   Primary Quality Session: Threshold TiZ Extension (e.g., 2 x 25 min @ 98% FTP)
- Wednesday: Zone 2 Endurance Base (1.5-2.5 hrs @ 60-68% FTP; strictly conversational)
- Thursday:  Secondary Quality Session: VO2max or Heavy Gym Strength
             (e.g., 4 x 4 min all-out @ 110+ RPM OR 3x5 heavy squats/deadlifts)
- Friday:    Easy Recovery Spin (30-45 min Zone 1) or Full Rest
- Saturday:  Long Endurance Ride with natural terrain / durability (3.5-5.0 hrs Zone 2)
- Sunday:    Moderate Base Ride (2.0-3.0 hrs Zone 2) OR Race Day
Total Quality High-Intensity Sessions: 2 (maximum 3 during peak build)
```

### 2. Modern Threshold Extension (Time-in-Zone Progression)
Instead of forcing static interval sets (e.g., perpetual $2 \times 20\text{ min}$) at artificially inflated wattages, progressively overload continuous duration at a verified threshold:

$$\text{Week 1: } 3 \times 15\text{ min (45 min TiZ)} \longrightarrow \text{Week 2: } 2 \times 25\text{ min (50 min TiZ)}$$
$$\text{Week 3: } 1 \times 35\text{ min} + 1 \times 20\text{ min (55 min TiZ)} \longrightarrow \text{Week 4: } 1 \times 50\text{--}60\text{ min (Continuous TTE)}$$

### 3. Modern Gym Strength Prescription for Cyclists
- **Frequency:** 2 days/week in base phase; 1 day/week in race season.
- **Core Compound Lifts:** Barbell Back Squat, Trap Bar Deadlift, Romanian Deadlift (RDL), Bulgarian Split Squat.
- **Loading Parameters:**
  - Repetitions: 3 to 5 reps per set.
  - Sets: 3 to 4 working sets.
  - Intensity: 2 to 3 Reps in Reserve (RIR) / RPE 7.5–8.5 (never to muscular failure).
  - Rest: 3–4 minutes between sets for full phosphagen ($PCr$) resynthesis.

### 4. The Autoregulated Feedback Loop
- **Daily File Analysis:** Review power, cadence, heart rate, and temperature daily.
- **Qualitative Athlete Dialogue:** Require athletes to record RPE, perceived fatigue, sleep quality, and life stress.
- **The "Audible" Rule:** If an athlete reports heavy legs (RPE 9/10 on warm-up) or significant life stress, immediately modify the session: convert the hard interval workout into an easy Zone 1-2 spin or prescribe a rest day. Never attempt to "make up" missed hard workouts by doubling up on subsequent days.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **"Fog of War" Coaching:**
   - Writing multi-week static training plans in spreadsheets without daily monitoring, leaving athletes stranded when life stress, illness, or fatigue disrupts the plan.
2. **Treating Openers as Mandatory:**
   - *Philosophical Rule (Nat Ward):* **"Openers are a privilege for the well-rested."** Prescribing hard sprint or threshold openers to an already fatigued athlete before a minor race merely adds unnecessary exhaustion.
3. **The "Make Up the Workout" Trap:**
   - When an athlete misses Tuesday's interval session, scheduling Tuesday's and Wednesday's sessions back-to-back or doubling the weekend volume. Missed workouts must be absorbed, not crammed.
4. **Confusing On-Bike Low Cadence with Strength Work:**
   - Forcing athletes to grind up hills at 50–60 RPM to "build leg strength" while avoiding the gym.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

| Coaching Element | Kolie Moore Early Flawed Method (2015) | Modern Empirical Cycling Method | Physiological Mechanism / Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rest Days** | Active recovery spin (1 hr Z1) every single day; zero off-days | 1–2 dedicated **full passive rest days** per week | Allows autonomic nervous system recovery and complete muscle glycogen synthesis. |
| **Weekly Hard Sessions** | 4–5 hard/stochastic sessions per week + gym | Strictly **2 (max 3) quality sessions** per week | Preserves interval freshness; prevents chronic sympathetic overdrive and overtraining. |
| **Gym Programming** | High-rep sets ($2\text{--}3 \times 15\text{--}20\text{ reps}$) for "maintenance" | Heavy, low-rep sets ($3\text{--}5\text{ reps}$ @ $\ge 80\%\text{ 1RM}$) | Stimulates Type II neural drive and RFD without inducing debilitating metabolic fatigue or DOMS. |
| **Threshold Progression** | Fixed $2 \times 20\text{m}$, forcing wattage higher prematurely | Progressive Time-in-Zone ($45 \to 60+\text{ min}$) at stable FTP | Extends Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE) via sustained mitochondrial and capillary flux. |
| **Athlete Feedback** | Unmonitored static spreadsheet; no comments required | Daily file review + qualitative RPE and fatigue dialogue | Closes the feedback loop; enables real-time autoregulation and proactive rest prescription. |
| **Handling Fatigue** | "Push through it; you need more training load." | **"Nine times out of ten, the solution is more rest."** | Stagnation is almost always caused by unabsorbed fatigue rather than a lack of workload. |

> [!TIP]
> **Fundamental Coaching Principle:** A successful training plan is not measured by its theoretical complexity on paper, but by the **athlete's ability to consistently execute high-quality workouts with full freshness and absorb the training stress through adequate rest and fueling.**
