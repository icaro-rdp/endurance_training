# Ten Minute Tips #27: Programming Over-Under Workouts — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Helson (Ten Minute Tips #27)_

---

## What Are Over-Under Workouts?

**Over-Under Intervals** are structured threshold sessions where power alternates between an intensity slightly above Functional Threshold Power (the "Over") and an intensity slightly below threshold (the "Under"). 

* **The Classical Theory:** Popular endurance folklore asserts that over-unders are a "magic bullet" designed to flood muscle tissue with lactate during the over and force slow-twitch fibers to oxidize that lactate during the under, thereby rapidly improving "lactate shuttling" (MCT1/MCT4 transporters) and "lactate tolerance."
* **The Empirical Reality:** Exercise physiology literature demonstrates that steady-state threshold training, VO2max intervals, and long endurance volume stimulate mitochondrial biogenesis and MCT expression just as effectively as over-unders. Over-unders are not a distinct physiological switch; they are **spicy threshold intervals** offering neuromuscular and psychological variety.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Lactate Flux vs. Metabolic Blowup
* **Lactate as Fuel:** Pyruvate and lactate exist in constant equilibrium. When power exceeds FTP, glycolytic flux outpaces mitochondrial oxidation, generating an accumulation of lactate and associated hydrogen ions/inorganic phosphates.
* **The Role of the Under:** The "under" must be metabolically sustainable (typically 85–90% FTP / Sweet Spot). If the under is paced correctly, oxidative Type I and trained Type IIa fibers utilize the circulating lactate as an aerobic substrate via **MCT1 transporters**.
* **The Failure Mode:** If the "over" is executed as an all-out anaerobic sprint, intramuscular metabolic byproducts ($H^+$, $P_i$, ADP) accumulate rapidly, impairing calcium handling and contractile function. The athlete is forced to drop to Zone 1/2 to recover, defeating the continuous aerobic load of the threshold interval.

```
Metabolic Balance in Over-Unders:
Over (105-115% FTP)  --> Fast Glycolytic Flux -> Recruits Type IIa Fibers -> Produces Lactate + H+
Under (85-90% FTP)   --> Sub-Threshold Aerobic -> MCT1 Shuttling -> Oxidizes Lactate in Mitochondria
```

### 2. High-Threshold Motor Unit Recruitment
During steady-state riding at Sweet Spot, the central nervous system recruits Type I and lower-threshold Type IIa fibers. 
* By adding brief surges (overs), the nervous system temporarily activates larger, higher-threshold motor units (Henneman's Size Principle).
* Repeating these surges teaches these higher-threshold motor units to become more fatigue-resistant and oxidative over multi-week mesocycles.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Core Structuring Frameworks

```
Over-Under Formats:
├── 1. The Beginner Acceleration Model:
│   └── 2–4 x 15–20 min Sweet Spot with a 10s surge (40-50% sprint effort) every 5 min.
├── 2. The Asymmetric 1:4 Ratio (Bread & Butter):
│   ├── 30s Over (105–115% FTP) / 2 min Under (85–90% FTP) for 20–30 min blocks.
│   └── 1 min Over (105–110% FTP) / 4 min Under (88–92% FTP) for 20–40 min blocks.
└── 3. The Race-Specific Intermittent Model (CX / Crit / MTB):
    └── 10–20 min blocks of 40s on (115% FTP) / 20s under (80% FTP), or 15s/15s.
```

### 2. Pacing Guidelines: Avoid the 120%+ Trap
* **The "Over" Target:** Overs should be modest: **20 to 50 Watts over FTP** (or roughly 105–112% FTP), or 40–50% of peak sprint power. They should feel like a firm acceleration, not a maximal sprint.
* **The "Under" Target:** The under must remain in high Zone 3 / low Zone 4 (85–92% FTP). If an athlete must drop below 80% FTP to survive, the overs were performed too aggressively.
* **Cadence Manipulation:**
  * **Criterium / Track Racers:** Perform the overs at **110–130+ RPM** to simulate rapid accelerations out of corners.
  * **Mountain Bike / Gravel Racers:** Perform the overs at **60–75 RPM** in a heavy gear to simulate grinding up steep, technical terrain.

### 3. Progression Architecture
Progress over-unders exactly like steady-state threshold sessions—by extending **Time in Zone (TIZ)** rather than making the overs harder:

```
Mesocycle Progression (Threshold Block):
Week 1: 4 x 15 min (60 min total work) — 30s over / 2 min under
Week 2: 3 x 20 min (60 min total work) — 1 min over / 4 min under
Week 3: 3 x 25 min (75 min total work) — 1 min over / 4 min under
Week 4: 2 x 35–40 min (70–80 min total work) — 1 min over / 4 min under
```

### 4. Tactical Rule: Always Start with the "Over"
In real-world racing (cyclocross, XC MTB, road racing), critical separations occur immediately from the start line or base of a climb. 
* Always start the interval block on the "Over" (e.g., standing start or 10–30s acceleration) before settling into the "Under" pace.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The Anaerobic Capacity Trap:** The single most common coaching error is prescribing overs at 125–150% FTP. This completely exhausts anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) within the first two cycles, turning a threshold aerobic session into an erratic anaerobic failure test.

1. **Rigid Symmetrical Programming (e.g., 2 min Over / 2 min Under at 115%/95%):** Symmetrical high-intensity over-unders accumulate unsustainable acidosis for most athletes, resulting in premature interval abandonment.
2. **Treating Over-Unders as Mandatory for TTE:** Athletes can achieve elite Time-to-Exhaustion (TTE) and high FTP solely through steady-state threshold and massive base volume. Over-unders are optional tools, not mandatory physiological prerequisites.
3. **Misinterpreting Normalized Power (NP):** Spiking overs artificially inflates NP while depressing true sustained metabolic work. Pacing must be evaluated based on average power and RPE consistency across all repetitions.
