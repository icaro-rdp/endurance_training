# Effective FTP Training: Progressive Overload & Extending TTE — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson (Watts Doc #14)_

---

## What Is Effective FTP Training?

Functional Threshold Power (FTP) represents the highest metabolic work rate sustainable in a quasi-steady state without rapid, progressive metabolic fatigue (Maximal Lactate Steady State / MLSS). Training FTP is not merely about pushing a single higher wattage number on a 20-minute test; it has **two distinct physiological dimensions**:

1. **Intensity (Height / Watts):** The maximal power output sustainable at MLSS.
2. **Duration (Time-to-Exhaustion / TTE):** The continuous duration an athlete can sustain that threshold power before task failure (typically 35 to 80+ minutes).

**Effective FTP training** prioritizes **progressive overload of duration ("extending TTE out")** before attempting to increase baseline power targets ("pushing wattage up").

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
                   The Two Dimensions of FTP Progression
  
  Power (Watts)
    ▲
    │         [Pushing Power "UP"]
    │         (Difficult to measure weekly; high risk of blowing up)
    │               ▲
    │               │
    │  FTP ─────────┼───────────────────────────┐
    │               │                           │  [Extending TTE "OUT"]
    │               │                           │  (Massive aerobic stimulus; reliable overload)
    │               │                           ▼
    │               └───────────────────────────────────────────────►
    └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────► Duration (Time)
                     Initial TTE (~35–45 min)     Target TTE (~60–90 min)
```

### 1. Why Chasing "Out" Precedes Chasing "Up"
* **Measurement Precision:** Daily biological fluctuations (hydration, glycogen, sleep) and power meter error margins ($\pm 1–2\%$, representing 4–8 Watts) make micro-adjustments in target wattage unreliable on a weekly basis.
* **Aerobic Stimulus via Time-in-Zone:** Aerobic mitochondrial adaptations (citrate synthase, PGC-1$\alpha$, capillarization) are driven by sustained muscular contractions and continuous oxidative flux. Increasing interval duration from 40 to 60–80 minutes provides a massive, reliable adaptive stimulus.
* **The "Starting Strength" Parallel:** Just as novice lifters exhaust linear weight progressions, cyclists who repeat the same $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ workout quickly plateau because total volume and metabolic strain remain static.

### 2. Evidence from MLSS Progression in Endurance Athletes
In a landmark 6-week study on endurance runners plateaued on steady mileage, replacing two weekly runs with progressive MLSS intervals yielded profound physiological shifts:
* **Protocol:** Interval volume started at **30 minutes total** ($3 \times 10\text{ min}$) in Week 1, increasing by 6 minutes each week up to **60 minutes total** ($3 \times 20\text{ min}$ / $2 \times 30\text{ min}$) in Week 6.
* **Outcomes:**
  * **TTE at Threshold:** Extended from **$44\text{ minutes}$ to $63\text{ minutes}$** (+43% increase).
  * **Threshold Velocity:** Increased from $13.8\text{ km/h}$ to $15.2\text{ km/h}$.
  * **Substrate Utilization:** At their *previous* threshold speed ($13.8\text{ km/h}$), fat oxidation increased substantially, drastically sparing glycogen.

### 3. The Quantitative & Qualitative Shift Above FTP
* **At or Slightly Below FTP (92–98%):** Lactate production equals clearance. Muscle fibers operate primarily in a sustainable oxidative steady state with low central nervous system and autonomic stress.
* **Above FTP (>102%):** Intracellular homeostasis rapidly breaks down ($[H^+]$ and inorganic phosphate $[P_i]$ accumulate), muscle fiber recruitment shifts heavily to fast-twitch glycolytic motor units, and $W'$ (anaerobic work capacity) is rapidly depleted.
* **The Training Trap:** Riding just 10–15 Watts too hard converts an extensive threshold session into an exhaustive, shortened glycolytic workout, reducing total time-in-zone by 50% and causing excessive autonomic fatigue.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

```
                 Kolie Moore's FTP Interval Progression Model
  
  [Baseline: Test FTP & TTE] ──► Baseline TTE: ~40–45 min
                                         │
  [Phase 1: Volume Accumulation]  ───────┼──► 4 x 10 min @ 95–100% (3 min rest)
                                         ├──► 3 x 15 min @ 95–100% (4 min rest)
                                         └──► 2 x 20 min @ 95–100% (5 min rest)
                                         │
  [Phase 2: Interval Consolidation] ─────┼──► 3 x 20 min @ 95–100% (5 min rest)
                                         ├──► 2 x 30 min @ 95–100% (6 min rest)
                                         └──► 1 x 45–60 min Continuous @ 92–97%
                                         │
  [Phase 3: Race-Specific Over-Ats] ─────┴──► 3 x 20 min w/ 10s Seated Burst every 5 min
```

### 1. Interval Structure and Density Guidelines
* **Minimum Effective Interval Duration:** 10 minutes (shorter durations like 5 minutes lack sustained threshold metabolic flux).
* **Work-to-Rest Ratio:** Keep recovery intervals short ($3\text{ to }5\text{ minutes}$ easy spinning) to maintain elevated heart rate and oxygen kinetics into the subsequent set.
* **Progression Path:**
  1. Start with high repetition, moderate length: $4 \times 10\text{ min}$ or $3 \times 12\text{ min}$ (40–36 min total).
  2. Consolidate into longer intervals: $3 \times 15\text{ min} \rightarrow 2 \times 20\text{ min} \rightarrow 2 \times 25\text{ min}$ (45–50 min total).
  3. Push extensive limits: $3 \times 20\text{ min} \rightarrow 2 \times 30\text{ min} \rightarrow 1 \times 60\text{ min}$ (60 min total).

### 2. Pacing Rules
* **Target Power:** Prescribe work at **$92\%\text{ to }98\% \text{ FTP}$**. Riding slightly below threshold produces virtually identical aerobic adaptations as exact 100% FTP with far less fatigue and higher completion consistency.
* **Cadence Selection:** Self-selected natural cadence (typically 85–95 RPM). Incorporate occasional low-cadence blocks (55–65 RPM) if building high-torque neuromuscular tolerance.

### 3. "Over-At" Bursts for Advanced Adaptations
To bridge steady-state threshold training to the stochastic demands of road racing, criteriums, and mountain biking:
* **Execution:** Within a $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ or $2 \times 30\text{ min}$ interval at 95% FTP, perform a **10-second seated maximal acceleration** every 3 to 5 minutes, then settle immediately back to threshold wattage.
* **Metabolic Purpose:** Spikes lactate and metabolic intermediates without dropping below threshold during recovery, forcing working muscle fibers to clear metabolites under continuous oxidative load.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The Vanity Wattage Trap:** Attempting to force every threshold workout 5–10 Watts higher than the last. This leads to blown workouts, shortened interval durations, high anaerobic strain, and plateaued aerobic fitness.

1. **Relying Exclusively on $2 \times 20\text{ min}$:**
   * Repeating $2 \times 20\text{ min}$ indefinitely without increasing interval duration or density produces diminishing returns once the body adapts to 40 minutes of total work.
2. **Setting FTP Based on Short Tests:**
   * Using ramp tests or 20-minute tests without blowouts inflates baseline FTP, causing athletes to train at $105\%+$ of true MLSS, leading to rapid burnout.
3. **Cutting Rest Intervals to Zero Prematurely:**
   * Rushing into 60-minute continuous efforts before consolidating broken sets ($3 \times 20\text{ min}$) often causes premature failure and mental exhaustion.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### FTP Training Progression Matrix

| Level / Phase | Target Time-in-Zone | Typical Workout Structure | Rest Between Sets | Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Introductory** | $30–40\text{ min}$ | $4 \times 10\text{ min}$ or $3 \times 12\text{ min}$ | $3\text{ min}$ | Establishing pacing & rhythm |
| **Intermediate** | $45–55\text{ min}$ | $3 \times 15\text{ min}$ or $2 \times 25\text{ min}$ | $4–5\text{ min}$ | Extending continuous duration |
| **Advanced** | $60–75\text{ min}$ | $3 \times 20\text{ min}$ or $2 \times 30\text{ min}$ | $5–6\text{ min}$ | Deep aerobic volume overload |
| **Elite / Extensive** | $80–90+\text{ min}$ | $4 \times 20\text{ min}$ or $1 \times 60–75\text{ min}$ | $5\text{ min}$ | World-class TTE & fatigue resistance |
| **Race-Specific** | $40–60\text{ min}$ | $2 \times 25\text{ min}$ w/ 10s burst every 5m | $5\text{ min}$ | Stochastic clearance & race readiness |

### Coach & Athlete Decision Checklist

* [ ] **Test Baseline FTP & TTE:** Establish accurate current threshold power and time-to-exhaustion using an open-ended long test.
* [ ] **Select Starting Interval Duration:** Begin with interval lengths equal to or slightly shorter than tested baseline TTE (e.g., $4 \times 10\text{ min}$ for a 40-min baseline).
* [ ] **Overload Time First:** Add 5–10 minutes of total time-in-zone per week before raising baseline target wattage.
* [ ] **Stay Strictly Below the Red Line:** Maintain target wattage between 92% and 98% FTP; never exceed 100% FTP during extensive blocks.
* [ ] **Incorporate Bursts for Race Preparation:** Add seated 10-second surges into threshold blocks 3–4 weeks out from priority events.
