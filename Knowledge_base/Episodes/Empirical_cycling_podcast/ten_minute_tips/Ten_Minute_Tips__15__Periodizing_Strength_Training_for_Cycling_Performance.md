# Periodizing Strength Training for Cycling Performance — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hanson (Ten Minute Tips #15)_

---

## What Is Strength Periodization for Cyclists?

Periodizing strength training for endurance cyclists is the art of **aligning resistance training stimuli with the annual cycling macrocycle**:
1. **Off-Season Base (Phase 1–3):** Heavy gym focus paired with low-intensity Zone 2 endurance riding. The body can handle the combined fatigue of resistance training and aerobic base volume.
2. **Late Base / Build Transition (Phase 4):** Transitioning from general bilateral force to cycling-specific unilateral neural drive and explosive rate of force development.
3. **In-Season / Competition Phase:** Slashing gym volume to submaximal maintenance levels (1 session/week or 1 session every 2 weeks) to dedicate $100\%$ of systemic recovery capacity to races and high-intensity interval workouts.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Binary Feedback of Strength:** Unlike cycling, where an athlete can "slog" through an interval workout at subpar power, heavy resistance training offers binary feedback: if recovery or neural drive is compromised, the barbell simply will not move.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
                     Annual Strength Periodization Blueprint
                     
  [ Phase 1: Adaptation ] ──► [ Phase 2: Hypertrophy ] ──► [ Phase 3: Bilateral Strength ]
    • Weeks 1–2                 • Weeks 3–6                  • Weeks 7–10
    • 8–12 reps, RPE 4–6        • 8–12 reps, RPE 7–8         • 4–6 reps, RPE 8–9
    • Recondition connective    • Muscle cross-section       • High-threshold motor
      tissues & eliminate DOMS    & glycogen capacity          unit recruitment
                                                                    │
                                                                    ▼
  [ In-Season Maintenance ] ◄── [ Deload & Taper ] ◄── [ Phase 4: Unilateral Neural ]
    • Race Season                 • Pre-Season Build           • Weeks 11–14
    • 1 session / 1–2 weeks       • 50% volume cut             • Single-leg squats FIRST
    • 2 sets × 5–8 reps (RPE 6)   • Dissipate joint stress     • Trap-bar deadlift
```

### 1. Phase 1: Anatomical Adaptation (Weeks 1–2)
* **Goal:** Re-establish neuromuscular coordination, joint mobility, and spinal stabilization.
* **Mechanism:** Gradually loads tendons, ligaments, and cartilage (which receive minimal blood supply compared to muscle tissue) to prevent acute tendinitis and severe DOMS.

### 2. Phase 2: Hypertrophy & Muscular Base (Weeks 3–6)
* **Goal:** Increase muscle fiber cross-sectional area and glycogen storage capacity in high-threshold motor units.
* **Cycling Impact:** Cyclists will not accidentally look like bodybuilders. Gaining 1–2 lbs of functional myofibrillar mass increases anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) and peak sprint torque.
* **Aerobic Pairing:** Pair strictly with low-intensity Zone 2 endurance riding. High-intensity cycling during this block creates severe systemic overload.

### 3. Phase 3: Foundational Bilateral Strength (Weeks 7–10)
* **Goal:** Maximize absolute force production and high-threshold Type II motor unit recruitment via heavy compound movements (4–6 reps at $80–85\%$ 1RM).
* **Bilateral Anchor:** Heavy bilateral squats build the absolute force ceiling that later supports single-leg performance.

### 4. Phase 4: Unilateral Neural Drive & Deficit Transfer (Weeks 11–14)
* **The Exercise Order Flip:** In earlier phases, bilateral squats are performed first. In Phase 4, **single-leg squats/presses are performed first** when the central nervous system is $100\%$ fresh.
* **Bilateral Deficit Transfer:** Elevates single-leg force output from $55–60\%$ of bilateral max up toward the $70–75\%$ ceiling, directly transferring gym strength to the pedal stroke.
* **Trap-Bar Deadlift Pairing:** Deadlifts provide purely concentric neural drive without an eccentric stretch-reflex, forcing maximal motor unit firing.

### 5. The Critique of Plyometrics for Cyclists
* **The Specificity Mismatch:** High box jumps and drop jumps are designed to decrease ground contact time in running and jumping sports. Cycling has no ground contact time; power is governed by cadence and crank rotation.
* **High Injury Risk:** Missed box jumps, shin lacerations, and severe Achilles/patellar tendon strain far outweigh any potential neuromuscular benefit.
* **The Alternative:** Use low-impact jump rope for basic bone mineral density, or perform high-torque on-bike standing starts for true sport-specific RFD.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

```
                   16-Week Strength Periodization Timeline
                   
  W1–W2:   Adaptation Phase (2x/wk, 8–12 reps, RPE 5–6)
  W3–W5:   Hypertrophy Phase (2x/wk, 8–12 reps, RPE 7–8)
  W6:      DELOAD WEEK (2x/wk, 50% load/volume)
  W7–W9:   Bilateral Strength Phase (2x/wk, 4–6 reps, RPE 8–9)
  W10:     DELOAD WEEK (2x/wk, 50% load/volume)
  W11–W13: Unilateral Neural Drive Phase (1–2x/wk, Single-Leg First)
  W14:     DELOAD / In-Season Transition
  W15+:    In-Season Maintenance (1x every 7–14 days, RPE 6–7)
```

### Phase-by-Phase Workout Prescriptions

#### Phase 1: Anatomical Adaptation (Weeks 1–2)
* **Frequency:** 2 sessions per week.
* **Primary Lift:** Back Squat or Leg Press — $2\text{ to }3\text{ sets of }8\text{–}12\text{ reps}$ @ RPE 5–6.
* **Secondary Lift:** Bulgarian Split Squat — $2\text{ sets of }8\text{ reps/leg}$ @ RPE 5.
* **Accessory:** 7-Way Hips & Dumbbell Rows — $2\text{ sets of }12\text{ reps}$.

#### Phase 2: Hypertrophy & Work Capacity (Weeks 3–6)
* **Frequency:** 2 sessions per week.
* **Primary Lift:** Back Squat or Front Squat — $3\text{ to }4\text{ sets of }8\text{–}10\text{ reps}$ @ RPE 7–8.
* **Secondary Lift:** Single-Leg Leg Press — $3\text{ sets of }8\text{–}10\text{ reps/leg}$ @ RPE 7.
* **Accessory:** Romanian Deadlift (RDL) — $3\text{ sets of }10\text{ reps}$ @ RPE 6.
* *Week 6: Deload (cut weight by 50%).*

#### Phase 3: Foundational Bilateral Strength (Weeks 7–10)
* **Frequency:** 2 sessions per week.
* **Primary Lift:** Heavy Back Squat — $3\text{ to }4\text{ sets of }4\text{–}6\text{ reps}$ @ RPE 8–9 (Rest 3–4 min).
* **Secondary Lift:** Bulgarian Split Squat — $3\text{ sets of }5\text{–}6\text{ reps/leg}$ @ RPE 7.5.
* **Accessory:** Trap-Bar Deadlift or Good Mornings — $2\text{ sets of }6\text{–}8\text{ reps}$ @ RPE 6.
* *Week 10: Deload (cut weight by 50%).*

#### Phase 4: Unilateral Neural Drive & Peak Transfer (Weeks 11–14)
* **Frequency:** 1 to 2 sessions per week (paired with on-bike build training).
* **Primary Lift (Unilateral First):** Heavy Single-Leg Squat or Heavy Single-Leg Press — $3\text{ to }4\text{ sets of }4\text{–}5\text{ reps/leg}$ @ RPE 8.5 (train weak/injured leg first).
* **Secondary Lift:** Trap-Bar Deadlift (High Handles) — $3\text{ sets of }3\text{–}5\text{ reps}$ @ RPE 8.
* **Accessory:** Light Dumbbell Rows & Core Anti-Rotation Planks.

#### In-Season Maintenance (Race Season)
* **Frequency:** 1 session every 7 to 14 days (scheduled post-long ride or on Monday active recovery).
* **Workout:**
  1. Back Squat: $2 \times 5 \text{ reps}$ @ $60–70\%$ 1RM (RPE 6, non-fatiguing).
  2. Single-Leg Press: $2 \times 6 \text{ reps/leg}$ @ RPE 6.
  3. 7-Way Hips: 1 set for lateral hip maintenance.
* **Duration:** 25–30 minutes total.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The Spring Transition Refusal:** Cyclists often fall in love with gym progress and refuse to decrease volume when outdoor interval training and racing begin. Continuing heavy 2x/week lifting during race blocks causes persistent leg heaviness, autonomic suppression, and race failure.

1. **Skipping the Adaptation Phase:** Jumping straight into heavy sets of 5 on Week 1 causes crippling 48-hour DOMS and inflamed patellar tendons.
2. **Under-Fueling Protein and Carbohydrates:** Inadequate post-lift nutrition ($<20\text{g}$ protein, low carbohydrates) extends recovery timelines from 48 hours to 96+ hours.
3. **Omitting Deload Weeks:** Tendons and ligaments adapt slower than contractile muscle; skipping deloads leads to chronic connective tissue inflammation.
4. **Performing High-Risk Plyometrics:** Prescribing high box jumps or depth jumps to fatigued endurance athletes leads to acute shin trauma and tendon tears with zero pedal-stroke transfer.

### Coach & Athlete Periodization Checklist

* [ ] **Map Calendar:** Align Phase 1–3 with off-season base training (Nov–Jan) and Phase 4 with early pre-season build (Feb–Mar).
* [ ] **Enforce Deload Weeks:** Schedule a 50% load deload every 3–4 weeks without exception.
* [ ] **Flip to Unilateral in Phase 4:** Place single-leg exercises first when moving into the high-torque specificity phase.
* [ ] **Transition to Maintenance:** Drop gym volume to 1 brief submaximal session per week 4–6 weeks prior to target A-races.
* [ ] **Maintain Daily Protein Intake:** Ingest 20–25g of high-quality protein every 3–4 hours to support muscle retention throughout the season.
