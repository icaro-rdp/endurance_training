# Ten Minute Tips #50: Fat And Carb Burning Myths — Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Hilson (Ten Minute Tips #50)_

---

## What Are the Core Fat & Carb Burning Myths?

Endurance sports culture is saturated with oversimplified metabolic models that treat human energy expenditure like a rudimentary bank account and misattribute substrate utilization to body composition and training adaptation.

This guide analyzes three fundamental myths:
1. **Myth 1:** *"You only need to replace the carbohydrates you burned on your ride."*
2. **Myth 2:** *"Maximizing fat oxidation on the bike is an effective strategy for body fat loss."*
3. **Myth 3:** *"Daily caloric needs equal calculated Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) plus mechanical bike kilojoules ($1\text{ kJ} \approx 1\text{ kcal}$)."*

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
[Contractile Demand] ──> [Cellular Stress: Ca2+, AMPK, PGC-1α] ──> [Aerobic Adaptation] (Independent of fuel)
                                       ▲
[Intra-Ride Fueling] ──> Maintains Blood Glucose & Spares Liver Glycogen
                                       ▼
[Endocrine Status]   ──> High Deficit: Suppressed Leptin/Thyroid/Testosterone ──> Impaired Recovery & LEA
```

### 1. The Myth of Substrate-Specific Replacement
- **The Fallacy:** If an athlete burns 3500 kcal on a 5-hour endurance ride (e.g., 2500 kcal from fat and 1000 kcal from carbohydrate), common lore suggests only eating 1000 kcal of carbs post-ride to "restore glycogen."
- **The Physiological Reality:** The human body does not isolate substrate accounting. Burning 3500 kcal creates a **systemic energy deficit**. If the athlete only replaces 1000 kcal, they carry a 2500 kcal acute deficit.
- **The Hourly Rate Problem:** Losing 1 lb of fat (3500 kcal) in 5 hours corresponds to $0.2\text{ lb/hour}$. Recommended sustainable fat loss is $\sim 0.5\text{ lb/week}$ ($0.003\text{ lb/hour}$). A single massive acute deficit is **over 60 times** the sustainable fat mobilization rate, triggering intense homeostatic resistance.

### 2. Adipose Tissue as an Active Endocrine Organ
Adipose tissue is not passive storage; it regulates metabolic rate and reproductive/immune health via hormonal signaling:
- **Leptin:** Secreted by adipocytes to signal energy sufficiency to the hypothalamus. Acute large energy deficits crater leptin levels, triggering ravenous hunger, binge impulses, and lethargy.
- **Thyroid Hormones ($T_3/T_4$):** Low energy availability downregulates thyroid output, reducing uncoupling protein (UCP) activation in mitochondria, lowering basal body temperature (feeling constantly cold), and blunting basal metabolic rate.
- **Anabolic Signaling ($IGF\text{-}1$, Testosterone):** Acute deficits suppress testosterone and growth hormone signaling, compromising muscle protein synthesis and next-day neuromuscular force generation.

### 3. Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) and the BMR + kJ Fallacy
The widely used calculation:
$$\text{Total Daily Calories} = \text{BMR} + \text{Bike kJ}$$
consistently underestimates actual energy expenditure:
- **Doubly Labeled Water ($^2\text{H}_2^{18}\text{O}$) Validation:** Studies on professional cyclists (such as Grand Tour riders) show that baseline non-bike metabolic expenditure ranges between **1.6 to 2.3$\times$ BMR** before factoring in mechanical work done on the bike.
- **Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (NEAT) & Cellular Repair:** High training loads elevate systemic protein turnover, cardiac recovery work, immune modulation, and thermic effect of food (TEF), requiring substantially more background energy than sedentary BMR formulas suggest.
- **Reporting Bias:** Self-reported dietary diaries under-report intake by 20–30% on average, leading athletes to believe they can sustain high training volumes on artificially low caloric intakes.

### 4. Carbohydrate Ingestion and Aerobic Signaling
- **Substrate vs. Adaptation Signaling:** Aerobic mitochondrial adaptations are triggered by mechanical contraction, intracellular calcium oscillations ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$/CaMK), AMP/ATP ratio shifts (AMPK activation), and sirtuins. The presence of exogenous carbohydrates in the bloodstream does not turn off these pathways.
- **Exogenous Glucose Mechanics:** Ingested carbohydrates directly maintain blood glucose levels, preventing hypoglycemia and sparing liver glycogen. They do not prevent muscle adaptations and drastically reduce post-exercise cortisol and immunosuppression.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. On-the-Bike Fueling Protocols
- **Endurance / Zone 2 Rides (>90 min):** Target **60–80 g/hr** of mixed carbohydrates (maltodextrin:fructose). Even at lower power outputs, intra-ride fueling attenuates post-ride hunger spikes and preserves glycogen for subsequent high-intensity days.
- **Threshold & High-Intensity Sessions:** Target **80–100+ g/hr** to maximize glycolytic throughput and maintain power output across interval sets.
- **Ultra-Endurance (>6–8 hours):** Incorporate small amounts of dietary fat and protein alongside carbohydrates to enhance satiety, prevent GI mucosal irritation, and meet baseline structural demands.

### 2. Caloric Deficit Guidelines for Weight Management

| Athlete Profile | Safe Rate of Weight Loss | Daily Caloric Deficit | Key Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lean / Well-Trained (<12–15% Body Fat)** | 0.25–0.5 lb / week ($\approx 0.1–0.2\text{ kg}$) | 200–350 kcal / day | Fuel all workouts fully on the bike; trim calories from non-training meals |
| **Moderate Body Fat (15–22% Body Fat)** | 0.5–1.0 lb / week ($\approx 0.25–0.45\text{ kg}$) | 350–500 kcal / day | Maintain high protein intake (1.6–2.2 g/kg); monitor sleep and mood |
| **High Body Fat (>25% Body Fat)** | 1.0–1.5 lb / week ($\approx 0.45–0.7\text{ kg}$) | 500–750 kcal / day | Progressive lifestyle habit changes; avoid crash diets |

### 3. Monitoring Energy Availability
- **Post-Ride Nutrition Timing:** Ingest a carbohydrate-rich meal (with 25–35g protein) within 45–60 minutes post-ride. Prolonged post-workout fasting significantly increases 24-hour markers of Low Energy Availability (LEA) and degrades next-day interval execution.
- **Symptom Checks for Excessive Energy Deficit:**
  - Constantly cold extremities in warm rooms
  - Irritability, brain fog, and obsessive food thoughts
  - Waking in the middle of the night with ravenous hunger
  - Elevated resting HR and suppressed heart rate response during intervals

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

```
                      [Dieting / Caloric Strategy]
                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
[Aggressive Deficit (>750 kcal/day)]               [Moderate Deficit (~300 kcal/day)]
         │                                                   │
  • Crashing Leptin / Thyroid                         • Preserved Thyroid & Leptin
  • Severe Recovery Impairment                        • High Workout Quality Maintained
  • Binge-Eating Rebound Cycle                        • Steady, Sustainable Fat Loss
```

1. **Fasting on the Bike to "Force Fat Adaptation":**
   Withholding carbs during 3–4 hour rides lowers workout quality, increases muscle protein breakdown, and triggers severe rebound hunger without accelerating mitochondrial biogenesis.
2. **Treating Calorie Calculators as Absolute Truth:**
   Online Harris-Benedict or Katch-McArdle formulas do not account for individual muscle mass, athlete training history, or elevated background recovery metabolism. Treat calculators as loose starting baselines and adjust based on scale trends and energy levels over 3–4 week cycles.
3. **Conflating Scale Weight Fluctuations with Fat Loss:**
   Day-to-day scale variations of 1–3 kg reflect glycogen hydration status (each gram of glycogen binds 3–4g of water), gut residue, and cortisol-induced water retention, not acute fat gain or loss.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Fueling & Energy Balance Rules

| Situation | Incorrect Approach | Physiologically Correct Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Long Zone 2 Base Ride** | Water only to maximize fat oxidation | 60–80 g/hr carbs; fuel to protect endocrine balance and recovery |
| **Post-Ride Recovery** | Skipping lunch to "prolong fat burning" | Consume carb + protein meal promptly to initiate glycogen synthesis |
| **Weight Loss Phase** | Slashing intake by 1000+ kcal/day | Modest 300–400 kcal deficit; keep on-bike carbs high, reduce rest-day fats/portions |
| **TDEE Calculation** | BMR + Bike kJ as strict ceiling | Multiply BMR by 1.5–1.8 activity factor *before* adding heavy training loads |

### Athlete Action Checklist
- [ ] **Fuel the Work:** Consume carbohydrates during any ride exceeding 75–90 minutes regardless of targeted intensity zone.
- [ ] **Differentiate Exercise Substrate from Energy Balance:** Remember that burning fat in muscle does not equal reducing adipose tissue; net 24-hour caloric balance dictates body composition.
- [ ] **Protect Baseline Health:** If experiencing chronic coldness, insomnia, or lethargy, increase caloric intake immediately to prevent Low Energy Availability (LEA).
- [ ] **Avoid Hourly Deficit Spikes:** Distribute nutrition evenly throughout the day rather than creating multi-thousand-calorie fasting windows around hard training blocks.
