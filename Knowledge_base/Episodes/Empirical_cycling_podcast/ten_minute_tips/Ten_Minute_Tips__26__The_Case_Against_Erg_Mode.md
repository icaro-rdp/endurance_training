# Ten Minute Tips #26: The Case Against Erg Mode — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Helson (Ten Minute Tips #26)_

---

## What Is ERG Mode (and Why the Skepticism)?

**ERG (Ergometer) Mode** is an automated smart-trainer feature that dynamically adjusts resistance to hold a rider at an exact target wattage regardless of cadence or gear selection. If your cadence drops, the trainer increases resistance (torque) to maintain the wattage; if your cadence rises, resistance decreases.

While marketed as the ultimate tool for precision training ("set it and forget it"), over-reliance on ERG mode introduces profound physiological and pedagogical flaws:

* **The Illusion of Perfection:** A perfectly flat, square-wave power file on a screen looks visually satisfying, but physiological systems do not function as static square waves. Real-world power demands constant neuromuscular calibration, terrain negotiation, and cadence modulation.
* **The "GPS Navigation" Analogy:** Relying entirely on ERG mode is like driving exclusively with turn-by-turn GPS navigation—you reach the destination without ever developing an internal mental map of the terrain. Athletes lose touch with Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE) and become incapable of pacing without digital constraints.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The RPE-Power Feedback Loop & Autoregulation
Training is not merely a mechanical stress; it is a neurological and bioenergetic dialogue between the brain, cardiovascular system, and contracting skeletal muscle.

* **Internal Sensor Calibration:** Under standard resistance/level mode, the athlete constantly reads sensory feedback (breathing depth, ventilation threshold, peripheral muscle burning, muscle recruitment tension) to adjust power output. This tightens the athlete's perception of critical thresholds (LT1, FTP, VO2 kinetics).
* **Fatigue-Induced Threshold Shifts:** LT1 and FTP are not static constants; on any given day, accumulated training fatigue, glycogen depletion, poor sleep, or life stress can depress physiological thresholds by 10–30 Watts.
* **The ERG Trap:** ERG mode imposes an external mechanical requirement that ignores internal metabolic state. An athlete whose FTP has dropped 20W due to acute fatigue is forced to ride above threshold in heavy metabolic acidosis, accumulating severe autonomic fatigue while trying to "pass" a workout.

```
Biological Feedback vs. ERG Mode:
Self-Paced Mode: Muscle Sensation / Ventilation -> Central Nervous System -> Modulate Power to Match Physiological State
ERG Mode:       Pre-Programmed Target -> Smart Trainer Resistance -> Forced Muscular Force Regardless of Fatigue
```

### 2. Why High-Intensity Work (VO2max & Anaerobic) Fails in ERG Mode
High-intensity interval training requires unconstrained maximal effort, not arbitrary percentages of FTP:

* **VO2max Physiology:** In trained endurance athletes, maximal stroke volume and maximal oxygen uptake ($VO_2max$) occur when working at maximal aerobic capacity. Because FTP-to-$VO_2max$ power ratios vary wildly across individuals (from 70% to 88%), setting ERG mode to a generic 120% of FTP almost always under-stimulates or prematurely blows up the athlete.
* **Hard-Start VO2 Kinetics:** Maximizing time near $VO_2max$ requires a hard initial start to rapidly deplete phosphocreatine and accelerate oxygen uptake kinetics, followed by natural power decay. ERG mode prevents this dynamic pacing.
* **Anaerobic Capacity and Sprints:** Maximal sprints and anaerobic intervals exhibit steep non-linear power degradation (e.g., peaking at 1400W and decaying to 600W). If an athlete attempts a 30-second all-out sprint on ERG mode, the trainer's attempt to clamp a fixed target triggers the **"Spiral of Death"**—as fatigue slows the pedal stroke, torque spikes exponentially until the rider stalls out completely.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Transitioning from ERG Mode to Self-Pacing (Resistance Mode)
Coaches and self-coached athletes should systematically wean themselves off ERG mode:

* **Use Standard Level/Resistance Mode:** Set the indoor trainer to a fixed resistance slope (Level 1–3 or Standard Curve) and use mechanical shifting and cadence to modulate power exactly as on the open road.
* **Negative-Splitting Strategy:** On long endurance rides or threshold blocks, begin 15–20W below target for the first 15–30 minutes, allowing muscle temperature and metabolic pathways to open up before settling into target RPE.
* **Threshold by Feel:** Perform threshold blocks (e.g., $2 \times 20\text{ min}$, $3 \times 15\text{ min}$) aiming for "maximal steady power." If breathing becomes ragged and RPE climbs to 9.5/10 early in the second interval, back off 10W immediately rather than grinding to exhaustion.

```
Pacing Progression without ERG:
Warm-Up (0-20 min)        --> Start easy (RPE 2/10), allow natural cardiac & metabolic warmup
First Interval Portion    --> Establish steady breathing, check RPE (6-7/10 for FTP)
Second Interval Portion   --> Maintain rhythm via gear shifts and cadence (85-95 RPM)
Final Interval Portion    --> Autoregulate; finish strong with 0.5-1 RIR (Reps in Reserve)
```

### 2. Legitimate, High-Value Use Cases for ERG Mode
While generally discouraged for high-intensity or standard threshold work, ERG mode has specific valid training applications:

1. **Strict Active Recovery (Zone 1):** For athletes prone to riding too hard on recovery days, locking ERG mode at 40–50% FTP ensures true metabolic rest.
2. **Virtual Rolling/Climbing Simulation (Zwift / RGT):** Simulating low-inertia, high-torque mountain climbs for athletes living in flat geographical regions (Midwest, flat coastal plains).
3. **Fartlek & Race Simulation:** Riding structured virtual courses where resistance shifts organically with the gradient, teaching athletes to carry speed over crests and manage race surges.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

> [!WARNING]
> **The ERG "Spiral of Death":** If cadence drops even slightly during a fatiguing interval, the trainer increases brake resistance to maintain wattage. This forces a further drop in cadence, creating an exponential torque spike that halts the pedal stroke entirely, risking knee joint strain and severe psychological frustration.

1. **Square-Wave Obsession:** Believing that brief 5–10 second power drops (e.g., taking a drink, adjusting position, or real-world coasting into a corner) ruin the physiological efficacy of an interval.
2. **Loss of Shifting & Cadence Mastery:** Athletes trained exclusively on ERG mode lose the neuromuscular ability to rapidly shift gears under load, modulate cadence over changing road gradients, and generate explosive out-of-the-saddle torque.
3. **Pass/Fail Mentality:** Viewing training through binary software completion criteria. If an athlete barely completes a $4 \times 8\text{ min}$ ERG threshold session at an agonizing 10/10 RPE, the software records a "pass," but the coach sees excessive autonomic overreaching that compromises the remainder of the training block.

| Scenario / Workout Type | ERG Mode Recommended? | Recommended Protocol | Primary Reason |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |

| **Threshold / Sweet Spot** | ❌ **No** | Resistance mode; pace by RPE and breathing threshold. | Allows autoregulation for daily fatigue and prevents excessive autonomic stress. |

| **Low-Inertia Climb Sim** | ✅ **Yes** | Virtual course grade simulation (Zwift/RGT). | Simulates uphill pedal mechanics and high torque for flatland riders. |
