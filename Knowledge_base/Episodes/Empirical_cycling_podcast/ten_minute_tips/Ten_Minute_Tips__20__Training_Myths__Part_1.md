# Ten Minute Tips #20: Training Myths, Part 1 — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Kyle Houston_

---

## What Are the Core Training Myths Examined?

Endurance sports culture is filled with legacy dogmas inherited from professional lore or misapplied scientific mechanisms:
1. **Myth 1:** *"Grinding in a big gear at 50 RPM on the bike counts as strength training."*
2. **Myth 2:** *"You must starve carbs (fasted riding / ketogenic diets) to become an efficient fat burner."*
3. **Myth 3:** *"Base training must strictly be done in the small chainring at low power."*

```
  ┌─────────────────────────────────┐      ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
  │       Pervasive Myth            │      │      Physiological Reality      │
  ├─────────────────────────────────┤      ├─────────────────────────────────┤
  │ Low cadence = Strength Work     │ ──►  │ Pure concentric, low peak force │
  │ Fasted / Keto = Fat Adaptation  │ ──►  │ Substrate forced, blunts PDH    │
  │ Small Ring Base = Universal Law │ ──►  │ Fails overload on low volume    │
  └─────────────────────────────────┘      └─────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. Myth 1: Low-Cadence Riding vs. Neuromuscular Strength Training

#### A. Mechanotransduction and the Eccentric Requirement
True strength adaptations (increased maximal force production, rate of force development, and myofibrillar hypertrophy) require:
- **High Mechanical Tension under Stretch:** Muscle sensors called **costameres** and titin kinase sense tension during the **eccentric (lengthening)** phase of a contraction. This mechanical strain triggers intracellular signaling cascades driving structural protein synthesis.
- **Maximal Motor Unit Recruitment:** High-threshold Type IIx motor units are only recruited when loads approach $>80–85\%$ of 1-Repetition Maximum ($\text{1RM}$).

#### B. Why Big Gear Grinding Fails as Strength Training
- **Concentric-Only Contraction:** In the cycling pedal stroke, muscles contract only concentrically (shortening from top-dead-center). There is zero loaded lengthening/eccentric phase.
- **Inadequate Force Production:** Even at $500\text{ Watts}$ and $50\text{ RPM}$, the peak force applied to the pedal is only ~400–600 Newtons (~40–60 kg of force per leg). For a trained athlete, this represents $<20–30\%$ of maximal voluntary isometric contraction.
- **Repetition Domain:** Grinding for 10 minutes at 50 RPM involves **500 pedal strokes per leg**. High repetitions at submaximal force stimulate muscular endurance and oxidative enzyme activity, not maximal neuromuscular strength.

```
  Parameter                  Gym Resistance Training (Squat)     Low-Cadence Cycling (50 RPM)
 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Contraction Type           Eccentric + Concentric              Purely Concentric
  Peak Force (% 1RM)         80–95% 1RM                          < 20–30% 1RM
  Mechanotransduction        High (Costamere/Titin strain)       Minimal (No eccentric stretch)
  Repetition Range           1–6 reps                            500–5,000+ reps
  Primary Adaptation         Rate of Force Development, RFD      Muscular endurance, glycolytic flux
```

### 2. Myth 2: Substrate Availability vs. Aerobic Adaptations

#### A. Substrate Selection Bioenergetics
Fatty acid oxidation requires transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria via **Carnitine Palmitoyltransferase 1 (CPT-1)** for $\beta$-oxidation into Acetyl-CoA.
- When carbohydrate availability is zero (fasted or ketogenic state), the muscle cell is forced to burn fatty acids purely through mass action.
- Up-regulating fat transport proteins (FAT/CD36, CPT-1) occurs in response to substrate presence, but **does not increase total mitochondrial volume or $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$**.

#### B. The Cost of Ketogenic / Fasted Adaptations
- **Pyruvate Dehydrogenase (PDH) Downregulation:** Chronic high-fat / low-carb diets downregulate PDH, the critical enzyme facilitating glycolytic flux into the Krebs cycle.
- **Blunted Top-End Power:** Athletes lose the enzymatic capacity to utilize glycogen rapidly during supra-threshold attacks, sprints, and sustained threshold efforts (validated in Burke et al. race-walking studies).
- **True Drivers of Mitochondrial Biogenesis:** Mitochondrial growth is triggered by **cellular energy flux**—intracellular calcium spikes ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$/CaMK), elevated AMP/ATP ratios (AMPK), and mechanical shear stress stimulating **PGC-1$\alpha$**, which is best achieved through high training volume and well-fueled high quality.

### 3. Myth 3: The "Small Chainring" Base Fallacy

#### A. Pro Lore vs. Amateur Reality
The rule of "riding strictly in the little ring for months" was developed by professional riders accumulating 25–35 hours per week in the off-season.
- At 30 hours/week, low intensity is mandatory to prevent systemic overtraining while accumulating 15,000+ kJ of work.

#### B. Failure of Overload in Time-Crunched Athletes
- An athlete riding 6–10 hours/week strictly in the little ring at 120–150W creates insufficient total mechanical work ($\approx 2,500–4,000\text{ kJ}$) to induce progressive aerobic overload after the initial 3–4 weeks.
- Base adaptations require progressive overload: if volume cannot expand due to time constraints, **intensity density** (Sweet Spot, Tempo, and sustained threshold blocks) must be applied.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. How to Structure Real Strength Training for Cyclists
- **Location:** Perform in the gym with barbells or heavy resistance.
- **Movement Patterns:** Deep back squats, front squats, Romanian deadlifts, unilateral split squats.
- **Loading Scheme:**
  - Sets: $3–5$ sets of $3–6$ repetitions.
  - Intensity: $80–90\%\text{ 1RM}$ with explosive concentric intent and controlled eccentric phase ($2–3\text{ seconds}$).
  - Rest: $2–3\text{ minutes}$ between sets to ensure full phosphagen ($\text{PCr}$) resynthesis.

### 2. Fueling for Endurance and Fat Oxidation
- **Fuel All Quality Work:** Ingest 60–90+ g/hr of carbohydrates during hard interval sessions and long base rides.
- **Allow Natural Fat Oxidation:** High aerobic base training naturally elevates fat oxidation capacity at sub-LT1 power without requiring carbohydrate starvation.

### 3. Base Training for Different Volume Tiers

| Athlete Time Availability | Base Strategy | Intensity Distribution | Overload Parameter |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High Volume ($15–25+\text{ hrs/wk}$)** | Pure Zone 2 / Sub-LT1 Endurance | Mostly small/middle ring, easy spinning | Weekly hours and total kJ |
| **Moderate Volume ($10–14\text{ hrs/wk}$)** | Mixed Zone 2 + Tempo blocks | Sub-LT1 with 1–2 steady tempo/sweetspot rides | Duration of long rides + progressive kJ |
| **Time-Crunched ($5–8\text{ hrs/wk}$)** | Density Base (Sweet Spot / Threshold) | Sub-threshold intervals + 1 longer endurance ride | Time-in-Zone progression ($3\times 20' \to 2\times 40'$) |

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **Relying on Low Cadence to "Strengthen the Knees/Legs":**
   - Imposing massive joint torque at 50 RPM without eccentric loading often causes patellar tendonitis rather than muscular hypertrophy.
2. **Fasting High-Intensity Sessions:**
   - Performing VO2max or threshold workouts fasted, resulting in premature glycogen depletion, elevated cortisol, muscle protein breakdown, and reduced power output.
3. **Copying Pro Winter Training Plans:**
   - Adopting a 20-hour easy spinning routine on an 8-hour weekly budget, leading to stagnation in aerobic power.

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

```
Decision Flow: Debunking Training Dogmas
```

1. **Need to increase sprint power and rate of force development?**
   - **DO:** Lift heavy weights in the gym ($3–5\text{ reps} @ >80\%\text{ 1RM}$) focusing on deep range of motion.
   - **DON'T:** Grind at 50 RPM on a flat road or do standing starts expecting significant hypertrophy.

2. **Need to increase fat oxidation and fuel efficiency?**
   - **DO:** Ride high volume below LT1 and progress aerobic duration while maintaining adequate carbohydrate fueling.
   - **DON'T:** Starve carbohydrates or adopt chronic ketogenic protocols that degrade glycolytic enzymes.

3. **Planning winter base training?**
   - **If $>15\text{ hrs/wk}$:** Prioritize low-intensity sub-LT1 volume.
   - **If $\le 8\text{ hrs/wk}$:** Incorporate Sweet Spot, Tempo, and threshold extensions to achieve sufficient metabolic stress.
