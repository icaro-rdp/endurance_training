# Homemade Hydration Mix Formulation, Osmolality, & Electrolyte Dynamics — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore (Ten Minute Tips #2)_

---

## What Is Exercise Hydration & Electrolyte Homeostasis?

During prolonged endurance exercise, thermoregulatory sweating causes the loss of both water and electrolytes from blood plasma. If fluid losses exceed 2–3% of total body mass without replacement, athletes experience:
* **Plasma Volume Reduction:** Lower end-diastolic filling pressure and decreased stroke volume.
* **Cardiovascular Drift:** A progressive elevation in heart rate to maintain cardiac output as blood volume falls.
* **Impaired Thermoregulation:** Decreased skin blood flow and elevated core body temperature, accelerating central fatigue.

Effective hydration during exercise is not simply about drinking plain water. Ingesting pure water in large volumes dilutes extracellular sodium (risking exercise-associated hyponatremia) and shuts down thirst mechanisms prematurely. A true hydration mix provides the precise balance of **water, sodium, and simple carbohydrates** required to maximize fluid absorption across the intestinal lumen into the bloodstream.

### 1. The SGLT1 Water Absorption "Piggyback"
Water moves across the intestinal epithelium passively down osmotic gradients. The presence of low concentrations of sodium and glucose actively accelerates this process:
* **Sodium-Glucose Linked Transporter 1 (SGLT1):** Actively transports 1 molecule of glucose and 2 sodium ions ($Na^+$) across the apical brush border membrane of enterocytes.
* **Hydration Flux:** As $Na^+$ and glucose enter the epithelial cells, they create a steep localized osmotic gradient that draws approximately **260 water molecules** across the membrane per transport cycle.
* Combining **Dextrose (pure glucose)** with **Sucrose (a disaccharide of glucose + fructose)** leverages both SGLT1 and GLUT5 transporters, optimizing absorption without saturating a single pathway.

### 2. Osmolality and Gastric Emptying: Hypotonic vs. Hypertonic
* **Hypotonic Solutions (~3–5% Carbohydrate / ~200–250 mOsm/kg):** Empty rapidly from the stomach into the duodenum and promote net water absorption into blood plasma faster than pure water.
* **Isotonic Solutions (~6–8% Carbohydrate / ~280–300 mOsm/kg):** Match plasma osmolality; provide balanced fluid and energy delivery.
* **Hypertonic Solutions (>8–10% Carbohydrate / >350 mOsm/kg):** High-carbohydrate fueling mixes draw water *out* of the bloodstream and into the gut lumen to dilute the solution before absorption can occur, delaying hydration and causing gastrointestinal distress under high heat stress.

```
 Solution Concentration vs. Primary Function
 
 [ 2% - 5% Solution ] ────────────────────────► [ 10% - 15%+ Solution ]
   Hypotonic Hydration Mix                         Hypertonic Fueling Mix
   • Maximizes fluid uptake                        • Maximizes carb intake (g/hr)
   • Optimal for hot/sweaty conditions             • Requires separate plain water
```

### 3. Electrolyte Losses: Sodium vs. Secondary Salts
* **Sodium ($Na^+$):** The predominant extracellular cation. Sweat sodium concentration ranges from **500 mg to 1,500+ mg per liter of sweat** depending on genetics, sweat rate, and heat acclimation. Maintaining sodium prevents hyponatremia and preserves plasma osmolality.
* **Potassium ($K^+$), Calcium ($Ca^{2+}$), Magnesium ($Mg^{2+}$):** Predominantly intracellular ions. Exercise sweat losses are tiny (typically 50–100 mg/L for potassium and calcium, <20 mg/L for magnesium). High-dose supplementation of potassium or magnesium during exercise is unnecessary and can induce gastrointestinal cramping.
* **The "Coconut Water" Fallacy:** Coconut water is rich in potassium (~600 mg/L) but extremely poor in sodium (~30–50 mg/L), making it ineffective for high-sweat exercise rehydration.

### 4. Sodium Citrate vs. Sodium Chloride (Table Salt)
* **Sodium Chloride ($NaCl$):** Highly unpalatable in water at effective sodium doses, imparting an unpleasant harsh salty/briny flavor that discourages voluntary drinking.
* **Sodium Citrate ($Na_3C_6H_5O_7$):** Contains ~23.5% elemental sodium by mass. It has a smooth, mildly tart, non-salty taste profile that enhances fruit flavor palatability while acting as an extracellular buffer without GI irritation.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### The Empirical Cycling DIY Hydration Mix Recipe

The baseline recipe yields a **~4–5% hypotonic solution** designed for a standard **500 mL (approx. 16–20 oz)** water bottle.

```
 Baseline DIY Hydration Mix Formulation (Per 500 mL Water)
 ┌──────────────────────┬────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ Ingredient           │ Mass (Grams)   │ Functional Role / Target                               │
 ├──────────────────────┼────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ Dextrose (Glucose)   │ 12.0 g – 14.0 g│ SGLT1 substrate, rapid energy delivery                 │
 │ Sucrose (Cane Sugar) │ 8.0 g – 10.0 g │ Fructose + Glucose source, gut transporter diversity   │
 │ Sodium Citrate       │ 1.0 g – 2.0 g  │ 235 mg – 470 mg elemental Sodium (adjust for sweat rate)│
 │ Calcium Lactate      │ 0.1 g – 0.2 g  │ Minor electrolyte support (optional)                   │
 │ Potassium Citrate    │ 0.05 g – 0.1 g │ Minor electrolyte support (optional / skippable)       │
 │ Citric Acid          │ 0.2 g – 0.5 g  │ Tartness, flavor enhancer, promotes salivation         │
 │ Ascorbic Acid (Vit C)│ 0.05 g – 0.1 g │ Adjusts mouthfeel dryness (optional)                   │
 │ Freeze-Dried Fruit   │ 1.5 g – 3.0 g  │ Natural flavor, ground to fine powder while cold       │
 └──────────────────────┴────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> [!NOTE]
> **Total Carbohydrates per Bottle:** ~20–24 grams (~80–95 kcal).
> **Total Solution Concentration:** ~4.2–4.8% (optimal hypotonic window for rapid absorption).

---

### Adjusting for Individual Sweat Profile & Environment

#### 1. Salty vs. Light Sweaters
* **Salty Sweaters** (white salt crust on skin/kit, stinging eyes, salt cravings): Increase **Sodium Citrate to 2.5–3.5 g per 500 mL bottle** (delivering **~600–820 mg elemental sodium**).
* **Light Sweaters / Cool Conditions:** Use **0.8–1.2 g Sodium Citrate** (~190–280 mg elemental sodium).

#### 2. Environmental Temperature Scaling
* **Extreme Heat (>30°C / 85°F) & High Humidity:**
  * Dilute the mix: Add the same single-serving recipe packet into **650–750 mL of water** (~3.0–3.5% solution).
  * Increases fluid delivery rate per gram of carbohydrate to match extreme sweat evaporation demands.
* **Cold Weather (<10°C / 50°F):**
  * Concentrate the mix: Dissolve the recipe packet into **400–450 mL of water** (~5.0–5.5% solution).
  * Sweat rates are low; fluid requirements decrease relative to energy demands.

### Preparation & Sourcing Best Practices

1. **Processing Freeze-Dried Fruit:**
   * Grind freeze-dried fruit (e.g., raspberry, lemon, mango, blueberry) in a high-speed blender or coffee grinder while completely dry and cold.
   * Grinding warm or humid fruit creates a sticky paste that coats the blade. Store the ground fruit powder in an airtight container with a desiccant pack.
2. **Bulk Batching:**
   * Multiply all dry ingredients by 20 or 50, blend thoroughly in a large dry vessel, and portion out using a single calibrated scoop (~25–28 g scoop).
3. **Anti-Doping & Ingredient Purity:**
   * Source pure food-grade or pharmaceutical-grade chemicals (e.g., USP/FCC grade).
   * Athletes subject to WADA/USADA anti-doping testing should purchase single-ingredient raw compounds from verified, third-party audited suppliers (NSF Certified for Sport or Informed Sport) to prevent cross-contamination.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

1. **The "High-Carb in the Heat" Mistake:**
   * Attempting to consume 90–120 g/hr of carbohydrates entirely in bottles during hot weather creates a dense hypertonic solution (>10–12%) in the gut, stalling gastric emptying and causing severe bloating and nausea. Separate hydration (hypotonic bottles) from high-density solid/gel fueling.
2. **Over-Relying on Table Salt ($NaCl$):**
   * Using pure table salt to hit high sodium targets makes the drink taste like seawater, suppressing the urge to drink. Always use sodium citrate for the primary sodium source in liquid solutions; obtain dietary chloride through salted real foods (e.g., boiled potatoes, rice cakes).
3. **Excessive Citric Acid:**
   * Adding too much citric acid makes the mix excessively acidic, eroding tooth enamel over long rides and irritating the stomach lining. Keep citric acid below 0.5 g per 500 mL.
4. **Ignoring Thirst and Over-Hydrating:**
   * Forced hyper-hydration beyond fluid loss does not improve performance and risks hyponatremia. Fluid intake should track sweat loss and thirst dynamics.

### Hydration Execution Checklist for Athletes
* [ ] **Estimate Sweat Rate:** Weigh yourself naked before and after a 60-minute hard ride (1 kg lost $\approx$ 1 L sweat).
* [ ] **Assess Salt Residue:** If clothing has white salt rings and eyes sting, start with at least 1.5–2.0 g of sodium citrate per bottle.
* [ ] **Select Bottle Dilution:** Use 500 mL water for temperate days; increase to 650–750 mL per packet on hot (>30°C / 85°F) days.
* [ ] **Separate Fuel vs. Fluid:** If targeting >60 g/hr of carbohydrate, take the excess carbs from energy gels or solid chews while keeping bottle osmolality strictly hypotonic.
* [ ] **Post-Ride Electrolyte Balance:** Consume chloride and balanced micronutrients through whole salted meals post-exercise.
