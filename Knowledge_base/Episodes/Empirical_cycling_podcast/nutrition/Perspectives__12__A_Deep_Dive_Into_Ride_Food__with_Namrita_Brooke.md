# A Deep Dive Into Ride Food: Carbohydrate Ratios, Gastric Emptying, Osmolality, & Intra-Ride Fueling Architecture — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Dr. Namrita Brooke (Perspectives #12)_

---

## What Is Intra-Ride Fueling Architecture?

Intra-ride fueling is the deliberate nutritional strategy used during endurance cycling to maximize exogenous carbohydrate oxidation, preserve endogenous glycogen stores (liver and skeletal muscle), maintain plasma glucose homeostasis, and minimize systemic physiological stress.

Rather than viewing ride nutrition as an isolated caloric transaction, modern sports nutrition treats on-the-bike fueling as an integral component of **whole-day energy availability** and the primary driver of **multi-day recovery kinetics**.

```
                        The Continuum of Endurance Fueling
                        
   [Daily Baseline Nutrition] ──► [Intra-Ride Exogenous Carbs] ──► [Post-Ride Glycogen Resynthesis]
        (6–10 g/kg/day)                 (60–120 g/h)                 (Rapid Recovery Window)
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms & Gastrointestinal Dynamics

### 1. Dual-Transporter Absorption Kinetics

Carbohydrate absorption across the intestinal brush border membrane is governed by two independent transport mechanisms:

1. **Sodium-Glucose Linked Transporter 1 ($\text{SGLT1}$):**
   - Transports glucose, maltodextrin, and amylopectin across enterocytes via active, sodium-dependent symport.
   - Saturates at approximately **$1.0\text{ g/min}$ ($\approx 60\text{ g/h}$)**.
   - Forcing pure glucose intake beyond $60\text{ g/h}$ leaves unabsorbed sugar in the gut lumen, creating an osmotic gradient that pulls water into the intestines.
2. **Glucose Transporter 5 ($\text{GLUT5}$):**
   - Transports fructose via facilitated diffusion independent of sodium.
   - Can absorb an additional **$30\text{--}50\text{ g/h}$** of fructose.
3. **The 1:0.8 to 2:1 Ratio:**
   - Combining maltodextrin (or glucose) with fructose in a **$1:0.8$ to $2:1$ ratio** utilizes both $\text{SGLT1}$ and $\text{GLUT5}$ simultaneously.
   - This unlocks total exogenous oxidation rates of **$90\text{--}120+\text{ g/h}$ ($1.5\text{--}2.0\text{ g/min}$)** with minimal gastrointestinal distress.

```
                      Intestinal Carbohydrate Transport
                      
            Intestinal Lumen                       Enterocyte Membrane
       ┌───────────────────────────┐             ┌─────────────────────┐
       │ Glucose / Maltodextrin    │ ──────────► │ SGLT1 (~60 g/h max) │ ──► Bloodstream (Muscle)
       ├───────────────────────────┤             ├─────────────────────┤
       │ Fructose                  │ ──────────► │ GLUT5 (~30-50 g/h)  │ ──► Liver (Lactate/Glucose)
       └───────────────────────────┘             └─────────────────────┘
```

### 2. Osmolality, Gastric Emptying, & Hydration Physics

**Gastric emptying** (the rate at which fluid and food exit the stomach into the duodenum) is the primary gatekeeper of nutrient delivery:

- **Plasma Osmolality Baseline:** Normal human blood plasma osmolality is **$280\text{--}295\text{ mOsm/kg}$**.
- **Hypotonic vs. Hypertonic Solutions:**
  - *Hypotonic Solutions ($< 280\text{ mOsm/kg}$):* Empties rapidly from the stomach; facilitates immediate passive water absorption across the intestinal epithelium via osmotic gradients.
  - *Hypertonic Solutions ($> 300\text{ mOsm/kg}$):* Concentrated simple sugar drinks, un-diluted gels, or high-electrolyte mixtures draw water *out* of systemic circulation into the intestinal lumen to dilute the solution. This leads to **acute dehydration, bloating, cramping, and delayed carbohydrate delivery**.
- **Molecular Weight Dynamics (Maltodextrin / Cluster Dextrin):**
  - High molecular weight polymers (maltodextrin, highly branched cyclic dextrin) contain hundreds of glucose units per molecule.
  - They deliver high carbohydrate mass ($80\text{--}100\text{ g}$) at a **substantially lower particle count (low osmolality)** compared to simple sucrose or monomeric glucose, accelerating gastric transit.

```
                     Solution Osmolality & Water Movement
                     
   Hypotonic Drink (< 250 mOsm):       [Intestine] ──► (Water Flux Out) ──► [Bloodstream] (Fast Hydration)
   Hypertonic Drink (> 350 mOsm):       [Bloodstream] ──► (Water Pulled In) ──► [Intestine] (Bloating & GI Cramps)
```

### 3. Gut Training Physiology

The gastrointestinal tract is a highly adaptable, trainable organ:
- **Transporter Up-Regulation:** Chronic exposure to high-carbohydrate diets ($> 8\text{ g/kg/day}$) and intra-workout fueling upregulates the transcription and membrane density of $\text{SGLT1}$ and $\text{GLUT5}$ transporters.
- **Gastric Volume Adaptation:** Consuming larger fluid boluses ($200\text{--}250\text{ mL}$ every $15\text{ minutes}$) trains stomach distensibility, increasing the gastric emptying rate during exercise.
- **Splanchnic Blood Flow Preservation:** Exercise diverts blood flow away from the viscera toward working skeletal muscle. Gut training maintains microvascular perfusion to the mucosal lining under high sympathetic drive.

### 4. Rebound Hypoglycemia in Early-Morning Training

A frequent pitfall for athletes training at 5:00–6:00 AM:
- **The Insulin-Contraction Double Spike:** If an athlete eats a high-glycemic carbohydrate meal $15\text{--}30\text{ minutes}$ prior to rolling out, resting plasma insulin spikes to clear blood glucose. When pedaling begins, muscle contractions trigger **calcium- and $\text{AMPK}$-mediated $\text{GLUT4}$ translocation**, driving massive glucose uptake into myocytes independently of insulin.
- **The Hypoglycemic Dip:** The combination of peak circulating insulin + exercise-induced glucose uptake causes blood sugar to plummet rapidly within the first 10–15 minutes, producing lightheadedness, sudden fatigue, and heavy legs.
- **The Solution:** Consume carbohydrate **within 0–5 minutes of mounting the bike** (or immediately during the warm-up). Once exercise has commenced, elevated epinephrine/norepinephrine naturally **suppresses pancreatic insulin secretion**, allowing smooth exogenous glucose utilization without hypoglycemia.

```
                      Rebound Hypoglycemia Mechanism
                      
   Eat Carbs 20 min Pre-Ride:   [High Insulin Spike] + [Contraction GLUT4 Uptake] ──► Severe Blood Sugar Crash
   Eat Carbs 0–5 min at Start:  [Sympathetic Drive]  ──► [Insulin Suppressed]      ──► Stable Blood Sugar
```

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Fueling Matrix by Duration and Intensity

| Workout Intensity / Duration | Target Carbohydrate Rate | Fueling Medium | Hydration Target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Recovery / Easy Z2 (< 90 min)** | $0\text{--}30\text{ g/h}$ | Water or light electrolyte | $500\text{ mL/h}$ |
| **Endurance Z2 (2–4 hours)** | $45\text{--}60\text{ g/h}$ | Real food (bars, bananas, oats) or drink mix | $500\text{--}750\text{ mL/h}$ |
| **Tempo / Threshold / Over-Unders (2–3 h)** | $60\text{--}90\text{ g/h}$ | Gels, chews, $2:1$ maltodextrin:fructose mix | $750\text{--}1000\text{ mL/h}$ |
| **Hard Race / Stage Race / Ultra (> 3 h)** | $90\text{--}120\text{ g/h}$ | $1:0.8$ Maltodextrin:Fructose + hydrogel mixes | $750\text{--}1000+\text{ mL/h}$ |

---

### 2. Solid vs. Liquid vs. Gel Fueling Strategy

- **Short & High-Intensity Events (Crits, TTs, CX, Interval Sessions):** Liquid carbohydrates and semi-liquid gels only. High ventilatory rate and compromised splanchnic blood flow prevent efficient mechanical mastication and digestion of solid food.
- **Long Road Races & Marathons (3–5 hours):** Primary baseline of liquid carbs ($60\text{ g/h}$) supplemented with energy gels and easily digestible solid bars ($30\text{--}40\text{ g/h}$).
- **Ultra-Endurance & Bikepacking (> 6 hours):** Introduce savory solid foods with small amounts of fat ($2\text{--}5\text{ g}$) and protein ($5\text{--}10\text{ g}$) every 3–4 hours to prevent taste fatigue, hunger pangs, and gastrointestinal monotony.

---

### 3. Early Morning Workout Fueling Protocol

```
   [05:00 AM - Wake Up]   ──► Drink 300–500 mL water + electrolytes; get kit on
   [05:25 AM - At Bike]   ──► Consume 1 gel or 30g carbohydrate chew immediately before pedaling
   [05:30 AM - Rolling]   ──► Start continuous bottle sipping (30–60g carbs/h) from minute 15
```

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall / Mistake | Underlying Physiological Error | Practical Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Under-Fueling Easy Days** | Assuming Zone 2 requires zero carbs; leads to chronic low energy availability ($\text{LEA}$). | Ingest $30\text{--}50\text{ g/h}$ on rides $> 2\text{ hours}$ to preserve glycogen for subsequent quality days. |
| **Gels Without Water** | Consuming hypertonic gels without water causes osmotic fluid shift into the gut. | Drink $\ge 150\text{--}200\text{ mL}$ of plain water with every concentrated energy gel. |
| **Trying 120 g/h Untrained** | Flooding untrained enterocytes causes severe nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. | Progressively titrate intake: $60\text{ g/h} \rightarrow 80\text{ g/h} \rightarrow 100\text{ g/h} \rightarrow 120\text{ g/h}$ over 6–8 weeks. |
| **Fear of Carb-Loading Water Weight** | Withholding carbs pre-race due to $1\text{--}2\text{ kg}$ glycogen-bound water gain ($3\text{ g }\text{H}_2\text{O}\text{ per g glycogen}$). | Embrace glycogen-associated water; it serves as a critical endogenous fluid reserve during competition. |
| **Excessive In-Ride Fat/Fiber** | High-fat, high-fiber bars delay gastric emptying and induce severe GI distress during hard efforts. | Keep intra-workout fat $< 3\text{--}5\text{ g/serving}$ and fiber $< 2\text{ g/serving}$. |
