# Pragmatic Physiology, Fructose Metabolism, Substrate Adaptation, & Ergogenic Aids — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Tim Podlogar (Perspectives #9)_

## What Is Pragmatic Physiology and Nutrition?

Pragmatic physiology is the application of exercise metabolism and nutritional biochemistry to real-world performance without dogmatic adherence to laboratory artifacts or reductionist trends. Rather than treating single laboratory metrics (e.g., peak fat oxidation rate, VO2max) as isolated training goals, pragmatic physiology recognizes that whole-body athletic performance is governed by mitochondrial flux, substrate availability, threshold boundaries, and actionable field metrics.

In elite sport (such as WorldTour cycling with Bora-Hansgrohe), nutrition serves primarily to **support training capacity and recovery**, rather than being used as a blunt tool to induce chronic metabolic stress.

```
                           The Pragmatic Hierarchy of Fueling
                           
      ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
      │ 1. Whole-Body Energy Availability & Caloric Balance         │
      ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
      │ 2. High Intra-Ride Carbohydrate Ingestion (60–120 g/h)       │
      ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
      │ 3. Multiple Transportable Carbohydrates (1:0.8 Malt:Fruct)   │
      ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
      │ 4. Targeted Strategic Stress (Delayed Carb Feeding)          │
      └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms & Scientific Frameworks

### 1. Fructose Biochemistry & Dual-Transporter Kinetics

Intestinal carbohydrate absorption is rate-limited by transport proteins located on the apical membrane of enterocytes:

- **SGLT1 Saturation:** Glucose and maltodextrin rely on the Sodium-Glucose Linked Transporter 1 ($\text{SGLT1}$), which saturates at approximately $60\text{--}70\text{ g/h}$ ($\approx 1.0\text{--}1.1\text{ g/min}$). Ingesting glucose beyond this threshold leads to gastrointestinal accumulation, osmotic water influx, bloating, and diarrhea.
- **GLUT5 Transporter:** Fructose is absorbed via the independent, facilitated-diffusion transporter $\text{GLUT5}$. Adding fructose bypasses $\text{SGLT1}$ saturation, allowing total exogenous carbohydrate oxidation to reach $90\text{--}120+\text{ g/h}$ ($1.5\text{--}2.0\text{ g/min}$).
- **The Optimal 1:0.8 Ratio:** Historical recommendations suggested a 2:1 glucose-to-fructose ratio ($60\text{ g/h}$ glucose + $30\text{ g/h}$ fructose). Modern exercise metabolism research demonstrates that a **1:0.8 maltodextrin-to-fructose ratio** maximizes exogenous oxidation rates and gastric tolerance while minimizing residual intestinal fructose.
- **Hepatic Metabolism & Lactate Generation:** Unlike glucose, which directly enters systemic circulation for muscle uptake, fructose is metabolized predominantly in the liver:
  $$\text{Fructose} \xrightarrow{\text{Fructokinase}} \text{Fructose-1-Phosphate} \longrightarrow \text{Dihydroxyacetone-P} + \text{Glyceraldehyde} \longrightarrow \text{Liver Glycogen / Glucose / Lactate}$$
  During exercise, the liver converts fructose into glucose and lactate, which are released into the bloodstream and oxidized by active skeletal muscle fibers as immediate fuel.

```
                     Intestinal Transporter Dynamics
                     
             Intestinal Lumen                      Enterocyte Membrane
       ┌───────────────────────────┐             ┌─────────────────────┐
       │ Glucose / Maltodextrin    │ ──────────► │ SGLT1 (~60 g/h max) │ ──► Blood Stream
       ├───────────────────────────┤             ├─────────────────────┤
       │ Fructose                  │ ──────────► │ GLUT5 (~30-50 g/h)  │ ──► Liver Metabolism
       └───────────────────────────┘             └─────────────────────┘
                                                            │
                                                            ▼
                                                [Glucose + Lactate Output]
```

### 2. Substrate Utilization vs. Aerobic Adaptation

A prevalent misconception in endurance training is treating **FatMax** (maximal fat oxidation rate) as a primary target of nutritional manipulation:

- **The Fallacy of Fat-Adaptation:** Restricting carbohydrates increases fat oxidation acutely because the body lacks alternative substrate; however, it downregulates pyruvate dehydrogenase ($\text{PDH}$) activity, blunts high-intensity glycolytic flux, and reduces suprathreshold power output.
- **Fat Oxidation as an Emergent Property:** Elite endurance athletes exhibit high baseline fat oxidation rates ($>1.0\text{ g/min}$) while consuming high-carbohydrate diets. This occurs because fat oxidation is governed by **mitochondrial surface area, capillary density, and type 1 muscle fiber proportion**—not by chronic dietary carbohydrate deprivation.
- **Mitochondrial Flux as the True Stimulus:** The primary signal for mitochondrial biogenesis (via $\text{AMPK}$, $\text{Pgc-1}\alpha$, $\text{p38 MAPK}$, and calcium/calmodulin-dependent kinase) is sustained metabolic flux through the electron transport chain, which is maximized through high volume and high mechanical work supported by adequate carbohydrates.

```
                  Determinants of High Fat Oxidation
                  
       Traditional Myth:     [Low-Carb Diet] ──► [Fat Adapted] ──► (Blunted Top-End)
       
       Physiological Reality:
       ┌───────────────────────────────┐
       │ Massive Mitochondrial Density │
       ├───────────────────────────────┤ ──► High Fat Oxidation + Preserved Glycolytic Power
       │ High Capillary-to-Fiber Ratio │
       ├───────────────────────────────┤
       │ High Type 1 Fiber Recruitment │
       └───────────────────────────────┘
```

### 3. Threshold Concepts: LT1 and the Heavy-Severe Boundary

Exercise intensity domains are anchored to specific physiological breakpoints:

```
    [ Moderate Domain ]      |       [ Heavy Domain ]       |      [ Severe Domain ]
0 W ──────────────────────── LT1 ────────────────────────── LT2 ──────────────────────── VO2max
    (No Lactate Increase)        (Elevated Steady Lactate)      (Non-Steady State / Task Failure)
```

1. **First Threshold (LT1 / VT1):**
   - *Definition:* The boundary between the moderate and heavy intensity domains.
   - *Testing Criteria:* First sustained rise in blood lactate above resting baseline ($+0.3\text{--}0.5\text{ mmol/L}$) or first ventilatory breakpoint ($\text{VE}/\dot{V}\text{O}_2$ increase without $\text{VE}/\dot{V}\text{CO}_2$ increase).
   - *Field Proxies:* Heart rate stability over 2–4 hours (absence of cardiac drift under euthermic, well-hydrated conditions) and conversational "talk test."
2. **Second Threshold (LT2 / MLSS / Critical Power / FTP):**
   - *Definition:* The boundary between the heavy and severe intensity domains; the highest workload where metabolic rate and blood lactate remain in steady state.
   - *Laboratory vs. Field:* MLSS measures blood lactate equilibrium, Critical Power ($\text{CP}$) reflects intramuscular bioenergetics, and FTP represents functional power at the inflection point of the duration curve. All converge within $\approx 10\text{--}15\text{ W}$ in well-calibrated athletes.
3. **The Redundancy of Elite $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ Testing:**
   - In novice athletes, $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ expands rapidly with training. In elite cyclists, $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ plateaus (typically $70\text{--}85\text{ mL/kg/min}$). Performance improvements occur exclusively via rightward shifts in LT1 and LT2 and increases in work capacity ($\text{W/kg}$ at LT2).

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Intra-Ride Fueling Architecture

| Training Domain / Duration | Target Ingestion Rate | Carbohydrate Composition | Primary Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Endurance (< 2 hours)** | $30\text{--}60\text{ g/h}$ | Glucose / Maltodextrin | Sparing liver glycogen; maintaining euglycemia. |
| **Tempo / Threshold (2–3 h)** | $60\text{--}90\text{ g/h}$ | $2:1$ or $1:0.8$ Malt:Fructose | Maximizing intestinal transport; supporting high flux. |
| **Race / Stage / Long (> 3 h)** | $90\text{--}120\text{ g/h}$ | $1:0.8$ Maltodextrin:Fructose | Dual-transporter saturation ($\text{SGLT1} + \text{GLUT5}$). |

### 2. Strategic Carbohydrate Periodization: Delayed Feeding Protocol

For time-crunched athletes ($< 15\text{ hours/week}$) seeking to maximize metabolic stress without inducing overtraining:

1. **Morning Protocol:** Wake up, consume water/black coffee, and begin riding below LT1.
2. **Hour 1:** Fasted riding (elevates free fatty acids, enhances $\text{AMPK}$ and $\text{Pgc-1}\alpha$ activation).
3. **Hour 2:** Introduce $30\text{--}60\text{ g/h}$ carbohydrates (maltodextrin/fructose mix).
4. **Physiological Rationale:** Prevents catastrophic liver glycogen depletion, preserves cognitive function, avoids post-ride rebound hyperphagia (bingeing), and maintains muscle protein synthesis.

```
                      Delayed Carbohydrate Feeding Timeline
                      
  [0:00 - Start] ──► [Hour 1: Water/Electrolytes] ──► [Hour 2: 30–60g Carbs] ──► [Finish & Meal]
   High Cellular Stress / FFA Surge            Preserve Liver Glycogen & Evert Binge
```

### 3. Evidence-Based Ergogenic Supplements

#### A. Sodium Bicarbonate ($\text{NaHCO}_3$)
- **Mechanism:** Expands extracellular bicarbonate pool ($\text{HCO}_3^-$), elevating blood $\text{pH}$ ($7.4 \rightarrow 7.5+$). This steepens the concentration gradient across the sarcolemma, accelerating the efflux of $\text{H}^+$ ions and lactate via monocarboxylate transporters ($\text{MCT1/4}$).
- **Dosage:** $0.3\text{ g/kg}$ body mass consumed $90\text{--}120\text{ minutes}$ prior to competition.
- **Limitation:** High risk of severe osmotic diarrhea and gastrointestinal distress.

#### B. Beta-Alanine
- **Mechanism:** Rate-limiting precursor to intramuscular **carnosine** synthesis. Carnosine acts as an intracellular $\text{H}^+$ buffer and enhances myofilament calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$) sensitivity in type 2 fibers.
- **Dosage:** $3.2\text{--}6.4\text{ g/day}$ divided into $0.8\text{--}1.6\text{ g}$ doses for 4–8 weeks (chronic loading required).

#### C. Creatine Monohydrate
- **Mechanism:** Expands intramuscular phosphocreatine ($\text{PCr}$) stores, enhancing rapid $\text{ATP}$ resynthesis during maximal anaerobic bursts ($< 15\text{ seconds}$).
- **Consideration:** Causes $1.0\text{--}2.0\text{ kg}$ water retention. Highly effective for sprinters and flat/rolling course power output; evaluate power-to-weight tradeoffs for pure climbers.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall / Dogma | Underlying Flaw | Correction / Pragmatic Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chronic Low-Carb Base** | Reduces $\text{PDH}$ enzyme activity; impairs high-intensity performance. | Fuel the work: high-carb availability for quality sessions, selective fasting for short Z2. |
| **Pure Glucose at > 60 g/h** | Saturates $\text{SGLT1}$ transporters; causes severe osmotic GI distress. | Add fructose in a $1:0.8$ ratio for all fueling above $60\text{ g/h}$. |
| **8-Hour Fasted "FatMax" Rides** | Depletes liver glycogen, elevates cortisol, and triggers catabolic muscle breakdown. | Cap fasted/carb-restricted sessions at $\le 2\text{ hours}$. |
| **Over-Reliance on Lab VO2max** | Fails to reflect real-world threshold, durability, or riding efficiency. | Base training zones on LT1 and LT2 field testing (TTE at FTP, Critical Power). |
| **Uncritical Consumption of MDPI / Open-Access Literature** | Predatory business models incentivize volume over rigorous peer review. | Critically evaluate study methodologies, author track records, and replication in respected journals. |
