# Fat Oxidation, Glycogen Sparing, & FatMax Fallacies — Complete Guide

_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore (Watts Doc #10 & #35)_

---

## What Is Substrate Utilization in Endurance Cycling?

Substrate utilization refers to the body's shifting preference between carbohydrates and fats to produce ATP, depending on exercise intensity and fuel availability. 
- At **rest or very low intensities**, ATP demand is low, and the body primarily relies on fat oxidation (though a basal level of carbohydrate metabolism always occurs, maintaining a small baseline of blood lactate).
- When transitioning to **higher intensities**, the body's ATP demand outpaces the slow process of fat oxidation. It immediately turns to phosphocreatine (for the first few seconds) and then to rapid glycolysis (carbohydrates) to meet the demand.
- Both fats and carbohydrates are utilized aerobically via the Krebs cycle and the electron transport chain. However, deriving ATP from fat is a slow process. Carbohydrates provide ATP much faster.

## The Glycogen Sparing Myth vs. Reality

A common theoretical benefit of low-carbohydrate, high-fat (LCHF) or ketogenic diets is "glycogen sparing." The hypothesis claims that by becoming "fat-adapted," an athlete will burn more fat at submaximal intensities, sparing valuable glycogen reserves for crucial high-intensity race moments (like a final sprint or steep climb).

**The Reality:**
A landmark 28-day protocol study compared athletes on a fat-adapted diet (followed by one day of carb-loading) against those on a standard high-carb diet. 
- The fat-adapted group indeed showed a significantly higher reliance on fat oxidation during submaximal intensities.
- However, both groups depleted their glycogen down to the same levels by the end of a simulated 100km race. 
- More importantly, the fat-adapted group experienced a massive drop in power during the high-intensity portions. During 1km and 4km sprint efforts, the fat-adapted group lost between 40 to 70 Watts on average compared to their high-carb performances. 
- EMG data showed both groups recruited the same amount of muscle fibers, meaning the fat-adapted muscle fibers were producing significantly less power.

## Downregulation of Pyruvate Dehydrogenase (PDH) with High Fat Diets

Why does power drop even when glycogen is restored via carb-loading? The answer lies in the cellular machinery. 

When you consume a strict LCHF diet, your body adapts by downregulating key carbohydrate-processing enzymes because they are not needed in high amounts. A critical enzyme down-regulated is Pyruvate Dehydrogenase (PDH), which facilitates the conversion of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA for the aerobic processing of carbohydrates.
- **Lost Top-End Power:** Without sufficient enzymatic machinery to rapidly process carbohydrates, high-intensity glycolytic capacity is severely impaired. 
- **No "Hack" Exists:** You cannot easily switch this machinery back on with a single day of carb-loading. The body takes time to up-regulate these enzymes again.
- Thus, the athlete is left with plenty of stored glycogen but a crippled ability to rapidly convert it into ATP when a surge or sprint demands it.

## FatMax Testing Fallacies & Measurement Limits

"FatMax" is heavily marketed as the precise intensity where fat oxidation is maximal. 

**Fallacy 1: FatMax is a sharp peak.**
In reality, FatMax is a wide, diffuse plateau rather than a precise point. For example, an athlete with a 400W FTP might achieve their maximal fat oxidation rate (e.g., ~1g/min) anywhere across a broad range from 180W to 300W. Prescribing a hyper-specific wattage target based on a FatMax test is physiologically unnecessary and practically flawed.

**Fallacy 2: Burning fat makes you better at burning fat.**
Riding exactly at your maximal fat oxidation rate does not uniquely trigger enhanced aerobic adaptations. The primary triggers for aerobic adaptations (such as mitochondrial biogenesis via PGC-1α) are calcium signaling and AMPK activation resulting from muscle contractions. Riding at any endurance pace (Zone 2) will generate these signals and improve your fat oxidation capacity over time, regardless of whether you are perfectly pinned at "FatMax" or eating carbohydrates during the ride.

**Fallacy 3: Burning more fat equals more body fat lost.**
Thermodynamics dictates weight loss. Burning 300 grams of fat on a ride does not mean you lose 300 grams of body fat if you eat those calories back, or if your overall daily caloric balance is at a surplus. Sedentary people lose weight in a caloric deficit; high-intensity athletes lose weight in a deficit. The substrate burned during exercise does not dictate long-term body composition—overall energy balance does.

## Practical Fueling & Training Recommendations

- **Carbohydrates are Required for Training:** To build FTP, VO2max, and sprint capacity, you must perform high-intensity work. High-intensity work absolutely requires carbohydrates. Restricting them impairs training quality.
- **Endurance is Built Over Time:** The best way to improve fat oxidation and "glycogen sparing" naturally is through years of consistent endurance training (Zone 2) and proper periodization, not dietary shortcuts. High-level athletes can oxidize massive amounts of fat *while* relying on a high-carbohydrate diet.
- **The Ultra-Endurance Exception:** Fat-adapted diets may be beneficial for extremely long, continuous, steady-state events (e.g., 24+ hour races) where carrying sufficient carbohydrate fuel is logistically impossible, and the pace never requires high-intensity glycolytic surges.

## Summary Checklist

- [ ] **Fuel the Work:** Consume carbohydrates to support the demands of high-intensity training.
- [ ] **Beware Dietary "Hacks":** Understand that keto/LCHF diets will likely blunt top-end power and anaerobic capacity.
- [ ] **Pace Zone 2 Properly:** Use heart rate, lactate, or perceived exertion to pace endurance rides rather than hunting for a specific "FatMax" wattage.
- [ ] **Focus on Caloric Balance for Weight Loss:** Don't conflate fat oxidized during exercise with total body fat reduction.
- [ ] **Embrace the Process:** Acknowledge that exceptional fat oxidation and endurance performance are built through years of consistent training, not 28-day diet protocols.
