# Perspectives #36: The Consequences Of Chronic Underfueling — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Dr. Traci Carson_

---

## What Are LEA and RED-S?

**Low Energy Availability (LEA)** is a state in which dietary energy intake is insufficient to support both the energy required for exercise and the basal physiological functions required for optimal health and biological homeostasis.

**Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S)** is the broader clinical syndrome resulting from chronic or severe LEA. Expanded by the International Olympic Committee (IOC) from the original Female Athlete Triad, RED-S encompasses 10 chronic health impairments (immunological, gastrointestinal, cardiovascular, endocrine, reproductive, bone health, metabolic, hematological, psychological, and growth/development) and 10 acute performance decrements (decreased endurance, increased injury risk, impaired training response, coordination loss, impaired judgment, decreased muscle strength, reduced glycogen stores, depression, irritability, and decreased concentration).

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

### 1. The Energy Availability Equation & Clinical Thresholds

$$\text{Energy Availability (EA)} = \frac{\text{Energy Intake (kcal)} - \text{Exercise Energy Expenditure (kcal)}}{\text{Fat Free Mass (kg FFM)}} \quad [\text{kcal/kg FFM/day}]$$

- **Optimal EA ($\ge 45\text{ kcal/kg FFM/day}$):** Full physiological homeostasis, normal endocrine function, and tissue remodeling.
- **Subclinical / Moderate Risk ($30\text{–}45\text{ kcal/kg FFM/day}$):** Potential mild metabolic compensation; acceptable for short, carefully periodized fat-loss phases in trained athletes.
- **Clinical LEA ($< 30\text{ kcal/kg FFM/day}$):** Severe physiological suppression. The body shuts down non-essential biological processes (reproduction, bone turnover, immune defense) to prioritize basal cellular survival.

### 2. Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Gonadal (HPG) Axis Suppression
- **Pulsatile GnRH Disruption:** The hypothalamus detects low cellular energy (low leptin, elevated ghrelin, low insulin, elevated cortisol). It suppresses pulsatile Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone (GnRH) release.
- **Endocrine Cascades:** Downstream Luteinizing Hormone (LH) and Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH) secretion collapses:
  - *Females:* Estrogen and progesterone drop $\to$ Functional Hypothalamic Amenorrhea (FHA).
  - *Males:* Luteinizing hormone suppression $\to$ low testosterone, loss of morning/nocturnal penile tumescence, low libido, and blunted muscle protein synthesis.
- **Thyroid Axis:** Downregulation of triiodothyronine ($T_3$) slows basal metabolic rate (adaptive thermogenesis).

### 3. Within-Day Energy Deficit (Within-Day LEA)
- 24-hour caloric equilibrium does not prevent endocrine disruption if large hourly energy deficits occur.
- **Example Scenario:** An athlete wakes up, consumes a 100 kcal banana, rides for 2.5 hours at 250W (burning 1500 kcal) with only water, creating a $-1400\text{ kcal}$ deficit by 9:00 AM. Even if the athlete consumes a massive 2500 kcal dinner, the sustained 8–10 hour daytime deficit elevates cortisol, suppresses thyroid signaling, and disrupts reproductive pulsatility.

### 4. Low Carbohydrate Availability (LCA)
- Even if total calories appear adequate via high dietary fat and protein, insufficient carbohydrate availability ($<5\text{–}8\text{ g/kg/day}$ for endurance athletes) directly impairs bone turnover markers (elevating CTX, depressing P1NP) and blunts high-intensity glycolytic power output.

### 5. Irreversible Bone Micro-Architecture Decay
- Estrogen and testosterone regulate osteoblast activity. Chronic suppression leads to rapid bone resorption outstripping bone formation.
- Osteopenia and osteoporosis can develop within 12–24 months in young athletes. Micro-architectural trabecular deterioration is frequently permanent, predisposing athletes to recurrent bone stress injuries (BSIs) and severe fracture risk later in life.

### 6. Differential Diagnosis: RED-S vs. Overtraining Syndrome (OTS)

| Feature | RED-S | Overtraining Syndrome (OTS) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Root Cause** | Energy substrate deficit ($EA < 30\text{ kcal/kg FFM}$). | Excessive training load/intensity relative to systemic recovery capacity. |
| **Primary Dysfunction** | Endocrine/metabolic suppression (low $T_3$, low estrogen/testosterone, low leptin). | Autonomic/central nervous system deregulation (parasympathetic/sympathetic imbalance). |
| **Resolution Timeline** | Weeks to months with caloric refeeding and carbohydrate restoration. | Months to years of training cessation and nervous system reset. |

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Diagnostic Indicators in Endurance Athletes

```
[Female Specific] ──> Menstrual irregularity (>35 day cycle), amenorrhea (absence of ≥3 consecutive cycles).
[Male Specific]   ──> Absence of morning/nocturnal erections, low serum free/total testosterone, suppressed libido.
[Universal Signs] ──> Recurrent bone stress injuries, chronic GI distress, resting bradycardia (<35-40 bpm out of proportion to fitness), "Instagram Fit" (can execute 2h ride but remains completely bedridden/lethargic the rest of the day).
```

### 2. Eliminating Within-Day Energy Deficits

```
[Wake Up / Pre-Ride (6:30 AM)]   ──> High-glycemic carb snack (50–75g carbs, e.g., oatmeal with maple syrup).
[On-Bike Fueling (7:00–9:30 AM)] ──> 60–90g/hr carbohydrates in liquid/gel form.
[Post-Ride Recovery (9:45 AM)]   ──> 1.2g/kg carbohydrate + 0.4g/kg protein within 45 minutes.
[Mid-Day Lunch (1:00 PM)]        ──> Balanced meal (complex carbs, lean protein, healthy fats).
[Dinner (7:00 PM)]               ──> Full evening replenishment.
```

### 3. Contraceptive Masking Protocol
- Oral contraceptive pills (OCPs) induce withdrawal bleeds that do **not** indicate a functioning endocrine system.
- To assess true hormonal recovery in athletes using hormonal IUDs:
  - Track Basal Body Temperature (BBT) via dedicated thermometry or calibrated wearable (e.g., Oura/Natural Cycles). A sustained $0.2\text{–}0.5^\circ\text{C}$ rise indicates ovulation and normal luteal function.

### 4. Progressive Caloric Refeeding Protocol
- Increase daily caloric intake by **$300\text{–}500\text{ kcal/day}$**, prioritizing carbohydrates ($\ge 6\text{–}10\text{ g/kg/day}$).
- Reduce training volume by 30–50% and eliminate all high-intensity threshold/VO2max work until natural menses or normal testosterone levels resume.
- Consult a multidisciplinary team: registered sports dietitian specializing in disordered eating, sports endocrinologist, and clinical psychotherapist.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall | Mechanism of Failure | Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The "Scale Weight Stability" Trap** | Athletes assume they cannot have RED-S because their weight is stable. In reality, metabolic rate ($T_3$) and NEAT have downshifted to match the low intake. | Evaluate subjective energy, hormonal markers, and performance repeatability rather than scale weight alone. |
| **Using Birth Control to "Fix" Period Loss** | OCPs supply exogenous hormones causing artificial bleeding while bone demineralization and metabolic slowing continue unchecked. | Address the underlying energy deficit; do not use OCPs as a standalone therapy for functional amenorrhea. |
| **High-Protein Diet Trend** | Endurance athletes substituting protein/fats for carbohydrates experience chronic glycogen depletion and blunted bone turnover. | Ensure carbohydrates form the foundation ($\ge 55\text{–}65\%$ of total calories) of the daily intake. |
| **Team/Coach Weigh-In Culture** | Public scale weigh-ins and skin-caliper monitoring promote restrictive eating behaviors and clinical eating disorders. | Eliminate aesthetic-focused metrics; focus exclusively on power-duration performance, recovery markers, and health. |

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### RED-S Risk Assessment & Action Matrix

| Clinical / Performance Presentation | Risk Stratification | Mandatory Intervention |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Amenorrhea $\ge 3$ months OR absent morning erections + Bone Stress Injury** | **HIGH RISK (Red Light)** | Immediate cessation of competitive training; complete blood panel (total/free T, estradiol, LH, FSH, $T_3$, ferritin, DEXA scan); multidisciplinary medical referral. |
| **Irregular cycles, suppressed libido, fatigue, normal bone density** | **MODERATE RISK (Yellow Light)** | Reduce training volume 25–40%; increase daily carbohydrate intake by $100\text{–}150\text{ g/day}$; eliminate within-day deficits; re-evaluate in 4–6 weeks. |
| **Normal menses / morning erections, consistent power, high energy throughout the day** | **LOW RISK (Green Light)** | Continue periodized training and fueling ($\ge 60\text{–}90\text{ g/hr}$ on-bike carbs; $\ge 45\text{ kcal/kg FFM/day}$). |
