# Perspectives #41: Macros, Determining Energy Needs, and Nutrient Timing — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Tim Podlogar_

---

## What Is Periodized Performance Nutrition?

Periodized performance nutrition in elite endurance cycling is the dynamic matching of energy intake, macronutrient composition, and fuel timing to specific metabolic training loads across microcycles and multi-day stage races.

Spearheaded by WorldTour performance nutritionist Dr. Tim Podlogar (Bora-Hansgrohe, Tudor Pro Cycling, University of Exeter), modern practice moves beyond generic caloric formulas. It treats nutrition as an active recovery and adaptation tool, prioritizing **cellular energy availability**, **glycogen turnover kinetics**, and **gastrointestinal tolerance** over static mathematical models.

---

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
[Mechanical External Work (e.g. 4000 kJ on Bike)]
                     │
                     ▼
[Gross Metabolic Efficiency Uncertainty (20% vs 25%)] ──► 900 kcal Calculation Delta!
                     │
                     ▼
[Daily Carbohydrate Allocation Budget]
   ├─► Intra-Ride Fueling (60–120 g/hr): Spares Liver Glycogen; Increases Exogenous Oxidation
   └─► Off-Bike Recovery Fueling (1.2 g/kg/hr): Directly Drives Muscle Glycogen Resynthesis
```

### 1. The Gross Efficiency Error Margin in Energy Calculations
- **Mechanical Work vs. Metabolic Turnover:** Power meters measure external mechanical work ($kJ$). Total metabolic energy turnover equals $\text{Work (kJ)} / (\text{Gross Efficiency} \times 4.184)$.
- **The 900-kcal Discrepancy:**
  - At **$20\%$ efficiency**, a $4000\text{ kJ}$ ride consumes **$4780\text{ kcal}$**.
  - At **$25\%$ efficiency**, the same ride consumes **$3824\text{ kcal}$**.
- **Dynamic Efficiency:** Gross efficiency changes with cadence, cadence-velocity dynamics, indoor trainer setups, and exercise intensity (rising up to LT1, then shifting as higher-threshold motor units are recruited). Static mathematical calorie calculators are approximations with an inherent $\pm 10\text{–}20\%$ error.

### 2. Intra-Ride Fueling vs. Off-Bike Glycogen Budgeting
- **Substrate Utilization at 120 g/hr vs. 90 g/hr:** Ingesting $>90\text{–}120\text{ g/hr}$ of dual-source carbohydrates (glucose:fructose $1:0.8$) suppresses endogenous hepatic glucose output (sparing liver glycogen). However, it does **not** significantly spare skeletal muscle glycogen at race intensities.
- **The Daily Budget Trade-Off:** Ingesting $120\text{ g/hr}$ vs. $90\text{ g/hr}$ on a 4-hour ride consumes an extra $120\text{ g}$ ($\sim 480\text{ kcal}$) of the daily carbohydrate allowance during the ride. In multi-day stage racing with a fixed total energy budget, this forces a reduction in off-bike dinner carbohydrates, compromising the post-ride window when muscle glycogen synthase activity is highest.

```
[1-Day Race / Classic / Ironman]:
  └── Maximize intra-race intake (100–140 g/hr). Next-day energy balance is irrelevant.

[Multi-Day Grand Tour / Stage Race]:
  └── Moderate intra-ride intake (60–90 g/hr on flat/tempo stages; 90–110 g/hr on mountain stages).
  └── Preserve caloric budget for off-bike recovery meals to maximize overnight muscle glycogen repletion.
```

### 3. Immediate vs. Delayed Post-Exercise Carbohydrate Ingestion
- **The Muscle Glycogen Paradox:** Delaying carbohydrate feeding by 2–4 hours post-workout allows muscle glycogen levels to eventually catch up by 24 hours, but **subsequent high-intensity performance drops by $>30\%$**.
- **Mechanisms of Failure:** Delayed feeding leaves the athlete in an extended catabolic state, elevating cortisol, forcing hepatic gluconeogenesis from muscle amino acids, delaying tissue protein synthesis, and compromising central autonomic recovery.

### 4. Protein Dynamics & The "Pasta-Grain Bias"
- **High Training Volume Breakdown:** Heavy endurance exercise accelerates muscle protein oxidation (especially leucine) and myofibrillar micro-trauma.
- **Incomplete Protein Dilution:** WorldTour cyclists consuming $6000\text{–}8000\text{ kcal/day}$ ingest large amounts of protein inadvertently from grains, pasta, and rice. These plant sources have low leucine content and incomplete essential amino acid (EAA) profiles. To ensure sufficient muscle protein synthesis (MPS), total daily protein targets must scale to **$2.0\text{–}2.5\text{ g/kg/day}$** (and up to $3.0\text{ g/kg}$ during extreme volume).

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Macro Target Architecture for Endurance Athletes

| Macronutrient | Baseline Training Target | High-Volume / Grand Tour Target | Primary Strategic Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Carbohydrates** | $5\text{–}8\text{ g/kg/day}$ | $10\text{–}14\text{ g/kg/day}$ | Fueling glycolysis, replenishing liver/muscle glycogen, sparing protein. |
| **Protein** | $2.0\text{ g/kg/day}$ | $2.5\text{–}3.0\text{ g/kg/day}$ | Muscle repair, mitochondrial enzyme synthesis, compensating for grain protein. |
| **Fat** | $0.8\text{–}1.0\text{ g/kg/day}$ | $1.2\text{–}1.5\text{ g/kg/day}$ | Essential fatty acids ($\omega\text{-3}$), fat-soluble vitamin absorption ($A, D, E, K$). |

---

### 2. Intra-Workout Fueling Matrix

```
[Ride Duration < 90 min (Low to Moderate Intensity)]:
  └── Water + Electrolytes (Carbs optional: 0–30 g/hr).

[Ride Duration 2.0 to 3.5 hrs (Zone 2 Base / Sweet Spot)]:
  └── 60 to 90 g/hr (2:1 Glucose:Fructose or simple sucrose/maltodextrin).
  └── Real-food options viable (Haribo, rice cakes, bananas, table sugar).

[Ride Duration > 3.5 hrs or High-Intensity Race Simulation]:
  └── 90 to 120 g/hr (1:0.8 Maltodextrin:Fructose or specialized hydrogels).
  └── Ingest systematically in 20-minute micro-doses.
```

---

### 3. Rapid Post-Workout Recovery Protocol

Begin immediately upon stepping off the bike:

```
[Phase 1: Minute 0 to 60 Post-Ride]:
  - 1.2 g/kg Carbohydrates (Liquid sucrose/maltodextrin + glucose).
  - 0.4 g/kg Fast-Digesting Protein (Whey protein isolate or soy/mycoprotein).

[Phase 2: Hours 2 to 4 Post-Ride]:
  - Continue high carbohydrate ingestion: 1.0–1.2 g/kg/hr via easily digestible solid meals (white rice, poultry, sweet potatoes).
  - Rehydrate with 150% of fluid lost in sweat (incorporating 500–700 mg/L sodium).
```

---

### 4. Low-Distress Pre-Race Glycogen Loading (24–36 Hours Pre-Race)

Traditional pasta loading causes severe gastrointestinal distress due to excess gluten, fiber, and sodium. Execute the following **Low-FODMAP, Low-Fiber Protocol**:

```
[Target: 10 to 12 g/kg Carbohydrates per 24 hours]:
  1. Base Foods: Steamed white jasmine/basmati rice, peeled white potatoes, cream of rice.
  2. Concentrated Simple Sugars: Table sugar (sucrose in water), fruit juice, Haribo / gummy sweets (gelatin/glucose).
  3. Dietary Restrictions:
       - Fiber: <10–15 g for the entire loading day (eliminate oats, whole grains, raw vegetables, beans).
       - Fats: Keep strictly below 30–40 g total.
       - Sodium: Avoid artificial sodium loading unless in extreme hot-weather prep.
```

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall | Mechanism of Failure | Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The "120g/hr Everywhere" Obsession** | Forcing 120g/hr on 2-hour easy base rides causes gastrointestinal distress and blows the daily caloric budget. | Scale intra-ride carbs to the metabolic demands of the session ($45\text{–}60\text{ g/hr}$ for easy base; $90\text{–}120\text{ g/hr}$ for hard race sims). |
| **High-Fiber Pre-Race Overload** | Loading with whole-grain pasta and oats increases GI transit time and causes massive fluid/fecal retention and bloating. | Transition strictly to refined, low-fiber carbohydrates ($<15\text{g fiber/day}$) during 24-hr loading. |
| **Neglecting Protein During High-Carb Diets** | Counting pasta protein as high-quality EAA sources results in sub-optimal muscle remodeling. | Ensure at least $1.6\text{ g/kg/day}$ of protein comes from complete sources (poultry, fish, dairy, whey, soy/mycoprotein). |
| **Acute Over-Drinking in Cold Weather** | Drinking large volumes of fluid during cold endurance rides induces excessive diuresis and hyponatremia due to peripheral vasoconstriction. | Drink to thirst in cold conditions; incorporate electrolyte tablets to maintain serum osmolality without forcing fluid volume. |
| **Misinterpreting Acute Morning Weight Fluctuations** | Reacting to a +1.5 kg scale jump after a hard interval day by restricting calories (confusing inflammation/glycogen water for fat gain). | Track 7-day rolling weight averages; educate athletes that $1\text{g glycogen stores } 3\text{–}4\text{g water}$. |

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### Performance Nutrition Decision Matrix

| Diagnostic / Scenario | Underlying Nutritional Status | Prescribed Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Athlete feels bloated, sluggish, and heavy (+2 kg) on race morning after carbo-loading** | Sodium and fiber overload from excessive bread/pasta ingestion. | Switch to **low-fiber white rice, potatoes, and gummy candy** for loading; cap sodium at baseline levels. |
| **Severe power drop-off during back-to-back mountain stages in a stage race** | Intra-ride fueling was overdone on flat transition days, displacing off-bike recovery glycogen meals. | Reduce flat-stage intra-ride carbs to $60\text{–}75\text{ g/hr}$; increase post-ride dinner carbs to $\ge 8\text{–}10\text{ g/kg}$. |
| **Athlete experiences persistent GI distress / bloating at $>90\text{ g/hr}$ intra-ride** | Saturation of intestinal SGLT1 glucose transporters; lack of fructose co-transport. | Shift formulation to a strict **1:0.8 Glucose:Fructose ratio**; perform laboratory exogenous carbohydrate oxidation testing. |
| **Athlete is heading to a 3-week high-altitude training camp** | Erythropoiesis will accelerate rapidly, requiring substantial iron stores. | Check serum ferritin; if ferritin $<50\text{ ng/mL}$, initiate clinical iron supplementation under medical supervision. |
| **Extreme fatigue and bonking in the final hour of a 5-hour hard group ride** | Inadequate intra-ride carbohydrate fueling leading to total hepatic glycogen depletion. | Enforce a minimum intake of **$80\text{–}90\text{ g/hr}$** starting at minute 15 of the ride; do not wait for hunger signals. |
