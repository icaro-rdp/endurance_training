# Perspectives #38: Training Intensity Distributions, NIRS, and Iliac Arteries — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Jem Arnold_

---

## What Are Training Intensity Distributions & Vascular Limitations?

Training Intensity Distribution (TID) refers to how an endurance athlete allocates training volume across three distinct physiological intensity domains:
- **Zone 1 (Low Intensity / Moderate Domain):** Below the First Lactate / Ventilatory Threshold ($< \text{LT1/VT1}$).
- **Zone 2 (Intermediate Intensity / Heavy Domain):** Between the first and second thresholds ($\text{LT1/VT1}$ to $\text{LT2/VT2/CP/FTP}$).
- **Zone 3 (High Intensity / Severe Domain):** Above the Second Lactate / Ventilatory Threshold ($> \text{LT2/VT2/CP/FTP}$).

The major debated paradigms include:
1. **Polarized (POL):** High Z1 ($\sim 75\text{–}85\%$), minimal Z2 ($< 5\text{–}10\%$), moderate-to-high Z3 ($\sim 15\text{–}20\%$).
2. **Pyramidal (PYR):** High Z1 ($\sim 75\text{–}80\%$), moderate Z2 ($\sim 15\text{–}20\%$), low Z3 ($\sim 5\%$).
3. **Threshold (THR):** Moderate Z1, heavy emphasis on Z2 ($\ge 35\text{–}50\%$), low Z3.

Concurrently, high-volume cycling in extreme aerodynamic postures can precipitate specialized vascular pathologies—specifically **Flow Limitations in the Iliac Artery (FLIA)**—which impair muscle oxygen delivery regardless of cardiac output or metabolic conditioning.

```
               │
               ▼
[Capillary Bed & Muscle Fiber Level] ──────── (NIRS SmO2 Balance: Delivery vs Consumption)
               │

[Cellular Respiration / VO2 Kinetics] ─────── (Mitochondrial Oxidative Phosphorylation)
```

### 1. TID Adaptation Mechanics Across Athlete Phenotypes
- **Individual Participant Data (IPD) Meta-Analysis:** By evaluating individual heart-rate-in-zone files rather than pooled study means, researchers showed that ~20% of participants do not execute their intended training distribution (e.g., prescribed POL but executed PYR due to intensity creep).
- **The Competitive Athlete Stimulus (Polarized Advantage):** Elite and competitive athletes possess highly developed peripheral mitochondrial density and capillary networks from years of base volume. To trigger further adaptations in cardiac stroke volume (eccentric left ventricular remodeling) and high-threshold Type IIa motor unit oxidative capacity, they require true Severe Domain (Zone 3) suprathreshold work.
- **The Recreational Athlete Stimulus (Pyramidal Advantage):** Sub-elite athletes still have substantial headroom for peripheral mitochondrial biogenesis and capillarization. The lower neuromuscular fatigue of Heavy Domain (Zone 2 sweet spot/tempo) allows higher total work volume without inducing autonomic overtraining.

### 2. Biological Noise vs. Measurement Uncertainty
- **Laboratory vs. Field Error:**
  - **$\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ Measurement Uncertainty:** $\pm 1\text{ to }5\%$ due to metabolic cart calibration, gas sensor drift, and ventilatory fluctuation.
  - **Time Trial Reliability:** $\pm 1\text{ to }3\%$ biological day-to-day fluctuation.
- **The Incremental Reality:** When comparing polarized vs. pyramidal interventions, the true average effect size is $\sim 1\text{–}2\%$. While this difference is decisive in elite podium finishes, it is smaller than daily biological noise. Thus, TID provides a probabilistic optimization rather than a guarantee of progress.

### 3. Near-Infrared Spectroscopy (NIRS) Mechanics & Limitations
- **Principles of Muscle Oxygenation ($\text{SmO}_2$):** NIRS emits near-infrared light into skeletal muscle (e.g., vastus lateralis) to measure the ratio of oxygenated to total hemoglobin/myoglobin ($[\text{HbO}_2] / [\text{Total Hb}]$).
- **Delivery vs. Utilization Equilibrium:** $\text{SmO}_2$ represents the dynamic net balance between convective oxygen delivery ($Q\text{O}_2$) and muscle oxygen uptake ($V\text{O}_2$).
- **The Calibration Pitfall:** While group means show $\text{SmO}_2$ breakpoints aligning with respiratory compensation point (RCP) / Critical Power, individual limits of agreement span $\pm 20\text{–}40\text{ Watts}$. NIRS should not be used as an isolated metric to define absolute power zones without concurrent ventilatory, lactate, or field TTE verification.

### 4. Pathophysiology of Flow Limitations in the Iliac Artery (FLIA)
- **Anatomical Configuration:** The external iliac artery traverses the pelvic brim anterior to the psoas major muscle before becoming the common femoral artery.
- **Biomechanical Mechanism:** Repetitive deep hip flexion ($\le 50^\circ$ torso-to-thigh angle in aero TT setups) combined with forceful psoas muscle contractions subjects the iliac artery to repetitive transverse mechanical kinking, friction, and tethering.
- **Histopathology (Endofibrosis):** Chronic mechanical trauma causes subendothelial smooth muscle cell proliferation and loose connective tissue deposition (intimal fibrosis), narrowing the lumen without typical atherosclerotic lipid plaque formation.
- **Hemodynamic Consequence:** At low power outputs, blood flow is unimpeded. At maximal workloads ($\ge 300\text{–}450\text{ W}$), required leg blood flow ($> 5\text{–}6\text{ L/min}$ per leg) exceeds the stenotic capacity of the kinked artery, creating acute local muscle ischemia, severe burning nociception, rapid $\text{SmO}_2$ desaturation, and unilateral power drop-off.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Training Intensity Distribution Framework by Athlete Level

```
[Recreational / Intermediate Athletes (<8–10 hrs/wk)]:
  └── 75% Zone 1 (Low Intensity Base)
  └── 20% Zone 2 (Tempo / Sweet Spot / Threshold)
  └──  5% Zone 3 (VO2max / Severe Domain Intervals)
  Rationale: Maximizes mitochondrial biogenesis and total kJ work without excessive autonomic fatigue.

[Competitive / Elite Athletes (>12–20+ hrs/wk)]:
  └── 80–85% Zone 1 (Strict Low Intensity Base < LT1)
  └──   0–5% Zone 2 (Minimal Heavy Domain)
  └── 15–20% Zone 3 (High-Intensity Severe Domain: 4x4m, 4x8m, 30/15s)
  Rationale: Maximizes central cardiac adaptations while preserving recovery for extreme high-intensity outputs.
```

---

### 2. Clinical Screening Protocol for FLIA / Iliac Endofibrosis

If an athlete reports severe unilateral leg burning, "dead leg" sensations, or sudden unilateral power loss strictly at high intensities:

```
[Step 1: Clinical Triage & Differential Diagnosis]
  │  Rule out common musculoskeletal issues (lumbar radiculopathy, Compartment Syndrome, piriformis).
  ▼
[Step 2: Provocative Cycling Ramp Test with Multi-Sensor Screening]
  ├─► Bilateral NIRS Sensors placed on Vastus Lateralis of both legs.
  ├─► Dual-sided Power Meter tracking Left/Right balance (e.g., shifts from 50/50 to 38/62).
  ├─► Continuous ramp to maximal exhaustion in aggressive TT aero position.
  ▼
[Step 3: Immediate Post-Exhaustion Assessment]
  ├─► Maintain aero tuck immediately post-test.
  ├─► Observe NIRS Re-oxygenation Kinetics:
  │     - Healthy leg: Rapid SmO2 rebound within 15–30s.
  │     - Affected leg: Severely blunted, sluggish SmO2 recovery (>60–120s).
  ├─► Measure Ankle-Brachial Pressure Index (ABPI) within 3 minutes post-exercise:
  │     - Normal: Post-exercise ABPI > 0.90–1.00.
  │     - Pathological (FLIA): Post-exercise ABPI < 0.70 (or >20 mmHg drop vs healthy limb).
  ▼
[Step 4: Advanced Specialist Imaging Referral]
  └── Duplex Doppler Ultrasound, MRA, or CTA with simulated hip flexion.
```

### 3. Non-Surgical Training & Ergonomic Modifications for Suspected FLIA

| Domain | Modification Protocol | Physiological Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cockpit / Bike Fit** | Increase front-end stack height, shorten crank arms ($172.5\text{mm} \to 160\text{–}165\text{mm}$), rotate pelvis anteriorly. | Opens the minimum hip angle at top-dead-center (TDC), relieving mechanical kinking across the psoas. |
| **High-Intensity Intervals** | Perform all Zone 3 intervals, threshold work, and hill climbs in an **upright or standing position**. | Prevents high arterial shear stress and flow occlusion during peak cardiac output. |
| **Aero Position Training** | Restrict aggressive aerodynamic positioning strictly to **Zone 1 low-intensity recovery rides**. | Low arterial flow requirements allow postural familiarization without precipitating ischemic tissue stress. |

---

### 4. VO2max Interval Prescription (Sustainable vs. Over-Optimized)

- **Hard-Start Intervals:** Accelerate all-out for 15–30 seconds before settling into $105\text{–}115\%$ FTP to rapidly deplete muscle phosphocreatine and accelerate $\text{VO}_2$ onset kinetics.
- **Sustainable Programming Rule:** While hard-start and micro-burst ($30/15$) protocols slightly accelerate time spent near $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$, their long-term efficacy matches traditional $4\times 5\text{-min}$ or $4\times 8\text{-min}$ long intervals. Prioritize athlete adherence and psychological sustainability across multi-week mesocycles over micro-optimization.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall | Mechanism of Failure | Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The "Polarized-for-All" Dogma** | Forcing low-volume recreational cyclists ($<6\text{ hrs/wk}$) into strict polarized training results in insufficient total training stress and under-developed sub-threshold stamina. | Prescribe pyramidal/sweet-spot distributions for time-crunched athletes to accumulate metabolic density. |
| **Treating Power Balance as an Injury Indicator** | Freaking out over routine $47/53\%$ left/right pedaling asymmetry in healthy, asymptomatic athletes. | Ignore natural asymmetry unless accompanied by unilateral ischemic pain and functional power collapse. |
| **Misinterpreting NIRS Absolute Values** | Using raw $\text{SmO}_2\%$ numbers across different muscle sites or athletes without performing an arterial occlusion calibration. | Focus strictly on relative slope kinetics, deoxygenation rate during intervals, and recovery half-times. |
| **Hospital Walk-Test Misdiagnosis of FLIA** | Referring suspected endurance athletes to standard clinical vascular labs that only perform 6-minute flat treadmill walks or Bruce protocols. | Ensure testing is conducted on a cycling ergometer reaching true peak aerobic power in the athlete's specific riding position. |
| **Post-Offseason Research Over-interpretation** | Concluding a training protocol is "superior" based on a 4-week study conducted on athletes returning from offseason detraining. | Account for logarithmic initial adaptation rates; recognize that long-term gains regress toward the mean. |

## Summary Checklist / Decision Table

### TID & Vascular Diagnostic Decision Table

| Diagnostic / Scenario | Underlying Physiological State | Prescribed Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Well-trained cyclist ($\text{VO}_2\text{max} > 60$, $\ge 12\text{ hrs/wk}$) plateaus on threshold/sweet-spot plan** | Peripheral mitochondrial ceiling reached; central cardiac stroke volume bottlenecked. | Transition to a **Polarized block**: drop Zone 2 work, enforce strict Zone 1 base, add 2x/week high-intensity Zone 3 intervals ($4\times 4\text{m}$ to $4\times 8\text{m}$). |
| **Time-crunched amateur ($5\text{–}7\text{ hrs/wk}$) seeks maximum fitness gain** | Low total hours cannot yield sufficient adaptations under strict polarized Zone 1 volume. | Execute a **Pyramidal model**: anchor weekly workouts around 2 Heavy Domain sessions (Zone 2 sweet spot/tempo) + 1 high-intensity session. |
| **Athlete develops unilateral deep quad/glute burning only at $>100\%$ FTP in TT position** | Suspected Flow Limitation in the Iliac Artery (FLIA) with arterial kinking. | Open hip angle via shorter cranks/higher stack; perform bilateral NIRS ramp test and post-exercise ABPI screening. |
| **NIRS $\text{SmO}_2$ drops to $15\%$ during steady tempo ride** | High local oxygen extraction or superficial probe placement over high-metabolism fibers. | Do not alter power based on raw $\%$ alone; check blood lactate and ventilatory rate before adjusting intensity. |
| **FTP test result changes by $\pm 4\text{ Watts}$ ($1.5\%$) between consecutive testing weeks** | Normal biological variability, sleep, nutrition, and power meter measurement uncertainty. | Treat change as statistical noise; maintain existing training zones rather than recalibrating workouts. |
