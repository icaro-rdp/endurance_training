# Perspectives #17: Evolution vs Revolution in Endurance Training — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore with Mikael Eriksson_

---

## What Is Evolution vs. Revolution in Training?

The coaching of endurance athletes continually oscillates between two philosophies: **Revolutionary paradigms** (rapidly adopting the latest scientific trend, biohack, or rigid commercial methodology) and **Evolutionary refinement** (the steady, critical, and nuanced optimization of foundational physiological principles).

Coaches and self-coached athletes frequently fall prey to novelty bias, implementing unverified lab findings—such as fasted training, strict polarized percentages, or extreme high-intensity interval regimes—without appreciating systemic context or ecological validity. An evolutionary framework prioritizes rigorous critical thinking, individualized gap analysis, and deliberate load management across disciplines, recognizing that while core exercise physiology principles remain unchanged, their practical execution requires continuous calibration.

## Key Physiological Mechanisms / How to Think About It

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    SYSTEMIC ENDURANCE STRESS MODEL                      │
├────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ SWIMMING:                      │ CYCLING:                               │
│ - Upper body oxidative flux    │ - High non-impact metabolic turnover   │
│ - Hydrostatic & thermal load   │ - Glycogen depletion / cellular flux   │
│ - Submaximal autonomic cost    │ - Primary systemic aerobic engine      │
├────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ RUNNING:                                                                │
│ - High mechanical impact & eccentric muscle damage                      │
│ - Lagging connective tissue remodeling (tendon/bone turnover)           │
│ - Primary driver of systemic injury risk (80% of triathlon injuries)    │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Multi-Discipline Systemic Fatigue & Intensity Management
- **Autonomic and Metabolic Summation:** Unlike single-sport endurance athletes who perform 2–3 key quality sessions per week, triathletes accumulate stress across three distinct kinetic modalities. High-intensity touches occur in swimming, cycling, and running within the same microcycle.
- **The Recovery Reserve Mandate:** If interval sessions are executed to absolute task failure (RPE 9–10), the resulting central nervous system (CNS) fatigue, autonomic imbalance, and localized glycogen debt impair subsequent sessions across all three sports. Quality workouts must be capped conservatively at **RPE 7–8**, intentionally leaving 1–2 repetitions in reserve.
- **True Low-Intensity Discipline:** Low-intensity base training must be performed at **RPE 2–3** (well below the first lactate threshold, LT1). Operating at the upper boundary of Zone 2/Tempo creates low-grade autonomic strain that degrades the capacity for high-quality interval execution.

### 2. The Physiology of Swim Interval Restructuring
- **The Traditional Pool Trap:** Swimmers traditionally perform sets of short repetitions on brief rest intervals (e.g., $40 \times 100\text{ m}$ with 10–15 seconds rest). Because each bout is brief, athletes easily sustain a pace corresponding to $3.0\text{–}5.0\text{ mmol/L}$ blood lactate without perceiving extreme acute fatigue.
- **Cardiovascular and Environmental Artifacts:** Water immersion causes hydrostatic compression of peripheral veins, increasing central blood volume, stroke volume, and venous return via the Frank-Starling mechanism. Combined with cold water conductive cooling, heart rate remains artificially suppressed despite elevated localized glycolytic flux.
- **The Evolutionary Adjustment:** To train the upper-body oxidative machinery without generating unintended metabolic debt, endurance swimming must be restructured into continuous aerobic swimming or long intervals with extended active/passive breaks strictly for psychological focus, maintaining true sub-LT1 lactate levels.

### 3. Connective Tissue Remodeling vs. Aerobic Engine Kinetics
- **The Divergence in Adaptation Rates:** Mitochondrial biogenesis, capillary neoformation, and left ventricular eccentric hypertrophy develop relatively rapidly (weeks to months). In contrast, bone mineral deposition, tendon collagen synthesis, and ligament remodeling occur on a significantly slower timescale (months to years) due to lower tissue vascularization.
- **The Cyclist-to-Runner Dilemma:** Well-trained cyclists possess exceptional cardiovascular and metabolic engines ($V\text{O}_2\text{max} > 65\text{–}75\text{ mL/kg/min}$). When transitioning to running, their aerobic fitness allows them to run paces that generate ground reaction forces far exceeding the mechanical tolerance of unconditioned connective tissue, causing rapid onset of stress fractures, Achilles tendinopathy, or tibial stress syndrome.
- **Epidemiology of Overuse:** Studies consistently indicate that $\approx 80\%$ of all overuse injuries in multi-sport athletes originate from the running discipline.

### 4. Extensive Threshold vs. High-Intensity Track Intervals in Running
- **Canova Methodology & Specificity:** Pioneered by Renato Canova in elite marathon runners, extensive threshold training prioritizes prolonged running at or near specific race velocity (marathon/half-marathon pace) rather than high-velocity anaerobic track intervals ($V\text{O}_2\text{max}$ track reps).
- **Substrate and Neuromuscular Economy:** Extensive threshold running maximizes lipid oxidation kinetics, motor unit recruitment efficiency, and running economy under fatigue, avoiding the severe mechanical shear and neuromuscular exhaustion induced by sprinting or hard $V\text{O}_2\text{max}$ track work.

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. Multi-Sport Microcycle Load Allocation

| Modality | Easy/Endurance Sessions | Quality Interval Sessions | Pacing & Execution Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Swimming** | Continuous or long reps ($400\text{–}1000\text{ m}$), RPE 2–3 | Threshold/Pacing sets ($100\text{–}200\text{ m}$ reps) | Never race warm-ups or recovery sets; keep stroke long and relaxed. |
| **Cycling** | $2.0\text{–}5.0\text{ hours}$ @ 60–75% LT1 power, RPE 2–3 | Sweet Spot / Extensive Threshold ($3\text{–}5 \times 15\text{–}20\text{ min}$) | Stop interval sets when target power requires grinding or RPE exceeds 8. |
| **Running** | $30\text{–}60\text{ min}$ flat, soft surface, conversational | Extensive Threshold / Marathon Pace ($3\text{–}4 \times 2\text{–}3\text{ km}$) | Strictly flat/controlled; leave 1 rep in reserve; prioritize gait stability. |

---

### 2. Cyclist-to-Running Progression Protocol
For aerobically conditioned cyclists with no recent running history:

```
[Phase 1: Weeks 1–3 - Neuromuscular & Connective Adaptation]
  - Frequency: 3 sessions per week on non-consecutive days.
  - Structure: Run/Walk intervals (e.g., 1 min jog @ easy conversational pace / 1 min walk) for 15–20 minutes total.
  - Constraint: Strictly flat, soft surface (dirt, grass, or smooth gravel). Zero high-intensity bursts.

[Phase 2: Weeks 4–6 - Continuous Aerobic Base Progression]
  - Frequency: 3 sessions per week.
  - Structure: 15–25 minutes continuous easy running at RPE 2–3.
  - Progression: Increase weekly volume by no more than 10% (measured in minutes).

[Phase 3: Weeks 7–10 - Introducing Duration & Density]
  - Frequency: 3–4 sessions per week.
  - Structure: 2x 30 min easy base runs + 1x 45 min long run.
  - Recovery: Schedule a 50% volume deload every 4th week.
```

### 3. Long-Course Triathlon Pacing & Race Simulation
- **The Pitfall of Fixed Percentage Rules:** Popular triathlon guidelines prescribe riding Half-Ironman (70.3) at 85% of FTP. This is only sustainable for elite athletes completing the $90\text{ km}$ bike leg in $\le 2\text{ hours}$. For an amateur riding $2.75\text{–}3.25\text{ hours}$, 85% FTP exceeds physiological sustainability and forces severe glycogen depletion, ruining the run.
- **Race Simulation Testing Set:**
  - **Protocol:** $5 \times 20\text{ minutes}$ at target race pace/power with 5 minutes easy spinning recovery, executed at the end of a fatigued training block.
  - **Validation Criteria:** If heart rate exhibits marked upward drift ($>5\%$) across intervals 3 to 5, or if cadence drops significantly at constant power, target race power is set too high and must be adjusted downward by 10–15 W.

---

### 4. Reverse Periodization as Gap Analysis
- Rather than viewing reverse periodization ($V\text{O}_2\text{max} \to \text{Threshold} \to \text{Race Specific Tempo}$) as an inviolable dogma, treat it as an individualized **gap analysis**:
  1. Determine athlete baseline: What is their current $V\text{O}_2\text{max}$ ceiling vs. fractional utilization?
  2. If the ceiling is high, do not force a high-risk $V\text{O}_2\text{max}$ block; focus directly on extensive threshold and aerobic durability.
  3. If the ceiling is compressed (FTP $>85\%$ of $V\text{O}_2\text{max}$ power), insert a concise 2-week block of $V\text{O}_2\text{max}$ work early when the athlete is fresh, then transition into race-specific preparation.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Error / Misconception | Physiological Mechanism of Failure | Practical Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Jumping on Research Fads (e.g. Fasted Training)** | Fasted training triggers acute cellular signaling but increases cortisol, elevates RED-S risk, impairs interval power, and does not improve race performance. | Fuel all key training sessions with adequate carbohydrates ($60\text{–}90\text{ g/hr}$); implement a 1-month "cooling-off" rule before applying new trends. |
| **The "40x100m" Swim Trap** | Brief work-to-rest intervals mask high blood lactate accumulation ($3\text{–}5\text{ mmol/L}$), creating unmonitored systemic fatigue that impairs biking and running. | Prescribe continuous aerobic swimming or long sets ($400\text{–}1000\text{ m}$) anchored strictly at conversational RPE 2–3. |
| **Over-Aggressive Run Acceleration for Cyclists** | Cyclists have large central aerobic engines but fragile connective tissue; running fast causes bone/tendon breakdown. | Restrict running to ultra-conservative walk/run progressions regardless of aerobic ease. |
| **Static 85% FTP Race Pacing** | Half-Ironman bike legs lasting $>2.5\text{ hours}$ cannot support 85% FTP without catastrophic glycogen depletion. | Scale race power based on expected duration ($75\text{–}82\%\text{ FTP}$ for 2.5–3.0 hr splits); validate via $5 \times 20\text{ min}$ simulation sets. |
| **Going to Failure in Quality Workouts** | Taking interval sets to task failure (RPE 10) causes severe autonomic fatigue across a 3-sport training week. | Stop sets when RPE reaches 7–8, leaving 1–2 repetitions in the tank. |

```
[ATHLETE ASSESSMENT & PROGRAMMING FLOWCHART]

  ├── High-risk runner / cyclist transitioning? ──► Cap run frequency at 3x/wk; soft surfaces only; walk/run intervals.
  └── Resilient, experienced runner? ───────────────► Progress extensive threshold & race-specific pace volume.

Step 2: Balance Weekly Quality Touches
  ├── Swimming quality: 1x/wk (technique/threshold pacing)
  ├── Cycling quality: 1–2x/wk (extensive threshold / sweet spot)
  └── Running quality: 1x/wk (controlled extensive threshold; RPE ≤ 8)
  └── ALL OTHER VOLUME: Strictly RPE 2–3 (Sub-LT1)

Step 3: Determine Periodization Needs (Gap Analysis)
  ├── Is FTP compressed near VO2max? ───────────────► Execute 2-week VO2max block early when fully fresh.
  └── Is VO2max ceiling adequate? ──────────────────► Skip VO2max block; focus on aerobic volume & TTE extension.

Step 4: Pre-Race Validation
  └── Execute 5x20 min at target race power under fatigue ──► If decoupling occurs, reduce race target by 5–8%.
```
