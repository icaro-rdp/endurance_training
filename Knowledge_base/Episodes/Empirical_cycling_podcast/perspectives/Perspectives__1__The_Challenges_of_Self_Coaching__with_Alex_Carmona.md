# Perspectives #1: The Challenges of Self-Coaching, Athlete Self-Knowledge, & Pandemic Periodization — Complete Guide
_Source: Empirical Cycling Podcast — Kolie Moore & Alex Carmona (Perspectives #1)_

---

## What Is the Challenge of Self-Coaching?

Self-coaching in endurance sport is the practice of designing, executing, and analyzing one's own physiological training program without external coaching oversight. While modern data platforms (e.g., WKO5, Intervals.icu, TrainingPeaks) provide athletes with unprecedented access to physiological modeling and power metrics, access to data does not equate to objective interpretation.

The fundamental difficulty of self-coaching is not physiological ignorance, but **psychological cognitive bias and emotional reactivity**. An athlete is emotionally invested in their athletic identity and performance outcomes. When self-coaching, this emotional proximity impairs the ability to objectively differentiate between necessary training fatigue and Non-Functional Overreaching (NFOR), leading to erratic decision-making, chronic overtraining, or premature abandonment of progressive training blocks.

```
                    The Self-Coaching Cognitive Feedback Loop
                    
   Ideal Objective Model:
   [Data / Internal Sensation] ──► [Objective Interpretation] ──► [Rational Adjustment]
   
   Biased Self-Coaching Model:
   [Acute Fatigue / Low Power] ──► [Anxiety / Fear of Fitness Loss] ──► [Force Extra Intensity] ──► [Maladaptation]
```

## Key Physiological Mechanisms & Behavioral Frameworks

### 1. The Physiological Imperative: "Know Thyself" (Phenotypic Profiling)

Effective self-coaching begins with understanding one’s physiological phenotype rather than applying average training guidelines derived from group studies.

```
                             Endurance Phenotype Spectrum
                             
         Glycolytic / Sprinter Phenotype                 Oxidative / Diesel Phenotype
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Fiber Composition       High % Type IIa / IIx Fast-Twitch    High % Type I Slow-Twitch
   $W'$ / Anaerobic Work   Large ($18\text{--}25+\text{ kJ}$)   Moderate-to-Low ($8\text{--}15\text{ kJ}$)
   Lactate Kinetics        Rapid accumulation; high $V_{La,max}$ Efficient clearance; low $V_{La,max}$
   Fatigue Sensitivity     High central & peripheral fatigue    High durability; slow fatigue onset
   Recovery Requirements   Longer rest intervals (e.g., 1:2)    Shorter rest intervals (e.g., 1:0.5)
   Training Response       Quick to peak, quick to burn out     Requires high volume; slow build
```

- **Anaerobic Work Capacity ($W'$) Interference:** Fast-twitch dominant athletes possess large anaerobic reserves ($W'$). When performing threshold or $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$ intervals, they can mask inadequate aerobic development by drawing heavily on anaerobic glycolysis. This produces misleadingly high average power outputs while failing to stimulate target oxidative adaptations.
- **Recovery Kinetics:** Glycolytic athletes experience deeper neuromuscular and autonomic fatigue from high-intensity interval training (HIIT) sessions, requiring $48\text{--}72\text{ hours}$ between hard stimuli, whereas high-volume diesel athletes can frequently absorb back-to-back extensive aerobic sessions.

---

### 2. Progressive Overload Mechanics: TTE Extension vs. Power Bumping

A universal pitfall for self-coached athletes is treating every training block as a quest to raise FTP power numbers.

```
                        Threshold Progression Models
                        
   Erroneous Model (Power Bumping):
   [2x20min @ 300W] ──► [2x20min @ 310W] ──► [2x20min @ 320W (Failed)] ──► [Glycolytic Burnout]
   
   Empirical Model (Extensive Duration Progression):
   [2x20min @ 300W] ──► [3x15min @ 300W] ──► [2x30min @ 300W] ──► [1x60min @ 300W (High Durability)]
```

- **Extensive Threshold Adaptations:** Holding Functional Threshold Power constant while extending Time-in-Zone (TiZ) from 40 minutes to 60–90 minutes drives mitochondrial enzyme density (citrate synthase), capillarization, and glycogen storage capacity in Type I fibers without over-recruiting glycolytic Type II fibers.
- **Power Inflation Failure:** Artificially increasing target power converts an aerobic, sub-maximal threshold session into a supramaximal glycolytic workout, causing excessive lactic acidosis, high autonomic stress, and early failure.

---

### 3. Psychology of Training and Motivation During Disruptions

During extended periods without racing (e.g., pandemic disruptions, injury rehab, long off-seasons), motivation shifts from extrinsic (podiums, race categories, peer validation) to intrinsic (process mastery, health, durability).

- **The Motivation Void:** Without fixed competitive deadlines, self-coached athletes frequently oscillate between two extremes:
  1. *The Apathy Trap:* Lack of urgency leads to skipped sessions, unstructured "junk miles," and loss of aerobic conditioning.
  2. *The Over-Compensatory Grind:* Excessive volume or high-intensity Zwift racing to artificially manufacture purpose, resulting in burnout.
- **Reframing Training Blocks:** Successful periodization during disruptions requires establishing internal benchmarks (e.g., completing a 60-minute continuous threshold effort, achieving aerobic decoupling $< 3\%$ on a 4-hour ride, mastering low-cadence torque climbing).

## Practical Application & Prescriptions

### 1. The Self-Coached Microcycle Architecture

To prevent chronic fatigue accumulation, self-coached athletes should organize weekly microcycles around clear physiological separation:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     Balanced Self-Coached Microcycle (7-Day)                          │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Monday:    Full Rest or Active Recovery (30–45 min @ < 50% FTP)                        │
│ Tuesday:   High-Quality Key Session 1 (Extensive Threshold: e.g., 2x25 min @ FTP)      │
│ Wednesday: Zone 2 Aerobic Endurance (60–90 min @ 65–70% FTP)                           │
│ Thursday:  High-Quality Key Session 2 (VO2max or Intensive Threshold)                  │
│ Friday:    Full Rest or Recovery Spin                                                  │
│ Saturday:  Long Aerobic Base Volume (3–5 hours @ 60–75% FTP)                            │
│ Sunday:    Aerobic Endurance with Cadence / Torque Drills (2–3 hours @ Zone 2)         │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2. Systematic Qualitative Feedback Protocol

Self-coached athletes must mandate post-ride qualitative logging to strip away emotional bias:

```
                          Post-Ride Qualitative Audit
                          
   [Workout Completed] ──► [Record 4 Qualitative Subjective Metrics]
                                      │
                                      ▼
   1. Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE: 1–10)
   2. Breathing Dynamics (Nasal, conversational, deep diaphragmatic, panting)
   3. Muscular vs. Cardiovascular Limiter (Leg heaviness vs. heart rate / lung burn)
   4. Motivation & Energy Level (Pre-ride willingness vs. post-ride exhaustion)
                                      │
                                      ▼
   [Cross-Reference with Power & HR Data to Detect Early Autonomic Fatigue]
```

#### Longitudinal Diagnostic Rules:
- If target power is achieved, but **RPE is $> 8.5/10$** on a standard endurance/tempo ride $\rightarrow$ Autonomic fatigue detected; insert an immediate rest day.
- If **Heart Rate fails to rise** during high-intensity intervals despite maximal effort $\rightarrow$ Sympathetic exhaustion / glycogen depletion; cancel subsequent sets.
- If **Resting Heart Rate is elevated $> 5\text{--}7\text{ BPM}$** or HRV is depressed for 3 consecutive days $\rightarrow$ Truncate current microcycle and initiate recovery.

## Common Pitfalls & Limitations

| Pitfall / Bias | Cognitive / Psychological Mechanism | Physiological Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **"More Is Always Better"** | Sunk cost fallacy and anxiety over fitness decay. | Accumulation of Non-Functional Overreaching (NFOR) and endocrine disruption. |
| **Chasing Normalized Power / TSS** | Behavioral reactivity to external dashboard metrics. | Replaces structured aerobic base with chaotic, unstructured high-intensity surges. |
| **Premature FTP Bumping** | Ego-driven performance validation. | Shifts training zones into supramaximal glycolytic territory, degrading aerobic adaptations. |
| **Ignoring Internal Feedback** | Emotional denial of fatigue signals. | Leads to prolonged overtraining syndrome, taking months to resolve. |
| **Copying Pro / Influencer Routines** | Mimicry bias without context of phenotypic difference. | High injury risk, poor adherence, and severe chronic fatigue. |
