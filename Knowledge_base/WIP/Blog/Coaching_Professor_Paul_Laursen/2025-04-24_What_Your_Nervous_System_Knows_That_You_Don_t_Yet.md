# What Your Nervous System Knows That You Don’t (Yet)

<abstract lang="en">
Why HRV trends may be the endurance athlete’s most overlooked ally.
</abstract>

Ever felt like you’re doing everything right, nailing your sessions, hitting the metrics - yet something still feels off? 

Maybe it’s lingering fatigue. A plateau in progress. That sense of “I should feel better than this.” 

It’s a pattern I’ve seen over and over in athletes I coach, and often the answer lies _beneath that surface -_ in how our nervous system is responding to the load (or life) we place on it. 

That’s where heart rate variability (HRV) comes in. 

### Why HRV Matters 

HRV is a window into the autonomic nervous system. It’s not about effort - it’s about response. High variability signals a relaxed, adaptable system; low variability often points to stress or incomplete recovery. 

It’s not a new idea. But recent [research](<https://www.jhse.es/index.php/jhse/article/view/hrv-guided-training-moderate-intensity-enhances-aerobic>) out of Japan put numbers to what many of us have seen in the field: Athletes guided by HRV data made comparable gains to traditional programs - with _lower volume, less time at high intensity, and less accumulated stress._

In a sport culture that often equates more with better, that’s a radical (and refreshing) idea. 

### Patterns, Not Snapshots

One-off HRV readings can be noisy. But patterns over time - especially trends like the **7-day average dipping below your 60-day baseline** \- offer more signal than noise. 

I’ve spent [years working on methods](<https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23852425/>) to interpret these signals in a meaningful way. One [new approach](<https://sportperfsci.com/sports-science-3-0-series-ai-assisted-hrv-monitoring-enhancing-training-load-response-and-decision-making/>) I’ve been involved in is using **AI systems** that track rolling HRV trends and flag when it’s time to back off, hold steady, or ramp up. This kind of contextual feedback loop - one that used to only be possible for elites - is becoming more accessible to everyday athletes.

Still, context matters. HRV isn’t perfect. No data point ever is. It can be thrown off by poor sleep, a big work deadline, or even nutrition. But when interpreted correctly, it’s a powerful nudge - a physiological voice saying, “Today might be a better day to recover.” 

### What This Means for You 

If you’ve ever felt stuck or fatigued despite disciplined training, the issue might not be your plan - but how your body is coping with it. 

That doesn’t mean throwing out the training manual. It means integrating feedback loops that acknowledge you’re a human. That you live a life outside of sport. That your readiness fluctuates. 

Tools are emerging to help you guide this process - some of which I’ve had a hand in developing. One of them is **[Athletica](<https://athletica.ai/home-page-new/?nab=1>), **a training platform that adjusts your training based on HRV, resting heart rate, and other contextual factors. But it’s not the only one. What matters more is _adopting the mindset_ that your body’s internal signals are just as important as your external metrics. 

### The Bigger Picture: Sports Science 3.0 

This shift - from static programs to responsive systems - is part of what my colleagues and I call **[Sports Science 3.0](<https://sportperfsci.com>)**. It’s the evolution of how we use data - not just to track, but to make decisions. 

In a [recent paper](<https://sportperfsci.com/sports-science-3-0-series-ai-assisted-hrv-monitoring-enhancing-training-load-response-and-decision-making/>), we explored how AI systems can help interpret HRV trends, and how to balance automation with the wisdom of a coach. AI can surface signals. But the real art lies in understanding when - and how - to act.

Because the best adaptations happen when you’re not on the edge.
