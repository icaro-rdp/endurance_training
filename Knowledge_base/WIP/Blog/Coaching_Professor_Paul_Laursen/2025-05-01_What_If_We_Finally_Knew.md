One of the things I love most about coaching, science, and life itself is curiosity.  
It’s the driver that gets us out the door to train, to test, to question old assumptions, and to look for a better way.  
This spirit of curiosity is exactly what has led me to our latest initiative with Athletica: the **[FIELD Study](<https://athletica.ai/blog-field-study-endurance-fueling-research/>)** — _Fueling Influence on Endurance Load & Development._

### Why I’m Excited About FIELD

  
For decades, we’ve lived inside a polarized fueling debate:

  * **High-carbohydrate fueling:** Pre-race day pasta dinners, sports drinks on the bike, high intake targets during events — and _clear_ success stories.

  * **Low-carbohydrate or ketogenic approaches:** Fat-adapted endurance athletes, steady energy, metabolic flexibility — and _equally clear_ success stories.

I’ve personally ridden both sides of this fence — as an athlete, as a coach, and as a scientist.  
I built tools like _NeverBonk_ to help athletes optimize carbohydrate intake.  
I’ve also coached, published, and personally trained using low-carbohydrate strategies with excellent results.  
Both work. Both fail sometimes.  
And that tension is what truly excites me.

Because the real question isn’t _which camp is better_ — it’s **why, for whom, and when?**

### Some Questions I’m Personally Wrestling With

  * Is the effectiveness of high- or low-carb fueling related to the athlete’s **age** , **sex** , or **training background**?

  * Are there **central nervous system (CNS)** effects at play — where carbohydrate intake during exercise might not just be about muscles, but about keeping the brain firing under stress?

  * Does the **duration or intensity** of an event matter more than we think?

  * Are **training adaptations** — not just race-day performance — different depending on background diet?

Traditional lab studies give us glimpses.  
But small sample sizes and artificial conditions can’t fully answer these questions.  
We need field data — real athletes, real training, real life.  
That’s what FIELD is about.

### The Promise (and Caution) of Sports Science 3.0  

I call this approach **Sports Science 3.0** :  
Moving research out of the lab and into the field — powered by wearables, smart platforms like [Athletica](<https://athletica.ai/home-page-new/?nab=1>), and a global network of athletes willing to share their journeys.  
It’s not perfect.  
But it’s a heck of a lot closer to the reality endurance athletes live every day.  
With FIELD, we’re layering a simple nutrition survey onto the training data athletes already collect. Then, alongside world-class scientists like **Tim Noakes** , **Jeff Volek** , **[Andrew Koutnik](<https://substack.com/@andrewkoutnikphd>)** , **Philip Prins** , and others, we’ll start exploring how fueling patterns relate to performance, fatigue resistance, and recovery — in thousands, not dozens, of athletes.  
No interventions. No gimmicks. Just respectful observation and open-minded analysis.
