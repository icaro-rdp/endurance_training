# Scandinavian Snapshot: How Team Danmark & Olympiatoppen Are (and Aren’t) Readying for AI

**It was a telling moment.**

We walked past one vacant sport science lab after another—VO2 equipment untouched, force plates gathering dust. These spaces once pulsed with activity, representing the pinnacle of high-performance sport science. But now, they feel more like museums of a fading era.

Meanwhile, in a small meeting room down the hall, I was with Norway’s AI task force. And the conversation? Anything but dusty.

## Why I Went?

I was in the region to spend time at the [University of Agder](https://www.uia.no/english/research/research-groups/health-and-sport-sciences/spade.html) (UiA), where I hold an adjunct professorship. Knowing I’d be nearby, I reached out to old colleagues across Scandinavia to see if they wanted to hear about our work at Athletica—a decade-long journey in building AI-assisted coaching.

It turned out that just months before, CEOs at both **[Team Danmark](https://www.teamdanmark.dk)** and **[Olympiatoppen](https://olympiatoppen.no)** had released internal mandates calling for focused exploration of AI. The timing couldn’t have been better.

## Team Danmark: Cost, Risk, and the Build vs. Partner Question

In Copenhagen, I met with Peter Christensen and his team. We had a rich exchange—open, inquisitive, thoughtful.

[ With Dr Peter Møller Christensen and Team Danmark in Copenhagen. [https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!B8b0!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F61a20bba-939f-4288-adc8-932feb6189f8_1080x1350.heic](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!B8b0!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F61a20bba-939f-4288-adc8-932feb6189f8_1080x1350.heic)  ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!B8b0!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F61a20bba-939f-4288-adc8-932feb6189f8_1080x1350.heic)With Dr Peter Møller Christensen and Team Danmark in Copenhagen.

But the key tension was clear: **"What will this cost us to build?"**

The rough math is sobering. Starting from scratch, an NSO might face:

  * A **5+ year** development timeline

  * **Millions of dollars** in funding

  * No guarantee of success

In contrast, I shared that Athletica’s system is already up and running. It’s built on validated science, with battle-tested algorithms, and could be **[white-labeled](https://athletica.ai/athletica-powered/)** or adapted for their unique needs—**for a fraction of the cost and time.**

Peter got it. The conversation left the door open for continued collaboration.

And here came a pivotal moment:

When I presented [Andrea Zignoli](https://andreazignoli.github.io/)’s paper on the **[computational pathways of knowledge](https://sportperfsci.com/the-computational-paths-of-knowledge-in-ai-coaching/)** , you could see it land. Professors and practitioners alike had an _aha_ moment. For a country like Norway—with its depth in applied sport science and openness to innovation—the potential was obvious.

With proper adoption, the whole tide could rise:

  * Access to good training spreads

  * Talent development improves

  * Coaches level up, even at the elite tier

And crucially, **a new generation of hybrid coaches emerges** : those who understand the tech and know where their human touch matters most.

## The FOMO Factor for Other National Sporting Organizations 

Why share all this? Because there’s a message here for **other National Sporting Organisations (NSOs)** :

> **If you're not already thinking about AI-assisted coaching, you're behind.**

Adoption isn’t about novelty. It’s about _readiness_ :

  * To offer better support to more athletes

  * To reduce cognitive load on coaching staff

  * To make high-quality guidance scalable and adaptive

Early adopters don’t just gain a tech advantage—they build **data flywheels** , learning loops, and staff who are fluent in AI collaboration. That’s the real moat.

## Final Thoughts: Protecting the Moat
