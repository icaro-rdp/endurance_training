# How to Truly Call Out Nutrition Misinformation

If you have 25 minutes, [Nicola Guess’s video ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pvm2Ij5_VR8)is the best piece of science communication I’ve watched this year. She’s a dietician and researcher at the University of Oxford, and she explains (far better than I could) where Nick Norwitz has gone wrong in his science communication, and why it matters.

I’m recommending it with a particular kind of authority, because I used to be where Norwitz’s audience is now.

When I started my PhD in late 2018, I was a fair way down the low-carb/keto rabbit hole, enthusiastic about ketones in a way that was erring on dogmatic. My PhD is focused on low carbohydrate and ketogenic diets, and I’m still fascinated by the science, but I take a much more balanced view these days. That shift came from years in academia: surrounding myself with people smarter than me, reading the literature extensively, and deliberately reading work that challenged my beliefs. Education is the best antidote to misinformation, in nutrition as much as anywhere else.

I was lucky. If I’d stopped after my masters, I might still be trying to persuade you to drop your last carbohydrate and put butter in your coffee.

That history is why the current wave of keto influencers, racking up hundreds of thousands of impressions, seizing on every study that confirms their priors, is so recognisable to me. I can see the patterns I used to fall into myself.

Norwitz is the clearest example. He runs one of the biggest keto accounts on Substack and X, and he’s published a string of n=1 papers. (I’ve published n=1 papers too, and I’ve read the textbooks on case study research, how to run them, how to interpret them. His are typically on himself, which is a problem Guess unpacks in the video). Articulating exactly where he goes wrong takes more time than I have for this post, but Guess does it, and she does it well. If you’ve heard of Norwitz, watch it. If you haven’t, watch it anyway, it’s a masterclass in how to communicate science honestly.
