# My First Research Publication: Carbohydrate Improves Performance in Ketogenic Ultra-endurance Athletes

### **Can Carbs Enhance Performance for Ketogenic Athletes? A Case Study**

Ultra-endurance athletes are always looking for ways to optimise performance, and diet plays a crucial role. This can be a really hard topic to study in a lab (try getting people to run an ultra marathon on a treadmill!). 

One commonly used strategy is the ketogenic diet, which I outlined in a [previous post](https://mcarpenter.substack.com/p/a-dive-into-how-ketogenic-diets-impact). The question we sought to answer in this study was ‘what happens to metabolism and performance when a ketogenic athlete reintroduces carbs for a race?’

A recent case study I conducted and [published in Kinesiology](https://hrcak.srce.hr/ojs/index.php/kinesiology/article/view/27167) explores this very question, shedding light on how carbohydrate supplementation might benefit even those who are fully adapted to a ketogenic lifestyle, as well as how this impacts a ketogenic athletes metabolism.

### **The Study: A Quick Dive**

This case study focused on a single ultra-endurance athlete who had been following a strict ketogenic diet for a prolonged period of time. The goal was to investigate whether consuming carbohydrates before and/or during a race could improve performance, even in someone whose body was primarily fuelled by fat. We also wanted to see how this impacted fat metabolism. One key reason people go keto is to increase fat oxidation and ‘spare carbohydrates’. What happens when you bring carbs back?

The athlete participated in a 67km ultra-marathon around Richmond Park (slightly more interesting than on a treadmill!). We had three conditions, in which the athlete stayed low carb, one where he consumed carbs before and in the race, and one where he had a short carbohydrate load in the 2 days before the ultra.

The key finding was that performance was better in both carbohydrate conditions. Alongside this, a key metric was the maintenance of blood glucose. Without carbohdyrate, the athlete saw blood glucose drop substantially, which is one mechanism by which we suggest carbohydrate helps support performance. This is shown in the figure below.

### **The Methods: How It Was Done**

The study was designed as a single-subject case study, meaning it focused on one individual. This is a limitation, but also a strength. Single case research is typically used in order to study a unique phenomenon, something that is difficult to study with larger study groups. Ultra-endurance running is a perfect example of a unique topic in which insight can be gained from single case research.

### **Implications: What This Means for Ultra-athletes**

This study has some important implications for athletes following a ketogenic diet:

The key takeaway is that for ultra-endurance athletes, carbohydrate supplementation during races is likely to enhance performance by supporting the maintenance of blood glucose concentration, and prevent netagtive impacts of hypoglycaemia. 

We also found fat oxidation to remain high even after carbohydrate intake. This is important as it should reassure ketogenic athletes that they can consume carbs without being overly concerned about it preventing fat oxidation (burning).

### **Methodological Limitations: What the Study Didn’t Address**

While the results are promising and there are some useful takeaways, there are a few important limitations of this study that must be considered:

  1. **Single-Subject Design** : As a case study, the findings are based on one individual. This makes it difficult to generalise the results to all ketogenic athletes.

  2. **Lack of Control** : We did this in Richmond park to make it as close to a ‘race’ as possible, and make it more applicable to real world ultras, rather than it being done on a treadmill. This means we had to counter other factors (wind, temperature etc). 

This formed the basis of a subsequent study (hopefully to be published soon), in which we studied the impact of carb intake on athletes on ketogenic diets with a larger sample size in order to make some more rigorous conclusions from a more tightly controlled study.

### **Conclusion: Carbs and Keto—A Winning Combination?**

This case study offers a glimpse into the potential benefits of carbohydrate supplementation for keto-adapted ultra-endurance athletes. While the ketogenic diet provides a solid foundation for fat adaptation, the addition of carbs during races is likely to support blood glucose maintenance and prevent negative impacts of hypoglycaemia. 

However, it’s important to approach these findings with caution for now. The study’s limitations mean that more research is needed to fully understand the relationship between carbs and keto adaptation. Do not try anything in races that you have not tried in training, and there is rarely a one size fits all, particularly in ultra running.
