# Enhancing Exercise Performance with Sodium Bicarbonate

I often get looks of amazement from students when I explain that simple baking soda, the ingredient they’ve only previously come across when baking a cake, is one of only a few supplements with strong evidence of performance benefit in some exercise modalities. But it’s true.

Sodium bicarbonate improves endurance performance by buffering hydrogen ions in the blood, reducing acidosis and delaying fatigue during high-intensity exercise. This helps maintain muscle function and prolongs time to exhaustion, particularly in activities relying on anaerobic metabolism.

To put it simply, the burning sensation during high intensity exercise that is wrongly attributed to lactic acid by most running pundits is actually an excess of hydrogen ions causing metabolic acidosis - a reduction in pH in the muscles. Increased bicarbonate in the blood enhances the ability to extract CO2 from the muscles and slow down this increasing acidosis. Therefore, in high intensity endurance events (think 2-8 minutes), sodium bicarbonate can improve performance.

Note - I will in future blog posts explore the potential use of sodium bicarbonate for longer endurance events in more detail, with suggestions of improvements in marathons and ultra marathons, but that’s beyond the scope for today’s deep dive. I will touch upon this today but expect more detail soon, it’s an exciting area.

The paper I’m basing today’s post on is a recent publicaiton from the European Journal of Applied Physiology, titled ‘**[Enhancing exercise performance and recovery through sodium bicarbonate supplementation: introducing the ingestion recovery framework’.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00421-024-05578-0) **This paper gives a fantastic outline of optimum dosing strategies, as well as times when bicarb ingestion may be beneficial.

Whilst most research looks at bicarb ingestion 60-90 minutes before exercise performance, this review also outlines the benefits to bicarb when recovery from exercise is important. For example, in sports with multiple races in a day. This is becaise taking bicarb may accelerate pH and bicarbonate recovery pharmacokinetics. It may also alter the ‘strong ion difference’ (SID), this can result in reduced cell membrane excitability due to reduced maximal Na+,K+-ATPase activity, crucial for maximal muscle contraction.

As a result, consuming sodium bicarbonate has clear rational for performance improvement, and also growing rationale for exercise recovery. But how should you take it? 

## Taking Sodium Bicarb Short Duration Performance

Most research has looked at performance over 2-8 minutes as this is where the rationale is strongest. Here, it seems that the optimal dose to increase bicarbonate is around 3 grams per kg bodyweight of bicarbonate (e.g, 240g for someone weighing 80kg), usually given 90 minutes pre exercise to allow time for bicarbonate concentration to increase. 

## Taking sodium bicarb for Endurance Exercise

This is an area of promise. Though there is a lack of research right now, the authors suggest that dosage should follow this pattern:

Initial dose following instructions above. Then using a “top-up” dose during exercise. An example given is ‘Grand Tour stage races (3–4 h). Use a pre-exercise dose and then top-up halfway through’. This could also apply to marathon runners and ultra runners, and with more easily consumable bicarb on the market, such as the [Maurten bicarb system](https://www.maurten.com/products/bicarb?gad_source=1&gclid=CjwKCAiA2cu9BhBhEiwAft6IxGVID9HB-8YMMKYCL_abvPjZiyttR1cfwFfR1S0mwGgxCKhA6IJRFBoCRe0QAvD_BwE), this is being increasingly used by elite endurance athletes.

## Taking Sodium Bicarb for Recovery

In short recovery periods (<30 minutes), there is not good evidence or physiological rationale for improvements. In events with 30-90 minutes between performance, this paper recommends 0.2–0.3 grams per kg of bicarb, taken either prior to or post the initial bout of exercise. In recovery of 90 minutes+, there is more research needed, but there is potential for use of bicarb taken after exercise to support recovery.

## Summary

Sodium bicarb is a really interesting supplement, with some clear benefits, particularly in short duration events (2-8 minutes). Though as mentioned, I’m really encouraged by some of the early research and thought on it’s use in longer duration events, something that I will certainly explore more in future.

Note, make sure to practice this in training, a common side effect of bicarb is gastrointestinal distress, not something you want on race day!
