# 23: Building a Training Program (Part 3): Specific Workouts

## Overview and Session Architecture

Having established macrocycle periodization and microcycle scheduling in the previous episodes, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli zoom in on the **anatomy of a single training session**. This episode dissects the operational parameters of interval design: selecting target intensity domains, prescribing repetition durations and work-to-rest ratios, determining progressive time-in-zone (TiZ), and adapting execution to terrain and pedaling cadence.

---

## 1. High-Intensity Severe Domain Workouts (Zone 3 / Above LT2)

Intervals targeting maximal oxygen uptake (VO2max) and high-end aerobic capacity operate in the severe metabolic domain, where blood lactate accumulates continuously and oxygen uptake drives toward VO2max.

### Interval Structures
1. **Long Aerobic Intervals (e.g., 4 x 4 min, 5 x 4 min, 4 x 5 min):**
   - **Intensity:** 105–120% FTP / 90–95% pVO2max.
   - **Work-to-Rest Ratio:** 1:1 or 2:1 (e.g., 4 min ON / 3–4 min recovery).
   - **Target Session Volume:** 15 to 25 minutes of total time in the severe domain.
2. **Short Intermittent Intervals (e.g., 30/30s, 40/20s, 30/15s):**
   - **Structure:** 2–3 sets of 10–12 repetitions.
   - **Intensity:** 120–135% FTP on work bouts, low-intensity active pedaling on recoveries.
   - **Mechanism:** Maintains high cardiovascular strain and pulmonary ventilation while partial phosphocreatine (PCr) resynthesis during short recoveries enables higher neuromuscular power output.

---

## 2. Heavy Domain & Threshold Workouts (Zone 2 in 3-Domain Model / Sweet Spot & LT2)

Training at or just below the Anaerobic Threshold (MLSS / LT2) aims to maximize sustainable power, mitochondrial enzyme activity, and lactate shuttling efficiency.

### Interval Structures
1. **Extensive Threshold / Sweet Spot (88–94% FTP):**
   - **Protocols:** 3 x 15 min, 2 x 20 min, 3 x 20 min, 2 x 30 min.
   - **Work-to-Rest Ratio:** 3:1 to 5:1 (e.g., 15–20 min work with 3–5 min recovery).
   - **Goal:** Building Time to Exhaustion (TTE) and metabolic durability without excessive autonomic exhaustion.
2. **Intensive Threshold Repeats (98–105% FTP):**
   - **Protocols:** 4 x 8 min, 5 x 8 min, 3 x 10 min.
   - **Work-to-Rest Ratio:** 2:1 to 3:1 (e.g., 8 min work / 2–3 min recovery).
   - **Target Session Volume:** Starting at 30 minutes total TiZ, progressing up to 50–60 minutes for well-conditioned athletes.

---

## 3. Minimum Effective Dose vs. Maximum Exhaustion

A frequent coaching pitfall among self-coached amateur cyclists is attempting to max out interval volume on every session (e.g., trying to do 60 minutes of threshold in one go, leading to multiple days of residual fatigue):
- **Diluting Stimulus Across Microcycles:** It is far more effective to accumulate 40 minutes of high-quality threshold work twice per week (80 min total) than to perform one catastrophic 60-minute session that compromises subsequent training days.
- **Progressive Overload within a Block:** Begin a mesocycle at the lower boundary of effective volume (e.g., 3 x 10 min) and progressively expand interval duration (4 x 10 min -> 3 x 15 min -> 2 x 25 min) before increasing target wattage.

---

## 4. Terrain Specificity and Pedaling Dynamics

- **Climbing vs. Flat Power Execution:** Climbing involves high inertial resistance and steady torque throughout the pedal stroke, whereas flat terrain requires continuous high-speed spinning against low inertia.
- **Position Specificity:** Time trialists and triathletes must execute threshold and sweet spot intervals in their aerodynamic aero bars to ensure neuromuscular adaptation matches competitive demands.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Target 15–25 min for VO2max, 30–60 min for Threshold:** Use total accumulated time-in-zone as your primary session metric rather than single interval length.
- **Apply the Minimum Effective Dose:** Avoid pushing every workout to failure; maintain enough reserve to execute the next planned session with high quality.
- **Progress Duration Before Intensity:** When training the threshold, extend interval length and total time-in-zone before ratcheting up the power target.
- **Match Terrain to Target Demands:** Perform high-torque and climbing-specific workouts on appropriate gradients and execute TT efforts in the aerodynamic tuck.
