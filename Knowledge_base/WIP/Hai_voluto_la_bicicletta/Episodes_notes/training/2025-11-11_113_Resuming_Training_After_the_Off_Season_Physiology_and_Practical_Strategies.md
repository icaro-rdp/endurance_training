# 113: Resuming Training After the Off-Season: Physiology and Practical Strategies

## Overview and Context

Following the off-season transition phase—typically lasting two to four weeks of reduced or zero riding—athletes face the challenge of resuming structured training. This restart is a delicate physiological period. Athletes are often eager to regain peak fitness rapidly, leading to the common mistake of applying excessive volume and high intensity too soon.

Understanding the biological timeline of **detraining** and the kinetics of **re-adaptation** is essential for designing a progressive, injury-free re-entry block. This episode provides an evidence-based roadmap for transitioning from the off-season break back into structured winter base preparation.

---

## 1. Physiological Detraining Dynamics: What is Lost During the Break?

Detraining affects physiological systems along distinct timelines:

### Rapid Losses (Days 1 to 14 of Inactivity):
- **Blood Plasma Volume Drop:** Plasma volume contracts by **8 to 12%** within 7–10 days of cessation. This reduces venous return, lowers end-diastolic volume, and decreases stroke volume, resulting in an elevated heart rate at any given submaximal power output.
- **Glycogen Storage Capacity:** Muscle glycogen stores and glycogen synthase activity decline by 20–30%, accompanied by the loss of bound intracellular water.

### Moderate Losses (Weeks 2 to 4 of Inactivity):
- **Mitochondrial Enzyme Activity:** Oxidative enzymes like **citrate synthase** and succinate dehydrogenase decline by 20–40%, impairing fat oxidation and shifting substrate utilization toward earlier carbohydrate combustion.
- **Ventilatory Thresholds:** The power output at LT1 and LT2 drops significantly, although maximal heart rate remains stable or slightly elevated.

### Preserved Structures (Long-Term Resilience):
- **Capillary Density and Cardiac Remodeling:** Microvascular capillarization around slow-twitch fibers and eccentric left ventricular cavity dimensions are largely preserved over 3–4 weeks of break, providing the foundation for rapid re-adaptation once training resumes.

## 2. Common Re-Entry Mistakes to Avoid

The hosts highlight three frequent errors made by cyclists during the early resumption phase:

1. **Immediate High-Intensity Testing (Premature FTP Tests):** Performing an all-out 20-minute or ramp test in the first week back generates severe muscle damage, high neuromuscular strain, and demoralizing test results due to plasma volume contraction and enzyme downregulation.
2. **Aggressive Volume Spikes:** Jumping from zero hours straight into 15+ hour training weeks overloads tendons, ligaments, and the immune system.
3. **The "Make Up for Lost Time" Mentality:** Attempting to compensate for the holiday break by cramming workouts produces non-functional overreaching.

---

## 3. The Multi-Sport and Cross-Training Bridge

The first 2 to 4 weeks of the new season represent the ideal window to incorporate non-cycling endurance modalities:

- **Hiking and Trail Walking:** Excellent low-intensity aerobic conditioning that strengthens joint connective tissues and provides mental freshness away from the bike.
- **Running:** Provides high cardiovascular stimulation in short durations and delivers mechanical impact loading essential for stimulating bone mineral density (BMD), which is neglected by non-impact cycling. *Rule:* Introduce running with very low volume (15–25 min) to prevent calf/Achilles injury.
- **Gym Conditioning and Core Stability:** Re-establishing movement patterns, mobility, and foundational strength before heavy lifting cycles begin.

## 4. Structured 4-Week Re-Entry Microcycle Architecture

A progressive framework for returning to structured cycling:

- **Week 1 (Re-Acclimatization):**
  - Focus: Low volume, unstructured frequency, light spinning, general gym mobility.
  - Target: 40–50% of peak weekly volume. Strictly Zone 1–2 aerobic riding ($< 	ext{LT1}$).
- **Week 2 (Cardiovascular Priming):**
  - Focus: Gradual volume increase, re-establishing pedaling cadence and torque drills (SFR / low-cadence tempo at 50–60 rpm).
  - Target: 60% of baseline volume.
- **Week 3 (Metabolic Foundation & Strides):**
  - Focus: Introducing short neuromuscular efforts (e.g., $5 	imes 10	ext{s}$ cadence spin-ups) and sustained Zone 2 / Tempo blocks.
  - Target: 75% of baseline volume.
- **Week 4 (Testing and Baseline Calibration):**
  - Conduct standardized physiological testing (lactate step test or field power-duration test) to set accurate zones for the winter base macrocycle.

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Expect Elevated Heart Rates Early:** A high heart rate in the first 10 days is driven by plasma volume contraction, not a loss of cardiovascular potential; plasma volume rebounds within 7–14 days of consistent riding.
- **Delay Formal Testing:** Wait 3 to 4 weeks after restarting before conducting baseline FTP or lactate testing.
- **Incorporate Cross-Training:** Use hiking, running, and gym work during the first month to build bone density, joint integrity, and mental freshness.
