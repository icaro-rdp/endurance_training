# 137: Prioritizing Training Targets: Selecting the Right Physiological Focus

## Overview and Physiological/Training Context

One of the most frequent dilemmas encountered by cycling coaches and self-coached athletes is deciding **what specific capacity to train next**. Athletes often fall into the trap of "training everything at once"—simultaneously attempting to improve sprinting, VO2max, anaerobic threshold, endurance base, and durability within the same microcycle. This lack of clear prioritization creates conflicting cellular signaling pathways (e.g., AMPK vs. mTOR interference) and produces mediocre, diluted adaptations.

This episode establishes a structured decision-making framework for prioritizing training targets across mesocycles. Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli analyze how to reverse-engineer event demands, profile the athlete's individual physiological limiting factors (phenotyping), assess previously completed training blocks, and sequence stimuli hierarchically to peak for target competitive goals.

---

## 1. The Three Pillars of Training Focus Selection

When determining the primary objective of an upcoming 3-to-6 week mesocycle, a coach must systematically evaluate three core pillars:

```
                     +---------------------------------------+
                     |    TRAINING FOCUS DECISION MATRIX     |
                     +---------------------------------------+
                                  /                                             /                         1. Event Specific Demands       2. Athlete Physiological Phenotype
           (Course profile, duration,       (VO2max, VLamax, FTP, Durability,
            stochasticity, climbing W/kg)    Neuromuscular Torque Profile)
                                 \              /
                                  \            /
                             3. Prior Training History
                             (Work completed in preceding blocks,
                              accumulated fatigue, adaptation gaps)
```

---

## 2. Pillar 1: Deconstructing Target Event Demands

Training must progressively converge on the specific physiological demands of the goal competition:

- **Long Granfondo / Alpine Stage:** Demands high $\text{Watts/kg}$ at LT2/FTP, high fat oxidation, excellent durability ($>3,000$ kJ), and sustained low-cadence climbing torque. High VLamax is a disadvantage.
- **Criterium / Circuit Race:** Demands high anaerobic work capacity ($W'$), high VLamax, rapid $PCr$ recovery kinetics, explosive cornering accelerations, and high cadence versatility.
- **Time Trial:** Demands maximum sustainable power at FTP in an aerodynamic posture with low variability index ($\text{VI} \approx 1.00$).

---

## 3. Pillar 2: Athlete Phenotype and Limiting Factor Identification

Individual athlete profiling dictates which capacity offers the highest return on investment (ROI):

1. **The "High Ceiling, Low Floor" Athlete (High $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$, Low FTP/Fractional Utilization):**
   - Already possesses a large aerobic engine (e.g., $\text{VO}_2\text{max} = 75$ mL/kg/min), but fractional utilization at threshold is only $72\%$.
   - **Priority:** Focus heavily on **Subthreshold, Sweet Spot, and Threshold extension** to raise the threshold closer to the ceiling; avoid spending time on more $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ intervals.
2. **The "Low Ceiling, Pushed Floor" Athlete (High Fractional Utilization, Low $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$):**
   - Threshold is operating at $88\%$ of $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$. Threshold power cannot rise further without lifting the aerobic ceiling.
   - **Priority:** Dedicate a focused 3–4 week block to **Severe-Domain HIIT and $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ intervals** to expand aerobic capacity.
3. **The "Fragile Engine" Athlete (High Fresh Power, Poor Durability):**
   - High power in 1-hour tests, but drops $25\%$ after 2,000 kJ.
   - **Priority:** Prioritize **high-volume Zone 2 base, gut training, and end-of-ride tempo efforts**.

## 4. Hierarchical Stimulus Sequencing: From General to Specific

Modern block periodization structures mesocycles from broad physiological foundations to razor-sharp race specificity:

```
  Base Phase (Winter)      ---> General Build            ---> Specific Pre-Competition
  [Aerobic Base (Zone 2)   ---> [VO2max Ceiling &         ---> [Race Simulation, Durability,
   Max Strength Gym,             Threshold Extension,           Over-Unders, High-Speed
   Torque / Cadence Drills]      Fat Oxidation Conditioning]    Pacelines, Pacing Drills]
```

- **Avoid the "Everything Bagel" Microcycle:** In any single 3-to-4 week block, select **one primary target** (e.g., Threshold Extension) and **one secondary maintenance target** (e.g., Neuromuscular maintenance), while keeping all other riding at low-intensity Zone 2.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Do Not Train Everything at Once:** Concentrate on one primary physiological priority per training block to deliver a clear, un-conflicted adaptive signal.
- **Reverse-Engineer the Target Event:** Tailor the final 6–8 weeks before your goal race to match the exact duration, terrain, and power-duration profile of the competition.
- **Identify Your Individual Bottleneck:** If your threshold is already $>85\%$ of $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$, focus on lifting $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$; if your ceiling is high but your threshold is low, focus on threshold extension.
- **Sequence General to Specific:** Build aerobic volume and gym strength first, expand threshold and $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ in the mid-season, and sharpen race-specific durability and pacing last.
