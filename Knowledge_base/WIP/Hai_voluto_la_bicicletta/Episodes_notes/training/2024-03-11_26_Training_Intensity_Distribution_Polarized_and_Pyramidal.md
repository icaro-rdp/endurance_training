# 26: Training Intensity Distribution (TID): Polarized and Pyramidal

## Overview and The 3-Domain Physiological Framework

Training Intensity Distribution (TID) defines the proportion of total training time allocated across distinct metabolic intensity zones. Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli break down the scientific underpinnings of TID, anchoring the discussion to the **3-Domain Physiological Model**:

1. **Zone 1 (Moderate Domain / Below LT1/VT1):** Blood lactate remains at baseline levels (~1–2 mmol/L); fat oxidation is maximized; minimal autonomic and hormonal stress.
2. **Zone 2 (Heavy Domain / Between LT1 and LT2):** Blood lactate is elevated above baseline but reaches a steady state (MLSS / Sweet Spot / Tempo); carbohydrate utilization increases substantially.
3. **Zone 3 (Severe Domain / Above LT2/VT2):** Blood lactate accumulates progressively without stabilization; VO2 drives toward VO2max; severe autonomic fatigue and fast-twitch fiber recruitment.

---

## 1. Comparing TID Models: Polarized vs. Pyramidal vs. Threshold

| Model | Zone 1 (Moderate / <LT1) | Zone 2 (Heavy / LT1–LT2) | Zone 3 (Severe / >LT2) | Primary Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Polarized (Seiler Model)** | ~80–85% | ~0–5% | ~15–20% | High-volume training blocks, peaking phases, elite endurance athletes |
| **Pyramidal Model** | ~75–80% | ~15–20% | ~5% | General preparation, base phases, stage racers, Gran Fondo riders |
| **Threshold-Centric** | ~50–60% | ~35–45% | ~5% | Time-crunched amateurs (<6 hrs/wk), early Sweet Spot training |

### Measurement Methodology: Session-Goal vs. Time-in-Zone
- **Time-in-Zone (TiZ):** Calculates exact seconds spent in each power/HR zone.
- **Session-Goal Approach:** Classifies entire sessions by their primary purpose (e.g., an interval session with warm-up and cool-down in Z1 is scored as a Zone 3 session). This method yields higher apparent Z3 percentages (~20%) compared to TiZ (~8–10%).

---

## 2. Scientific Evidence: The Filipas et al. (2022) Study

The hosts review the landmark study by **Filipas et al. (2022)** (*Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*), which investigated 60 well-trained runners over a 16-week period divided into four groups:
1. **Continuous Polarized (POL-POL):** 16 weeks of polarized training.
2. **Continuous Pyramidal (PYR-PYR):** 16 weeks of pyramidal training.
3. **Pyramidal transitioning to Polarized (PYR -> POL):** 8 weeks pyramidal followed by 8 weeks polarized.
4. **Polarized transitioning to Pyramidal (POL -> PYR):** 8 weeks polarized followed by 8 weeks pyramidal.

### Key Study Findings
- **Superiority of Periodized TID:** The group that performed **Pyramidal in the first 8 weeks and switched to Polarized in the second 8 weeks (PYR -> POL)** achieved the greatest improvements in VO2max, 5km running performance, and time to exhaustion.
- **Mechanism:** Early pyramidal training builds extensive aerobic capacity and threshold density; subsequent polarized training sharpens high-intensity VO2max power while shedding cumulative metabolic fatigue.

---

## 3. Practical Prescriptions: High Volume vs. Time-Crunched Cyclists

- **High-Volume Cyclists (>12–15 hrs/week):** Polarized or Pyramidal distribution is mandatory. Doing too much Zone 2 (heavy domain / sweet spot) at high volumes leads to chronic autonomic overreaching and glycogen depletion.
- **Time-Crunched Cyclists (<6–8 hrs/week):** A strict 80/20 polarized model may yield insufficient absolute stimulus (e.g., only 45 minutes of intensity per week). These athletes benefit from a higher proportion of Zone 2 (Sweet Spot / Tempo) to generate adequate adaptive strain within a restricted time budget.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Periodize Your TID Across the Season:** Use a Pyramidal TID during base and general prep phases, then transition to a Polarized TID during pre-competition and race phases.
- **Respect Zone 1 Intensity:** Keep low-intensity base rides truly easy (below LT1 / talk-test pace) so high-intensity sessions can be executed with maximum power.
- **Scale TID to Weekly Volume:** High-volume athletes should strictly limit threshold/heavy domain volume, whereas time-crunched athletes can utilize more sweet spot density.
- **Understand Metric Differences:** Differentiate between Time-in-Zone and Session-Goal percentages when interpreting scientific TID literature.
