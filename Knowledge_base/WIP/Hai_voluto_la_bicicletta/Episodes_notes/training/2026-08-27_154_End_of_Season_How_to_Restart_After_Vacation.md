# 154: End-of-Season Periodization: Reset and Return to Training After Vacation

## Overview and Physiological Definition

The late-summer period (August to September) represents a critical crossroads in the annual endurance cycling macrocycle. Athletes typically return from 1 to 3 weeks of summer holidays characterized by altered training habits—ranging from complete sedentary rest to unstructured recreational riding, cross-training, or family travel.

Upon returning, athletes experience divergent biological responses: some feel unexpectedly powerful and "supercompensated," while others feel sluggish, heavy-legged ("ingolfati"), and deconditioned. This episode analyzes the **physiological timelines of short-term detraining**, explains why post-break sensations vary, and outlines structured protocols for executing a productive late-season racing block (September–October) versus initiating a planned transition phase.

Understanding the biological decay rates of specific physiological systems prevents athlete panic upon returning to the bike:

### The Good News: Structural Adaptations Persist
Capillary networks, mitochondrial structural proteins, and cardiac left-ventricular eccentric hypertrophy built over months and years do **not disappear in 10 to 14 days**. The sluggish sensation experienced during the first rides is primarily a transient hemodynamic (plasma volume) and neuromuscular coordination artifact, fully reversible within 5 to 7 days of structured riding.

## 2. Supercompensated vs. "Ingolfati": Diagnosing the Response

### Why Do Some Athletes Return Stronger?
Athletes who experience an immediate surge in performance were typically suffering from **unresolved non-functional overreaching or autonomic burnout** prior to vacation. The mandatory 7–10 day break allowed the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis to normalize, muscle glycogen and liver stores to fully supercompensate, and deep muscular microtrauma to heal.

### Why Do Others Feel Sluggish?
Well-managed athletes who were not chronically overreached experience the transient effects of plasma volume contraction and neuromuscular rustiness. Submaximal heart rates appear elevated, and the legs feel heavy due to down-regulated calcium handling in the sarcoplasmic reticulum.

---

## 3. Structuring the Return: The 7-Day Re-Priming Microcycle

Athletes must resist the urge to immediately perform all-out FTP tests or brutal interval sessions on day one. A structured re-priming sequence rapidly restores plasma volume and neuromuscular recruitment:

```
[ Day 1 ] Easy Zone 1/2 Spin (60-75 min) + 4x10s High-RPM Spin-Ups (Neuromuscular Reset)
    |
[ Day 2 ] Steady Zone 2 Aerobic Base (90-120 min) with steady Cadence (Stimulate Plasma Vol)
    |
[ Day 3 ] Short Subthreshold Openers: 3x6 min at Sweet Spot (88-92% FTP) - Re-engage Oxidative PDH
    |
[ Day 4 ] Complete Rest or Active Recovery (30 min Z1)
    |
[ Day 5 ] Aerobic Priming: 2 sets of 5x30/30s at 115% FTP (Re-activate VO2 Kinetics)
    |
[ Day 6/7 ] Weekend Long Base Ride with Controlled Tempo Climbs
```

## 4. Strategic Decision: Late-Season Peak vs. Season Transition

By early September, athletes must choose one of two distinct pathways:

| Strategic Pathway | Target Goals | Periodization Strategy | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Path A: Late-Season Peak** | Gran Fondos, Hill Climbs, Late TTs (Sept–Oct) | 4-week build block focusing on event-specific intensity, followed by a 10-day taper. | 4 to 6 weeks |
| **Path B: Transition / Off-Season** | Mental reset, physical recovery, strength base | Gradual volume reduction, unstructured cross-training (running, hiking, MTB), gym strength base. | 4 to 8 weeks |

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Do Not Panic Over Early Sluggishness:** A 7–14 day vacation causes transient plasma volume loss and neuromuscular rustiness, not a permanent destruction of aerobic fitness.
- **Re-Prime Before Testing:** Spend 5–7 days performing progressive aerobic re-priming, spin-ups, and short subthreshold efforts before attempting max tests or heavy interval blocks.
- **Recognize Chronic Fatigue Relief:** If you feel exceptionally fast after a complete stop, your pre-vacation training load was excessively fatiguing; adjust future microcycle recovery ratios.
- **Clarify Late-Season Objectives:** Decide deliberately between targeting a late-season peak in September/October or transitioning early into structural recovery and gym strength preparation.
