# 77: Training Zones and Their Practical Application

## Overview and Physiological Context

Training zone models provide a standardized language for coaches and athletes to prescribe interval workouts, manage training intensity distribution, and track workload. However, significant confusion exists regarding how zones are established, how different models correlate, and how physiological thresholds align with arbitrary percentage-based systems.

In this episode, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli break down the science of training zones. The discussion contrasts the **3-Domain Metabolic Model** with the classic **Coggan 7-Zone Power Model**, examines the divergence between power and heart rate zones, and provides guidelines on using training zones flexibly without falling into rigid compartmentalization.

---

## 1. The Physiological Continuum vs. Discrete Zone Models

Human physiology operates along a continuous metabolic spectrum rather than discrete, isolated compartments. Training zones are simplified conceptual models designed to target specific physiological adaptations.

```
       3-Domain Metabolic Model vs. Coggan 7-Zone Power Model
       
  [ Moderate Domain ] ──> Zone 1 (Active Recovery: < 55% FTP)
  (Below LT1 / VT1)       Zone 2 (Endurance / Aerobic Base: 56 - 75% FTP)
  
  [ Heavy Domain ]    ──> Zone 3 (Tempo: 76 - 90% FTP)
  (Between LT1 & LT2)     Zone 4 (Threshold / FTP: 91 - 105% FTP)
  
  [ Severe Domain ]   ──> Zone 5 (VO2max / Aerobic Capacity: 106 - 120% FTP)
  (Above LT2 / VT2)       Zone 6 (Anaerobic Capacity: 121 - 150% FTP)
                          Zone 7 (Neuromuscular Power: > 150% FTP / All-out)
```

---

## 2. Threshold Anchors: LT1 vs. LT2 and the Limitations of % FTP

Most commercial training software calculates 7 zones using fixed percentages of **Functional Threshold Power (FTP)** (e.g., Zone 2 = 56–75% FTP). However, this creates significant individual prescription errors:

### The Problem with Fixed Percentage Zones
- **Individual LT1 Variability:** The first lactate threshold (LT1 / aerobic threshold) can vary widely between athletes—ranging from **$60\%$ of FTP** in untrained or glycolytic athletes up to **$80\% - 82\%$ of FTP** in elite, highly oxidative WorldTour riders.
- **The "Over-Cooking" Risk in Zone 2:** If an athlete with an LT1 at 65% FTP blindly trains at the upper boundary of Coggan Zone 2 (75% FTP), they are constantly riding in the *Heavy Domain* (Zone 3 tempo), accumulating excessive autonomic fatigue and suppressing fat oxidation.

## 3. Power Zones vs. Heart Rate Zones vs. RPE

Each monitoring modality reflects a different aspect of exercise intensity:

| Modality | Primary Strength | Inherent Limitation | Best Used For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Power (Watts)** | Instantaneous external work; zero lag; unaffected by heat | Ignores biological strain, fatigue, and environmental stress | High-intensity intervals (Z4–Z7), pacing climbs, TTs |
| **Heart Rate (bpm)** | Direct reflection of internal cardiovascular strain | Kinetic lag (30–60s); cardiac drift; influenced by heat/caffeine | Long Zone 2 endurance rides, monitoring dehydration/drift |
| **Perceived Exertion (RPE)** | Integrates overall central and peripheral fatigue | Subjective; influenced by motivation and mental state | Auto-regulating daily intensity and interval pacing |

### The Kinetic Lag of Heart Rate
During short high-intensity intervals (e.g., 30-second microbursts or 2-minute VO2max reps), heart rate requires 60–90 seconds to elevate to steady state. Relying on heart rate to pace short intervals results in extreme over-powering during the first half of the repetition.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Anchor Zones to True Physiological Markers:** When possible, establish Zone 2 based on direct metabolic testing (LT1 / Fatmax) rather than generic percentages of FTP.
- **Combine Power and Heart Rate:** Use power to govern instantaneous mechanical workload during intervals, but monitor heart rate to ensure long base rides remain below the aerobic threshold.
- **Do Not Obsess Over Box Boundaries:** Recognize that riding 5 Watts above an arbitrary zone line does not instantly change the biological adaptation; focus on the primary intent of the session.
- **Respect Zone 2 Ceilings:** If riding long endurance base miles, keep the intensity comfortably below LT1 (conversational pace) to maximize mitochondrial signaling while minimizing recovery cost.
