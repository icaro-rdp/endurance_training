# 48: VO2max: Long vs Short Intervals

## Overview and Physiological/Training Context

Maximal Oxygen Consumption (VO2max) represents the upper ceiling of an athlete's aerobic power and is a primary determinant of performance in endurance cycling. To stimulate adaptations in both central cardiac output (stroke volume, left ventricular filling) and peripheral muscle oxygen extraction (capillarization, mitochondrial enzyme density), training must accumulate significant time at or near maximal oxygen uptake (typically defined as $>90-95\% \text{ VO2max}$, or "time at VO2max" / $T@\text{VO2max}$).

In this episode, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli break down the two primary methodologies for prescribing High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT) aimed at VO2max development: **Long Continuous Intervals** (e.g., 3 to 8 minutes sustained) versus **Short Intermittent Intervals** (e.g., 30/15s, 40/20s, or 30/30s microbursts). They explore the physiological trade-offs, oxygen uptake kinetics, blood lactate dynamics, muscular fatigue mechanisms, and individual athlete profiling.

---

## 1. Long Continuous Intervals (e.g., 4x4 min, 4x8 min, 5x5 min)

Long continuous intervals represent the classical model of aerobic capacity training, widely popularized by exercise physiologist Stephen Seiler and endurance literature.

### Execution and Intensity
- **Repetition Duration:** 3 to 8 minutes per repetition (total accumulated work time: 16 to 30 minutes).
- **Target Intensity:** 105% to 115% of Functional Threshold Power (FTP) or approximately 85–92% of Peak Power Output (PPO).
- **Recovery:** Work-to-rest ratio typically 1:1 or 2:1 (e.g., 4 minutes work / 3–4 minutes active recovery).

### Physiological Characteristics:
- **Fast VO2 Peak:** Because work is uninterrupted, oxygen uptake rises steadily through the primary phase of aerobic kinetics ($	au \approx 30-45 \text{ s}$) and reaches $>90\% \text{ VO2max}$ within 90 to 120 seconds.
- **High Glycolytic and Psychological Strain:** Continuous efforts in the severe intensity domain generate high blood lactate accumulation ($>8-12 \text{ mmol/L}$), rapid intramuscular acidosis ($	ext{pH}$ drop), and severe perceived exertion (RPE 9–10/10).
- **Cardiovascular Stimulus:** Extended continuous time under high stroke volume and high ventricular wall stress drives eccentric cardiac remodeling.

---

## 2. Short Intermittent Intervals (e.g., 30/15s, 40/20s, 30/30s)

Short intermittent interval protocols (frequently associated with Bent Rønnestad's research) utilize alternating work and micro-recovery bouts.

### Execution and Intensity
- **Repetition Structure:** Sets of 8 to 15 repetitions of 30 seconds on / 15 seconds off (or 40s on / 20s off), repeated for 2 to 3 sets with 5–8 minutes of recovery between sets.
- **Target Intensity:** 120% to 135% of FTP (or 95–105% of PPO) during the "on" phase; easy spinning (~50% FTP) during the "off" phase.

### Physiological Characteristics:
- **Oxygen Kinetics and Aerobic Preservation:** Because the recovery bout is extremely short (15–20 seconds), cardiac output, pulmonary ventilation, and myoglobin desaturation do not return to baseline. Oxygen consumption remains elevated across the set, accumulating extensive $T@\text{VO2max}$.
- **Lower Lactate and Glycogen Sparing:** The micro-recoveries allow partial resynthesis of intramuscular phosphocreatine (PCr) and rapid partial clearance of metabolic byproducts, allowing athletes to maintain significantly higher average power output with lower systemic acidosis compared to long intervals.
- **Neuromuscular and Race Specificity:** Intermittent bursts replicate the stochastic power surges, accelerations, and micro-recoveries characteristic of road racing, criteriums, cyclocross, and MTB.

## 3. Physiological Comparison: Long vs. Short Intervals

| Dimension | Long Intervals (e.g., 4x5 min) | Short Intervals (e.g., 3 sets of 10x 30/15s) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Average Power Output** | Moderate-High (105–115% FTP) | Very High (120–135% FTP) |
| **VO2 Kinetics** | Continuous rise to plateau | Fluctuating near peak ceiling |
| **Blood Lactate** | High sustained accumulation | Moderate/Buffered accumulation |
| **Neuromuscular Demand** | Lower peak torque | High explosive torque / cadence |
| **Intramuscular Acidosis** | Severe (rapid pH drop) | Moderate (PCr buffering) |
| **Time at $>90\% \text{ VO2max}$** | Highly efficient per set | Excellent, high total accumulated time |
| **Perceived Exertion (RPE)** | High mental toll / sustained burn | High cardiovascular / breathless sensation |

## 4. Athlete Phenotyping: Matching Intervals to Physiology

Choosing between long and short intervals should be guided by the athlete's metabolic profile and competitive discipline:

### The "Diesel" / High Aerobic Capacity Rider:
- Characterized by high fractional utilization, low $V\text{La}_{\text{max}}$, and high percentage of Type I slow-twitch muscle fibers.
- **Response:** Excels at long continuous intervals (e.g., 4x8 min or 5x5 min) due to superior aerobic kinetics and fatigue resistance, but may struggle to hit high power targets in short microbursts.

### The "Punchy" / Glycolytic Rider:
- Characterized by high $V\text{La}_{\text{max}}$, high anaerobic work capacity ($W'$), and fast-twitch Type II fiber dominance.
- **Response:** Excels at short intermittent intervals (30/15s); their anaerobic capacity buffers the short bursts, and micro-recoveries prevent premature catastrophic failure. Long continuous intervals may cause excessive early lactate buildup and premature interval termination.

---

## 5. Periodization and Workout Sequencing

- **Phase 1 (Early Build):** Introduce short intermittent intervals (30/15s) to stimulate high cardiac output and neuromuscular velocity without overwhelming psychological burnout or excessive acidosis.
- **Phase 2 (Late Build / Specific):** Progress to long continuous intervals (4x4 min or 4x5 min) to build mental stamina, sustained lactate buffering, and specific time-trial/climbing capacity.
- **Phase 3 (Taper / Race Sharpening):** Return to short microbursts (e.g., 1–2 sets of 30/15s) to maintain peak neuromuscular sharpness and VO2max without accumulating deep residual fatigue.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Both Methods Are Highly Effective:** Both long and short HIIT protocols elicit significant improvements in VO2max, power at VO2max, and endurance performance.
- **Use 30/15s for Higher Power and Stochastic Demands:** Prescribe short intervals to achieve higher mechanical power output and simulate race accelerations with lower intramuscular acidosis.
- **Use 4x5 min for Sustained Mental and Lactate Resilience:** Prescribe long intervals to train continuous mental discipline, severe-domain steady pacing, and sustained cardiovascular stretch.
- **Match the Protocol to the Rider:** Prescribe short intervals for punchy/glycolytic riders to avoid early interval failure, and utilize long intervals for diesel aerobic athletes.
