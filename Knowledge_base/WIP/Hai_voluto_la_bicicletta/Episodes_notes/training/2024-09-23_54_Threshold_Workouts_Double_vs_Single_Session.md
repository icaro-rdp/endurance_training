# 54: Threshold Workouts: Double vs Single Session Microcycles

## Overview and Physiological/Training Context

In contemporary endurance sports science, few training methodologies have generated as much interest as the "Norwegian Model"—a framework popularized by middle-distance runners (such as the Ingebrigtsen brothers) and triathletes (Kristian Blummenfelt and Gustav Iden) that utilizes **Double Threshold Days** (two controlled sub-threshold sessions performed in a single day, morning and afternoon). Traditionally, cycling training has relied on single, high-volume threshold workouts (e.g., 3x15 min, 4x10 min, or 2x20 min at 95–105% FTP).

In this episode, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli review a recently published exercise physiology study that directly compared the acute physiological, autonomic, and neuromuscular responses to **a single large threshold workout** versus **the same total threshold volume split into two sessions on the same day**. They examine the mechanics of lactate clearance, glycogen depletion dynamics, autonomic recovery, and practical applications for competitive cyclists.

---

## 1. Study Design: Single vs. Double Threshold Session

The analyzed study investigated the acute metabolic and fatigue markers of two different session distributions with identical total time-in-zone:

- **Protocol A (Single Session):** 6 repetitions of 10 minutes at lactate threshold (total: 60 minutes of threshold work) performed in a single extended workout.
- **Protocol B (Double Session):** 3 repetitions of 10 minutes in the morning (Session 1: 30 minutes at threshold), followed by 4–6 hours of passive recovery, and 3 repetitions of 10 minutes in the afternoon (Session 2: 30 minutes at threshold).

### Physiological Parameters Measured:
- Blood lactate concentrations ($	ext{La}^-$).
- Heart rate variability (HRV) and autonomic nervous system recovery.
- Muscle glycogen utilization.
- Neuromuscular rate of force development (countermovement jump and peak torque).
- Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE) and subjective muscular soreness.

---

## 2. Key Findings: Acute Stress and Fatigue Kinetics

The study yielded critical insights into how the body processes divided vs. continuous threshold volume:

### 1. Work Quality and Power Maintenance
- In the **Double Session** protocol, athletes sustained higher average power outputs and maintained cleaner pedaling kinematics during the second half of the work (repetitions 4–6 in the afternoon) compared to the late repetitions of the continuous single session.

### 2. Blood Lactate and Acidosis Management
- Splitting the volume prevented cumulative blood lactate accumulation. Morning lactate levels cleared during the intermediate recovery window, allowing the afternoon session to begin with normal resting blood pH and low baseline lactate ($<2.0 \text{ mmol/L}$).

### 3. Neuromuscular Fatigue and Muscle Damage
- Neuromuscular force reduction was less pronounced following the double session, as shorter individual bouts minimized severe muscle fiber recruitment breakdown.

### 4. Autonomic Strain and Recovery Time
- Despite the benefits in power quality, the double session imposed a prolonged duration of elevated sympathetic nervous system activation, as the body underwent two separate warm-up/cool-down cycles and two bouts of post-exercise metabolic elevation.

---

## 3. The Norwegian Model: Philosophy and Sub-Threshold Control

Bazzana and Nardelli emphasize the non-negotiable prerequisite of the double threshold concept: **Strict Intensity Control**.

- **Sub-Threshold Precision:** The Norwegian model succeeds because intervals are performed strictly *below* or *at* the Second Lactate Threshold (LT2, typically between **2.5 and 3.5 mmol/L** blood lactate, approx. 88–94% FTP / Sweet Spot).
- **The Failure Mode:** If an athlete performs the morning session too hard (>105% FTP, $>4.0 \text{ mmol/L}$ lactate), glycogen is depleted, autonomic stress spikes, and the afternoon session becomes a destructive, non-functional overreaching event.

---

## 4. Practical Feasibility and Amateur Applications

While double threshold days offer compelling physiological advantages for full-time professional athletes, their implementation in amateur cycling requires careful consideration:

- **Logistical Complexity:** Performing two sessions in one day requires two separate warm-ups, kit changes, bike preparations, post-ride showers, and precise mid-day carbohydrate replenishment.
- **Time-Crunched Athletes:** For most working cyclists, executing a single well-structured 90-minute workout (e.g., 3x12 min or 2x20 min) is far more realistic and delivers 90–95% of the physiological stimulus without disrupting daily life schedules.
- **Targeted Application (Training Camps / Weekends):** Double threshold days can be selectively deployed during weekend blocks or training camps where full recovery between sessions is possible.

---

## 5. Nutrition Between Double Sessions

If executing double threshold days, mid-day nutritional recovery is paramount:

- **Carbohydrate Replenishment:** Consume **1.0 to 1.2 g/kg/hour of carbohydrates** during the 4 hours between sessions, prioritizing fast-digesting carbohydrates (rice, pasta, oats, fruit smoothies) to maximize liver and muscle glycogen resynthesis before the afternoon workout.
- **Hydration:** Rehydrate with fluid containing sodium (500–750 mg/L) to replace morning sweat losses.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Double Sessions Maximize Work Quality:** Splitting high threshold volumes (e.g., 60 min total) into two bouts allows higher power output and lower acidosis in the second half of the work.
- **Intensity Must Remain Sub-Threshold:** Double threshold workouts only work if intensity is strictly controlled below LT2 (Sweet Spot / low Zone 4); pushing into VO2max or anaerobic zones will cause rapid failure.
- **Single Sessions Remain the Gold Standard for Amateurs:** For time-crunched cyclists, single 60–90 minute threshold workouts provide optimal balance between physiological adaptation and lifestyle feasibility.
- **Fuel Aggressively Between Sessions:** Prioritize high-carbohydrate meals (1.0–1.2 g/kg/h) and sodium-rich fluids between double sessions to ensure adequate glycogen availability.
