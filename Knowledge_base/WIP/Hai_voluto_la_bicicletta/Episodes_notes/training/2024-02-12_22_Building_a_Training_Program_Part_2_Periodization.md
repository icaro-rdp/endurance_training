# 22: Building a Training Program (Part 2): Periodization

## Overview and Periodization Principles

Periodization represents the strategic temporal organization of training stimuli into macrocycles, mesocycles, and microcycles to produce peak physiological adaptation at designated target dates while minimizing the risk of non-functional overreaching. In this second installment, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli examine how to construct annual and seasonal blocks, manage volume-intensity trade-offs, and respect the distinct biological adaptation kinetics of different energetic systems.

---

## 1. Classical Periodization Models: Linear vs. Block/Undulating

### Linear Periodization (Matveyev Model)
- **Structure:** Progresses predictably from high volume / low intensity (general preparatory phase) to moderate volume / moderate intensity (specific preparatory phase) to low volume / high intensity (pre-competitive and competitive peaking).
- **Application & Limitations:** Effective for novice athletes or single-peak seasons, but rigid for modern cycling schedules featuring multiple racing blocks spread across 6–8 months.

### Block Periodization (Issurin Model) & Undulating Approaches
- **Structure:** Concentrates training stimuli into dedicated mesocycle blocks (typically 3 to 6 weeks) targeting one or two compatible physiological capacities (e.g., Accumulation / Base, Transmutation / Threshold, Realization / VO2max & Taper), while maintaining other capacities with minimal maintenance doses.
- **Advantages:** Minimizes conflicting intracellular signaling (e.g., AMPK vs. mTOR interference) and allows riders to achieve multiple fitness peaks throughout an extended competitive calendar.

## 2. Adaptation Kinetics Across Physiological Domains

Training stimuli produce biological adaptations at markedly different speeds and with different decay rates:

| Physiological Domain | Typical Adaptation Timeframe | Time to Plateau / Decay | Biological Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **VO2max (VO2max / HIIT)** | **4 – 6 Weeks** | Rapid plateau (6–8 wks); fast decay | Plasma volume expansion, stroke volume, severe-domain motor unit recruitment |
| **Anaerobic Threshold (LT2 / FTP)** | **8 – 12 Weeks** | Moderate plateau; moderate decay | Fractional utilization, lactate clearance (MCT1/4), capillary density |
| **Aerobic Base (Zone 2 / LIT)** | **Months to Years** | Slow, perpetual adaptation; very slow decay | Mitochondrial biogenesis, PGC-1alpha, fatty acid oxidation enzymes |

### The 'No Watertight Compartments' Principle
While a mesocycle may have a primary objective (e.g., anaerobic threshold expansion), human physiology operates on a continuum. Low-intensity base rides continue to stimulate cardiac eccentric remodeling, and high-intensity interval training enhances peripheral mitochondrial function.

---

## 3. Microcycle Architecture and Weekly Load Distribution

Within a mesocycle, distributing workouts throughout the 7-day microcycle requires balancing stimulus density against neuromuscular and energetic recovery:
- **Dose-Response and Density:** Spacing high-intensity interval sessions (e.g., Tuesday and Thursday or Wednesday and Saturday) ensures glycogen repletion and autonomic nervous system recovery between hard bouts.
- **Session Sequencing:** Placing the most demanding neuromuscular or threshold workouts following rest or active recovery days maximizes output quality.
- **Amateur vs. Professional Frameworks:** Amateurs must fit training into fixed non-work hours (concentrating volume on weekends), whereas professional athletes adapt their schedule entirely to optimize physiological recovery windows.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Respect Biological Timelines:** Allocate 8–12 weeks when building threshold capacity, 4–6 weeks for high-intensity VO2max sharpening, and maintain continuous year-round aerobic base volume.
- **Adopt Block Periodization for Multiple Peaks:** Use focused 3–4 week mesocycle blocks to concentrate specific physiological adaptations rather than attempting to train every quality simultaneously.
- **Manage Volume-Intensity Reciprocity:** As interval intensity and specificity increase approaching key competitions, reduce total training volume to manage systemic fatigue.
- **Prioritize Workout Spacing:** Separate high-intensity training days with at least 48 hours of low-intensity endurance or complete rest to allow full substrate and autonomic recovery.
