# 73: Training for Maximum Power on the Bike

## Overview and Training Context

Endurance cycling performance requires not only aerobic efficiency but also the capacity to generate high neuromuscular force and instantaneous power during critical race surges, sprint finishes, and steep climbs. While heavy resistance training in the gym is widely acknowledged as the gold standard for developing maximal force, many athletes lack access to gym facilities, experience logistical barriers during the competitive season, or seek on-bike alternatives.

In this episode, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli review a landmark study by **Barranco-Gil et al. (2024)** investigating the effectiveness of an on-bike maximum power training protocol. The discussion examines how on-bike sprint intervals compare to traditional gym lifting, the underlying neuromuscular adaptations, and how coaches can integrate on-bike maximal force work into a training macrocycle.

---

## 1. The Barranco-Gil Study: On-Bike Power vs. Gym Resistance

The study examined whether explosive, low-cadence, high-resistance on-bike sprints could replicate the neuromuscular and performance gains typically achieved through traditional gym squats and leg presses.

```
       Barranco-Gil On-Bike Maximum Power Protocol
       
       Structure: 3-4 Sets of 3-4 Repetitions (4-6 seconds per sprint)
       ├── Execution: Seated or standing maximal sprint from low speed (< 10-15 km/h)
       ├── Gearing: Heavy gear (e.g., 53x12 or 52x11) @ 30-40 initial RPM
       ├── Effort: 100% All-Out / Maximal Voluntary Effort from pedal stroke 1
       ├── Intra-Set Rest: 2.5 - 3.0 minutes easy spinning (complete phosphagen recovery)
       └── Inter-Set Rest: 6 - 8 minutes easy spinning Zone 1
```

### Key Findings of the Study
- **Dynamic Force Output:** Pushing maximally at low cadences on the bike generates approximately **$70\% - 75\%$ of Maximum Voluntary Contraction (MVC)**.
- **Gym vs. Bike Overload:** While gym resistance training achieves higher absolute force loads ($> 85\%\text{ 1RM}$), the on-bike protocol produced statistically comparable improvements in sprint peak power, mean power over 15 seconds, and cycling economy.
- **Biomechanical Specificity:** On-bike sprints train the exact hip, knee, and ankle joint angular velocities and coordination patterns utilized in competition.

---

## 2. Neuromuscular Mechanisms and the Henneman Size Principle

Maximal on-bike power efforts activate muscle fibers through specific neuromuscular pathways:

$$\text{Power (Watts)} = \text{Torque (Force } \times \text{ Crank Length)} \times \text{Cadence (Angular Velocity)}$$

1. **High-Threshold Motor Unit Recruitment:** According to the **Henneman Size Principle**, small Type I slow-twitch fibers are recruited first. Generating maximum torque from near-zero RPM demands the immediate recruitment of high-threshold **Type IIx fast-twitch motor units**.
2. **Rate of Force Development (RFD):** Explosive intent enhances the central nervous system's ability to fire action potentials rapidly (neural firing frequency), improving acceleration from slow speeds.
3. **ATP-PCr Energetics:** Keeping efforts strictly under 6 seconds relies almost exclusively on stored intramuscular ATP and Phosphocreatine (PCr), preventing severe blood lactate accumulation or excessive glycolytic acidosis.

## 3. Practical Applications and Programming Strategies

| Scenario | On-Bike Max Power Application |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Athletes Without Gym Access** | Primary method for developing neuromuscular force and sprint acceleration |
| **In-Season Strength Maintenance** | Preserves maximal force qualities when travel or racing prevents gym attendance |
| **Pre-Race Neuromuscular Priming** | Performed 24–48 hours prior to an event (1–2 sets) to activate neural drive without fatigue |

### Execution Precautions
- **Avoid Fatigue-Induced Intervals:** If peak wattage drops by more than $7\% - 10\%$ between repetitions, terminate the session; continuing under fatigue trains glycolytic lactic endurance rather than pure neuromuscular force.
- **Long Rest Intervals are Non-Negotiable:** Full resynthesis of phosphocreatine requires at least 2–3 minutes of active recovery between 5-second reps.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Push Maximally for 4–6 Seconds:** To recruit high-threshold Type II motor units on the bike, sprints must be executed with 100% maximal intent from pedal stroke one.
- **Start from Low Cadence:** Initiate sprints from low speed in a heavy gear (30–40 RPM) to maximize circumferential pedal torque.
- **Allow Full Phosphagen Recovery:** Rest 2.5 to 3 minutes between sprints; rushing the recovery shifts the training stimulus from neuromuscular power to glycolytic tolerance.
- **Excellent for In-Season Maintenance:** Use on-bike maximal sprints 1–2 times weekly during the summer racing season to maintain the strength gains built in the gym during winter.
