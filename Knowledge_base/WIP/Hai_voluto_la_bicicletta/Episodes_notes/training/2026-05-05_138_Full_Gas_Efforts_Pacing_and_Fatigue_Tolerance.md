# 138: Maximal "Full Gas" Efforts: Pacing, Fatigue Tolerance, and Performance Testing

## Overview and Physiological/Training Context

In the modern era of power-meter-dominated training, cyclists often become rigidly tethered to target numbers, ERG mode, and predefined wattage zones. While structured percentage-based training provides precision, an over-reliance on numbers can create psychological governors and degrade an athlete's intuitive pacing ability. In real-world racing, victories are not won by maintaining 98% of FTP; they are determined by self-regulated, maximal all-out efforts (**lavori a tutta / full gas**).

In this episode, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli explore the scientific rationale and practical integration of "full gas" maximal efforts. They discuss why unconstrained efforts are essential for calibrating the Central Governor and internal RPE, how maximal efforts deplete anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) and stimulate true $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$, and how coaches can program self-regulated maximal efforts across segments, climbs, and end-of-ride durability tests.

---

## 1. The Physiology of "Full Gas": Beyond Prescribed Wattage

When an athlete executes an effort "a tutta" (maximal, unconstrained, open pacing):

- **Complete Motor Unit Recruitment:** Unlike fixed-target submaximal intervals, an open maximal effort demands complete recruitment of the entire motor neuron pool—from Type I through high-threshold Type IIx fibers—as fatigue sets in.
- **Full $W'$ Depletion & $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ Saturation:** Over 3 to 8 minutes, an all-out effort completely exhausts the finite anaerobic work capacity ($W'$), forcing the pulmonary and cardiovascular systems to operate at $100\%\ \text{VO}_2\text{max}$.
- **Breaking the "Number Prison":** Cyclists frequently limit their output because a screen displays a power number they believe is their maximum. Unconstrained efforts frequently reveal that an athlete's true physical capacity is 15–30 Watts higher than their calculated model.

---

## 2. Psychological Conditioning and the Central Governor

- **Pacing Competence (Internal Biofeedback):** Real racing does not offer power target prompts; riders must intuitively gauge muscle acidity, breathing frequency, core temperature, and remaining distance to optimize energy distribution. Full gas efforts condition this internal pacing sensor.
- **Fatigue Tolerance and Mental Resilience:** Pushing through severe metabolic acidosis ($[\text{La}^-] > 10 - 14\ \text{mmol/L}$) and high RPE (9.5–10/10) strengthens cognitive tolerance to exertion, training the brain's insular cortex to tolerate extreme physiological perturbation without premature termination.

---

## 3. Practical Structuring of "Full Gas" Workouts

Coaches can program maximal efforts through several distinct modalities:

### A. Segment / Hill Climb Best Efforts (Open Pacing)
- **Application:** On a known 4 to 8 minute climb:
  - **Directive:** Ride from the bottom to the top as fast as possible, with no power meter screen visible (or screen turned to map view).
  - **Goal:** Develop intuitive pacing and measure true current maximal aerobic power (MMP).

### B. Progressive Intensity Ending in "Full Gas"
- **Application:** $4 \times 4$ minute interval set:
  - Intervals 1–3: Controlled @ 105–110% FTP.
  - Interval 4: **All-out "Full Gas"** (empty the tank).
  - **Goal:** Provides the structured aerobic stimulus in early reps while testing mental resolve and $W'$ depletion in the final rep.

### C. Fatigued Best Effort (Durability Benchmark)
- **Application:** At the conclusion of a 3.5-hour Zone 2 ride ($>2,200$ kJ burned):
  - Execute a **single maximal 6-minute effort** on a climb.
  - **Goal:** Directly evaluates fatigue resistance and performance under metabolic depletion.

---

## 4. Coaching Guidelines, Frequency, and Precautions

- **High Neuromuscular & Autonomic Fatigue:** True maximal efforts impose severe stress on the central nervous system (CNS), glycogen stores, and neuroendocrine axis.
- **Prescription Frequency:** Full gas sessions should not be performed more than **once every 7 to 14 days** during build phases. Overusing "a tutta" sessions leads rapidly to non-functional overreaching.
- **Recovery Requirement:** Ensure adequate sleep, carbohydrate replenishment, and at least 48 hours of low-intensity riding following a maximal session.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Escape the Number Prison:** Turn off the power screen occasionally and execute unconstrained maximal efforts to discover your true physiological ceiling.
- **Develop Intuitive Pacing:** Real-world racing requires internal calibration of fatigue, breathing rate, and effort; full gas efforts build vital self-pacing competence.
- **Test Under Fatigue:** Place maximal 5-to-8 minute efforts at the end of long endurance rides to benchmark real-world durability.
- **Respect the Recovery Cost:** Maximal all-out efforts generate heavy central nervous system fatigue; use them sparingly (every 1–2 weeks) and support them with dedicated recovery.
