# 75: What is the Optimal Training Intensity Distribution?

## Overview and Physiological/Training Context

How an endurance athlete distributes training volume across different physiological intensity zones—known as **Training Intensity Distribution (TID)**—is one of the most widely debated topics in exercise physiology and coaching.

In this episode, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli analyze recent scientific studies and meta-analyses comparing the three primary TID models: **Polarized**, **Pyramidal**, and **Threshold**. The discussion evaluates the physiological mechanisms underlying each model, the influence of weekly training volume, and how coaches should periodize intensity distributions across the competitive season.

---

## 1. The 3-Domain Model and TID Definitions

Training intensity distribution is scientifically classified using the **3-Domain Physiological Model** based on ventilatory and blood lactate thresholds:

```
Domain 1: Moderate Intensity (Zone 1) ──> Below LT1 / VT1 (< 2.0 mmol/L lactate)
Domain 2: Heavy Intensity (Zone 2)    ──> Between LT1 and LT2 (2.0 - 4.0 mmol/L lactate)
Domain 3: Severe Intensity (Zone 3)   ──> Above LT2 / VT2 (> 4.0 mmol/L lactate)
```

```
       Comparison of Training Intensity Distribution Models
       
       1. Polarized Model (80 / 0 / 20)
       ├── Zone 1 (LIT): ~75 - 80% of total training time
       ├── Zone 2 (MIT): ~0 - 5% (deliberately avoided)
       └── Zone 3 (HIT): ~15 - 20% (high-intensity intervals)
       
       2. Pyramidal Model (75 / 20 / 5)
       ├── Zone 1 (LIT): ~70 - 80% of total training time
       ├── Zone 2 (MIT): ~15 - 20% (Tempo & Sweet Spot density)
       └── Zone 3 (HIT): ~5 - 10% (targeted severe domain work)
       
       3. Threshold-Centric Model (50 / 40 / 10)
       ├── Zone 1 (LIT): ~50 - 60% of total training time
       ├── Zone 2 (MIT): ~35 - 45% (Threshold & Sweet Spot focus)
       └── Zone 3 (HIT): ~5 - 10%
```

## 2. Scientific Comparison and Research Findings

Comparative studies (such as Stöggl & Sperlich, 2014; Rosenblat et al., 2019) have systematically evaluated these distributions across well-trained cyclists, runners, and cross-country skiers:

### Key Findings from the Literature
1. **Superiority over Threshold-Centric Models:** Both Polarized and Pyramidal models consistently produce superior gains in $\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}$, Time to Exhaustion (TTE), and peak power output compared to purely Threshold-centric models.
2. **Polarized vs. Pyramidal Parity:** Direct comparisons between well-structured Polarized and Pyramidal programs show very similar adaptations in highly trained endurance athletes.
3. **The Role of Total Training Volume:**
   - **High Volume ($> 12 - 15\ \text{h/week}$):** Polarized and Pyramidal models are essential to prevent autonomic nervous system overreaching and chronic sympathetic fatigue.
   - **Time-Crunched ($< 6 - 8\ \text{h/week}$):** Athletes can tolerate higher relative percentages of Zone 2 (Sweet Spot/Threshold) because absolute weekly fatigue is limited by time constraints.

---

## 3. Periodization of TID Across the Macrocycle

Rather than rigidly adhering to a single distribution year-round, elite cycling preparation typically shifts between models depending on the phase of the season:

| Phase | Dominant TID Model | Primary Physiological Goal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General Base Phase** | **Polarized (85% Z1 / 15% Z3)** | Build extensive mitochondrial volume in Z1 while touching high neuromuscular power in Z3 |
| **Specific Build Phase** | **Pyramidal (75% Z1 / 20% Z2 / 5% Z3)** | Build extensive threshold density, race-pace tempo, and climbing durability |
| **Pre-Competition / Taper** | **Polarized (80% Z1 / 20% Z3)** | Reduce volume, maintain high-intensity speed, and shed accumulated fatigue |

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Keep Easy Days Truly Easy:** The most common training error is riding too hard on Zone 1 days (drifting into Zone 2), which accumulates residual fatigue and compromises quality on high-intensity days.
- **Choose Based on Weekly Volume:** If you train $> 12\ \text{hours/week}$, adopt a strict Polarized or Pyramidal distribution; if you train $< 8\ \text{hours/week}$, you can afford a higher proportion of Zone 2 tempo/sweet-spot work.
- **Periodize Your TID:** Use a polarized distribution during early base and peaking phases, and shift toward a pyramidal distribution when building event-specific threshold durability.
- **Never Abandon High Intensity Entirely:** Maintain regular exposure to Zone 3 suprathreshold/VO2max efforts throughout the year to preserve neuromuscular coordination and cardiac stroke volume.
