# 105: Optimizing Bike Computer Data Fields for Training and Racing

## Overview and Cognitive Ergonomics

Modern head units (Garmin, Wahoo, Hammerhead, Bryton) offer dozens of real-time metrics across customizable data pages. However, displaying too much data can create **cognitive overload**—distracting the rider, impairing situational awareness, and leading to poor pacing or even crashes in bunch racing.

During high-intensity exercise, cognitive bandwidth decreases as cerebral oxygenation and executive function are challenged. The bike computer's display must be structured with ergonomic clarity: presenting only the essential metrics needed for that specific training or racing context. This episode provides structured display layouts for every riding scenario.

---

## 1. Power Metric Smoothing: Instantaneous vs. 3s vs. 10s Power

- **Instantaneous (1s) Power:** Highly volatile due to the natural torque variation across the pedal stroke (dead spots at 12 and 6 o'clock). Staring at 1s power causes erratic over-corrections.
- **3-Second Average Power (Standard Anchor):** The gold-standard primary metric for steady riding and interval execution. It dampens pedal-stroke noise while providing immediate feedback on effort changes.
- **10-Second / 30-Second Average Power:** Useful for long, steady climbs or time trials to monitor pacing stability over extended durations.
- **Lap Power:** Critical during structured intervals to maintain the prescribed average target across the entire repetition.

## 2. Screen Architecture by Session Type

The hosts recommend creating dedicated profiles and screen pages tailored to distinct riding objectives:

### A. Endurance Base Rides / Long Group Rides
*Goal:* Maintain low-intensity discipline, monitor fueling, and navigate safely.
- **Field 1:** 3s Power (to ensure power stays below LT1)
- **Field 2:** Heart Rate (to monitor cardiovascular drift and thermal strain)
- **Field 3:** Elapsed Ride Time
- **Field 4:** Distance
- **Field 5:** Total Work in Kilojoules (kJ) (essential for calculating intra-workout carbohydrate needs)
- **Field 6:** Cadence
- **Field 7:** Map / Navigation (on secondary page)

### B. Structured Interval Sessions (Quality Workouts)
*Goal:* Flawless interval execution without data clutter.
- **Field 1:** Lap Time (time remaining in the interval)
- **Field 2:** Lap Power (average power of the current rep)
- **Field 3:** 3s Power (real-time execution check)
- **Field 4:** Cadence (critical for torque drills, SFR, or high-cadence intervals)
- **Field 5:** Lap Heart Rate (monitoring cardiovascular response at the end of the block)
- **Field 6:** Repetition Counter / Workout Step Target

### C. Time Trials and Hill Climb Tests
*Goal:* Strict pacing discipline against a predefined physiological limit.
- **Field 1:** Lap Time
- **Field 2:** Lap Power (or Target Power)
- **Field 3:** 3s / 10s Power
- **Field 4:** Cadence
- **Field 5:** Heart Rate
- **Field 6:** Distance / Elevation Remaining

### D. Mass-Start Road Races, Criteriums, and Granfondos
*Goal:* Maximal safety, tactical focus, and minimal distraction.
- *Recommendation:* Minimalist display (2 to 4 fields maximum). In a tight peloton, looking down at the computer is hazardous.
- **Field 1:** Speed (useful for peloton momentum checks)
- **Field 2:** Elapsed Time / Distance
- **Field 3:** 3s Power (for reference during breakaways or sustained climbs)
- **Field 4:** Heart Rate
- *Tip:* Hide complex metrics like Normalized Power (NP), TSS, or balance during races; analyze them post-race.

## 3. Kilojoules (kJ) as a Real-Time Fueling Tracker

Monitoring **Work in Kilojoules (kJ)** provides an objective, real-time indicator of metabolic energy expenditure:

- **Nutritional Application:** Tracking kJ helps athletes pace their carbohydrate intake (e.g., aiming for 30–90 g of carbohydrates per 500–800 kJ expended depending on intensity).

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Match the Screen to the Mission:** Use distinct bike computer profiles for base rides, interval workouts, and races.
- **Use 3-Second Smoothing:** Eliminate the visual noise of instantaneous 1s power by defaulting to 3s power for live execution.
- **Rely on Lap Metrics:** Use the manual lap button to isolate intervals; monitor Lap Power and Lap Time during high-intensity efforts.
- **Simplify for Racing:** In mass-start events, minimize screen fields to keep your eyes on the road and peloton dynamics.
- **Track Work in kJ:** Use cumulative kilojoules to guide intra-ride fueling and assess the true metabolic cost of the ride.
