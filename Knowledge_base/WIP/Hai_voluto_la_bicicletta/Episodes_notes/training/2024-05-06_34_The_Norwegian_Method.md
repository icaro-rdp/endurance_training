# 34: The Norwegian Method

## Overview and The Rise of the Norwegian System

The extraordinary international success of Norwegian endurance athletes—including middle-distance Olympic champion Jakob Ingebrigtsen, Ironman world champions Kristian Blummenfelt and Gustav Iden, and speed skaters—has brought the **Norwegian Method** to the forefront of endurance sports science. In this episode, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli break down the physiological pillars of the Norwegian model, citing the review by Casado et al. (2023) and analyzing how this framework applies to competitive cycling.

---

## 1. Core Pillars of the Norwegian Training Framework

The Norwegian Method is characterized by four interconnected principles:

1. **Strict Lactate-Guided Intensity Control:** Training intensities are governed in real time using portable capillary blood lactate analyzers rather than relying solely on pace, heart rate, or perceived exertion.
2. **Controlled Sub-Threshold & Threshold Intensity:** Work intervals are strictly clamped below or at the anaerobic threshold (**2.0 to 3.5 mmol/L blood lactate**), completely avoiding Zone 3 / severe-domain acidosis.
3. **Double-Threshold Training Days:** Performing two separate threshold interval sessions on the same day (morning and afternoon), typically twice per week.
4. **Massive Low-Intensity Base Volume (Zone 1 / LIT):** High weekly volume (150–220 km/week in running; 20–30+ hours in triathlon/cycling) performed strictly below the aerobic threshold (LT1, <1.5 mmol/L).

---

## 2. Double-Threshold Day Architecture

```
[ DOUBLE-THRESHOLD MICROCYCLE STRUCTURE ]
├── MORNING SESSION (Extensive / Lower Threshold):
│   └── 5 x 6 min or 4 x 8 min @ 2.0 – 2.5 mmol/L (Sweet Spot / Sub-LT2) | Rest: 60–90s
└── AFTERNOON SESSION (Intensive / Upper Threshold):
    └── 10 x 3 min or 20 x 1 min @ 3.0 – 3.5 mmol/L (At LT2 / MLSS) | Rest: 60s
```

### Why Double Thresholds Work
- **High Volume at Minimal Autonomic Cost:** By capping blood lactate at 2.0–3.5 mmol/L, muscular glycogen depletion and autonomic nervous system (sympathetic) stress remain modest.
- **Double Intracellular Signaling:** Two separate bouts of exercise within 8 hours induce two distinct peaks in **PGC-1alpha gene expression**, doubling the cellular stimulus for mitochondrial biogenesis compared to a single workout.

---

## 3. Applying the Norwegian Model to Cycling: Opportunities and Pitfalls

### Transferability to Cycling
- **High Feasibility:** Cycling is non-impact, making double-session days mechanically easier to tolerate than in running (lower eccentric muscle damage).
- **Time and Logistics:** Conducting two distinct sessions per day requires substantial time availability, strict nutritional re-fueling between sessions, and portable lactate testing equipment.

### Limitations in Road Cycling
- **Stochastic Race Demands:** Road cycling is not steady-state; races are won through punchy attacks, sprint finishes, and repeated surges above 150% FTP. Relying exclusively on controlled sub-threshold training without severe-domain VO2max or anaerobic repeatability can leave a cyclist ill-prepared for peloton surges.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Cap Threshold Intensity to Prevent Burnout:** Keep threshold intervals controlled (below 3.5 mmol/L / 90–95% FTP) to allow high weekly volume and rapid recovery.
- **Double Threshold Maximizes Cellular Signaling:** For advanced athletes with schedule flexibility, splitting threshold work into morning and afternoon bouts doubles PGC-1alpha mitochondrial signaling.
- **Keep Base Truly Easy:** Ensure low-intensity volume stays below LT1 (<2.0 mmol/L / talk pace) to maintain freshness for threshold days.
- **Complement with Race-Specific Surges:** Cyclists must supplement Norwegian threshold blocks with race-specific microbursts and VO2max intervals ahead of competitive road events.
