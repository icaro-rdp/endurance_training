# 119: Managing Cycling Training During the Holidays

## Overview and Practical Context

The holiday season (Christmas and New Year) presents a unique scheduling dynamic for cyclists. On one hand, holiday time off from work provides extra hours to train; on the other hand, it introduces family obligations, festive meals, travel, disrupted sleep, and cold winter weather.

Athletes frequently mismanage this window, either swinging into extreme training overload or abandoning structure entirely. This episode provides practical, science-backed strategies for managing training during the holidays—whether executing a home volume camp, focusing on quality workouts, or spending a week skiing off the bike.

---

## 1. The Core Mistake: The "Triple-Load" Acute Spike

The most widespread coaching error during holiday breaks is simultaneously pulling all three training load levers: **Volume**, **Intensity**, and **Frequency**.

- **The Acute Workload Trap:** An athlete normally training 6–8 hours per week suddenly jumps to 18–20 hours while simultaneously performing hard interval sessions and riding every single day.
- **The Physiological Cost:** This massive acute spike in the Acute-to-Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR $> 1.5$) overwhelms connective tissues and suppresses the immune system. Compounded by cold outdoor temperatures, rich festive meals, and late nights, this frequently leads to respiratory illness, extreme fatigue, or tendonitis by early January.

---

## 2. Two Structured Holiday Training Strategies

To capitalize on holiday availability safely, athletes should choose **one** of two deliberate training pathways:

### Strategy A: The Low-Intensity Volume Camp
*Ideal For:* Cyclists with abundant free time, good weather or indoor setups, and strong baseline conditioning.
- **Rule of Execution:** Increase volume by **30 to 50% maximum** above your chronic average (e.g., from 8 hours to 11–12 hours).
- **Intensity Discipline:** Keep all extra volume strictly in **Zone 1 and Zone 2** ($< 	ext{LT1}$). Avoid high-intensity interval workouts during high-volume weeks to prevent autonomic exhaustion.
- **Frequency:** Include at least 1–2 complete rest days or active recovery spins.

### Strategy B: The Targeted Quality Week
*Ideal For:* Cyclists with constrained time due to family/social commitments.
- **Rule of Execution:** Keep total volume moderate ($5	ext{--}8 	ext{ hours}$), but execute **2 to 3 high-quality sessions** (e.g., gym strength, torque tempo, or structured sweet-spot/threshold intervals).
- **Efficiency:** Utilize smart indoor trainer sessions ($60	ext{--}75 	ext{ min}$) to achieve high metabolic density without disrupting family schedules.

---

## 3. The Ski Vacation and Off-Bike Alternative

Many endurance athletes spend the winter holidays skiing (downhill alpine skiing, ski mountaineering/touring, or cross-country skiing):

- **Cross-Country Skiing & Ski Touring (The Gold Standard):** Provides an exceptional whole-body aerobic stimulus, driving high cardiac output and VO2 utilization with zero joint impact and low orthopedic injury risk.
- **Downhill Alpine Skiing (Eccentric Muscle Loading):** Alpine skiing involves intense isometric and eccentric quadriceps contractions. While cardiovascular demand is moderate, eccentric loading causes significant delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS).
- **The Return to the Bike:** After a week of downhill skiing, do not schedule hard cycling intervals immediately. Ride easy Zone 1–2 spins for 3–4 days to flush out eccentric muscle stiffness before resuming structured threshold work.

---

## 4. Managing Holiday Nutrition and Mental Reset

- **Enjoying Festive Meals Without Guilt:** Two or three celebratory dinners will not derail a season. Avoid restrictive guilt-driven behaviors or compensatory extreme fasting.
- **Bank Sleep and Psychological Freshness:** Use holiday time off to prioritize 8–9 hours of deep sleep and mental rejuvenation with family.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Never Pull All Three Levers:** If you increase weekly training volume, keep the intensity strictly in Zone 2; do not increase volume and intensity simultaneously.
- **Cap Acute Volume Increases at 30–50%:** Avoid extreme jumps in weekly hours to protect the immune system and avoid early-January illness.
- **Cross-Country Skiing is Ideal Cross-Training:** Leverage winter mountain sports like Nordic skiing for high-quality aerobic base conditioning.
- **Ease Back After Downhill Skiing:** Allow 3–4 days of easy spinning to recover from eccentric quadriceps loading before attempting hard cycling intervals.
- **Prioritize Sleep and Recovery:** Treat the holiday break as an opportunity to bank deep physical and psychological rest.
