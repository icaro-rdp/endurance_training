# 118: High-Intensity vs Moderate-Intensity Block Training in Cycling

## Overview and Context

Block periodization—concentrating a high density of targeted training stimuli into a short microcycle (typically 1 week) followed by dedicated recovery and low-intensity maintenance—is a proven methodology for breaking performance plateaus in well-trained endurance athletes.

Building upon earlier discussions of block training (Episode 80), this episode reviews a landmark study by renowned Norwegian sports scientist **Bent Rønnestad and colleagues**. The study directly compared the physiological and performance adaptations resulting from a **1-week High-Intensity Training (HIT) block** versus a **1-week Moderate-Intensity Training (MIT / Threshold) block** in competitive cyclists.

---

## 1. Study Design and Methodology (Rønnestad Protocol)

The study recruited well-trained competitive cyclists and investigated the acute and delayed adaptations of two distinct 1-week concentrated training blocks:

### The Experimental Protocols:
- **HIT Block (High-Intensity Shock Microcycle):**
  - Consisted of **5 high-intensity interval sessions** within 7 days.
  - Interval Structure: Short intermittent microbursts (e.g., $3 	imes 10 	imes 30	ext{s on} / 15	ext{s off}$ at $pprox 120	ext{--}130\%$ FTP or $4 	imes 4 	ext{ min}$ maximal aerobic intervals).
  - Maximized time spent above $90\% 	ext{ VO}_2	ext{max}$.
- **MIT Block (Moderate-Intensity / Threshold Microcycle):**
  - Consisted of **5 threshold / sweet-spot sessions** within 7 days.
  - Interval Structure: Continuous or long threshold intervals (e.g., $4 	imes 15 	ext{ min}$ or $3 	imes 20 	ext{ min}$ at $88	ext{--}95\%$ FTP), totaling approximately 60 minutes of time-in-zone per session ($pprox 5 	ext{ hours}$ of threshold work in one week).
- **Follow-Up and Recovery:** Both blocks were followed by a recovery week consisting of low-intensity base riding (Zone 2) before comprehensive post-testing.

---

## 2. Key Physiological and Performance Outcomes

The study evaluated adaptations across several performance benchmarks:

1. **Maximal Oxygen Uptake ($	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$):**
   - *HIT Block:* Produced a statistically significant increase in $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ ($+3	ext{ to }5\%$).
   - *MIT Block:* Maintained $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ with minimal acute change.
2. **Power Output at 4 mmol/L Blood Lactate (Anaerobic Threshold / LT2):**
   - Both HIT and MIT blocks generated meaningful improvements in threshold power ($+2	ext{ to }4\%$), demonstrating that both high-intensity and moderate-intensity stimuli effectively push threshold capacity.
3. **15-Minute Time Trial Performance:**
   - Both groups demonstrated significant improvements in 15-minute mean maximal power, confirming that concentrated training density stimulates performance gains.
4. **10-Second Peak Sprint Power:**
   - *HIT Block:* Maintained or slightly improved sprint power due to the high-velocity neuromuscular recruitment of 30/15 microbursts.
   - *MIT Block:* Showed a slight temporary suppression in explosive sprint power due to the heavy volume of continuous sub-threshold muscular grinding.

---

## 3. Time Above 90% $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ as the Critical Metric

A central finding highlighted by Rønnestad's research is the importance of **accumulated time spent $> 90\% 	ext{ VO}_2	ext{max}$**:

- **The Cardiovascular Trigger:** Maximal central cardiovascular adaptations (stroke volume expansion, left ventricular filling, and vascular compliance) require sustaining cardiac output near maximal levels.
- **Short Intervals Excel:** Intermittent 30/15 intervals allow athletes to accumulate 15–20+ minutes above 90% $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ per workout with lower blood acidosis and lower RPE compared to continuous 4-minute or 8-minute intervals.

---

## 4. Practical Programming: When to Apply HIT vs. MIT Blocks

Coaches should strategically deploy HIT and MIT blocks based on the macrocycle phase and athlete phenotype:

- **When to Use an MIT Block:**
  - *Phase:* Early season / Build phase (Base 2 / Build 1).
  - *Goal:* Expanding muscular durability, increasing fractional utilization, and building threshold resistance without excessive central nervous fatigue.
- **When to Use a HIT Block:**
  - *Phase:* Pre-competition / Peaking phase (4–6 weeks prior to target event).
  - *Goal:* Rapidly lifting the $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ ceiling, sharpening anaerobic repeatability, and preparing for race-pace surges.
- **Prerequisite:** Athletes must enter a block with low baseline fatigue and strictly execute a 7–10 day recovery/deload period immediately afterward to allow adaptation to manifest.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Both Blocks Drive Threshold Gains:** Both HIT and MIT blocks effectively improve lactate threshold power and 15-minute time trial performance.
- **HIT Boosts the Ceiling:** A 1-week concentrated HIT block specifically increases $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ and preserves sprint power.
- **MIT Builds Muscular Grinding Capacity:** An MIT block reinforces steady-state sub-threshold endurance with lower central autonomic strain.
- **Respect the Deload Week:** The concentrated fatigue of 5 hard sessions in 7 days requires a dedicated low-intensity recovery week; without rest, block training leads directly to non-functional overreaching.
