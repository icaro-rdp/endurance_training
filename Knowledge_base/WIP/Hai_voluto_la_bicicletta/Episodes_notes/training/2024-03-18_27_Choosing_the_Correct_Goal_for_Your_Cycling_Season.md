# 27: Choosing the Correct Goal for Your Cycling Season

## Overview and The Psychology of Goal Setting

A training program cannot succeed in a vacuum; its effectiveness depends entirely on the clarity and precision of the target objective. In this episode, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli address a common coaching dilemma: athletes who train hard without defining specific, quantifiable goals. The hosts explain how vague aspirations ('getting fitter' or 'dropping riding buddies on climbs') fail to provide the structural constraints needed to optimize annual periodization, pacing strategies, and workload modeling.

---

## 1. The SMART Criteria Applied to Cycling

Effective goal selection must satisfy the **SMART** framework:
- **Specific:** Clearly defined target (e.g., 'Complete the Maratona dles Dolomites in under 5 hours 30 minutes' rather than 'ride a good gran fondo').
- **Measurable:** Anchored in verifiable metrics (power-to-weight ratio, finishing time, threshold power, segment time).
- **Achievable / Realistic:** Aligned with the athlete's current physiological baseline, training availability, and biological limits. Unrealistic goals foster chronic frustration and burnout.
- **Relevant:** Personally meaningful to the athlete to sustain intrinsic motivation through difficult winter training blocks.
- **Time-Bound:** Tied to specific target dates on the competitive calendar.

---

## 2. Triad of Goals: Outcome vs. Performance vs. Process

A critical sports psychology concept is balancing three hierarchical layers of goals:

```
[ OUTCOME GOAL ]    ->  e.g., Top 10 finish in category (High external uncontrollability)
       ▲
[ PERFORMANCE GOAL] ->  e.g., Sustain 4.2 W/kg for 45 min on final climb (Under athlete's control)
       ▲
[ PROCESS GOAL ]    ->  e.g., Daily sleep >= 8 hrs, 90 g/hr carb fueling, 2 weekly threshold sessions
```

1. **Outcome Goals:** Finishing position, podiums, beating rivals. While exciting, they depend heavily on uncontrollable external factors (weather, mechanicals, competitor fitness).
2. **Performance Goals:** Self-referenced metrics completely within the athlete's control (e.g., holding 300W on a 30-minute climb, hitting target body composition).
3. **Process Goals:** The daily operational habits (nutrition, sleep, hydration, executing specific intervals at target cadences) that inevitably yield performance improvements.

---

## 3. Matching Physiological Profiling to Event Demands

Choosing the correct goal requires matching the athlete's **Phenotype and Power-Duration Profile** with event demands:
- **Gran Fondo / Long Climbs:** Requires high fractional utilization of VO2max, high FTP (W/kg), high fat oxidation, and metabolic durability over 3000+ kJ.
- **Criteriums & Circuit Races:** Requires anaerobic work capacity (W'), high repeatability of short micro-bursts (500–800W for 10–20s), and rapid recovery.
- **Time Trials:** Requires sustained absolute power, aerodynamic positioning (low CdA), and optimal cadence efficiency.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Anchor Macrocycles to SMART Goals:** Define 1–2 primary 'A-goals' per season to give your training periodization purpose and direction.
- **Focus on Process Goals Daily:** Shift mental energy from uncontrollable race outcomes to actionable daily processes (sleep, intra-workout fueling, workout execution).
- **Match Goals to Your Phenotype:** Select target events that suit your natural physiology, or commit to a multi-month periodized block to develop required limiting factors.
- **Set Intermediate Milestones:** Establish testing benchmarks every 6–8 weeks to measure progression and adjust training loads dynamically.
