# 148: Heat Stress and Training Adaptation: When and How to Adjust Workouts

## Overview and Physiological Definition

Training and racing in extreme environmental heat (>30–35°C / 86–95°F) imposes substantial physiological and thermoregulatory strain on the human body. Under thermal stress, the cardiovascular system faces a severe competing demand: it must simultaneously deliver oxygenated blood to working skeletal muscles and shunt large volumes of cardiac output to cutaneous blood vessels for evaporative heat dissipation (sweating).

This competition induces rapid **cardiovascular drift**, accelerates glycogen depletion, elevates core body temperature, and increases perceived exertion (RPE) at any given mechanical power output. This episode explores the physiological mechanisms of heat stress, establishes quantitative thresholds for modifying training sessions, and outlines acclimation protocols versus avoiding non-productive thermal exhaustion.

## 1. Physiological Consequences of Thermal Stress

### 1. Hemodynamic Conflict and Stroke Volume Collapse
- **Cutaneous Vasodilation:** Blood flow to the skin increases from ~0.5 L/min at rest up to 6–8 L/min during severe heat exercise.
- **Plasma Volume Depletion:** Progressive sweat loss (1.0–2.5 L/h) decreases effective blood volume, reducing end-diastolic filling pressure (preload) and stroke volume.
- **Cardiac Drift:** Heart rate elevates substantially (often +15–25 bpm higher for identical watts) to maintain systemic blood pressure and cardiac output.

### 2. Accelerated Glycogenolysis and Metabolic Shift
Elevated core temperature stimulates sympathetic catecholamine release (epinephrine), which up-regulates **muscle glycogen phosphorylase**. Consequently, carbohydrate oxidation accelerates dramatically while fat oxidation is blunted, causing premature glycogen depletion and early lactate accumulation even at moderate Zone 2/3 power outputs.

### 3. Central Nervous System (CNS) Thermal Governor
When core body temperature surpasses **38.5–39.0°C**, the hypothalamus initiates protective central inhibition. Voluntary motor unit recruitment declines, manifesting as an insurmountable increase in RPE and sudden power loss to prevent heat stroke.

## 2. Decision Framework: When and How to Modify Workouts

Coaches and athletes must not treat heat as a test of willpower. Attempting to force winter/spring power targets in 35°C heat results in excessive autonomic strain and multi-day fatigue without superior fitness gains.

### Quantitative Adjustments for High-Heat Sessions

| Session Type | Power Target Adjustment | Interval / Rest Modification | Hydration & Cooling Mandate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Zone 2 Base Endurance** | Reduce power by **10–20 W** (Shift to HR ceiling) | Maintain steady cadence; avoid steep low-airflow climbs | 750–1000 mL/h; 500–800 mg/L sodium |
| **Sweet Spot / Threshold** | Reduce power by **5–8%** (-15 to -25 W) | Split long blocks: e.g., $3 \times 10\text{ min}$ instead of $1 \times 30\text{ min}$; Extend rest to 1:0.75 | Pre-cooling (ice slushies); Ice in jersey pockets |
| **$\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$ Intervals** | Maintain power by **shortening duration** | Switch to microbursts (e.g., $30/15\text{s}$ or $40/20\text{s}$) to preserve high airflow and prevent thermal locking | Execute early morning (<08:00) or indoors with industrial fans |

## 3. Hydration Kinetics and Heat Acclimation Protocols

### Electrolyte and Fluid Balance
- **Sweat Sodium Concentration:** Sweat is hypotonic, containing ~500 to 1,500 mg of sodium ($Na^+$) per liter.
- **Hypotonic Hydration:** Consuming pure water during high-volume sweating dilutes plasma sodium, triggering hyponatremia and gastrointestinal sloshing. Formulate drinks with **500–1,000 mg of sodium per liter** and 4–6% carbohydrate concentration to optimize SGLT1-mediated intestinal water absorption.

### Heat Acclimation (HA) Methodology
True heat acclimation induces plasma volume expansion (+10–15%), lowered resting core temperature, earlier onset of sweating, and lower sweat electrolyte losses.
- **The Ideal Protocol:** 10 to 14 consecutive days of 60 minutes of low-intensity exercise (Zone 1/2) in the heat, or **passive post-ride thermal loading** (immersing in a 40°C hot bath for 20–30 minutes immediately following a regular workout).

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Watts Do Not Equal Strain in the Heat:** At 35°C, holding standard threshold power imposes severe cardiovascular and thermoregulatory strain. Adjust power targets downward by 5–10% or pace primarily via heart rate and RPE.
- **Shorten Interval Durations:** When performing high-intensity work in hot conditions, replace long sustained blocks with intermittent microbursts (e.g., 30/15s) to maintain high airflow and limit core temperature spikes.
- **Prioritize Sodium and Fluid Volume:** Target 750–1,200 mL/h of fluid containing 500–1,000 mg/L of sodium; avoid drinking plain water in large volumes during heavy sweating.
- **Separate Quality from Acclimation:** Execute high-intensity interval sessions in the cool morning or indoors with fans; use low-intensity rides or post-exercise hot baths for heat adaptation.
