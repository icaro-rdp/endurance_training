# 32: Training VO2max: Practical Examples

## Overview and Physiological Objectives

In this follow-up to Episode 2, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli transition from the theoretical mechanisms of maximal oxygen uptake ($VO_2max$) to **practical interval architecture**. The primary goal of VO2max training is to maximize **time spent at or near VO2max ($t > 90\% VO_2max$)** to induce central cardiac adaptations (stroke volume expansion, left ventricular compliance) and peripheral microvascular remodeling.

The hosts analyze classic interval formats, fast-start variations, and short intermittent protocols, detailing how each influences oxygen uptake kinetics.

---

## 1. Long Sustained Intervals (The Classic Protocol)

### Classical 4 x 5 min / 5 x 4 min
- **Intensity:** 105–115% FTP / 90–95% 5-minute power ($pVO_2max$).
- **Recovery:** 1:1 work-to-rest ratio (e.g., 4–5 min active recovery between reps).
- **The Kinetics Challenge:** Oxygen uptake kinetics exhibit a lag at the onset of exercise (Phase I and Phase II kinetics take 60–90 seconds to reach target VO2). In a constant-load 4-minute interval, only the final 2–2.5 minutes may be spent above 90% $VO_2max$.

---

## 2. Advanced Protocol: Fast-Start (Decreasing-Power) Intervals

To accelerate oxygen uptake kinetics and maximize effective time above 90% $VO_2max$, coaches use **Fast-Start Intervals**:

```
[ Work Interval: 5 Minutes Total ]
├─ First 90–120 sec: 115–125% FTP (Fast start to rapidly drive VO2 up)
└─ Final 180–210 sec: 102–108% FTP (Holding near-VO2max while buffering acidosis)
```

- **Mechanism:** The hard initial surge rapidly depletes phosphocreatine (PCr) stores and causes immediate venous deoxygenation, forcing pulmonary oxygen consumption to ramp up rapidly (reducing the time constant $\tau$).
- **Physiological Advantage:** The rider reaches >90% $VO_2max$ within 45–60 seconds, extending total effective stimulus time to 3.5–4 minutes per repetition with lower mental resistance than a steady all-out effort.

---

## 3. Short Intermittent Protocols (Rønnestad 30/15s & 40/20s)

### Structure & Execution
- **Protocol:** 3 sets of (10 to 13 repetitions of 30s ON at 120–135% FTP / 15s OFF at 50% FTP), with 3–4 minutes easy recovery between sets.
- **Why It Works:** 
  1. The 15-second recovery is too short for pulmonary oxygen consumption and cardiac output to return to baseline.
  2. The brief recovery allows partial resynthesis of intramuscular phosphocreatine, enabling higher average mechanical power output across the entire set.
  3. Total accumulated time $>90\% VO_2max$ is often significantly greater than during continuous long intervals.

---

## 4. Cadence and Muscular Torque Considerations

- **High Cadence (95–105+ RPM):** Highly recommended during VO2max intervals. High pedaling frequencies engage the muscle pump mechanism, enhancing venous return to the heart, maximizing end-diastolic filling, and reducing local muscular peripheral torque fatigue.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Prioritize Time Above 90% VO2max:** Design sessions that accumulate 15 to 25 minutes of total time near maximal oxygen uptake.
- **Use Fast-Start Protocols to Speed Kinetics:** Begin 4–5 minute intervals with a 90-second surge at 115–120% FTP before settling into threshold-plus power.
- **Incorporate 30/15 Intermittent Sets:** Use Rønnestad-style micro-intervals to accumulate high aerobic time with superior average power output.
- **Spin at High Cadence (95–105 RPM):** Keep pedaling cadence elevated during VO2max intervals to maximize cardiovascular preload and stroke volume.
