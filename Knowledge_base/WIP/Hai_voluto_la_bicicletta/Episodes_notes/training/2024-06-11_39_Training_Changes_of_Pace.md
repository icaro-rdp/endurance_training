# 39: Training Changes of Pace and Acceleration Dynamics

## Overview and Deconstructing the 'Change of Pace'

A frequent complaint among competitive cyclists is: *'I have good steady-state power, but I lack the change of pace (cambio di ritmo) when the group accelerates.'* In this episode, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli deconstruct what the 'change of pace' actually represents in exercise physiology. 

The hosts explain that acceleration repeatability is not an isolated physiological entity; rather, it is the dynamic interaction between an athlete's **aerobic foundation (FTP / VO2max)**, **anaerobic work capacity ($W'$ / glycolytic reserve)**, and **neuromuscular torque capabilities**.

---

## 1. The Physiological Determinants of Acceleration

When a race group surges from 300W to 600W on a climb or out of a corner, two physiological reservoirs are engaged:

```
[ TOTAL ENERGY DURING A SURGE ]
├── AEROBIC CEILING (VO2max / FTP): Governs baseline speed and oxidative lactate clearance
└── ANAEROBIC CAPACITY (W' / Anaerobic Work Capacity): Finite battery powering efforts > CP/FTP
```

### The Aerobic Dominance of Surge Tolerance
- **The Aerobic Engine Preserves Anaerobic Battery:** An athlete with an FTP of 350W who rides at 300W is in the moderate/heavy domain (using minimal $W'$). An athlete with an FTP of 300W riding at 300W is at 100% capacity; any acceleration forces them deep into the severe domain, rapidly burning their finite anaerobic tank.
- **Aerobic Clearance of Surges:** A larger aerobic engine and higher capillary/mitochondrial density speed up **$W'$ reconstitution ($W'_{bal}$)** between repeated surges.

---

## 2. Anaerobic & Neuromuscular Bottlenecks

- **Rate of Force Development (RFD):** Quick accelerations require instantaneous recruitment of high-threshold motor units without waiting for metabolic kinetics to ramp up.
- **Lactate Buffering & Intracellular pH:** Repeated glycolytic spikes produce intracellular $H^+$ accumulation; without adequate carnosine/bicarbonate buffering and MCT transporters, muscular contractile failure occurs.

---

## 3. Practical Workouts to Train the Change of Pace

To build acceleration repeatability, workouts must combine steady aerobic baselines with punchy severe-domain spikes:

### 1. Tempo/Sweet Spot with Microburst Surges
- **Structure:** 3 x 15 min at Sweet Spot (88–92% FTP), with a **15-second explosive surge at 150–180% FTP every 2 to 3 minutes**.
- **Mechanism:** Forces the body to produce lactate during the surge and immediately clear it while continuing to pedal under steady load.

### 2. Microburst Repeats (40/20s or 30/15s)
- **Structure:** 2–3 sets of 10 reps (40s @ 125–135% FTP / 20s @ 50% FTP).
- **Mechanism:** Trains rapid $W'$ depletion and partial reconstitution under high cardiovascular load.

### 3. Late-Ride Fatigued Surges (Durability Training)
- **Structure:** Performing 5 x 1-minute all-out accelerations (with 2 min recovery) **after accumulating 2000–3000 kJ of mechanical work**.
- **Mechanism:** Conditions motor unit recruitment patterns when fast-twitch fibers are pre-fatigued and glycogen stores are partially depleted.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Build Your Aerobic Base First:** A higher FTP and VO2max protect your anaerobic reserve, making race accelerations far less metabolically costly.
- **Train Clearance Under Load:** Incorporate steady sweet spot intervals with punchy 15-second surges to train lactate shuttling under continuous tension.
- **Utilize 40/20 and 30/15 Microbursts:** Program short intermittent intervals to condition rapid $W'$ replenishment.
- **Practice Surges Under Fatigue:** Place acceleration drills at the end of long endurance rides to build real-world competitive durability.
