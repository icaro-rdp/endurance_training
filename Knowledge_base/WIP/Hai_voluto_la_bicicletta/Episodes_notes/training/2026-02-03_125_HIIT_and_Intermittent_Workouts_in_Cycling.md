# 125: High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT) and Intermittent Protocols in Cycling

## Overview and Physiological/Training Context

High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT) represents one of the most effective and time-efficient modalities for driving central cardiovascular and peripheral muscular adaptations in endurance athletes. In cycling, interval sessions are broadly categorized into **continuous long intervals** (e.g., 4x4 min or 5x5 min at 105–115% FTP) and **intermittent micro-intervals** (e.g., 30/30s, 40/20s, 30/15s, or Tabata 20/10s).

This episode breaks down the physiological targets of HIIT—specifically the accumulation of time spent at or near maximal oxygen uptake ($>90\%\ \text{VO}_2\text{max}$, often designated as $T@\text{VO}_2\text{max}$)—while contrasting how different work-to-rest ratios, intensities, and execution strategies (such as hard-start or decreasing intensity protocols) modulate aerobic kinetics, anaerobic work capacity ($W'$), and muscular fatigue.

---

## 1. Physiological Targets of HIIT: Maximizing Time at $>90\%\ \text{VO}_2\text{max}$

The primary stimulus driving central cardiovascular adaptations (increased left ventricular stroke volume, maximal cardiac output $Q$, and arterial-venous oxygen difference) is accumulating sufficient time in the severe intensity domain:

- **Target Metric ($T@\text{VO}_2\text{max}$):** The total time accumulated during a workout where oxygen consumption exceeds $90\%$ of true $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$.
- **The Kinetics Lag:** Upon initiating a high-intensity bout from baseline, oxygen uptake requires **60 to 90 seconds (the fast phase of $\text{VO}_2$ kinetics)** to ramp up toward maximal values. In continuous long intervals, the first 60–90 seconds of each bout are "lost" to this ramp phase.
- **The Intermittent Advantage:** By using short recovery intervals (e.g., 15–30 seconds), oxygen consumption does not drop back to baseline during the rest phase. Consequently, subsequent work bouts immediately re-engage the aerobic engine at high $\text{VO}_2$ levels without repeating the full ramp delay.

---

## 2. Comparing Intermittent Protocols and Work-to-Rest Ratios

| Protocol | Work Duration / Intensity | Rest Duration / Intensity | Primary Physiological Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **30/30s** | 30s @ 120–130% FTP | 30s @ 50% FTP (Easy) | Balanced aerobic-anaerobic flux; moderate $W'$ depletion; excellent for beginners/activation |
| **40/20s** | 40s @ 115–125% FTP | 20s @ 50% FTP | Rapid $\text{VO}_2$ saturation; strong aerobic stimulus; high metabolic acidity |
| **30/15s (Rønnestad)** | 30s @ 120–135% FTP | 15s @ 50% FTP | High time $>90\%\ \text{VO}_2\text{max}$; partial phosphocreatine (PCr) resynthesis; superior power output |
| **Tabata (20/10s)** | 20s @ 150–170% FTP (Maximal) | 10s @ 0–40% FTP | Extreme anaerobic glycolysis and rapid $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ attainment; very high neuromuscular fatigue |
| **Continuous (4x4–5x5 min)** | 4–5 min @ 105–115% FTP | 3–4 min @ 50% FTP | Sustained cardiovascular preload; high stroke volume stimulation; high central strain |

---

## 3. Phosphocreatine (PCr) Kinetics and Lactate Dynamics

The physiological reason intermittent micro-intervals allow greater total work output than continuous intervals lies in **intracellular energy buffering**:

1. **PCr Resynthesis in Short Breaks:** During a 15–30 second recovery, approximately 50–70% of degraded phosphocreatine is rapidly resynthesized via mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation without requiring complete clearance of metabolic byproducts.
2. **Myoglobin Re-oxygenation:** Muscle myoglobin rapidly re-oxygenates during brief pauses, buffering the onset of severe intracellular acidosis when the next 30-second bout begins.
3. **Pacing Trap:** If an athlete starts the initial intervals of a 30/15 or 40/20 set too aggressively ($>140\%$ FTP), anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) is depleted within 3–4 repetitions, causing premature power failure before aerobic steady-state kinetics can be achieved.

---

## 4. Decreasing Intensity Interval Training (DIIT) and Hard-Start Concepts

- **Hard-Start Intervals:** Initiating a 3–5 minute interval with a 15–30 second surge at 130–150% FTP rapidly accelerates $\text{VO}_2$ kinetics, shortening the transition phase and increasing total time spent at $>90\%\ \text{VO}_2\text{max}$ over the duration of the bout.
- **Decreasing Intensity (DIIT):** Starting at high power and progressively decreasing target wattage (e.g., 1 min @ 120%, 2 min @ 105%, 2 min @ 95% FTP) maintains maximal oxygen uptake while accommodating the progressive decline in neuromuscular freshness and rising intracellular acidosis.

---

## 5. Practical Structuring and Microcycle Programming

### Typical Rønnestad 30/15 Set Structure
- **Warm-up:** 20 min progressive Zone 2 with $3 \times 10$s high-cadence spin-ups.
- **Main Body:** $3 \times \text{sets of } (10 \times [30\text{s ON } @ 125\%\ \text{FTP} / 15\text{s OFF } @ 50\%\ \text{FTP}])$.
- **Inter-Set Recovery:** 4 to 5 minutes of active spinning in Zone 1/2.
- **Cool-down:** 15 min easy endurance.

### Frequency and Microcycle Placement
- **Base/Build Period:** 1 to 2 HIIT sessions per week integrated into a polarized or pyramidal intensity distribution (80% low intensity, 15–20% high intensity).
- **Pre-Race Activation:** 1 mini-set (e.g., $1 \times 6$ reps of 30/30s) performed 48 hours prior to a competition to prime aerobic enzymes and neuromuscular recruitment without inducing residual structural fatigue.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Accumulate Time at the Ceiling:** The primary goal of $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ interval training is maximizing total duration spent above $90\%\ \text{VO}_2\text{max}$ ($T@\text{VO}_2\text{max}$).
- **Intermittent Protocols Yield Higher Power:** Micro-intervals (e.g., 30/15s) allow higher average mechanical power output and lower neuromuscular fatigue compared to continuous intervals of equivalent total work duration.
- **Pace the First Repetitions:** Avoid sprinting all-out in the first 2–3 repetitions of a micro-interval block; establish a sustainable severe-domain power to avoid early $W'$ depletion.
- **Hard Starts Accelerate Kinetics:** Utilize a 15–20 second hard start at the beginning of long aerobic intervals to bypass the oxygen uptake lag.
