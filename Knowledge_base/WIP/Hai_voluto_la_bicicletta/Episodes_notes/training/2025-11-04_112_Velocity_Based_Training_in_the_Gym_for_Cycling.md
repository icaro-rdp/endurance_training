# 112: Velocity-Based Training in the Gym for Cycling

## Overview and Context

Strength training in the gym is now universally recognized as an essential component of endurance cycling preparation. Heavy resistance training improves cycling economy, increases time-to-exhaustion at maximal power, delays muscle fiber fatigue via enhanced motor unit recruitment, and strengthens connective tissues.

However, traditional percentage-based strength training (prescribing loads as a fixed percentage of 1-Repetition Maximum, e.g., $80\% 	ext{ 1RM}$) presents significant challenges for endurance cyclists: daily neuromuscular fatigue from cycling alters true 1RM on a daily basis. To solve this, elite cycling programs—highlighted by viral photos of Tadej Pogačar using linear encoders connected to barbells—have adopted **Velocity-Based Training (VBT)**. This episode explores the biomechanics, technology, and programming of VBT for cyclists.

---

## 1. Principles of Velocity-Based Training (VBT)

VBT uses measurement devices (linear position transducers/encoders or optical accelerometer-based cameras) to measure the **Mean Propulsive Velocity (MPV)** or **Mean Concentric Velocity** of the barbell in meters per second ($	ext{m/s}$).

### Key Advantages for Cyclists:
- **Daily Auto-Regulation:** Because 1RM fluctuates by $\pm 10	ext{--}15\%$ based on prior cycling fatigue, sleep, and nutrition, tracking barbell velocity allows athletes to adjust the load on the bar in real time to match their true physiological state.
- **Maximal Neuromuscular Intent:** Real-time velocity feedback encourages the athlete to push the load with maximal voluntary intent on every concentric repetition, maximizing motor unit recruitment and rate of force development (RFD).
- **Targeting Specific Strength Qualities:** Different movement velocities correspond to distinct neuromuscular adaptations:
  - *Maximal Strength:* $< 0.50 	ext{ m/s}$ (Loads $> 80	ext{--}85\% 	ext{ 1RM}$).
  - *Strength-Speed / Power:* $0.50	ext{--}0.75 	ext{ m/s}$ (Loads $60	ext{--}80\% 	ext{ 1RM}$).
  - *Speed-Strength:* $0.75	ext{--}1.00 	ext{ m/s}$ (Loads $40	ext{--}60\% 	ext{ 1RM}$).
  - *Starting Strength / Velocity:* $> 1.00 	ext{ m/s}$ (Loads $< 40\% 	ext{ 1RM}$).

## 2. Managing Fatigue with Velocity Loss Cutoffs

In bodybuilding and powerlifting, sets are often taken close to muscular failure. For endurance cyclists, training to failure generates excessive structural muscle damage (DOMS), metabolic acidosis, and prolonged neuromuscular fatigue that impairs subsequent on-bike workouts.

- **Velocity Loss (%VL) Monitoring:** VBT manages intra-set fatigue by stopping the set when repetition velocity drops by a specific percentage relative to the first (or fastest) repetition:
  - *Low Velocity Loss ($10	ext{--}15\% 	ext{ VL}$):* The optimal zone for endurance cyclists. It triggers high neuromuscular power and maximal strength adaptations while producing minimal metabolic fatigue and virtually zero structural damage, allowing high-quality cycling workouts the next day.
  - *High Velocity Loss ($> 30	ext{--}40\% 	ext{ VL}$):* Induces high hypertrophy and metabolic stress, but causes severe muscle soreness and degrades cycling performance.

## 3. Cycling-Specific Gym Exercises and Movement Selection

Cyclists require multi-joint compound movements that mirror the hip, knee, and ankle extension patterns of the pedal stroke:

1. **Trap Bar Deadlift / Romanian Deadlift:** Maximizes posterior chain strength (gluteus maximus, hamstrings, spinal erectors) with lower spinal shear stress than conventional straight-bar deadlifts.
2. **Squat Variations (Safety Bar Box Squats, Back Squats):** Builds quad and glute peak torque and strengthens tendon stiffness.
3. **Unilateral Exercises (Bulgarian Split Squats, Step-Ups):** Corrects left-right asymmetries and mimics the single-leg force production of pedaling.
4. **Core Stability and Anti-Rotation:** Planks, Pallof presses, and bird-dogs to stabilize the pelvis and prevent energy leakage on the saddle.

## 4. Seasonal Periodization: Off-Season to Competition Phase

- **Transition & Early Base (Hypertrophy & Anatomical Adaptation):** $2	ext{--}3 	ext{ sessions/week}$. Focus on learning movement mechanics, tendon conditioning, moderate loads ($15	ext{--}20\% 	ext{ VL}$).
- **Late Base / Pre-Competition (Maximal Strength & RFD):** $2 	ext{ sessions/week}$. Heavy loads moved at maximal intent ($< 0.50	ext{--}0.65 	ext{ m/s}$), low reps ($3	ext{--}5 	ext{ reps}$), strict $10	ext{--}15\% 	ext{ VL}$ cutoff.
- **In-Season Maintenance:** $1 	ext{ session every } 7	ext{--}10 	ext{ days}$ ($2	ext{--}3 	ext{ sets of } 3	ext{--}4 	ext{ reps}$). Preserves neuromuscular gains throughout the race season with zero residual fatigue.

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Train Movement Velocity, Not Just Weight:** Push every concentric repetition with maximal explosive intent to maximize neuromuscular recruitment.
- **Use Low Velocity Loss Cutoffs (10–15%):** Terminate sets before severe velocity drop-off occurs to avoid excessive muscle damage and protect on-bike training quality.
- **Auto-Regulate Daily Loads:** If movement speed is slow at a warm-up weight, reduce the working load rather than forcing a predetermined weight.
- **Prioritize Multi-Joint Compound Movements:** Focus on trap bar deadlifts, box squats, and split squats to strengthen the primary cycling prime movers.
- **Maintain Strength In-Season:** A single, brief strength maintenance session per week preserves force production and bone density throughout the racing year.
