# 142: Same Watts, Different Sensations: How Intensity Zones Change

## Overview and Physiological Definition

In modern endurance cycling, power meters offer an objective measurement of **external load** (mechanical work in watts). However, the human body does not perceive watts; it responds to **internal load**—the physiological and metabolic strain imposed on the cardiovascular, autonomic, and neuromuscular systems.

A rider producing 220 watts on a cool, rested morning in Zone 2 may experience a completely different physiological environment when producing the same 220 watts on a hot afternoon after four hours of riding or following high-intensity intervals. This episode examines why static training zones defined by baseline testing shift dynamically during exercise, and how athletes and coaches should interpret discrepancies between power, heart rate, and Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE).

```
   [ EXTERNAL LOAD ]                                [ INTERNAL LOAD ]
     Mechanical Work                               Physiological Strain
   ===================                             ====================
     Power: 220 Watts   ------ Factors ------>     Heart Rate: 130 bpm -> 158 bpm
     Cadence: 90 rpm       - Core Temp (Heat)      Lactate: 1.2 mM -> 2.8 mM
     Duration: Constant    - Glycogen Status       Ventilation: Stable -> Labored
                           - Dehydration           RPE (Borg): 3/10 -> 6/10
                           - Motor Unit Fatigue    Autonomic: Sympathetic Surge
```

## 1. Mechanisms Driving Internal Load Shifts at Fixed External Power

### 1. Cardiovascular Drift and Plasma Volume Reduction
During prolonged steady-state exercise, heart rate exhibits a progressive upward drift despite constant mechanical power.
- **Thermoregulatory Vasodilation:** To dissipate heat, blood flow is shunted to cutaneous capillary beds.
- **Dehydration and Stroke Volume:** Sweat loss reduces circulating plasma volume, decreasing venous return (end-diastolic volume) and cardiac preload.
- **Compensatory Tachycardia:** According to the cardiac output equation ($Q = \text{Stroke Volume} \times \text{Heart Rate}$), heart rate must increase to maintain systemic cardiac output and muscle perfusion.

### 2. Motor Unit Fatigue and Fiber Type Recruitment
- As glycogen-rich Type I slow-twitch fibers experience fatigue and substrate depletion, the central nervous system recruits Type IIa and Type IIx fast-twitch motor units (following the **Henneman Size Principle**).
- Type II fibers have lower oxidative efficiency, higher glycogen consumption rates, and generate greater blood lactate and metabolic byproducts ($H^+$, $P_i$), increasing internal metabolic strain and perceived exertion at identical external watts.

### 3. Core Temperature and Sympathetic Activation
Elevated core body temperature triggers a systemic surge in sympathetic nervous system activity and circulating catecholamines (epinephrine, norepinephrine). This accelerates hepatic and muscular glycogenolysis, shifting substrate utilization away from fat oxidation and toward carbohydrate consumption even within nominal "Zone 2" power ranges.

## 2. The Concept of the "Day Zone" and Dynamic Thresholds

Static power zones (e.g., Zone 2 = 56–75% FTP) assume a pristine, homeostatic baseline. In practice, an athlete's physiological thresholds are dynamic and move along the power axis based on acute state variables:

| Acute Condition | Effect on LT1 / FatMax | Effect on LT2 / FTP | Physiological Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fresh & Well-Fueled** | Baseline (High) | Baseline (High) | High fat oxidation, stable cardiac response |
| **High Heat & Humidity** | Shifts Left (-15 to -30 W) | Shifts Left (-15 to -25 W) | Early cardiovascular drift, premature lactate accumulation |
| **Late Ride Fatigue (>2500 kJ)** | Shifts Left (-20 to -40 W) | Shifts Left (-20 to -35 W) | Type II fiber recruitment, elevated ventilation and RPE |
| **Glycogen Depleted** | Shifts Right for Lactate / Shifts Left for Power | Collapses Sustainable TTE | Blunted lactate response, severe RPE escalation |

```
Standard (Fresh) Zone 2:   [---------- 180W to 240W ----------]
Fatigued / Heat Zone 2:    [---- 150W to 200W ----]
                                ^
                                Shift of internal metabolic threshold (LT1)
```

## 3. Practical Prescriptions: Managing Power vs. Heart Rate vs. RPE

### When Power Should Be the Master Metric
- **Short High-Intensity Intervals (Zone 5/6, $30\text{s}$ to $4\text{ min}$):** Neuromuscular power and maximal aerobic kinetics take precedence. Cardiovascular response lags behind instantaneous mechanical output; pacing must rely on power and RPE.

### When Heart Rate and RPE Must Override Power Targets
- **Prolonged Zone 2 Base Rides:** The goal of Zone 2 is specific mitochondrial and metabolic signaling without autonomic and sympathetic overload. If a rider aims for 210 W but, due to heat or fatigue, heart rate drifts into Zone 3/4 and breathing crosses the first ventilatory threshold (VT1), the rider is no longer achieving low-intensity aerobic conditioning. Watts must be dialed down to maintain true internal Zone 2 strain.
- **Extreme Environmental Heat:** In ambient temperatures exceeding 32°C (90°F), attempting to hold nominal threshold power imposes extreme cardiovascular and thermoregulatory stress that can precipitate non-functional overreaching.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Differentiate Internal from External Load:** Power meters measure external work done; heart rate, lactate, core temperature, and RPE reflect the true biological cost incurred by the athlete.
- **Respect the Shift in Low Intensity:** Zone 2 is a physiological state (below LT1/VT1), not a fixed wattage number. When fatigue or heat triggers cardiac drift and elevated ventilation, reduce power to stay within the true aerobic metabolic domain.
- **Monitor Aerobic Decoupling ($Pw:HR$):** A decoupling rate greater than 5% during steady-state endurance rides indicates significant cardiovascular drift, inadequate hydration, or accumulated muscular fatigue.
- **Auto-Regulate Long Sessions:** Structure long endurance rides by targeting power in the first half and transitioning to heart rate ceiling / RPE governor in the second half.
