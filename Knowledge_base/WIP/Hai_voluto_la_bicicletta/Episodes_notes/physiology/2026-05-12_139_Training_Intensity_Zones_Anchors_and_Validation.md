# 139: Establishing Training Intensity Zones: Anchors, Models, and Practical Validation

## Overview and Physiological/Training Context

In modern cycling coaching, setting training intensity zones is often reduced to the automated application of standard percentage tables (e.g., Coggan 7-zone model based on Functional Threshold Power). However, blindly applying static percentages across all athletes fails to account for fundamental differences in physiological profiles, fiber typology, anaerobic capacity ($W'$), and metabolic threshold boundaries.

In this episode, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli deconstruct the science of training intensity zones. They contrast the **3-zone physiological domain model (Moderate, Heavy, Severe)** with traditional multi-zone models, explain why percentage-based zones fail above the anaerobic threshold, and provide a rigorous, practical framework for anchoring zones to physiological boundaries (LT1/VT1 and LT2/VT2/CP) and validating them through real-world athlete feedback.

---

## 1. Physiological Anchors vs. Arbitrary Percentages

Training zones must reflect true underlying metabolic state transitions, not arbitrary mathematical percentages:

```
[ MODERATE DOMAIN ]       [ HEAVY DOMAIN ]            [ SEVERE DOMAIN ]
<-------------------|-------------------------|--------------------------->
                 LT1 / VT1                 LT2 / VT2 / CP / MLSS
          (Aerobic Threshold)       (Anaerobic / Critical Power Threshold)
```

1. **First Physiological Anchor (LT1 / VT1 - Aerobic Threshold):**
   - **Boundary:** Separates the **Moderate Domain** from the **Heavy Domain**.
   - **Metabolic State:** Below LT1, blood lactate remains at resting baseline ($<1.5 - 2.0$ mmol/L); fat oxidation is high; breathing is comfortable (can speak full sentences).
2. **Second Physiological Anchor (LT2 / VT2 / CP / MLSS - Anaerobic Threshold):**
   - **Boundary:** Separates the **Heavy Domain** from the **Severe Domain**.
   - **Metabolic State:** The highest sustainable workload where blood lactate production equals maximal oxidative clearance rate (Maximal Lactate Steady State). Above this point, $\text{VO}_2$ slow component drives oxygen uptake to $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$, and $W'$ is progressively depleted.

## 2. Comparing Zone Models: 3-Zone, 5-Zone, and 7-Zone Systems

| System | Zones | Primary Anchors | Strengths & Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **3-Zone (Scientific Model)** | Z1: Moderate<br>Z2: Heavy<br>Z3: Severe | LT1 (VT1) and LT2 (CP/MLSS) | **Strengths:** Exact physiological fidelity.<br>**Weaknesses:** Lacks sub-threshold granularity for pacing. |
| **5-Zone (Classic Endurance)** | Z1: Active Recovery<br>Z2: Endurance<br>Z3: Tempo<br>Z4: Threshold<br>Z5: VO2max | LT1, Sweet Spot, LT2, VO2max | **Strengths:** Intuitive, excellent for base and build periodization. |
| **7-Zone (Coggan FTP Model)** | Z1 to Z7 (% FTP) | Single Anchor (FTP = 100%) | **Strengths:** Ubiquitous in software.<br>**Weaknesses:** Inaccurate above Zone 4 due to $W'$ variability. |

## 3. Why Percentage-of-FTP Models Fail Above Threshold (Zones 5, 6, 7)

A critical limitation of setting all zones as a fixed percentage of FTP (e.g., Zone 5 = 106–120% FTP) is individual anaerobic heterogeneity:

- **The Diesel vs. Puncher Disparity:**
  - Two cyclists with the exact same FTP of **300 Watts**:
    - **Athlete A (Diesel / Low VLamax / Small $W'$ = 12 kJ):** 5-minute maximal power is 350 W (116% FTP). Prescribing $5 \times 4$ min @ 120% FTP (360 W) will result in immediate failure on rep 2.
    - **Athlete B (Puncher / High VLamax / Large $W'$ = 25 kJ):** 5-minute maximal power is 430 W (143% FTP). Prescribing 115% FTP (345 W) provides an insufficient stimulus for $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ development.
- **The Solution:** Use FTP/CP strictly for sub-threshold and threshold zones (Zones 1–4). For supra-threshold training (Zones 5–7), anchor prescriptions to **specific duration Mean Maximal Power (e.g., 5-min MMP for $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$, 1-min MMP for anaerobic capacity)** or use open maximal pacing.

---

## 4. Practical Protocols for Zone Calibration and Validation

Coaches should validate and calibrate zones using practical, low-cost field tests:

1. **Validating Zone 2 (LT1 / Moderate Domain):**
   - **The Talk Test:** The athlete must be able to speak complete, multi-sentence paragraphs comfortably without gasping between words.
   - **Heart Rate Decoupling:** During a 2-hour steady Zone 2 ride, heart rate drift (Decoupling / Pw:HR) should remain $<3 - 5\%$.
2. **Validating Zone 4 (LT2 / CP / Threshold Domain):**
   - **Extended Field Test:** 30-to-40 minute sustained time trial or Critical Power multi-test model (e.g., 3-min and 12-min maximal tests).
   - **Session Verification:** If an athlete cannot complete $3 \times 10$ minutes @ 98–100% FTP with 3 minutes rest without experiencing progressive heart rate drift and leg lockup, the threshold is set too high.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Zones Reflect Physiology, Not Math:** Training intensity zones must be anchored to real biological transition points (LT1/VT1 and LT2/CP), not arbitrary percentages.
- **Do Not Use % FTP Above Threshold:** Use duration-specific power records (e.g., 5-min MMP) or self-regulated pacing for $\text{VO}_2\text{max}$ and anaerobic intervals.
- **Validate Zone 2 With the Talk Test:** If you cannot carry on a conversation while riding in Zone 2, you have crossed above LT1 into the Heavy domain.
- **Continuously Refine Zones:** Validate training zones through workout execution, heart rate stability, and athlete feedback rather than treating testing numbers as unchangeable gospel.
