# 7: Testing Methodologies: FTP Test vs. Critical Power vs. Metabolic Cart Testing

## Overview and Diagnostic Objectives

Accurate physiological testing is fundamental to designing individualized training zones, tracking adaptations, and defining pacing strategies in competitive cycling. However, confusion often arises regarding the differences, strengths, and limitations between the three primary testing methodologies:
1. **Functional Threshold Power ($FTP$) Field Testing** (e.g., 20-minute time trial).
2. **Critical Power ($CP$) and $W'$ Mathematical Modeling** (multi-bout field or ergometer testing).
3. **Laboratory Metabolic Cart and Blood Lactate Diagnostics** (gas exchange analysis and lactate step testing).

In this episode, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli evaluate each diagnostic method from sports science and practical coaching perspectives.

---

## 1. Functional Threshold Power ($FTP$) Testing

Popularized by Andrew Coggan and Hunter Allen, $FTP$ is operationally defined as the highest power output a cyclist can maintain in a quasi-steady state for approximately 60 minutes.

### The 20-Minute Protocol ($FTP = 0.95 \times P_{20}$)
- **Protocol:** A 5-minute all-out blow-out effort, followed by 10 minutes easy recovery, then an all-out 20-minute time trial. $FTP$ is estimated as $95\%$ of the mean 20-minute power output ($P_{20}$).
- **Physiological Flaws & Pitfalls:**
  - *The Anaerobic Capacity Bias:* Cyclists with a high glycolytic power ($VLa_{max}$) and large anaerobic work capacity ($W'$) generate high 20-minute power through anaerobic energy contribution, artificially inflating the estimated $FTP$.
  - *The 95% Factor is Not Universal:* Scientific studies demonstrate that the true ratio between 60-minute power (or $MLSS$) and 20-minute power varies widely between **$88\%$ and $96\%$** depending on athlete phenotype.
  - *Pacing Dependency:* Poor pacing (going out too hard) ruins test validity.

---

## 2. The Critical Power ($CP$) and $W'$ Model

The Critical Power concept (Monod & Scherrer, 1965; Moritani et al., 1981) mathematically models the hyperbolic relationship between power output and time to exhaustion:

$$P(t) = CP + \frac{W'}{t} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad W = CP \times t + W'$$

Where:
- **$CP$ (Critical Power):** The mathematical asymptote of the power-duration curve (expressed in Watts), representing the upper boundary of the heavy intensity domain.
- **$W'$ (Anaerobic Work Capacity):** A finite amount of work that can be performed above $CP$ (expressed in Joules or kiloJoules), derived from phosphocreatine, anaerobic glycolysis, and muscle myoglobin oxygen stores.

### Multi-Bout Protocol
- Requires **2 to 3 all-out maximal efforts** of distinct durations (e.g., $3\text{ min}$, $7\text{ min}$, and $12\text{ min}$) separated by full recovery.
- Plotting total work ($J$) against time ($s$) produces a linear regression where the slope equals $CP$ and the y-intercept equals $W'$.
- **Scientific vs. Practical Distinction:** $CP$ is systematically slightly higher ($5\text{--}10\%$) than true physiological $MLSS$ because it represents a mathematical asymptote rather than a biological equilibrium.

## 3. Laboratory Metabolic Cart and Blood Lactate Diagnostics

The laboratory assessment remains the gold standard for comprehensive bioenergetic profiling:
- **Metabolimeter (Spiroergometry):** Continuous breath-by-breath measurement of oxygen uptake ($VO_2$) and carbon dioxide production ($VCO_2$).
  - Accurately measures true $VO_2max$ and Ventilatory Thresholds ($VT_1$ and $VT_2$).
  - Calculates substrate utilization rates (fat vs. carbohydrate oxidation) to determine **$FatMax$** and carbohydrate burn rates.
- **Blood Lactate Step Testing:** Incremental protocol (e.g., $30\text{--}50\text{ W}$ steps lasting $3\text{--}5\text{ minutes}$ each with earlobe blood sampling) to map the individual lactate curve, identifying true $LT_1$ ($2.0\text{ mmol/L}$ or baseline rise) and $LT_2$ (lactate turnpoint/inflection).

## 4. Comparative Diagnostic Matrix

| Metric / Method | Equipment Required | Key Strengths | Main Limitations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **20-min FTP Test** | Power meter, indoor trainer or outdoor climb | Simple, low cost, easily repeatable in field. | Overestimates threshold in anaerobic riders; fixed $95\%$ factor is arbitrary. |
| **Critical Power ($CP / W'$)** | Power meter, stopwatch | Robust two-parameter mathematical model; quantifies both aerobic and anaerobic reserves. | Requires multiple all-out maximal bouts; $CP$ is slightly above true physiological $MLSS$. |
| **Metabolic Cart & Lactate Test** | Metabolic gas analyzer, lactate analyzer, lab ergometer | Absolute gold standard; determines $VO_2max$, $VT_1/VT_2$, $FatMax$, and exact substrate oxidation. | High cost, requires specialized equipment, calibration sensitivity, lab access needed. |

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Understand What You Are Measuring:** A 20-minute test measures 20-minute power, not pure steady-state aerobic threshold; avoid treating $0.95 \times P_{20}$ as an infallible law.
- **Utilize CP for Functional Modeling:** For road and criterium racers, the $CP / W'$ model provides valuable insights into both sustained climbing ability ($CP$) and repeated surge capacity ($W'$).
- **Periodic Lab Testing for Metabolic Calibration:** When possible, conduct periodic metabolic or lactate step tests to calibrate base endurance zones ($LT_1 / FatMax$) and ensure training targets match actual cellular physiology.
