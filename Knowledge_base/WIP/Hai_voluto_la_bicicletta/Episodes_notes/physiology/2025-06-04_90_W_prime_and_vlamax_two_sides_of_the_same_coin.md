# 90: W' and VLamax: Two Sides of the Same Coin?

## Overview and Conceptual Distinction

In performance analysis, coaches and sports scientists frequently evaluate an athlete's ability to operate above threshold using two prominent parameters: **W' (W-prime)** from the Critical Power model and **VLamax (Maximal Glycolytic Rate)** from metabolic simulation models (e.g., Mader, INSCYD).

While both parameters relate to high-intensity capacity beyond the sustainable aerobic threshold, they originate from fundamentally different paradigms:
- **W' (W-prime):** A **mathematical parameter** representing a finite amount of mechanical work (measured in kilojoules, kJ) that can be performed above Critical Power (CP).
- **VLamax:** A **physiological rate metric** representing the maximum rate of lactate production by skeletal muscle glycolysis during all-out effort (measured in mmol/L/second).

---

## 1. Mathematical Model (W') vs. Physiological Measurement (VLamax)

```
        CRITICAL POWER MODEL (W')                      METABOLIC PROFILE (VLamax)
   ┌─────────────────────────────────┐           ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
   │ • Mathematical / Hyperbolic     │           │ • Biological / Physiological    │
   │ • Work capacity in Joules (kJ)  │           │ • Maximum Lactate Rate (mmol/L/s)│
   │ • P = (W' / t) + CP             │           │ • Determined by Glycolytic Power │
   │ • Energetic tank above CP       │           │ • Modulates Threshold & FatMax  │
   └─────────────────────────────────┘           └─────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Critical Power Equation
In the 2-parameter hyperbolic Critical Power model, power output ($P$) for any exhaustive effort lasting between 2 and 20 minutes is expressed as:

$$P = \frac{W'}{t} + CP$$

Where:
- **CP (Critical Power):** The asymptote representing the highest sustainable aerobic power output without continuous fatigue accumulation.
- **W' (W-prime):** The curvature constant representing the total work capacity available above CP, comprising energy from PCr depletion, anaerobic glycolysis, and stored muscle oxygen (myoglobin/hemoglobin).

### The Physiology of VLamax
- **Definition:** Maximal rate of lactate production during maximal short-duration sprints (typically 15–20s all-out test).
- **Function:** Quantifies the glycolytic engine's maximal power. A high VLamax (>0.60–0.90 mmol/L/s) allows explosive sprinting and high anaerobic power, but accelerates carbohydrate combustion at subthreshold intensities, pulling the anaerobic threshold (FTP/CP) downward.

---

## 2. The Interplay: Are They Two Sides of the Same Coin?

While a high VLamax generally correlates with a large W', they are not strictly equivalent:

| Athlete Profile | VLamax Level | W' Size | Critical Power / FTP | Best Competitive Discipline |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pure Time Trialist / Climber** | Low (<0.30–0.40 mmol/L/s) | Moderate / Small (12–18 kJ) | High (% of VO2max is high, 85–90%) | Grand Tour GC, long TTs, mountainous gran fondos |
| **Criterium / Puncheur** | Moderate-High (0.50–0.65 mmol/L/s) | High (20–28 kJ) | Moderate-High | Short steep climbs, breakaway attacks, hilly classics |
| **Track / Road Sprinter** | Very High (>0.75–0.95 mmol/L/s) | Very High (>28–35 kJ) | Lower (% of VO2max is lower, 65–75%) | Bunch sprints, track kilo, short match sprints |

---

## 3. The Physiological Trade-Off: Threshold vs. Anaerobic Capacity

The central paradox in cycling physiology is that **you cannot simultaneously maximize anaerobic threshold and maximal glycolytic power**:

```
High VLamax (Sprinter) ──► High Glycolytic Flux ──► Early Lactate Accumulation ──► LOWER FTP / CP
Low VLamax (Climber/TT) ──► Suppressed Glycolysis ──► High Fat Oxidation ──► HIGHER FTP / CP
```

- **Why a High VLamax Lowers FTP:** At submaximal intensities (e.g., 250W), an athlete with a high VLamax produces more lactate per second than an athlete with a low VLamax. Because lactate production exceeds mitochondrial clearance at lower absolute wattages, the anaerobic threshold occurs at a lower power output.
- **Why a High W' Helps in Modern Racing:** In races with stochastic attacks (e.g., Flemish classics), having a large W' enables repeated surges above CP. Using the dynamic **W' balance (W'bal)** model, an athlete can deplete and recharge W' during sub-CP lulls.

## 4. Testing and Diagnostic Protocols

1. **Testing W':**
   - Requires multiple maximal, exhaustive field or lab efforts (e.g., 3 min, 7 min, and 12 min maximal tests).
   - W' is calculated via linear regression ($Work = W' + CP \times t$).

2. **Testing VLamax:**
   - Requires a standardized 15-to-20-second maximal all-out sprint from a stationary or low-speed start.
   - Capillary blood lactate is sampled pre-sprint and at 1-minute intervals post-sprint (up to 8–10 minutes) to determine peak post-exercise lactate accumulation:
   
$$VLamax = \frac{La_{peak} - La_{pre}}{t_{sprint} - t_{alac}}$$

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **W' is a Work Capacity; VLamax is a Metabolic Rate:** W' measures total energy in Joules available above threshold; VLamax measures how rapidly glycolysis produces lactate.
- **Match Physiology to Race Demands:** Long, steady time trials and long climbs require suppressing VLamax to maximize FTP and fat oxidation; criteriums and short punchy races require a higher VLamax and large W'.
- **Training to Lower VLamax:** High-volume low-intensity base (Zone 2), sweet spot / threshold intervals, and heavy torque cadence drills (SFR).
- **Training to Raise VLamax / W':** Maximal sprint interval training (SIT: 10–20s all-out efforts with full recovery) and heavy resistance strength training.
