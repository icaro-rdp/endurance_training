# 70: Fatmax and Maximal Fat Oxidation (MFO)

## Overview and Physiological Definition

Substrate utilization during endurance exercise is governed by the dynamic interplay between lipid oxidation (free fatty acids and intramuscular triglycerides) and carbohydrate oxidation (muscle/liver glycogen and blood glucose).

This episode examines two fundamental parameters of endurance bioenergetics:
1. **MFO (Maximal Fat Oxidation):** The absolute highest rate of lipid oxidation achieved during exercise, expressed in grams per minute ($\text{g/min}$) or grams per hour ($\text{g/h}$).
2. **Fatmax:** The specific exercise intensity (power output in Watts, % VO2max, or % FTP) at which MFO occurs.

Understanding these metrics allows coaches and athletes to optimize low-intensity endurance training zones, improve metabolic durability, and preserve precious endogenous glycogen stores for decisive race moments.

---

## 1. Bioenergetics of Substrate Oxidation: The Crossover Concept

As exercise intensity increases, the relative contribution of fat oxidation declines while carbohydrate oxidation rises exponentially—a phenomenon established by Brooks and Mercier as the **Crossover Concept**.

```
Substrate Utilization vs. Exercise Intensity
100% ──┐                                         ┌── Carbohydrate Oxidation (%)
       │                                        ╱
       │   [Fatmax]                            ╱
       │      │                               ╱
 50% ──┼──────●── Fat Oxidation Rate (g/min) ╱
       │     ╱ ╲                            ╱
       │    ╱   ╲                          ╱
       │   ╱     ╲                        ╱
  0% ──┴──┴───────┴──────────────────────┴─────── 100% VO2max
       Rest     ~60-70% VO2max          LT2 / FTP
```

### Physiological Determinants of Fat Oxidation
- **Enzymatic Capacity:** Carnitine Palmitoyltransferase-1 (CPT-1) activity (the rate-limiting mitochondrial gatekeeper for long-chain fatty acid transport).
- **Mitochondrial Volume & Capillarization:** Type I slow-twitch muscle fiber density and mitochondrial surface area.
- **Glycolytic Suppression of Lipolysis:** As power increases, accelerated glycolysis produces acetyl-CoA and malonyl-CoA, which directly inhibit CPT-1 and suppress fatty acid entry into the mitochondrial matrix.

## 2. Laboratory Assessment: Indirect Calorimetry Protocols

MFO and Fatmax are measured via open-circuit indirect calorimetry (metabolic cart) during a graded exercise test (GXT).

### Calculating Substrate Oxidation from Gas Exchange
Using stoichiometric equations (Frayn, 1983):
$$\text{Fat Oxidation (g/min)} = 1.67 \times \dot{V}\text{O}_2 - 1.67 \times \dot{V}\text{CO}_2$$
$$\text{Carbohydrate Oxidation (g/min)} = 4.55 \times \dot{V}\text{CO}_2 - 3.21 \times \dot{V}\text{O}_2$$

### Methodological Protocol Guidelines
- **Step Duration:** Steps must be at least **3 to 5 minutes long** to ensure cardiorespiratory and substrate equilibrium at each stage.
- **Pre-Test Standardization:** Tests must be conducted after an overnight fast (10–12 hours) or standardized low-glycemic pre-test meal; acute pre-exercise carbohydrate ingestion elevates insulin, which immediately blunts lipolysis and falsely depresses MFO.

## 3. Typical Values and Athletic Distribution

| Athlete Category | Typical MFO Rate | Fatmax Intensity (% VO2max / % FTP) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Untrained / Sedentary** | $0.20 - 0.40\ \text{g/min}$ ($12 - 24\ \text{g/h}$) | $40 - 50\%\ \text{VO}_2\text{max}$ (~45–55% FTP) |
| **Trained Amateur Cyclist** | $0.50 - 0.75\ \text{g/min}$ ($30 - 45\ \text{g/h}$) | $55 - 65\%\ \text{VO}_2\text{max}$ (~60–70% FTP) |
| **Elite WorldTour / Pro Triathlete** | $1.00 - 1.50+\ \text{g/min}$ ($60 - 90+\ \text{g/h}$) | $65 - 75\%\ \text{VO}_2\text{max}$ (~70–80% FTP) |

### Performance Significance of High MFO
An elite rider oxidizing fat at $1.2\ \text{g/min}$ ($72\ \text{g/h} \approx 650\ \text{kcal/h}$ from fat) at 250 Watts saves substantial intramuscular glycogen. A less metabolically efficient rider at the same 250 Watts might burn predominantly glycogen, exhausting liver and muscle carbohydrate stores within 2 hours.

---

## 4. Training and Nutritional Methods to Improve MFO and Fatmax

1. **Extensive Low-Intensity Volume (Zone 2 Base):** Long continuous riding ($3 - 5+\ \text{hours}$) at or slightly below Fatmax power (~60–70% FTP) upregulates FAT/CD36 transporters and CPT-1 enzyme density.
2. **Carbohydrate Periodization ("Fuel for the Work Required"):** Performing select low-intensity sessions with low exogenous carbohydrate availability (or "sleep-low" protocols) amplifies AMPK and PGC-1alpha transcription.
3. **High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT):** While HIIT burns pure glycogen during intervals, it stimulates mitochondrial biogenesis and enhances overall oxidative enzyme capacity, supporting higher fat oxidation at lower submaximal baselines.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Distinguish MFO from Fatmax:** MFO is the *rate* (how many grams of fat burned per minute), while Fatmax is the *workload/power* (at what wattage that peak burning occurs).
- **Fatmax Usually Aligns with Zone 2 / LT1:** For most well-trained cyclists, Fatmax occurs near the first lactate threshold (LT1), around 60–70% of FTP / 55–65% of VO2max.
- **Standardize Pre-Testing Conditions:** Ingesting carbohydrates prior to metabolic testing spikes insulin, which potently inhibits lipolysis and produces artificially low MFO numbers.
- **High Fat Oxidation Enhances Durability:** Developing a high MFO preserves endogenous glycogen for high-intensity attacks and final climbs in long races.
