# 141: VO2max, Economy, and Thresholds: What Matters Most?

## Overview and Physiological Definition

Endurance performance in cycling is frequently summarized by a single metric—Functional Threshold Power (FTP), Lactate Threshold (LT), or Critical Power (CP). However, physiological thresholds do not exist in isolation; they represent the functional output of three fundamental physiological determinants established in the classical **Joyner-Coyle Model of Endurance Performance**:

1. **Maximal Oxygen Uptake ($\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$):** The maximal rate at which oxygen can be taken up, transported by the cardiovascular system, and consumed by working skeletal muscle.
2. **Fractional Utilization ($\text{\%}\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$ at Threshold):** The percentage of maximal aerobic capacity that an athlete can sustain at steady state without progressive metabolic acidosis.
3. **Gross Mechanical Efficiency / Cycling Economy ($GE$):** The ratio of external mechanical work produced on the pedals to total metabolic energy expended (typically ranging between 18% and 25% in trained cyclists).

This episode reviews recent scientific literature analyzing how these components interact and determines which physiological lever offers the greatest return on investment across different athlete phenotypes and career development phases.

## 1. The Joyner Model Deconstructed: The Three Pillars

### The Bioenergetic Equation of Sustainable Power
The mechanical power output ($P$) produced at the anaerobic threshold can be mathematically expressed as:

$$P_{\text{threshold}} = \dot{V}O_{2\text{max}} \times \%\dot{V}O_{2\text{max@LT2}} \times GE \times k$$

Where $k$ is an energy equivalent constant (~20.9 kJ per liter of $O_2$ consumed under mixed substrate oxidation).

### The Interdependence of Determinants
- **VO2max as the Ceiling:** An athlete cannot maintain an absolute oxygen consumption above their $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$ at steady state. $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$ dictates the absolute aerobic potential.
- **Fractional Utilization as the Exploitation Factor:** Untrained individuals reach LT2 at ~60–70% of $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$, whereas elite cyclists achieve fractional utilization rates of 80–90% of $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$.
- **Efficiency as the Power Multiplier:** Two riders with an identical $\dot{V}O_{2}$ of 4.5 L/min at threshold will produce vastly different power outputs if one operates at 20% efficiency (yielding ~315 W) and the other at 24% efficiency (yielding ~378 W).

## 2. Scientific Evidence: What Truly Drives Threshold Progression?

Recent longitudinal studies evaluate whether changes in threshold power correlate more strongly with shifts in $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$, changes in fractional utilization, or improvements in gross efficiency:

### In Novice and Moderately Trained Cyclists
- **Primary Driver:** **$\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$ Expansion.** In riders with limited endurance training history (<3–5 years), the cardiovascular delivery system (stroke volume, cardiac output) and peripheral mitochondrial density are far from optimized.
- **Training Adaptations:** Both high-volume Zone 2 base training and structured High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT) yield rapid 10–25% improvements in absolute $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$, which directly elevates the entire power-duration curve.

### In Highly Trained and Elite Cyclists
- **Primary Driver:** **Fractional Utilization and Durability.** Elite riders frequently hit a genetic or physiological ceiling for nominal $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$ (e.g., 75–85 mL/kg/min).
- **The Ceiling Effect:** Further increases in threshold power cannot rely on expanding $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$. Instead, performance gains stem from:
  1. Shifting the anaerobic threshold from 80% to 88% of $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$ through extensive threshold intervals, sweet spot density, and glycolytic flux ($V_{La\text{max}}$) reduction.
  2. Preserving high fractional utilization after substantial energy expenditure (durability over 2,000–3,500 kJ).
  3. Incremental gains in mechanical economy via pedaling dynamics, core stability, and optimized neuromuscular recruitment.

## 3. Diagnostic Interpretation and Coaching Pitfalls

### Case Study: The "Stagnant Threshold" Paradox
Coaches often encounter athletes whose $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$ improves following an intense 6-week HIIT block, yet their 40-minute threshold power remains unchanged or even slightly declines.
- **Physiological Mechanism:** The intensive suprathreshold work may have up-regulated glycolytic enzyme activity (increasing $V_{La\text{max}}$), leading to excessive lactate production at submaximal intensities. Consequently, the lactate accumulation threshold shifts leftward even though the maximum aerobic ceiling increased.
- **Coaching Solution:** Follow the $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$ block with an **extensive threshold / Sweet Spot block** combined with high-volume Zone 2 riding to down-regulate excess glycolytic flux and lift fractional utilization back toward 85%.

### Diagnostic Matrix for Training Prioritization

| Athlete Diagnostic Profile | Limiting Factor | Recommended Training Focus | Target Interval Architecture |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High Fractional Utilization (>85%), Low Absolute $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$** | Aerobic Ceiling Bottleneck | $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$ Development | $4 \times 4\text{ min}$ / $5 \times 5\text{ min}$ at 105–115% FTP; $30/15\text{s}$ microbursts |

| **High Aerobic Power, Poor Durability / High Fatigue Decay** | Substrate Efficiency & Capillarization | Zone 2 Volume & Fat Oxidation | Long rides ($3\text{--}5\text{ h}$) with sub-LT1 fueling and late-ride tempo intervals |

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Understand the Three Pillars:** Threshold power is an emergent property resulting from maximal aerobic capacity ($\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$), fractional utilization ($\text{\%}\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$ at LT2), and gross mechanical efficiency ($GE$).
- **Tailor Stimuli to Training Age:** Novice athletes should focus on expanding the aerobic ceiling ($\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$), while mature, highly trained cyclists must emphasize fractional utilization, metabolic clearance, and fatigue resistance.
- **Beware the $V_{La\text{max}}$ Interaction:** Elevated $\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}$ does not instantly yield higher threshold power if accompanied by increased glycolytic flux. Periodize extensive threshold training after aerobic capacity blocks.
- **Efficiency Is Long-Term:** Improvements in gross cycling economy occur slowly over years of high-volume training through neuromuscular optimization and fiber-type shift toward oxidative efficiency.
