# 5: Anaerobic Threshold: Physiology, Lactate Steady State, and Training Protocols

## Overview and Terminology

The term **"Anaerobic Threshold"** is ubiquitous in endurance training, yet frequently misunderstood. In popular sports jargon, it is often mischaracterized as a sudden switch where the body stops using oxygen and transitions exclusively to anaerobic metabolism.

In reality, energy systems operate along a continuous spectrum. Aerobic and anaerobic pathways work simultaneously at all exercise intensities. The anaerobic threshold represents the **Maximal Lactate Steady State ($MLSS$)** or **second lactate threshold ($LT_2$)**—the highest power output or workload where blood lactate production is perfectly balanced by systemic lactate clearance and oxidation.

---

## 1. Physiological Foundations and Lactate Metabolism

### Lactate is a Fuel, Not a Metabolic Waste
- **Lactate Production:** During glycolysis, glucose is broken down into pyruvate. When glycolytic flux exceeds mitochondrial pyruvate entry, pyruvate is converted into lactate by the enzyme lactate dehydrogenase ($LDH$).
- **The Lactate Shuttle (Brooks):** Lactate is not a fatigue-inducing waste toxin. Instead, it is an essential energy intermediate. It is shuttled via monocarboxylate transporters ($MCT\text{-}1$ and $MCT\text{-}4$) from fast-twitch glycolytic fibers to slow-twitch oxidative fibers, heart muscle, and the brain, where it is converted back to pyruvate and oxidized for ATP production.
- **Etiology of Acidosis:** Cellular metabolic acidosis is caused by the accumulation of hydrogen ions ($H^+$) released during ATP hydrolysis, not by lactate itself. Blood lactate concentration serves as an accurate, measurable biomarker of intracellular glycolytic flux and metabolic stress.

### The Three Metabolic Domains
1. **Moderate Intensity Domain ($< LT_1 / VT_1$):**
   - Blood lactate remains at baseline ($1.0\text{--}1.8\text{ mmol/L}$).
   - Sustained by oxidative phosphorylation of fats and carbohydrates; near-zero cardiovascular drift.
2. **Heavy Intensity Domain (Between $LT_1$ and $LT_2 / MLSS$):**
   - Blood lactate elevates above baseline but stabilizes at a new steady state ($2.0\text{--}4.0\text{ mmol/L}$).
   - Increased carbohydrate oxidation and motor unit recruitment; sustainable for 1 to 3 hours.
3. **Severe Intensity Domain ($> LT_2 / MLSS$):**
   - Blood lactate and $VO_2$ rise continuously until $VO_2max$ and exhaustion occur ($VO_2$ slow component).
   - Time to exhaustion is strictly finite ($2\text{--}30\text{ minutes}$) governed by the hyperbolic Critical Power / $W'$ model.

## 2. Threshold Testing Pitfalls: 4.0 mmol/L Fixed vs. Individual MLSS

- **The 4.0 mmol/L Fallacy (Mader/OBLA):** Historically, many commercial fitness tests assigned the anaerobic threshold to a fixed blood lactate value of $4.0\text{ mmol/L}$.
- **Individual Biological Variability:** Scientific research demonstrates that true individual $MLSS$ blood lactate concentrations range from **$2.5\text{ mmol/L}$ to $7.0\text{ mmol/L}$** depending on muscle fiber composition, $VLa_{max}$, and training status.
- **Accurate Identification:** True $LT_2$ is identified on an incremental step test as the point of rapid non-linear curvature in blood lactate, or via multiple 30-minute constant-load validation bouts (where lactate drifts $<1.0\text{ mmol/L}$ across the final 20 minutes).

## 3. Training Protocols to Elevate Anaerobic Threshold

Improving threshold performance involves increasing **Threshold Power Output (Watts)** and extending **Time to Exhaustion ($TTE$) at Threshold**.

### 1. Sustained Steady-State Threshold Intervals
- **Structure:** $2 \times 20\text{ min}$, $3 \times 15\text{ min}$, or $1 \times 40\text{--}50\text{ min}$ continuous at $95\text{--}100\%$ FTP ($LT_2$).
- **Recovery:** $5\text{--}8\text{ min}$ easy spinning between blocks ($1:3$ or $1:4$ work-to-rest ratio).
- **Physiological Adaptation:** Stimulates mitochondrial density, elevates peripheral capillary beds, and optimizes $TTE$ in the heavy domain.

### 2. Over-Under Intervals (Lactate Clearance Dynamics)
- **Structure:** $3\text{--}4$ sets of $(3 \times [2\text{ min at } 105\text{--}110\%\text{ FTP (Over)} / 2\text{ min at } 88\text{--}92\%\text{ FTP (Under)}])$, with $5\text{ min}$ recovery between sets.
- **Physiological Adaptation:** The "Over" surge pushes muscle into the severe domain, flooding fibers with lactate and $H^+$; the "Under" segment forces working Type I fibers to clear and oxidize lactate at near-threshold metabolic rates, training $MCT\text{-}1$ transporter kinetics.

### 3. Polarized Low-Intensity Volume (Zone 2 Base)
- High-volume Zone 2 training expands the mitochondrial oxidative pool and capillarization, increasing baseline systemic lactate clearance capacity without accumulating central autonomic fatigue.

### 4. VO2max Intervals (Expanding the Ceiling)
- When an athlete's threshold reaches $88\text{--}92\%$ of $VO_2max$, the threshold becomes constrained by the aerobic ceiling. Performing high-intensity $VO_2max$ intervals ($4 \times 4\text{ min}$ or $30/15\text{s}$) lifts the ceiling, allowing threshold power to progress further.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Continuous Bioenergetics:** Anaerobic threshold is the dynamic point of maximal lactate steady state, not an absolute switch to anaerobic metabolism.
- **Avoid Generic 4.0 mmol/L Rules:** Individual threshold lactate values vary widely; evaluate lactate inflection curves or power-duration metrics rather than rigid concentration markers.
- **Combine Steady and Over-Under Work:** Use steady intervals to build mental stamina and mitochondrial volume, and over-unders to optimize physiological lactate shuttling and clearance under race conditions.
