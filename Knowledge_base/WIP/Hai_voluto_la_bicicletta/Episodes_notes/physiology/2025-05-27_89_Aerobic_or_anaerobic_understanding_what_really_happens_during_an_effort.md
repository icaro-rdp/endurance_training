# 89: Aerobic or Anaerobic? Understanding What Really Happens During an Effort

## Overview and Bioenergetic Context

A persistent misconception in sports training is that the human body switches between distinct "anaerobic" and "aerobic" compartments depending on exercise duration or intensity. Fitness trackers and commercial smartwatches frequently reinforce this misconception by labeling 3-minute or 5-minute maximal interval sessions as "predominantly anaerobic workouts."

In reality, all three human energy pathways—the **ATP-Phosphocreatine (PCr) system**, the **anaerobic glycolytic system**, and the **aerobic oxidative phosphorylation system**—activate simultaneously from the very first pedal stroke. The relative percentage contribution of each pathway shifts along a continuous dynamic spectrum determined by effort duration, intensity, and metabolic kinetics.

## 1. The Three Bioenergetic Systems and Their Kinetics

```
Energy Contribution (%)
100 % ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
      │\   ATP-PCr
      │ \   System
      │   50 % │───\───────────── Aerobic / Oxidative System ───────────────
      │    \   Anaerobic     /
      │     \  Glycolytic   /
      │      \ System      /
  0 % └───────┴───────────┴────────────────────────────────────────►
      0 sec   10 sec     60-75 sec (50/50 Crossover)       3-5 min+
```

### The Three Interconnected Pathways

1. **Phosphagen System (ATP-PCr):**
   - **Rate:** Extremely rapid (highest maximal power / ATP turnover rate).
   - **Capacity:** Extremely limited (stores exhausted in ~5 to 10 seconds of maximal sprinting).
   - **Substrate:** Intramuscular ATP and Phosphocreatine via the creatine kinase reaction.

2. **Anaerobic Glycolytic System (Glycolysis to Lactate):**
   - **Rate:** High (fast ATP regeneration without requiring immediate oxygen).
   - **Capacity:** Moderate (~30 to 90 seconds of maximal effort).
   - **Substrate:** Muscle glycogen and glucose broken down to pyruvate, generating lactate and metabolic protons (H+).

3. **Aerobic Oxidative System (Mitochondrial Respiration):**
   - **Rate:** Slower ramp-up due to oxygen transport and enzyme kinetics (Phase II VO2 kinetics take ~60–90 seconds to reach steady-state).
   - **Capacity:** Virtually unlimited (utilizing glycogen, glucose, intramuscular triglycerides, and free fatty acids).

## 2. The Aerobic-Anaerobic Crossover Timeline

Scientific research using isotopic tracers, respiratory gas analysis, and muscle biopsy data demonstrates that the aerobic system dominates energetic contribution much earlier than commonly assumed:

| Effort Duration | Anaerobic Contribution (ATP-PCr + Glycolysis) | Aerobic Contribution (Oxidative Phosphorylation) | Dominant Physiological Pathway |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **10 seconds** (Maximal Sprint) | ~85–90% | ~10–15% | Phosphagen (PCr) + Fast Glycolysis |
| **30 seconds** (e.g., Wingate Test) | ~55–60% | ~40–45% | Glycolytic with rapidly rising Aerobic |
| **60–75 seconds** (1-minute Attack) | **~50%** | **~50%** | **The Exact 50/50 Energetic Crossover** |
| **2 minutes** (e.g., Short Berg Climb) | ~30–35% | ~65–70% | **Predominantly Aerobic** |
| **3 minutes** (e.g., VO2max interval) | ~15–20% | ~80–85% | **Overwhelmingly Aerobic** |
| **5 minutes** (e.g., Standard Long Interval)| ~5–10% | ~90–95% | **Almost Exclusively Aerobic** |

## 3. Bioenergetics of Intermittent Micro-Intervals (30/15s and 40/20s)

Intermittent high-intensity protocols (e.g., 3 sets of 10 x 30s at 120% FTP with 15s recovery) are often erroneously described as "anaerobic training." 

### The Intermittent Paradox
- **First 2–3 Repetitions:** Heavy initial reliance on anaerobic glycolysis and PCr depletion.
- **Subsequent Repetitions (Reps 4 to 10):** Because the 15-second recovery window is too short to allow significant glycolytic clearance or full PCr resynthesis, the body is forced to rely almost entirely on **maximal aerobic oxidative phosphorylation (VO2max)** to sustain the mechanical workload.
- **Outcome:** The athlete accumulates extended time at >90–95% VO2max while exercising at power outputs significantly above critical power, training both central cardiac stroke volume and peripheral oxygen extraction.

---

## 4. Coaching Implications and Training Fallacies

- **The "Anaerobic Interval" Myth:** Prescribing 3-to-5-minute efforts to "train the anaerobic system" is a physiological error; these efforts are classic aerobic VO2max stimulants. True anaerobic capacity is trained with maximal efforts under 30–45 seconds with long recovery (1:5 to 1:10 work-to-rest ratio).
- **Beware Smartwatch Classifications:** Automated algorithms that label workouts as "anaerobic" simply based on high peak heart rate or sudden power spikes ignore real muscle bioenergetic kinetics.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Aerobic Dominance Occurs at 60–75 Seconds:** Any all-out effort lasting longer than 1 minute relies primarily on aerobic oxidative metabolism.
- **5-Minute Power is 90%+ Aerobic:** A 5-minute maximal climb is not an anaerobic test; it reflects maximal aerobic power (MAP) and VO2max.
- **True Anaerobic Intervals Require Long Rest:** To train glycolytic capacity or sprinting without aerobic compensation, keep efforts short (10–30s) and recovery long (2–5 minutes) to allow partial PCr restoration.
- **Intermittent Surges Train the Aerobic Engine:** Protocols like 30/15 or 40/20 utilize short bursts to force the cardiovascular and mitochondrial systems to operate at near-maximal VO2.
