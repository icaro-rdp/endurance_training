# 129: Over-Under Intervals: Lactate Clearance Dynamics and Threshold Accuracy

## Overview and Physiological/Training Context

**Over-Under intervals**—alternating between power outputs slightly above anaerobic threshold (the "Over" phase) and slightly below threshold (the "Under" phase)—are widely prescribed in endurance cycling. Frequently marketed as a magical protocol to "train lactate clearance" or "flush acid from the legs," over-unders are often misunderstood both physiologically and practically.

In this episode, Daniele Bazzana and Stefano Nardelli examine the true cellular and systemic mechanisms governing lactate kinetics. They debunk popular misconceptions regarding lactate clearance, explain the critical role of **Monocarboxylate Transporters (MCT1 and MCT4)**, and analyze the single most common coaching mistake: setting over-under intensities from an **overestimated Functional Threshold Power (FTP)**, which transforms a sustainable metabolic workout into a catastrophic failure.

---

## 1. Lactate Physiology: Fuel, Shuttle, and Clearance Mechanisms

To understand over-under training, one must dispel the persistent myth that lactate is a toxic metabolic waste product:

- **Lactate as an Energetic Substrate:** Lactate is an essential carbohydrate fuel produced by glycolysis. Under high metabolic rates, pyruvate production exceeds mitochondrial pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH) flux, converting pyruvate to lactate via Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH) and regenerating cytosolic $NAD^+$ to sustain glycolysis.
- **The Cell-to-Cell Lactate Shuttle:** Lactate produced in glycolytic Type II fast-twitch fibers is exported into the interstitium and bloodstream via **MCT4 transporters** and imported into oxidative Type I slow-twitch fibers, the myocardium, and the liver (Cori cycle) via **MCT1 transporters**, where it is converted back to pyruvate and oxidized in the Krebs cycle.
- **Clearance Dynamics:** Lactate clearance is maximal at sub-threshold aerobic intensities (Zone 2 / low Zone 3, $\sim 60-75\%\ \text{VO}_2\text{max}$), where high capillary perfusion and oxidative fiber respiration are elevated.

---

## 2. The Over-Under Mechanism: Why the "Under" Phase is Decisive

During an over-under session:

1. **The "Over" Phase (e.g., 105–115% FTP / Above Critical Power):**
   - Exercise enters the **Severe Intensity Domain**.
   - Anaerobic glycolysis surges, increasing intracellular lactate and $H^+$ concentrations and partially drawing down the anaerobic work capacity ($W'$).
2. **The "Under" Phase (e.g., 85–92% FTP / Below Critical Power / Heavy Domain):**
   - Exercise returns to the **Heavy Intensity Domain**.
   - Power is low enough that net lactate production rate falls below maximal oxidative clearance rate.
   - Oxidative Type I fibers actively consume and oxidize the circulating lactate generated during the Over phase while sustaining high mechanical work.

## 3. The Danger of Overestimating the Threshold (FTP)

The primary reason athletes fail over-under workouts is an **inaccurate, inflated FTP anchor**:

- **The Ramp / 20-Min Test Trap:** Standard 20-minute tests ($95\%$ of 20-min power) or automated ramp tests often overestimate true Maximal Lactate Steady State (MLSS) and Critical Power (CP) by 15 to 30 Watts in riders with high anaerobic capacity ($W'$ or high VLamax).
- **The Physiological Catastrophe:** If an athlete's true MLSS is 280 W, but their software sets FTP at 310 W:
  - Prescribed "Under" @ 90% of 310 W = **279 W** (right at the boundary of failure).
  - Prescribed "Over" @ 110% of 310 W = **341 W** (deep in the severe domain).
  - In reality, the "Under" phase never allows net lactate clearance or $W'$ reconstitution. Intracellular $H^+$, inorganic phosphate ($P_i$), and ADP accumulate relentlessly, forcing the athlete to blow up after 1–2 repetitions.

## 4. Practical Over-Under Interval Protocols

### A. Classic Macro Over-Unders (Sustained Blocks)
- **Structure:** 3 sets of 10 to 12 minutes total work per set.
- **Pattern:** Repeating $[2\text{ min @ 88-92\% FTP (Under)} / 1\text{ min @ 108-112\% FTP (Over)}]$ for 3–4 cycles per set.
- **Recovery:** 5 minutes easy spinning between sets.
- **Target:** Enhances MCT1/MCT4 transporter density, mental resilience, and race-specific surges on climbs.

### B. Micro Over-Unders (Dynamic Surges)
- **Structure:** 2–3 sets of 8 to 10 minutes.
- **Pattern:** Repeating $[40\text{s @ 90\% FTP (Under)} / 20\text{s @ 120\% FTP (Over)}]$.
- **Target:** Simulates criterium and road race breakaways where short surges are punctuated by high-tempo cruising.

---

## 5. Coaching Diagnostics: Validating Threshold Accuracy

A properly calibrated over-under session provides immediate diagnostic feedback:

- **Successful Calibration:** The athlete feels the burn during the Over phase, but distinctly perceives a slight muscular easing and respiratory stabilization during the second minute of the Under phase.
- **Failed Calibration (Overestimated):** The athlete feels progressively worse during the Under phase, with heart rate continuing to climb upward (cardiac drift) and legs locking up with acidity, indicating that the Under is positioned above MLSS.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **The "Under" is Where the Magic Happens:** Over-unders do not train clearance during the hard part; clearance occurs during the high-power Under phase when oxidative fibers consume lactate as fuel.
- **Calibrate Strictly to True MLSS/CP:** Never use an inflated 20-minute or ramp test FTP for over-unders. If in doubt, calibrate the Under phase to 85–88% FTP.
- **Perception Must Stabilize:** If heart rate and leg fatigue continue to spiral upward during the Under phase, immediately reduce target power by 10–15 Watts.
- **Specific Preparation for Racing:** Over-unders are ideal pre-competition workouts because real road racing consists of surges above threshold followed by high-speed pack cruising.
