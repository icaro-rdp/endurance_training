# 87: The Science of Victory in the Monument Classics

## Overview and Physiological Demands

Cycling's five Monument Classics—**Milano-Sanremo**, **Ronde van Vlaanderen (Tour of Flanders)**, **Paris-Roubaix**, **Liège-Bastogne-Liège**, and **Il Lombardia**—represent the pinnacle of single-day endurance performance. Spanning 250 to 300 kilometers with race durations of 6 to 7.5 hours, these events require a unique combination of extreme aerobic capacity, exceptional metabolic durability (fatigue resistance), massive total energy expenditure, and high-power anaerobic repeatability after thousands of kilojoules have been burned.

Winning a Monument is not determined by an athlete's fresh, rested power-duration curve; it is decided by the power an athlete can produce **after expending 4,000 to 5,500 kilojoules (kJ)** of mechanical work.

---

## 1. Comparative Analysis of the Five Monuments

Each Monument presents distinct topographical, mechanical, and pacing characteristics that shape physiological demands:

```
[ Milano-Sanremo ]    ──> Ultra-High Speed, Low NP/AP Ratio, High Nervous Tension, Explosive 6-min Aerobic Finale (Poggio)
[ Ronde van Vlaanderen ] ──> Highly Stochastic, Massive NP/AP Spread, Steep Cobbled Bergs, Repeated Anaerobic W' Depletions
[ Paris-Roubaix ]      ──> Sustained Flat High Torque, Low NP/AP Spread, Severe Mechanical Vibration Stress, Relentless Zone 3/4
[ Liège-Bastogne-Liège ] ──> High Total Elevation Gain (>4,000m), Sustained 3-8 min Severe Domain Climbs, High W/kg Demands
[ Il Lombardia ]       ──> Pure Climbers' Monument, Long Alpine Passes (>15-30 min), Sustained Threshold Density in Autumn
```

### Detailed Course Demands

| Monument | Distance / Elevation | Work Profile | Normalized vs. Average Power | Decisive Effort Profile |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Milano-Sanremo** | ~295 km / ~2,000 m | Long steady aerobic draft; extreme final 30 km | Closest NP to AP until the Cipressa/Poggio | 4–6 min at >6.5–7.5 W/kg on the Poggio after 280 km |
| **Tour of Flanders** | ~270 km / ~2,500 m | Highly stochastic; 18–20 short steep cobbled climbs | Very large NP/AP spread (>50–70 W differential) | Repeated 1–3 min bursts at >7.0–8.5 W/kg (Kwaremont/Paterberg) |
| **Paris-Roubaix** | ~260 km / ~1,500 m | Flat, extreme rolling resistance & vibrations; ~55 km cobbles | High steady AP and NP; minimal coasting time | Sustained 350–420 W on flat sectors; relentless high torque |
| **Liège-Bastogne-Liège** | ~260 km / ~4,400 m | Continuous medium climbs in the Ardennes | Large NP spread; high cumulative climbing load | 3–6 min efforts at >6.5–7.2 W/kg (La Redoute, Roche-aux-Faucons) |
| **Il Lombardia** | ~240 km / ~4,500 m | Long continuous mountain passes (Madonna del Ghisallo, Sormano) | Mountainous profile; high sustained threshold density | 15–30 min ascents at >5.8–6.3 W/kg late in the race |

## 2. Energetics and Intra-Race Fueling

The metabolic demands of a 6-to-7-hour Monument are immense:

- **Total Energy Expenditure:** Cyclists expend **4,500 to 6,500 kJ** of mechanical work (translating to a metabolic expenditure of 5,500 to >7,500 kcal given ~22–24% human gross efficiency).
- **Carbohydrate Combustion Rates:** In modern WorldTour racing, where average speeds exceed 43–45 km/h, athletes oxidize carbohydrates at rates of 3.0 to 4.5 g/min during high-intensity phases.
- **Intra-Race Fueling Architecture:** Winning performances require gut-trained carbohydrate intake of **100 to 120 g/hour** (using optimal 1:0.8 or 2:1 glucose-to-fructose hydrogel/maltodextrin formulations) combined with meticulous fluid and sodium replacement (500–1000 mg/h sodium).

## 3. Durability: Power-Duration Curve Degradation

The critical physiological differentiator in Monuments is **Durability**—the resistance of the power-duration relationship to fatigue:

```
Power (Watts)
  ▲
  │   Fresh / Rested Curve
  │   ══════════════════════════════════════
  │
  │   Post-4000 kJ Fatigued Curve (Elite Winner: Minimal Drop <5-8%)
  │   - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  │
  │   Post-4000 kJ Fatigued Curve (Sub-Elite: Severe Drop >15-25%)
  │   ......................................
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────► Duration (Seconds / Minutes)
```

- **Fresh vs. Fatigued MMP:** An amateur or sub-elite rider may produce 7.0 W/kg for 5 minutes when fresh. However, after 4,000 kJ, their capacity drops by 20–30%. In contrast, Monument winners experience less than a 5–8% decrement in 5-minute maximal power after 5,000 kJ.
- **Physiological Drivers of Durability:** High slow-twitch (Type I) muscle fiber percentage, extraordinary capillary density, high mitochondrial volume, superior fat oxidation capacity at subthreshold intensities (sparing intramuscular glycogen), and high motor unit fatigue resistance.

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Durability Decides the Classics:** Peak fresh wattage is irrelevant if it cannot be expressed after burning 4,000+ kilojoules of energy.
- **Fueling is Non-Negotiable:** Consuming 100–120 g/h of carbohydrates is required to preserve glycogen stores for the race-defining attacks in the final hour.
- **Match Training to Event Stochasticity:** Prepare for Flanders with repeated high-torque anaerobic micro-surges (W' depletion/repletion drills); prepare for Roubaix with sustained, high-torque Zone 3/4 flat power endurance.
- **Train the Fatigued State:** Structure training sessions that place high-intensity threshold or VO2max intervals at the end of 4-to-5-hour endurance rides.
