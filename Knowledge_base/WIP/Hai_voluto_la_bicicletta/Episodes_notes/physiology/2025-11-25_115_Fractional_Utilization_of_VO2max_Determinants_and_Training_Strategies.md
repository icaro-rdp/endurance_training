# 115: Fractional Utilization of VO2max: Determinants and Training Strategies

## Overview and Physiological Context

In the classical Joyner & Coyle model of endurance performance, three physiological determinants establish an endurance athlete's capacity:
1. **Maximal Oxygen Uptake ($	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$):** The maximal rate of oxygen transport and consumption (the "engine size").
2. **Fractional Utilization ($\% 	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$):** The percentage of $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ that can be sustained continuously at the second lactate threshold (LT2 / MLSS / FTP) without continuous blood lactate accumulation.
3. **Gross Mechanical Efficiency / Economy:** The mechanical power output produced per unit of metabolic energy consumed.

While $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ establishes the upper ceiling, **fractional utilization** dictates how much of that aerobic potential is actually usable over prolonged durations. This episode analyzes the physiology of fractional utilization, reviews recent scientific publications (Odden 2024, Johansen 2025), and details how to train this parameter directly and indirectly.

---

## 1. Physiological Determinants of Fractional Utilization

Fractional utilization reflects the metabolic balance between oxidative and glycolytic energy pathways:

- **Typical Values Across Populations:**
  - *Untrained Individuals:* $50	ext{--}65\% 	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ at LT2.
  - *Trained Endurance Athletes:* $70	ext{--}80\% 	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ at LT2.
  - *World-Class Elite Cyclists:* $85	ext{--}92\% 	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ at LT2.
- **Peripheral Biological Drivers:**
  - *Mitochondrial Density & Enzyme Kinetics:* High concentrations of oxidative enzymes (citrate synthase, succinate dehydrogenase) allow high rates of pyruvate oxidation, delaying reliance on anaerobic glycolysis.
  - *Capillary Diffusion Capacity:* High microvascular capillarization ensures adequate oxygen transit time and rapid substrate exchange.
  - *Lactate Shuttling Capacity:* Elevated MCT1 transporter abundance enables rapid uptake and combustion of lactate in Type I fibers.
  - *Low Glycolytic Power ($	ext{VLamax}$):* A lower $	ext{VLamax}$ reduces lactate production at submaximal intensities, shifting the threshold to a higher percentage of $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$.

---

## 2. Reviewing Recent Scientific Studies: Odden (2024) and Johansen (2025)

The hosts analyze recent sports science investigations exploring the trainability and independence of fractional utilization:

- **Odden et al. (2024) & Johansen et al. (2025):** These studies examined whether fractional utilization can be trained independently of changes in $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$, evaluating high-volume low-intensity training versus threshold-centric training blocks.
- **Key Findings:**
  - In highly trained cyclists whose $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ is near its genetic ceiling, performance improvements occur almost entirely through increases in fractional utilization and durability.
  - High-intensity interval training (HIIT) increases the ceiling ($	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$), while extensive sub-threshold and threshold training expands the fractional utilization beneath that ceiling.
  - There is a physiological ceiling effect: fractional utilization rarely exceeds $90	ext{--}92\% 	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ because some aerobic reserve is required to clear baseline metabolic flux.

## 3. Direct vs. Indirect Training Stimuli

To optimize fractional utilization, coaches utilize two complementary approaches:

### A. Direct Stimuli (Pushing Fractional Utilization Upward)
Target the peripheral cellular machinery directly at or just below LT2:
1. **Extensive Threshold Intervals:**
   - *Example:* $3 	imes 15	ext{--}20 	ext{ min}$ at $95	ext{--}100\%$ FTP.
   - *Adaptation:* Drives continuous high flux through the mitochondrial respiratory chain under stable acid-base conditions, expanding time-to-exhaustion.
2. **Sweet Spot / Extensive Tempo Blocks:**
   - *Example:* $2 	imes 30	ext{--}45 	ext{ min}$ at $85	ext{--}92\%$ FTP.
   - *Adaptation:* Stimulates mitochondrial biogenesis and fat oxidation kinetics with lower autonomic stress than continuous threshold work.

### B. Indirect Stimuli (Raising the $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ Ceiling)
When fractional utilization reaches high levels ($pprox 85	ext{--}90\%$) and plateaus, further threshold gains are constrained by the aerobic ceiling.
- **Solution:** Execute a dedicated 3–4 week $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ block (e.g., $4 	imes 4 	ext{ min}$ or $30/15$ HIIT) to elevate maximal aerobic power, creating headroom for subsequent threshold expansion.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Fractional Utilization Defines Usable Power:** A high $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ is meaningless in endurance events if fractional utilization is low; maximizing $\% 	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ at threshold is the primary driver of performance in trained athletes.
- **Balance the Ceiling and the Floor:** When threshold plateaus near $85	ext{--}90\%$ of $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$, lift the ceiling with a focused $	ext{VO}_2	ext{max}$ block before resuming threshold work.
- **Use Extensive Intervals to Build Utilization:** Prescribe long threshold intervals ($2 	imes 20	ext{ min}$ or $3 	imes 15	ext{ min}$) to maximize peripheral mitochondrial adaptations and time-in-zone.
- **Phenotypic Adaptation:** Slow-twitch diesel riders naturally achieve high fractional utilization; fast-twitch sprinters require careful sub-threshold work to lower $	ext{VLamax}$ without blunting explosive power.
