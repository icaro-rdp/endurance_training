# 83: Supplements and Cycling: Which Ones Really Work? (Part 2)

## Overview and Physiological Context

Building upon the Tier 1 buffers and stimulants covered in Part 1, this episode investigates advanced metabolic compounds, vascular modulators, hyperhydration strategies, and clinical micronutrients. 

In modern endurance cycling, nutritional periodization integrates compounds that modify the oxygen cost of exercise, expand circulating plasma volume, or support post-exercise cellular recovery. Concurrently, identifying widely marketed supplements that offer minimal physiological value protects athletes from unnecessary financial expenditure and gastrointestinal distress.

---

## 1. Dietary Nitrates (Beetroot Juice)

Dietary inorganic nitrate (NO3-) represents one of the few nutritional interventions proven to reduce the oxygen cost of submaximal exercise.

```
Dietary Nitrate (NO3-) ──[Oral Bacteria]──> Nitrite (NO2-) ──[Gastric & Tissue Acidosis]──> Nitric Oxide (NO)
                                                                                                  │
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────┐
   ▼                                                                                                         ▼
Vasodilation & Microvascular Perfusion                                                   Enhanced Mitochondrial P/O Ratio
(Enhanced capillary transit time & O2 delivery)                                         (Reduced ATP and O2 cost of work)
```

### Physiological Mechanisms
1. **The Nitrate-Nitrite-Nitric Oxide Pathway:** Ingested nitrate (NO3-) is concentrated in saliva and converted to nitrite (NO2-) by commensal oral anaerobic bacteria on the tongue. In acidic or hypoxic conditions, nitrite is reduced systemically to **Nitric Oxide (NO)**.
2. **Mitochondrial Efficiency:** NO optimizes mitochondrial respiration by reducing proton leakage across the inner mitochondrial membrane, thereby increasing the P/O ratio (amount of ATP generated per molecule of oxygen consumed).
3. **Reduced Oxygen Cost:** Cyclists perform identical submaximal workloads (Zone 2/Zone 3) with a 3–5% reduction in whole-body VO2.

### Protocol and Responder vs. Non-Responder Dynamics
- **Dosage:** 400 to 800 mg (~6 to 12 mmol) of inorganic nitrate, typically supplied as 1–2 concentrated beetroot juice shots (e.g., 70–140 mL Beet It Sport).
- **Timing:** Ingested 2.5 to 3.5 hours prior to competition or training, allowing plasma nitrite concentrations to peak.
- **Elite vs. Amateur Differences:** Recreationally trained cyclists often display larger relative improvements than world-class professionals. Elite cyclists already possess maximal baseline skeletal muscle capillarization, high baseline endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) activity, and optimized mitochondrial enzyme density.
- **Critical Caution:** Do not use antibacterial mouthwash or chewing gum, as this destroys oral nitrate-reducing bacteria and completely blocks the ergogenic effect.

## 2. Exogenous Ketone Esters

Exogenous ketones—primarily (R)-3-hydroxybutyl (R)-3-hydroxybutyrate—have generated immense interest across professional WorldTour peloton environments.

### Acute Exercise vs. Recovery Dynamics
- **During Exercise (Substrate Sparing vs. Glycolytic Suppression):**
  - Ketone ingestion elevates circulating beta-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) to 1.5–3.0 mmol/L without requiring a low-carbohydrate ketogenic diet.
  - While BHB serves as an alternative oxidative fuel for cardiac and skeletal muscle tissue, high circulating ketone levels can downregulate pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH), impairing maximal glycolytic flux required for high-intensity attacks and sprint finishes.
  - Ketones must never replace carbohydrate intake; they should only be co-ingested alongside standard high-carbohydrate fueling (90–120 g/h).

- **Post-Exercise Recovery and Multi-Day Stage Races:**
  - **Glycogen Resynthesis:** Co-ingestion of ketone esters with carbohydrate post-exercise accelerates muscle glycogen resynthesis.
  - **Anabolic Signaling:** Upregulates mTORC1 signaling and stimulates post-exercise muscular protein synthesis.
  - **Anti-Catabolic & EPO Modulation:** Research demonstrates increased nocturnal endogenous Erythropoietin (EPO) concentrations and attenuated symptoms of non-functional overreaching during multi-week stage blocks.
  - **Cognitive Preservation:** Preserves cognitive function, reaction time, and decision-making during severe mental and physical exhaustion.

## 3. Glycerol and Hyperhydration Strategies

During endurance events conducted under severe environmental heat stress (temperatures >30°C), dehydration exceeding 2–3% body mass impairs cardiac output and elevates core body temperature.

- **Mechanism:** Glycerol distributes evenly throughout total body water compartments and temporarily reduces free water clearance by the kidneys.
- **Hyperhydration Protocol:** Ingesting 1.0 to 1.2 g/kg glycerol combined with 20–25 mL/kg fluid 2 to 3 hours prior to competition expands circulating plasma volume and total body water by 600–1000 mL.
- **Benefits:** Delays thermal strain, maintains stroke volume, and reduces cardiovascular drift during hot endurance races.

## 4. Micronutrient Biomarkers: Iron, Vitamin D, and Omega-3

Micronutrient supplementation must be governed by objective diagnostic blood biomarker testing rather than speculative over-the-counter consumption.

```
[ Blood Biomarker Screening ] ──> [ Targeted Micronutrient Correction ] ──> [ Periodic Re-Testing ]
  • Serum Ferritin & Hb             • Iron + Vitamin C (Hepcidin window)     • Avoid toxicity / iron overload
  • 25(OH) Vitamin D                • 2000-4000 IU/day D3                    • Maintain clinical homeostasis
  • Omega-3 Index                   • High-EPA/DHA Fish Oil                  • Optimize membrane fluidity
```

1. **Iron & Ferritin (Oxygen Transport Architecture):**
   - **Biomarkers:** Serum ferritin (<30–50 mcg/L in endurance athletes indicates non-anemic iron deficiency) and transferrin saturation.
   - **The Hepcidin Window:** Exercise-induced inflammation causes IL-6 spikes that elevate **hepcidin**, blocking intestinal iron absorption for 3–6 hours post-exercise. Iron supplements must be taken in the morning on an empty stomach with Vitamin C, away from high-intensity training sessions.

2. **Vitamin D3:**
   - Essential for musculoskeletal remodeling, innate immune defense, and steroid hormone synthesis. Endurance athletes training indoors or in northern latitudes frequently display serum 25(OH)D <30 ng/mL, requiring 2000–4000 IU/day D3 maintenance.

3. **Omega-3 Fatty Acids (EPA/DHA):**
   - Modulates inflammatory resolution cascades and enhances erythrocyte membrane deformability, improving capillary microcirculation.

## 5. Overhyped Supplements with Low Empirical Evidence

- **Branched-Chain Amino Acids (BCAA):** Entirely redundant if total daily dietary protein intake is adequate (1.6–2.0 g/kg/day); complete protein sources (whey, milk, eggs, meat) provide superior leucine thresholds and essential amino acid balance.
- **L-Carnitine:** Muscle uptake is extremely limited without massive co-ingestion of high-glycemic carbohydrates; oral bioavailability is under 15%.
- **Glutamine:** Rapidly metabolized by enterocytes in the gut; confers minimal systemic recovery benefits for well-nourished endurance athletes.

---

## Key Takeaways and Practical Recommendations

- **Beetroot Juice Timing is Critical:** Ingest 400–800 mg nitrates 2.5–3.5 hours before competition. Never use antibacterial mouthwash when consuming nitrates.
- **Ketone Esters Excel in Recovery:** The strongest evidence for exogenous ketones is post-exercise and during multi-day stage races to accelerate glycogen resynthesis and blunt systemic overreaching.
- **Test Before Supplementing Iron:** Never ingest supplemental iron without a complete blood count and serum ferritin panel; excess iron is toxic and acts as a powerful pro-oxidant.
- **Prioritize Evidence Over Marketing:** Allocate nutritional budgets toward proven staples (carbohydrate gels, hydration mixes, caffeine, recovery proteins) before investing in low-efficacy compounds.
