# New Study: MCT Oil Increases EPO concentrations - Endurance Ramifications

My first blog post here discussed a fairly recent paper that found Ketone Ester ingestion to increase EPO concentration. This has started a whole new field of study around the use of ketones to increase EPO, which may enhance endurance performance. Well, a [new study](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/abs/10.1152/ajpendo.00415.2024?casa_token=hQiryIp7aqEAAAAA:dpEhaBsGaK2ZO0vgjUG7CDy7Eisz6emS31b0dKLvlnXyT4n2Xz1LurVQLccKFzLlJnIrPaNGKlAk-g) has demonstrated C8 MCT oil to do the exact same thing, increase EPO production substantially (by 38%!). I’m going to outline why this is such an encouraging finding in this blog, in addition to outlining how you can use this to support your own endurance performance.

## Why Does Increasing EPO Concentration Matter?

Erythropoietin (EPO) is a hormone produced in the bone marrow that is responsible for the formation of red blood cells. Having given 3 lectures in 2025 already around blood physiology i’m almost sick of discussing this, but red blood cells form around 45% of total blood volume (usually between 37-49%). 

Importantly in endurance exercise, red blood cells are responsible for (amongst other things), transporting oxygen around the body. For an endurance athlete, your oxygen carrying capacity is a key performance metric (termed VO2max, standing for the maximum volume of oxygen that can be transported around the body). 

Put simply, the more oxygen you can get to the muscles, the greater your capacity to respire aerobically, producing energy to fuel the exercise you are doing. In real terms and oversimplified slightly, the more oxygen you can get to the muscles, the more watts you can maintain, or the faster pace you can maintain in aerobic conditions.

This is why[ Tour de France cyclists back in the late 90’s would do anything to increase their red blood cell count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doping_at_the_Tour_de_France), from taking EPO injections, to simply blood doping (increasing total blood content). Now drug testing is more rigorous this has become less common, though still certainly takes place.

## What Did This Study Find?

The study found [here](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/abs/10.1152/ajpendo.00415.2024?casa_token=hQiryIp7aqEAAAAA:dpEhaBsGaK2ZO0vgjUG7CDy7Eisz6emS31b0dKLvlnXyT4n2Xz1LurVQLccKFzLlJnIrPaNGKlAk-g), demonstrated a 38% increase in EPO concentrations. This would be expected to increase red blood cell volume, and therefore increase oxygen carrying capacity of the blood. 

This may be through the same mechanism that ketone esters increase EPO (though the mechanism isn’t clear, ketones are likely to mediate this response), as C8 MCT oil is known to increase ketone bodies. What is most encouraging about this study is that this 38% value was following a morning fast, suggesting sustained elevations in EPO when consuming C8 MCT oil, rather than just following ingestion. With this occurring within 8 days of taking the supplement.

This is especially exciting since Ketone Esters are prohibitively expensive, whereas [C8 MCT oil ](https://shop.ketosource.co.uk/products/pure-c8-mct-oil)is reasonably priced, making it possibly for most people to consume this and realise the potential benefits.

## What was the protocol?

The protocol was as follows:

2 x 10g on days 1 - 2 

2 x 20g on days 3 - 5 

2 x 30g on days 6 - 7

**Now to caveat quickly, consuming C8 MCT oil can cause GI distress, so it’s important to increase the dose slowly. This is likely why there is a gradual progression here in dose. Do not jump straight into 30g/day x2!!**

## Caveats

It’s important to note here that whilst this is a very encouraging study, the study didn’t measure red blood cell mass, so whilst increasing EPO concentration should result in increased red blood cell mass, it wasn’t direclty measured.

This was also a short term study, so whether this would continue is again, not known. Though I would be surprised if the effect stops completely.

Finally, this was not done on athletes, and there weren’t performance tests, so maybe some individuals would respond differently, we can’t be totally sure.
