# Strength Training for Endurance Cyclists: Practical Guidelines

**Source:** knowledgeiswatt.substack.com/p/42-strength-training-for-endurance **Date:** November 8,

In the previous KisW Research Notes we saw that heavy gym strength training improves cycling 1s W/kg
and 40min W/Kg as well as durability both in pro and amateur cyclists. We also saw that unilateral
exercises enhance cycling performance more when compared to bilateral ones (Read More Here).

In this KisW Research Note we summarize the practical guidelines (based on the results of scientific
studies) given by Prof. Bent Rønnestad and Prof. Inigo Mujika about how to implement strength
training with endurance cyclists in an influential review published on Scandinavian Journal of
Medicine & Science in Sports in 2014.

I’ve added a short comment for each guideline to give context and to make understand better the
principle they suggested

WHAT DID THEY SUGGEST?

1. INVOLVE CYCLING SPECIFIC MUSCLE GROUPS AND IMITATE PEDALLING

MOVEMENTS

This is for training specificity principle which states:

‘Closer the training routine to the desired outcome, better the performance result.’

Exercises that can mimic well cycling movements are: single leg squat, single leg leg press, single
leg lunge. On the other hand, leg extension is less similar to the pedalling movement. To further
follow the specificity principle, the above cited exercise can be performed trying to replicate the
same range of movement of pedalling. So, moving the knee from almost full-extension to 90 degrees.
In other words it is not necessary to go for ass-to-grass when doing the squat.

2. BEGIN IMMEDIATELY JUST AT THE END OF THE SEASON

In this way there is more time to focus on strength training and it is less likely to have to manage
the gym schedule with concurrent high load and/or high intensity of training on the bike, which
could be tricky or counterproductive in some scenario.

3. AT THE BEGINNING IT IS COMMON TO FEEL HEAVY AND SORE LEGS THE FIRST

DAYS AFTER A GYM STRENGTH SESSIONS

And this is a good reason to follow point 2. In fact, DOMS (Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness, that is
feeling sore legs 1-3 days after hitting the gym) is something that usually tends to improve after
having some experience in lifting.

4. FIRST DEVELOP A PROPER LIFTING TECHNIQUE WITH LIGHTER LOADS

This is very important to avoid injuries. So, at the very beginning you could start with light
weight or just bodyweight and keep some repetitions in reserve (2-3). So, for example doing 7 reps
with a weight you could lift 10 times (3 repetition in reserve).

5. LIFTING VELOCITY

Once that you’ve learned a good technique, focus on trying to performing the concentric phase of the
lift (when you go up) as quick as possible. Even if the velocity is pretty low because of the
weight, what matters is that you are trying to move it as fast as possible.

6. HOW MANY SETS AND REPS? 4-10 RM FOR 2/3 SETS WITH 2-3’ RECOVERY

This is the protocol that showed benefits on cyclists of different performance level (from amateur
to pro).

What does 4-10 RM means? *RM means “repetitions maximum”. So, for example, 10RM means using a weight
that permits to do maximum 10 repetitions.

7. 4 OR 10 RM? DAY UNDULATED PERIODIZED PROGRAM

What does it mean? Alternating one session with higher load and one with lower load, while
progressing form 10 to 4 RM through the weeks.

Here an example:

Week 1-3: 1st session 3x 10RM; 2nd session 3x 6RM

Week 4-6: 1st session 3x 8RM; 2nd session 3x 5RM

Week 7-10: 3x 6RM; 2nd session 3x 4RM
8. ANNUAL PERIODIZATION

Preparatory period: where there are no races (or no goal races) , 2 session per week for 10-12 weeks
(nov-feb in the northern hemisphere?) are enough to induce the performance gains. If you follow
periodization best practices, during this period the amount of high intensity training should be not
huge and so you should manage pretty easily these two gym sessions within the bike training program.

Competitive period: when you have planned races during the week or the training load on the bike is
very high and/or include a relevant time spent at high intensity, performing only one session per
week with reducing the volume (1 or 2 sets per exercise instead of 3) while maintaining high the
intensity (5-6 RM) have been shown to be enough to maintain for 3/4 months the previously obtained
strengthrelated performance gains.

GABRIELE’S COMMENT:

These are very good general guidelines and tips that I suggest to apply

On the other hand, these should be also adapted to the single athlete. For example, a rider with low
strength levels can benefit from including all the above advices for gym training for several years.
On the other hand a rider who has already high strength levels, high peak power output, high
anaerobic reserve, while a relatively low critical power, could just do some strength training
during the winter and then focus 100% (no gym training) on improving his/her aerobic qualities on
the bike during spring and summer. However this is just a personal opinion without data to support
it.

Gabriele Gallo, PhD in Exercise and Sport Sciences
