# Similar Aerobic Adaptations When Total Daily Workload is the Same

**Source:** knowledgeiswatt.substack.com/p/105-single-vs-multiple-daily-training **Date:** October
4, 2025

In the last years, the Norwegian Double-Threshold Days have become very popular. This method
consists of doing two sessions at medium/high intensity (the majority of Norwegians do them at an
intensity between 2.0-4.0 mmol/L of blood lactate) within the same day: one in the morning and one
in the afternoon. As a consequence of the great success Norwegian endurance athletes achieved in the
last years, performing multiple daily training session has become quite attractive within the
cycling world.

So, it can be interesting to see some scientific data which can help us to learn more about this
training strategy.

When the total daily workload is the same, do multiple daily training sessions induce greater
physiological adaptations compared to performing only one?

A study published by Mendes and Colleagues in 2022 on Scientific Reports (Federal University of the
Jequitinhonha and Mucuri Valleys, Brazil) can help to answer this question. (1)

WHAT DID THEY DO?

30 Male Wistar Rats (!) were divided into 3 different groups, which for 8 weeks trained in three
different ways:

1. Control Group: they did not perform any training.

2. 1x Day High Intensity Training (HIT) Group: 5 times per week, they performed single daily sessions of 15 min treadmill run, which consisted of:

3 min of warm-up, followed by six bouts of 1 min at 85-100% of speed at VO2max interspersed by 1 min
of passive recovery.

3. 3x Day High Intensity Training (HIT) Group: 5 times per week, they performed three daily sessions of 5 min with an interval of 4 h between sessions (8AM, 12PM and 4 PM), which consisted of:

1 min of warm-up, followed by two bouts of 1 min at 85-100% of speed at VO2max interspersed by 1 min
of passive recovery (total of 5 min).

As you can see, the overall daily duration, intensity and work done was similar between 1xDay HIT
Group and 3xDay HIT Group.

WHAT DID THEY FIND?

Image from Mendes et al. (1).

The maximal oxygen consumption (VO2max) resulted unchanged in the Control Group, while improved in a
similar between the 1xDay and the 3x Day Group:

Control Group: from 52±6 to 50±3 ml/min/kg 1xDay HIT Group: from 49±4 to 68±5 ml/min/kg 3xDay HIT
Group: from 52±5 to 67±3 ml/min/kg

In line with VO2max changes, the mitochondrial density (index of muscular aerobic capacity) of the
gastrocnemius muscle improved similarly between the 1xDay and 3xDay HIT Groups.

Image from Mendes et al. (1).

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAYS

When the total daily workload (both volume and intensity) is the same, multiple daily HIT training
sessions induce similar aerobic adaptations when compared to performing only one.

GABRIELE’S COMMENT

No much more to add. Even if the study is on rats and not humans, and the workouts were very shorts,
I think these data can be used as a ‘proof of concept’:

When the total daily workload (both volume and intensity) is the same, multiple daily HIT training
sessions induce similar aerobic adaptations when compared to performing only one.

Please, note the premise: ‘When total daily workload is the same’.

So, these data don’t mean that double-threshold days are not useful and effective at all. Can they
permit to accumulate more daily volume at high intensity (and so stimulate greater adaptations)
without compromising recovery? If yes, in which scenario can they be more effective?

We will see this and more about double-threshold days in the next KIW Research Note!
