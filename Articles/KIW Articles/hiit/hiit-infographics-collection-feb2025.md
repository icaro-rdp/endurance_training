1. Research Summary and Comment

LITERATURE BRIEFING:

Both in well-trained amateur and professional cyclists, short HIT intervals (3x13min
alternating 30s on and 15s off) seems to provide superior physiological and performance
adaptations compared to Long HIT intervals (4x5min with 2.5min rec.) when both formats
are performed at the maximum sustainable intensity. (1, 2)

When considering long HIT intervals, a fast start for each interval (1m30s at 100%
VO2max power followed by 3m30s at 85% of VO2max power) permits to spend more
time at a high percentage of VO2max (higher aerobic load) with a lower perceived effort
compared to maintain a steady pace (all 5min at 90% of VO2max power). (3)

When considering long high intensity intervals performed at the maximum sustainable
intensity, 4x8 min seems a better solution compared to 4x4min and 4x16min to induce
superior aerobic physiological and performance adaptations. (4)

Basing on these principles, here the HIT formats (in order of effectiveness) you should
pick if you want to optimize HIT’s derived aerobic adaptations:

1. Intermittent short intervals

2. Under-over intervals

3. 8 min constant pace intervals

4. 5 min fast start intervals

Note that the above stated order of effectiveness of these formats is based on studies
that evaluated the average responses of a group of cyclists, without taking into
account the physiological profile of each rider.

A further explorative analysis on this topic (6) showed that:

In riders with second lactate threshold at a lower %of VO2max (~ <83%)
(sprinters and punchy guys?), the short intermittent intervals permits to
spend more time at a high % of VO2max, and so they should be more effective
than other HIT formats in improving aerobic qualities.

In riders with second lactate threshold at a high % of VO2max (climbers. time
trialist?), the under-over and constant pace HIT formats are practically equally
effective as the short intermittent intervals in accumulating time at a high
fraction of VO2max. Given this, the under-over and constant pace HIT could
be more appropriate for them, as they represent an effort more similar to their
race demands compared to the intermittent short intervals.

Ingesting sodium bicarbonate (0.4 g/kg) before HIT sessions for some weeks can boost
training adaptations and performance. (7)
