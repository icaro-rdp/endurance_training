# 4x8' Better Than 4x4' or 4x16'

**Source:** knowledgeiswatt.substack.com/p/42-hit-long-intervals-4x8-better  
**Date:** November 5, 2024

High intensity training (HIT, that is doing intervals above second threshold/FTP/critical power) is
a powerful stimulus to improve endurance performance. However, it requires a great physical
and mental effort and leads to fatigue in the following days. As a consequence, it must be
carefully dosed and can represent only a small part of the annual training program. So, it is
important understanding how to perform HIT sessions to get the most from this
powerful but limited use weapon

In previous KisW Research Notes, we saw that short (30s on-15s off) high-intensity intervals
stimulate superior physiological and performance adaptations compared to long (4-5 minutes)
high intensity intervals both in well-trained and elite cyclists. When performing the 4-5 minutes
long intervals, we saw (read more here) that a fast start can be beneficial as it permits to ride at
higher fraction of VO2max (a factor which have been linked to HIT efficacy) when compared to
keeping a constant pace throughout the 4-5 minutes.

In this KisW Research Note we continue to explore the differences in physiological and
performance improvement after different HIT sessions.

Which high intensity long intervals session is better to stimulate physiological
adaptations and improve performance? 4x4min, 4x8min or 4x16 min?

A study published by Stephen Seiler and Colleagues (University of Agder, Norway) on
Scandinavian Journal of Medicine and Science in Sports in 2013 tried to answer this question.
(1)

WHAT DID THEY DO?

They recruited 35 well-trained amateur cyclists (age ~40, VO2max ~52) which for 7
weeks were divided into 4 different training groups:

Control (CON): they just rode about 9 hours per week at low intensity.

4x4 Group: they completed 4hrs at low intensity + 2xweek 4x4 min with 2min
recovery HIT sessions.

4x8 Group: they completed 4hrs at low intensity + 2xweek 4x8 min with 2min
recovery HIT sessions.

4x16 Group: they completed 4hrs at low intensity + 2xweek 4x16 min with 3min
recovery HIT sessions.

All the high intensity intervals sessions were performed at the maximum sustainable
intensity for the prescribed duration. These resulted in the following average physiological
parameter:

4x16 min: 4.9 mMol/L blood lactate, 100% of second threshold power

4x8 min: 9.6 mMol/L blood lactate, 113% of second threshold power

4x4min: 13.2 mMol/L blood lactate, 131 % of second threshold power

The blood lactate reported is the average of the sample taken after the third and the fourth
intervals during all the HIT sessions.

WHAT DID THEY FOUND?

Physiological and Performance changes after the 7 training weeks tended to be
superior in 4x8min Group compared to the other groups.

Here the changes on average for each parameter in each group:

VO2max:

CON: from 52.7 to 54.5 ml/min/kg (+3.4%)

4x4: from 50.4 to 53.2 ml/min/kg (+5.5%)

4x8: from 52.8 to 58.3 ml/min/kg (+10.4%)

4x16: from 51.1 to 54.4 ml/min/kg (+6.5%)

Power at 4 mmol/L of blood lactate:

CON: from 222 to 239 W (+7.7%)

4x4: from 220 to 238 W (+8.2%)

4x8: from 241 to 280 W (+16.2%)

4x16: from 228 to 249 W (+9.2%)

% changes in Vo2max and power output at 4 mmol/L of blood lactate concentration in the four different
groups. Data from Seiler et al. (1).

Time to Exhaustion at 80% of VO2max power (VO2max power was calculated
before starting the 7 training weeks):

CON: from 10.9 to 12.1 min (+11%)

4x4: from 9.7 to 15.8 min (+63%)

4x8: from 11.9 to 22.7 min (+91%)

4x16: from 8.5 to 13.8 min (+62%)

% changes in time to exhaustion at 80% of VO2max calculated before starting
the 7 weeks. Data from Seiler et al. (1)

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAYS:

When considering long high intensity intervals performed at the maximum
sustainable intensity, 4x8 min seems a better solution compared to 4x4min and
4x16min to induce superior aerobic physiological and performance adaptations.

GABRIELE’S COMMENT

These data show another effective weapon to add to our high intensity arsenal:
8min intervals. While in this study they were performed at the maximum sustainable
intensity (which resulted in 113% of second threshold), I would suggest to lower a little bit
the intensity in order to be able to repeat the workout many times even when the weekly
training load is high. I would say that 105-110% can be enough to stimulate adaptations,
but this is just a personal consideration.

When implementing this intervals during a training plan, I would consider the following
basic training principles:

progressive overload: starting from a 2x8 and then progressing to 3x8 and 4x8
can be a good strategy (I would not exceed 4 intervals during the same workout).

specificity: I see this workout more specific (and so more effective to apply) for
road cycling climbers/time trialists or for mountain bike marathon, as it mimics more
the long steady pace efforts of these races. For cyclocross and MTB XCO the 30-15
short intervals saw previously could mimic more the specific race demands and so
they can be more effective.
