# Strength Training During Competition Period: Yes or No?

**Source:** knowledgeiswatt.substack.com/p/64-strength-training-during-competition  
**Date:** March 3, 2025

In previous KisW Research Notes, we saw that:

during the preparatory period, 10 weeks of heavy gym strength training improves both
short (30s) and long (40min) duration w/kg despite a small increase in body mass (less
than 1kg) in elite cyclists. Read more here

heavy gym strength training positively impacts also durability (fatigue resistance during
very long efforts). Read more here

unilateral gym strength training seems better than bilateral to improve cycling
performance. Read more here

You can read the practical guidelines on how to perform gym strength training to improve
cycling performance here.

From a periodization point of view, it is usually easier to perform gym strength training during
the preparatory period, given the lower training intensity and lack of competitions. On the other
hand, during the competition period, the higher training intensity combined with the presence of
competitions and the related travels, make more difficult to perform gym strength training. So, it
is common that even many top-level professional cyclists did not perform any gym strength
training during the competition period. For example, two case studies analysing training
patterns of 5 riders finishing in the top 5 of final general classification of Giro d’Italia and Tour
de France between 2015 and 2020 reported that none of the cyclists performed gym strength
training during the competition period. (1,2)

Is maintaining gym strength training during the competition period beneficial for road
cycling performance?

Let’s try to answer using data from two studies form the Norwegian (Lillehammer University)
research team leady by Professor Bent Rønnestad.

1. WHAT HAPPENS WHEN GYM STRENGTH TRAINING IS INTERRUPTED
DURING THE COMPETITION PERIOD?

The Study:

Rønnestad BR, Hansen J, Hollan I, Spencer M, Ellefsen S. Impairment of Performance
Variables After In-Season Strength-Training Cessation in Elite Cyclists. Int J Sports Physiol
Perform. 2016 Sep;11(6):727-735.

How Did They Train?

14 elite cyclists (mean age 20, mean VO2max 75) were divided into a Gym+Bike group
(G+B) and a bike only group (B).

During the 25 weeks of the preparatory period, the 2 groups trained similarly (~13.5
hrs/week) on the bike. The only between groups difference was that G+B performed gym
heavy strength training sessions (3 × 4-10 RM, 1 to 2 days per week) with the same
exercises described here), while B did not perform any gym strength training.

*B performed ~1.5hrs per week more on the bike to match the total G+B training volume.

*RM means “repetitions maximum”. For example, 10RM means doing 10 reps using a
weight that permits to do maximum 10 repetitions.

When the competition period started, for 8 weeks, G+B interrupted completely gym
strength training and performed similar training to the ‘bike only’ group.

What Did They Find?

Adaptations during the 25 weeks preparatory favoured G+B compared to B only:

30s maximal mean power (W/Kg): +2±5% vs. -2±4%

maximal aerobic power output (W/Kg): +3±3% vs. -3±6%

power output at 4mmol/L (W/kKg): +4±3% vs. -5±14%

However, from pre to post the 8 weeks of the competition period (so comparing
week 25 vs week 33), the physiological and performance parameters decreased more in
G+B compared to B only:

30s maximal mean power (W/Kg): -4±4% vs. +1±2%

maximal aerobic power output (W/Kg): -2±4% vs.+ 4±7%

power output at 4mmol/L (W/Kg): 0±6% vs. +9±14%

In addition, some thigh muscle mass gain obtained during the preparatory period only by G+B
group, came back to baseline after 8 weeks of stopping gym training.

Doing easy maths, we can appreciate that after all the protocol (week 1 vs week 33) the
changes in performance and physiological parameters were very similar between the two
groups. So all the superior cycling related physiological and performance improvement
obtained by heavy gym strength training are nullified after only 8 weeks stopping to hit
the gym.

Let’s see now the opposite scenario: what happens if strength training is maintained
even during the competition period?

2. WHAT HAPPENS WHEN GYM STRENGTH TRAINING IS MAINTAINED EVEN
DURING THE COMPETITION PERIOD?

The Study

Rønnestad BR, Hansen EA, Raastad T. In-season strength maintenance training increases
well-trained cyclists' performance. Eur J Appl Physiol. 2010 Dec;110(6):1269-82.

How Did They Train?

12 well-trained amateur cyclists (VO2max ~66, age ~30) were divided into a gym+bike
group (G+B) and a bike only group (B) that trained similarly for 12 weeks of the
preparatory period, with the only difference that G+B performed 2xweek gym heavy
strength training sessions structured again as those described here.

When competition period started, for 13 weeks, B continued to train only on the bike,
while G+B maintain one gym strength training session but reduced the frequency and
load of these sessions: 1x week, 1/2 x 5 @80-85% 1RM.

What Did They Find?

Performance adaptations during the 12 weeks preparatory favoured G+B compared
to B only:

Changes in 40min Time Trial Power between week 1 and week 12:

+8±2% vs +4±1%

Differently from study 1, in this case the performance advantage obtained during
the preparatory period by ‘G+B’ vs ‘B only’ were retained and even amplified after
13 weeks of the competition period (25 weeks from the start of the study):

Changes in 40min Time Trial Power between week 1 and week 25:

+14±3% vs. +4±1%,

In this case, also the thigh muscle mass gains obtained in the preparatory period by the G+B
group were retained after 13 weeks of competition period.

STUDY 1+STUDY 2 PRACTICAL TAKEAWAYS:

Stopping strength training during the competition period nullifies the cycling
performance adaptations previously obtained in the gym during the preparation
period.

During the competition period, 1 session per week with low volume per session is
enough to maintain the previously obtained gains and further improve
performance.

The nullified or retained positive performance adaptations seem to be linked to
maintaining or losing thigh muscle mass.

GABRIELE’S COMMENT

These data globally suggest that one gym strength training session per week even
during the competition period should be maintained to optimize performance.

Of course, this should be carefully inserted within the competition period
microcycles (which are likely to include a lot of high intensity work both from
training sessions and race) to allow the right balance between load and recovery to
optimize race performance.

Gabriele Gallo, PhD in Exercise and Sport Sciences
